# Dragon Horns, Pulau Tioman — publication evidence

Checked 2026-10-21. Scope is the Dragon Horns big-wall cluster above Kampung Mukut, not the Nipah, Juara or bouldering areas on the same island. theCrag is a community database. Ferry facts come from operators, not from the Marine Department.

## Article facts

**Rock and style.** Granite spires, jungle big-wall climbing. Two summits; the east face is described as looking substantially taller than the south face, "maybe 600 meters". Established routes reach about 300 m on the south tower; the east peak takes sun from morning to noon.

**Routes and grades.** 14 routes across three listed subareas: South Tower 12, North Tower 1, Mystery Wall 1. French 5c+–7c+ on the crag page; the hardest pitch on Naga is 6b+ and the easiest full line in the table is Freebird at 6a. 54 ascents logged. The breadcrumb also shows a "Puncak Anak" entry with a 100 m trad route, but it is absent from the area count table.

**Season.** The north-east monsoon dominates November–February. The ferry operator's schedule page marks April–September as peak season and warns that sailings can be suspended, especially December–February. Its FAQ says the ferry may be prohibited for a whole day by the authorities and does not run from Tanjung Gemok in monsoon. One commercial speedboat site claims a hard 1 November–28 February closure for all operators; that claim was not confirmed against a Marine Department notice, so treat the exact dates as contested. Practical reading: plan March–October, and treat November–February as unreliable rather than formally closed.

**Access.** Fly or drive to Mersing or Tanjung Gemok, ferry 1.5–2 hours to Genting, then water taxi to Kampung Mukut. Mukut has no jetty usable by the public ferry — this contradicts older beta that says take the ferry to Mukut. From Mukut, follow the shoreline to stilt huts, take the established path uphill, cut left through dense brush to a ravine or riverbed, then skirt left along the wall base to the high point that marks the start of Waking Dream. Hours of jungle bushwhacking with gear, food and water; mobile coverage is limited and the only support is villagers.

**Fees and permissions.** No climbing fee or permit found. Boat transfers are the real cost: theCrag quotes a speedboat at US$50–75 one way from Berjaya, or RM50–70 return for the speedboat to Mukut; those figures are undated. No marine-park climbing permit found.

**Bolts and gear.** Naga has bolted anchors and bolts but is runout on easier ground; a small cam rack helps. Benjo Delight is mostly trad with pitons and one short aid section, and all but one anchor is bolted. Waking Dream needs a full trad rack plus knifeblades, lost arrows and hooks. No dated bolt-condition report for any route was found, and no rebolting organisation is named.

**Contacts.** Tam and Uncle Sam at Simukut Hiilview are the named local contacts; a logbook with beta is kept at Kampung Mukut.

**Emergency.** 999. No nearest hospital, clinic or rescue service on Tioman could be verified.

## Gaps

- No Marine Department primary source for the claimed 1 November–28 February closure; operator pages disagree.
- No verified ferry fare, timetable or Mukut water-taxi price for the 2026–27 season.
- Waking Dream length and grade conflict between theCrag (300 m, 7c+ aid) and the 2013 blog (400 m, V 5.9 A2).
- No current topo for most routes; only Freebird's topo host is named.
- No dated anchor or bolt inspection.
- Nearest medical facility and rescue capability unverified.
- No climbing fee or permit requirement confirmed either way by a land manager.
