## Crag-level facts

- Grade histogram for the 326 routes: 4a×2, 4a+×2, 4b×2, 4c×4, 5a×5, 5b×16, 5b+×1, 5c×22, 5c+×3, 6a×36, 6a+×35, 6b×33, 6b+×32, 6c×32, 6c+×12, 7a×17, 7a+×19, 7b×26, 7b+×12, 7c×6, 7c+×3, 8a×2, 8a+×2.
- Summary: "Ha Long Bay region of Vietnam, a sweep of water from which approximately 1,600 limestone karst islands soar, lies in the Gulf of Tonkin just off Vietnam's northern coastline and is part of the UNESCO world natural heritage." "Many of the walls that rise directly from the water are ideal for Deep Water Solo (DWS). Other faces stand over uninhabited sandy beaches, making for a unique and isolated sport climbing paradise."
- Getting there: "The easiest way to climb in Ha Long Bay is by staying on the southern main island of Cat Ba. In Cat Ba Town there is a wide selection of Hotels... From there, you can easily reach the crags on the island and in the bay." "Getting to Cat Ba from Hanoi is quite easy and usually takes about five hours. You actually don't need to go to Ha Long City if you are mainly interested in climbing."
- Independent climbing: "To explore the sport climbing potential of the bay you will need a basketboat with driver and the access passes for the beaches. Both can be easily organised at the Asia Outdoors shop." (the shop reference is stale — Asia Outdoors closed; Cat Ba Island page now names Cat Ba Climbing and Langur Adventures.)
- DWS safety: "Going on your own or with an unlicensed basket boat driver can be potentially dangerous. First of all it is not so easy to find the right crags and secondly you cannot start climbing out of the water, since there is a notch at the base of the cliffs: you'll need a boat. Thirdly, you need intimate knowledge of the cliffs to avoid climbing at too low a tide or hitting one of the underwater rocks."
- Tides and season: "The winter months are ideal for sport climbing, when the temperatures are cool (15-20 degrees celcius) and there is only moderate precipitation (5-25 mm a month). The downside of the winter are the unfavourable tides, which make DWS less enjoyable and only occasionally possible. A tide of over 2 meters is necessary to safely DWS in the bay and there are much more days with high tide during the daytime in the summer than in the winter." "The ideal time to have fun on Ha Long Bay's DWS and sport climbing cliffs are the spring (April - May) and autumn months (September - November). The weather is generally good and there are generally over 15 days a month where you can DWS."
- Legality: "climbing within Ha Long Bay remains illegal, and the harbour police do enforce often, this means climbing at 'The Face' or DWS at 'Le MeKong' is a challenge, and although some climbers have managed to get there with no issues, others have been swiftly moved on by the harbour police in the bay. Boat drivers leaving Cat Ba island to go to Ha Long Bay (The Face etc.) also risk loosing their boats." "please DO NOT BRIBE officials, some have tried this in the past, and it sets a bad president that will lead to more complications as time goes on."
- Ethics: "Even stainless bolts tend to only last a few years before rusting out. All efforts should be made to rebolt old routes (and equip new ones) with the best possible technology (titanium bolts glued in with Hilti RE-500)."
- History: first climbers from the 1990s / early 2000s — Lynn Hill, Todd Skinner, Scott Milton, Paul Piana, Greg Child, Arnaud Petit; Greg Child and Andy Parkin established early traditional multi-pitch lines in the bay; Neil Gresham, Tim Emmett, Seb Grieve, Grant Farquhar, Chris Lindner; Tim Emmett and Klem Loskot featured in Big Up Productions' Dosage Vol III; Chris Lindner in The Players.
- Guidebook paragraph (older, from the 2016 edition era): second printed guide for the area after Erik Ferjentsik's 2008 "Vietnam – A Climber's Guide"; 2016 edition, 140 pages, more than 120 sport routes on 13 crags 4a–8a and more than 230 DWS lines on 24 cliffs 4a–7c+; 17 GBP.

## Handling notes

- DWS routes carry FR grades like sport routes, so the four-tier tables can hold them, but the article separates the sport-climbing tables from the DWS tables because the hazards are different (tide, water entry, no rope).
- Taxi Girl is flagged with a warning badge on the crag page, and Monkey wall's nine routes are all unnamed — the article says so rather than presenting them as pickable lines.
- Peanut Butter & Jelly is listed as 4 pitches / 39 bolts / 72 m, and the Slo Pony Wall sector carries a "Free-Hanging Fall Risk and Rope Ascending Skills Required" access warning. Both facts are printed.
- The 150 m DWS line "The Long Way to Come Home" at Sea Face is the longest route in scope; the article uses it to explain why a 70 m rope is not a complete answer for this area.
- The two Crag pages disagree about who sells beach access passes (Asia Outdoors on the Lan Hạ page, Cat Ba Climbing and Langur's on the Cat Ba Island page). Asia Outdoors has closed, so the article points to the two current operators and flags the stale reference.
- The Climb Europe / Needle Sports 2023 country guide is the paper reference; the 2016 Ha Long Bay guide quoted in the crag page's own text is the older edition of the same area. The article lists both with their years and says which is the paper source of truth.
