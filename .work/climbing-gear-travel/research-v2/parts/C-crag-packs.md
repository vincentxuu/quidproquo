## Notes / unverified / discontinued

- **Popular models discontinued or on the way out:** Mystery Ranch Tower 47 (official 404, EU retailers say "no longer available"); Mammut Neon Gear 45 (official page says "not available anymore", successor Neon 45); Metolius Freerider (official page: "Product is being discontinued"); Arc'teryx Konseal 40 (official page no longer resolves); the original Metolius Crag Station (USD 159.95, sold out, dropped from the Packs collection; the Crag Station Classic replaces it).
- **Grivel Rocker 45:** a new version is launching (1402 g, preorder). Retailers are still clearing the old version (2087 g). Don't mix the two weights.
- **Beal Combi Cliff II volume:** the official page says 35 L; several retailers say 45 L. The table uses the official figure.
- **BD Pipe Dream 45:** no official weight is listed. The Shopify grams value (454) shows up on several unrelated BD items too, so it's treated as a placeholder and left out.
- **Patagonia Cragsmith 32L:** the US page couldn't be fetched through Jina (Patagonia queue page) or Exa (not found), and the NZ product URL returns 404. AU still lists it (search snippet, not opened). Status is unconfirmed.
- **Rope tarp column:** "N" means there's only a rope strap or attachment point, with no tarp included.
- **Brands checked, no crag pack in this category:** Ortovox (Trad 35 is an alpine climbing pack, not a crag pack); Deuter (Gravity Expedition 45+ is an alpine/mountaineering pack); Simond/Decathlon (only the Klimb rope bag and alpine packs turned up, no crag pack); Ocún (only the Ropemat rope mat/bag); Petzl (only the KLIFF in its sport line; the BUCKET is a pro rope bag); Fish (haul bags only). The Edelrid "Spotter" in the brief is actually the City Spotter, a 20 L upcycled city pack, so it's not a crag pack.
- **Reviews before 2020, excluded:** Gripped on the Crag Station (2019-12), Climbing.com Petzl Kliff Editors' Choice (2019), UKC rope-bag group test (2017), UKC "Crag Packs for Trad Climbers" (older), and Alpinist/climbinggearreviews on the Cragsmith 45 (2018).
- **Opened but not cited:** OutdoorGearLab's "best climbing backpack" (2023-09) covers alpine/multi-pitch packs, not crag packs.
