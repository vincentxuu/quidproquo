## Actually fetched sources

- S1 Corky Carroll's Surf School, [Surf Lingo Every Beginner Should Know](https://www.surfschool.net/blog/surf-lingo-for-beginners/): wind section defines offshore as land → ocean; actual example discusses offshore/glassy conditions. Paddling section describes propulsion on a board to catch or pass waves. Supports professional meanings, not all four self-written questions.
- S2 WB Surf Camp, Liz (2025-07-23), [How to Read the Lineup Before You Paddle Out](https://wbsurfcamp.com/surfing-new-breaks-how-to-read-the-lineup-before-you-paddle-out/): explicitly suggests asking a local where the paddle-out is; discusses finding a paddle-out path and observing currents, breaking waves, tides and wind. Supports both communicative purpose and paddle-out terminology. Do not turn the article into a universally safe entry prescription.
- S3 REI, [Rock Climbing Terms & Lingo Guide](https://www.rei.com/learn/expert-advice/rock-climbing-glossary.html): belay involves managing a climbing rope to limit a fall; beta means information about a climb; crux means hardest move or move sequence; route means path/moves up a climb. Term definitions are direct evidence; self-written conversation is adapted.
- S4 REI, [Rock Climbing Techniques & Moves](https://www.rei.com/learn/expert-advice/climbing-techniques.html): discusses individual moves and looking for foot placements; once a foot is set it stays still while making the next move. Supports distinction between move, foot placement and foothold; does not supply our question wording. Contributor Jay Parks has AMGA Single Pitch Instructor certification and was a senior REI Outdoor School instructor.
- S5 British Council, [Requests, offers and invitations](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/grammar/english-grammar-reference/requests-offers-invitations): could you + verb is a polite request structure. Supports request construction, not climbers' frequency of the exact sentence.
- S6 REI, [Rock Climbing Commands & Communication](https://www.rei.com/learn/expert-advice/communication-climbing.html): distinguishes climber/belayer readiness and other climbing commands. Useful boundary: requesting a partner is distinct from the subsequent agreed readiness communication. Do not teach “Could you belay me?” as a readiness command.

## Limits

All eight card/swap sentences are adapted teaching examples. None was found verbatim in the fetched sources. S2 directly supports the local paddle-out question context, but the precise card wording remains adapted. Existing sentences have no identified grammatical contradiction; term and intent alignment do not establish “most natural,” “most common,” regional preference, or an attested native exchange. Keep these claims out of product copy. Remaining evidence gaps are exact conversational attestation for the broad wave question and authored swaps; mark their full-sentence evidence scope honestly rather than treating professional terminology pages as complete utterance proof.
