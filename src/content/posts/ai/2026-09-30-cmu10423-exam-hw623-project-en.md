An exam tests reasoning under a fixed question. A paper report tests your judgment of someone else’s evidence. A project tests your ability to produce reproducible evidence. These are distinct learning tasks, now taught separately while this URL remains an assessment map.

## A worked comparison

Teaching example: computing a lower average loss for model A tests arithmetic. Reading a paper additionally requires tracing data, baselines, and conditions. A project must retain code, data versions, seeds, and outcomes. If A is tested on 100 easy cases and B on 100 hard ones, averages do not establish that A is the better model. Explain the problem in an exam, identify it in a report, and run a shared-test-set comparison in a project. Each step produces different evidence.

## Check your understanding

Do working code, fluent writing, or a correct exam option independently validate a research conclusion?

<details>
<summary>Answer and criterion</summary>

No. Code needs sound evaluation, writing needs evidence and attribution, and an answer needs reasoning that survives a variation. The three lessons provide separate criteria.

</details>
