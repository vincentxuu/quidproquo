A conventional autoencoder compresses an input into a vector and reconstructs it. A VAE instead assigns each input a latent distribution, connecting reconstruction and generation within a probabilistic model. We separate the two ELBO terms, calculate Gaussian KL, and identify the reparameterization gradient. The lecture's closing zero/few-shot section is covered in the ICL guide, not presented as a VAE mechanism.

Prerequisites are Gaussian means and variances, expectations, and autograd. Rather than merely adding a KL term, begin with the reason an approximate inference distribution is needed.

## Generation and inference point in different directions

The generative model samples z∼p(z), then x from pθ(x|z), often choosing p(z)=N(0,I). Training observes x and asks which latent values explain it. The exact posterior pθ(z|x) is generally difficult to evaluate, so an encoder supplies a tractable qφ(z|x), such as a diagonal Gaussian with learned mean and log variance.

This q distribution performs approximate inference. Reconstruction can encode an image and decode its sampled z; unconditional generation samples z directly from the prior. Both use the decoder, but their latent inputs have different sources.

## Derive the bound

For a fixed x, log pθ(x) equals ELBO plus KL(qφ(z|x)||pθ(z|x)). KL is nonnegative, so ELBO is a lower bound. Expanding it gives E_q[log pθ(x|z)]−KL(qφ(z|x)||p(z)).

Minimizing the negative ELBO therefore combines reconstruction negative log likelihood with a penalty measuring the approximate posterior's divergence from the prior. Reconstruction asks the latent to explain x; the KL constrains its distribution. It is not an image-distance term.

Multiplying KL by an arbitrary β does not preserve this exact likelihood-bound interpretation. A β-VAE uses a modified objective. Our calculation uses the original β=1 decomposition.

## Calculate two inputs completely

In this original scalar example, input A has x=1 and encoder outputs μ=1, σ=0.5. Input B has x=0 and outputs μ=0, σ=1. The decoder is p(x|z)=N(z,1), and the prior is N(0,1).

Reparameterize as z=μ+σε. Draw ε=0 for A and ε=1 for B, giving z=1 in both cases. Unit-variance Gaussian negative log likelihood is (x−z)²/2 + ln(2π)/2. Its squared-error part is zero for A and 0.5 for B; the common constant is about 0.9189.

The scalar KL is (μ²+σ²−1−ln σ²)/2. A contributes approximately 0.8181 and B contributes zero. Omitting the shared reconstruction constant, this one-draw batch estimate of negative ELBO is approximately 0.6591; including it gives 1.5780.

A single draw is not the exact expectation. Here E[(x−z)²/2]=((x−μ)²+σ²)/2, giving 0.125 for A and 0.5 for B. The exact expected batch objective without the constant is approximately 0.7216. Monte Carlo estimates can lie above or below the expectation.

## Follow gradients through randomness

The random ε is independent of the encoder parameters, while z=μ+σε is differentiable in μ and σ. For A's sampled ε=0, ∂z/∂μ=1 and ∂z/∂σ=0. Its reconstruction residual is zero, so this draw provides no reconstruction gradient. KL still contributes derivative μ=1 with respect to μ and σ−1/σ=−1.5 with respect to σ.

Perfect reconstruction for one draw therefore does not minimize the entire objective. σ must remain positive; a common implementation outputs log variance and uses σ=exp(logvar/2).

## Interpret collapse and discrete latents carefully

A powerful decoder can ignore z, making q resemble the prior with a small KL while the latent carries little input information. Falling KL alone does not establish useful representations. Check reconstruction, generation, and latent usage together.

The slides also discuss VQ-VAE, which quantizes continuous representations into a codebook. Quantization and codebook learning use different machinery; this Gaussian KL is not automatically the loss for discrete codec tokens. The audio representation guides revisit that route. Here the core target is continuous variational inference and its gradients.

## Variations with answers

Change A's encoder to μ=0, σ=1. KL becomes zero, but expected reconstruction half-squared error becomes ((1−0)²+1)/2=1, compared with 0.125 before. Matching the prior has a reconstruction tradeoff.

For ε=2, μ=1, and σ=0.5, z=2, ∂z/∂μ=1, and ∂z/∂σ=2. Reparameterization enables differentiation through a sampled value; it does not remove randomness. Assignment 2 next checks diffusion tensors, and latent diffusion later connects compressed representations to generation.
