Bitchu (備中, びっちゅう) is not one cliff but a group of limestone crags scattered across the Takahashi River basin around Takahashi City, Okayama Prefecture. The local climbers' group TCNet (高梁川流域クライミング交流会) says in its [area guide](https://takahashigawa-climb.net/area/bitchu/) (in Japanese) that "大小25のクライミングエリアが点在" (25 climbing areas, large and small) between the Hayama area in Nariwa-cho and the Shirataki crag in Yoshii-cho, Ibara City, with the densest cluster around the Yuze Climbing Park (用瀬クライミング広場) in Bitchu-cho.

What stands out is the municipal support. Takahashi City has an [ordinance establishing the Yuzedake Free Climbing Park](https://www.city.takahashi.lg.jp/reiki_int/reiki_honbun/r052RG00000575.html) (in Japanese), whose stated purpose is to promote "フリークライミング" and exchange with city dwellers. The flip side: most walls, approaches, and parking spots are on private land, so there are more rules than at a typical crag, and they differ by sub-area.

## What kind of crag is it

| Item | Details | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Location | Takahashi City, Okayama, and surroundings (former Bitchu-cho, Kawakami-cho, Nariwa-cho, etc.) | [TCNet](https://takahashigawa-climb.net/area/bitchu/), [CLIMBING-net](https://www.climbing-net.com/iwaba_detail/%E5%82%99%E4%B8%AD/) |
| Rock | Limestone | CLIMBING-net, [ROCKTOPO](https://www.rocktopo.net/areas/153) |
| Angle | 90–110 degrees | CLIMBING-net |
| Style | Mostly bolted sport climbing, a few boulder problems | ROCKTOPO |
| Development | Started around 1988 by Shigeru Moriwaki, Koshiro Habara, and others; JMCC members including Hideaki Daiku and Makoto Kitayama soon opened 2 Runze and Jarou-iwa | CLIMBING-net |

CLIMBING-net notes that because it is steep limestone, climbers come not only from the Kyoto–Osaka–Kobe region but also from the Kanto area. TCNet calls it one of Japan's top limestone areas "名実ともに" (in name and in fact).

### Size and grade distribution (checked 2026-10-12)

The numbers vary widely between sites:

- **[ROCKTOPO](https://www.rocktopo.net/areas/153)**: 347 routes — 343 face, 1 crack, 3 boulder problems. Face routes by grade: ungraded 4, 5.2+ 37, 5.10a+ 55, 5.11a+ 104, 5.12a+ 108, 5.13a+ 28, 5.14a+ 7.
- **[CLIMBING-net](https://www.climbing-net.com/iwaba_detail/%E5%82%99%E4%B8%AD/)**: the route-count field says "300本"; the overview says over 300 routes.
- **[TCNet](https://takahashigawa-climb.net/area/bitchu/)**: "総ルート数300本以上", with many routes of 5.13 and harder.
- **[theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/japan/area/920977260)**: only 41 routes under "Bitchu / 備中"; English coverage is thin.

The number of sub-areas also differs: the TCNet area guide says 25, while TCNet's own [FAQ](https://takahashigawa-climb.net/faq/) says "現在約30のクライミングエリアがあります" (about 30 areas).

ROCKTOPO groups the crags into six sub-areas (2026-10-12):

Who it suits: the bulk of the routes sit in the 5.11 and 5.12 grades, with 35 routes at 5.13 and up (ROCKTOPO), so this is a destination for intermediate-to-advanced sport climbers. Beginner terrain is limited; the area TCNet marks "初級～中級者向き" (beginner to intermediate) is 2 Runze (5.9–5.12b). With only a handful of boulder problems, it is not a bouldering trip.

## When to go

| Source | Season |
|---|---|
| ROCKTOPO | Season field lists all 12 months |
| CLIMBING-net | "通年。夏は奥の院、川、権現谷が良い。冬は権現谷以外。" (Year-round. In summer, Oku-no-in, Kawa, and Gongendani are good; in winter, anywhere except Gongendani.) |
| TCNet area guide | Year-round; in summer, Kawa area, Gongen, and Katsuyama are relatively cool if there is no seepage; in winter, the sunny New Area, Sugita Rock, and Jarou-iwa are the popular choices |

In practice:

- **There is always somewhere to climb**; choosing a season really means choosing a sub-area.
- **Winter**: head for south-facing, sunny walls. TCNet says the New Area is "南向きのため冬でも晴れた日は暖かく" (south-facing, so warm on clear winter days) and rates November–April as best; Jarou-iwa is also listed for November–April. The shared caveat: days with strong seasonal winds are cold.
- **Summer**: CLIMBING-net and the TCNet area guide both list Gongendani as a summer option, but TCNet's own [Gongendani route guide](https://takahashigawa-climb.net/area/bitchu/gongen/) says otherwise: it gets a lot of sun and is hot in summer, conditions are poor from the rainy season through midsummer, autumn to late autumn is best, and winter is "寒すぎて無理" (too cold). Both views are listed here; check current local information before you go.
- **Rainy season and after rain**: limestone seeps. TCNet's [FAQ](https://takahashigawa-climb.net/faq/) says most walls are steeper than vertical and stay out of direct rain, but seepage, condensation, and drips often wet the rock — soaked walls on a sunny day are everyday business on limestone. The 2 Runze and Gongendani guides both mention frequent seepage in early summer and summer.

## Getting there

The sub-areas are spread out, so the directions below are to **Yuze hut** (用瀬小屋, in Shito-Yuze, Bitchu-cho, Takahashi City, inside the Yuze Climbing Park), which TCNet uses as the base. Other sub-areas such as Hayama and Gongendani require further driving; without a car you are quite limited.

### Public transport

1. Take the JR Hakubi Line to **Bitchu-Takahashi Station (高梁駅)**.
2. Transfer to a Bihoku Bus bound for Sakamoto, Nishi-Aburano, or Hirakawa and ride about 30 minutes to **Yuzebashi (用瀬橋)** ([CLIMBING-net](https://www.climbing-net.com/iwaba_detail/%E5%82%99%E4%B8%AD/), [TCNet](https://takahashigawa-climb.net/area/bitchu/)). [Jorudan's stop page for Yuzebashi](https://mb.jorudan.co.jp/os/bus/3308/stop/174867.html) (in Japanese) lists two routes serving it: Takahashi–Furumachi–Hirakawa and Takahashi–Nariwa–Sakamoto.
3. Walk from Yuzebashi: TCNet says 10 minutes to Yuze hut; CLIMBING-net says 3 minutes (its destination is given as "用瀬の嶽").

None of these pages list frequencies or times; check the timetable on the [Bihoku Bus](https://www.bihokubus.com/) website (in Japanese) before you go.

### By car

TCNet's driving times to Yuze hut:

- From **Kayo IC** on the Okayama Expressway, via Takahashi, Route 313, and Nariwa: about 30 minutes.
- From **Hokubo IC** on the Chugoku Expressway: about 40 minutes.
- From **Fukuyama-higashi IC** on the Sanyo Expressway, via Route 313 and Ibara: about 50 minutes.

All routes end by turning off Route 313 onto Prefectural Road 33. Soon after, a sign for "用瀬クライミング広場" points you right across a blue bridge; turn left and follow the narrow road along the left bank of the Nariwa River to the hut. TCNet notes that local traffic has priority on this road. CLIMBING-net lists only the first two interchanges, with the same times.

Parking: ROCKTOPO lists nine parking spots (広場駐車場, 夫婦岩の駐車場, 長屋坂前の駐車スペース, 天龍の駐車場, and others) but gives no capacities. TCNet's [rules page](https://takahashigawa-climb.net/iwaba_manner/) says each area has its own parking arrangements and asks for particular care at Hayama, Nagayazaka, Gongendani, Kawa, River Park, and others; if a lot is full, carpool or walk from Yuze.

### Staying

The Yuze Climbing Park has Yuze hut and an auto campground. The ordinance's schedule sets "base fees" of ¥1,000 per person per night for the hut and ¥500 per person per night for the campground; the actual fee is set by the city or the designated manager within 0.5–1.5 times the base ([Article 11](https://www.city.takahashi.lg.jp/reiki_int/reiki_honbun/r052RG00000575.html)). The fee currently charged was not found in the sources checked. Separately, TCNet announced in April 2026 that the [Hirakawa Ikoi-no-Ie](https://takahashigawa-climb.net/information/1053154/) facility is currently not available for overnight stays.

## Rules to know before you go

TCNet's [area rules and manners page](https://takahashigawa-climb.net/iwaba_manner/) covers the whole basin:

- **Toilets**: use public toilets or convenience stores beforehand, and carry a portable toilet for emergencies. Some areas are water sources; TCNet says a toilet problem at Nagayazaka once brought it close to a climbing ban.
- **Parking**: follow each area's rules and do not inconvenience residents or tourists.
- **Trash**: pack out everything.
- **Fires**: even where not legally prohibited, refrain from fires in the forest.
- **Private land**: TCNet says most crags in the basin are private land climbed through the landowners' goodwill, and approaches and parking are often private too.
- **Nature parks**: some sub-areas lie within prefectural natural parks or quasi-national parks; take care with scenery and conservation.
- **Visible areas**: do not leave quickdraws, ropes, or other gear in place; brush off chalk; near houses, avoid shouting.
- **Rockfall**: TCNet's [areas needing special care](https://takahashigawa-climb.net/iwaba_manner/tokuni/) lists 2 Runze, 1 Runze, and Center Rock as having very frequent rockfall; wear a helmet.
- **Local rules**: some sub-areas are closed to climbing or under voluntary restraint, and others have their own rules on parking, approach paths, or leaving draws; check TCNet and [JFA](https://freeclimb.jp/activities/deal-with/issue-list/) before you go. TCNet itself notes that these notes are compiled from regular climbers and "公式に取り決められた規制等ではありません" (are not officially agreed regulations).

Registration and access fees: none of the TCNet, CLIMBING-net, ROCKTOPO, or JFA pages checked for this article mention registration or a fee for climbing. Article 7 of the ordinance says using the "park" requires the mayor's permission, but it does not say whether that covers simply going climbing, and no clarification was found.

## Representative areas and routes

These are the areas with the most complete information in TCNet's route guides and on ROCKTOPO. Names are kept in the original Japanese.

**用瀬の嶽・2ルンゼ (2 Runze)** ([TCNet](https://takahashigawa-climb.net/area/bitchu/nirunze/)): one of the oldest areas, about 5–10 minutes' walk from Yuze hut. 5.9–5.12b, rated beginner-to-intermediate; season "通年（初夏～夏は染み出すことが多い）" (year-round; often seeping in early summer and summer). TCNet calls 寺島ルート (5.12a) and 半分バナナ (5.11c) the gateway 5.12 and 5.11 of Bitchu; ROCKTOPO's popular ダンゴのリズム (5.10c) is here too. Lots of rockfall, so a helmet is essential.

**用瀬の嶽・じゃろう岩 (Jarou-iwa)** ([TCNet](https://takahashigawa-climb.net/area/bitchu/jarouiwa/)): about 5 minutes along the footpath from Yuze hut, 5.10d–5.14c, average angle 120 degrees, for intermediate and advanced climbers. Signature routes are ターンパイク (5.13d) and バイタミンR (5.14C), which TCNet says is the second-hardest graded route in Bitchu. There are no good warm-ups, so most people warm up at 2 Runze or nearby.

**ニューエリア (New Area)** ([TCNet](https://takahashigawa-climb.net/area/bitchu/newarea/)): developed around 1994, south-facing, 5.10a–5.13d with the most depth in the 5.12s; TCNet highlights 美しくカンテ (5.12c) and 水の泡. About 40 minutes' walk from Yuze hut. **Do not use the quarry company's concrete road** on the approach — it is private, weekends included — and take the trail instead. TCNet announced in May 2026 that an electric fence against wild animals goes up next to the parking lot from rice planting to harvest; during that period, move your car out by 17:00 or park at Yuze and walk ([notice](https://takahashigawa-climb.net/iwaba-info/1053173/)). This happens every year; check that year's notice for dates.

**権現谷 (Gongendani)** ([TCNet](https://takahashigawa-climb.net/area/bitchu/gongen/)): a big white wall right beside a forest road — TCNet describes the approach as "０" (zero). 5.11a–5.13b, average angle 110 degrees; flagship routes are もも (5.12a) and 下には下がおる (5.12a). Because it sits on the road, stop climbing when a vehicle passes and keep gear off the roadway ([Gongendani rules](https://takahashigawa-climb.net/iwaba_manner/gongen/)). The forest road access is often cut by fallen trees or construction: TCNet reported the Nagatani side closed in September 2024 and, in May 2026, a fallen tree blocking the Imohara side about 300 m in ([notice](https://takahashigawa-climb.net/iwaba-info/1053159/)).

**羽山トンネルサイド (Hayama Tunnel Side)** ([TCNet](https://takahashigawa-climb.net/iwaba_manner/hayama/)): a wall that faces a public road in a sightseeing spot inside the Takahashi River Upper Reaches Prefectural Natural Park. TCNet's 2021 rules give priority to vehicles and pedestrians, require parking at the Hayama Gorge lot about 150 m upstream, ban bouldering inside the tunnel, and close individual routes; read the full rules before going.

ROCKTOPO's popular routes for the whole area (2026-10-12): ゴーゴーヘブン (5.10a), ダンゴのリズム (5.10c), ストライクバック (5.11b/c).

## Where to find more information

- **TCNet**: route guides for each area, rules, and approach and parking notices — the most complete local resource. **Note: TCNet [announced](https://takahashigawa-climb.net/sonota/1053176/) in June 2026 that its website will close completely on November 23, 2026.** News will move to [Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/takahashigawaclimb2016.net) and [Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/takahashigawa_climbing_network/), and old pages are preserved on a GitHub Pages archive ([pages](https://tcnetclimb-create.github.io/pages/), [posts](https://tcnetclimb-create.github.io/Posts/)). The TCNet links in this article will break after the closure; look for the same page titles on the archive.
- **[ROCKTOPO Bitchu](https://www.rocktopo.net/areas/153)**: route lists for the six sub-areas, grade distribution, nine parking spots, and user logs; the page header tells you to read TCNet's rules first. Offline download requires login.
- **[CLIMBING-net Bitchu](https://www.climbing-net.com/iwaba_detail/%E5%82%99%E4%B8%AD/)**: Yama-kei's overview, development history, season, and access; the guidebook field lists "中国・四国・九州".
- **[JFA access-issue list](https://freeclimb.jp/activities/deal-with/issue-list/)**: the Okayama section lists closures, voluntary restraints, and cautions within the Bitchu area.
- **[theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/japan/area/920977260)**: in English but only 41 routes, so of limited use.
- **[Bihoku Bus](https://www.bihokubus.com/)**: timetables for local buses from Takahashi Station.
