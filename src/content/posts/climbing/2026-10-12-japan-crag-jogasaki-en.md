Jogasaki is a lava sea cliff that runs for more than ten kilometres along the east coast of Izu. Most of the climbing is crack climbing on your own gear, with sport routes and bouldering as well. The cliffs sit inside a national park, right next to tourist paths, suspension bridges and private land. In some sectors you rappel down to the shore and climb back up an exit route at the end of the day.

This guide helps you decide whether Jogasaki suits your grade, which months to go, which station to walk from without a car, where to park, which rules could get the whole crag closed if broken, and which sector to start in at each of the four grade bands.

## What kind of crag is this

Jogasaki (城ヶ崎) is in Ito, Shizuoka Prefecture, on the east coast of the Izu Peninsula. The [Ito Tourism Association](https://itospa.com/spot/detail_54002.html) describes the coast as formed by lava that flowed out when Mt. Omuro erupted about 4,000 years ago (「約4000年前、大室山が噴火した時に流れ出した溶岩によってできた」). From Tokyo it is about two hours by car from the Atsugi interchange on the Tomei Expressway.

Sources disagree on the rock. [ROCKTOPO](https://www.rocktopo.net/areas/9) and [CLIMBING-net](https://www.climbing-net.com/iwaba_detail/%E5%9F%8E%E3%83%B6%E5%B4%8E/) both say andesite (安山岩). [theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/japan/jogasaki) tags it "Andesite Basalt" and describes it as "Dark grey basalt, sports and trad climbing."

CLIMBING-net's overview explains the crag's character. Development only began in 1981, and from the start it was treated as a free-climbing venue (「初めからフリークライミングの岩場として扱われた」). Along more than ten kilometres of cliff, every corner in the columnar jointing hid a crack, and early climbers climbed them one after another. After JAO held the Seaside Cup in 1987, face routes followed, and the Seaside sector became the most popular area. CLIMBING-net gives the angle as 90 to 110 degrees.

ROCKTOPO's grade chart shows the cracks lean easy to moderate. Of 255 cracks, 76 are 5.2–5.9, 94 are 5.10, 44 are 5.11, 30 are 5.12, 10 are 5.13 and 1 is 5.14. The 105 sport routes cluster at 5.10–5.12, with 5.11 the largest band (36 routes). Among the 128 boulders, 29 are around 1-dan (shodan), 14 around 2-dan and 5 are 3-dan or harder.

CLIMBING-net marks the crag as suitable for beginners and intermediates, but the same page warns that outside Seaside you should assume no maintenance since first ascent (「開拓後の整備はいっさいされていないと思ったほうがよい」). You place your own gear, judge fixed gear yourself, and some routes have no lower-off. Outside Seaside, Family and Kadowaki-minami-no-iso, nuts and cams are the default. In practice, "beginner-friendly" here means climbers who can place trad gear, or who go with someone who can. CLIMBING-net also asks that pairs of beginners not descend into Seaside at all (「初心者、初級者同士の方はシーサイドには降りないようにお願いしたい」).

## When to go

| Source | Months | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| [ROCKTOPO](https://www.rocktopo.net/areas/9) | December–April | 「シーズン 1月 2月 3月 4月 12月」 |
| [CLIMBING-net](https://www.climbing-net.com/iwaba_detail/%E5%9F%8E%E3%83%B6%E5%B4%8E/) | November–May | 「シーズン 11月~5月」 |
| [theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/japan/jogasaki) | Colder months | Dark rock facing mostly S, SE or SW: "the colder months of the year are preferred" |

The two Japanese sites disagree on the edges; both include December through April, and only CLIMBING-net adds November and May. theCrag's reasoning is that the rock is dark and mostly south-facing, so cold months are better. None of the three sources goes further than that on why summer is bad, and no temperature, humidity or sea-state figures turned up.

Two more seasonal hazards:

- **Fixed gear at the start of the season.** CLIMBING-net: because of the sea, all fixed gear degrades much faster than at other crags, so take extra care especially at the start of the season (「とくにシーズン始めは注意されたい」).
- **After typhoons.** The [Jogasaki Boulder Guide](https://jogasaki.bouldering-guide.com/) says typhoons and waves can move boulders or wipe out problems (「台風や波の影響で岩が動いたり課題が消滅したりすることがあります」). ROCKTOPO's Kosaitsuna boulder entry also notes some problems have disappeared because the shore got rough.

Peak season also means crowds. Mountain Project warns that the exit route can jam when everyone tries to leave at once, and suggests bringing headlamps.

Trip reports suggest midwinter is the easier time. [Kii Sampo](https://kiisampo.com/jogasaki-toranoana-rp/) climbed at Seaside two days before the coldest week of the year and wrote that the south-facing wall gets little north wind (「南向きのシーサイドはあまり北風も入らない」); the worry was that it was too warm for good friction. [nicecats](https://nicecats.sakura.ne.jp/mountains/2025/250218.html) went to Funamushi Rock during a once-in-a-decade cold wave and found the rock not that cold (「岩はそれほど冷たくなく」). On the other hand, [Kojitsu Sanso staff](https://www.kojitusanso.jp/tozan-report/detail/?fm=10884) found the rock greasy once the midday sun hit it, and [Shuho Toko-kai](https://syuho.but.jp/?p=6556) wrote that by early April Colossus was too slick to try (「ヌメヌメで勝負にならなかった」). For hard routes, go from December to February and climb the sunny walls in the morning.

### By car

- **Interchanges.** From Tokyo, about 2 hours from the Atsugi interchange on the Tomei Expressway; from Aichi, about 1 hour 30 minutes from the Nagaizumi-Numazu interchange ([Ito Tourism Association](https://itospa.com/spot/detail_54002.html), [Jogasaki Boulder Guide](https://jogasaki.bouldering-guide.com/)). Then head south on Route 135 along the east coast.
- **Traffic.** CLIMBING-net warns this is a major tourist area and weekend traffic to and from Tokyo can be heavy (「休日の東京方面からの行き帰りの渋滞は、十分に覚悟していただきたい」).
- **Roadside parking.** Every Jogasaki Boulder Guide sector page says never park on the road, and that locals may report you. Mountain Project: "illegal parking and roadside parking is strictly prohibited," and no overnight parking is currently allowed.

Sources conflict on parking in three places, so double-check before you go:

- **Kadowaki capacity.** ROCKTOPO says about 30 cars; the Ito Tourism Association says 123 cars plus 3 microbuses. They may be counting different things; the sources don't say.
- **Where to park for Nichirenzaki.** ROCKTOPO lists a lot near Renchakuji, but the [Jogasaki Boulder Guide Nichirenzaki page](https://jogasaki.bouldering-guide.com/area/nichirenzaki/) says the Renchakuji lot is not available (「蓮着寺駐車場は利用不可です」) and suggests the paid Izu Kaiyo Koen lot instead (¥550 per day, 8:00–17:00, locked outside those hours; the page dates this to February 2022).
- **Around Hashidate.** CLIMBING-net says the free Hashidate lot fills with climbers' cars on weekends, which annoys locals, so use a paid lot (¥500) on weekends. The Jogasaki Boulder Guide says the Hashidate tourist parking is not to be used (「橋立観光駐車場は不可です」).

## Rules to know before you go

None of the sources checked for this post mentions registration or an access fee. But this is a crag that sits right next to a climbing ban (「登攀禁止と隣り合わせ」). The [JFA access-issue list](https://freeclimb.jp/activities/deal-with/issue-list/) flags "Izu / Jogasaki coast" as "use with care, follow the rules." After accidents on consecutive days in February 2009, the prefecture, city, police and fire service inspected the site in April. Climbing has continued only after JFA negotiated with the authorities, and JFA asks climbers to follow each sector's rules (「各エリアの利用ルールを遵守してほしい」).

[CLIMBING-net](https://www.climbing-net.com/iwaba_detail/%E5%9F%8E%E3%83%B6%E5%B4%8E/) adds what concerned officials: the run of accidents, and bolts and slings left inside a national park. The coast is also both a national park and private land (「国立公園であると同時に私有地でもある」). CLIMBING-net notes it is still not an officially approved area (「今なお公に認められたエリアではなく」).

Ito City's tourism division, which manages the Jogasaki coast, contacted JFA in April 2022 ([JFA notice, 2022-04-15](https://freeclimb.jp/access/6859/)) after a climbing death that month at Yoshida Beach in Minami-Izu. The city made three points. More people are asking it about safety. Jogasaki is not a climbing area the city officially sanctions, so the city will not put up signs or make announcements itself. The city knows people climb there and is not considering a ban, but would have to if accidents became frequent (「事故が頻発するようであれば検討せざるを得ない」). In other words, whether climbing continues here depends on the accident record.

Recent JFA notices for Jogasaki:

- **Camping**: on [2025-03-29](https://freeclimb.jp/access/10179/) JFA reported someone tent-camping in a Jogasaki car park. It reminded climbers that tent camping is banned in Izu outside designated sites (「伊豆では、テント泊は指定地区以外は禁止」), and that copycats could get car parks or the crag closed.
- **Bolts**: JFA's [rebolting record](https://freeclimb.jp/activities/environment/rebolt-achievement/) has only two Jogasaki entries, both from December 2007, listed under 「ファミリークラック・エリア（日蓮崎南面）」: Eve and Indian Doll (インデェアンドール), refitted with ring hangers (FIXEリング付きハンガー). JFA has posted no rebolting or closure notice for Jogasaki since 2023.

[Mountain Project](https://www.mountainproject.com/area/107001454/jogasaki) lists the rules:

- No new bolts anywhere on the Jogasaki coast without permission from JFA and the national park, and the same for rebolting or retro-bolting. Don't develop new routes.
- Leave no fixed ropes, gear or tat. All fixed ropes have been removed and none are allowed.
- In some sectors, getting out means climbing a 5.7 exit route.
- No dogs. No camping (CLIMBING-net also says camping is banned along the whole coast, 「海岸一帯はキャンプ禁止」). No cutting trees or lighting fires. Pack out all rubbish.
- Use a toilet before you lower in; there are none below. The Jogasaki Boulder Guide also says there are no toilets near the boulders, so use the ones at the parking lots or a convenience store.
- Weekends bring crowds of tourists; keep a low profile and keep the noise down.

[Mountain Project's Family Crack Area page](https://www.mountainproject.com/area/118010500/family-crack-area) adds that tourists cross the suspension bridge above, and "any violations of local ethics or disruptive behavior could easily result in a ban on climbing." Hiking trails run above the crag and rockfall is frequent, so wear a helmet.

**Seaside hazards.** The Jogasaki cliffs sit within reach of the waves, and these points decide what you carry and when you leave:

- **Corroded bolts and anchors.** CLIMBING-net says never trust everything to a single bolt (「ボルト1本にすべてを託すようなことは絶対にしないこと」). Outside Seaside, bolts and fixed pitons are almost untrustworthy (「ほとんど信用できない」), and even Seaside has some original bolts. theCrag notes the gear is "very exposed to the salt of the sea." A [Kojitsu Sanso report from March 2014](https://www.kojitusanso.jp/tozan-report/detail/?fm=10884) includes a photo of an anchor carabiner destroyed by salt and suggests carrying spares.
- **Rappelling in and climbing out.** Seaside is reached by rappel (ROCKTOPO has a [Seaside rappel point](https://www.rocktopo.net/approach_informations/105)), and theCrag warns some sectors need a rappel down to the beach. CLIMBING-net says falls happen almost every year on the climb back out of Seaside (「特にシーサイドの登り返しでは、毎年のように墜落が起きている」). The way out follows bolts and metal staples, moving a self-belay up as you go. [kubo (January 2025)](https://kuboclimbingta.com/2025/01/19/climbing_at_jyougasaki_seaside/) warns that the tree used for the self-belay at the rappel point is dead and rotten (「既に枯れており、かなり腐っていた」), and [genter (February 2026)](https://www.yamareco.com/modules/yamareco/detail-9255164.html) found the entrance looser than the year before (「去年よりザレていて」). If you cannot rappel, you cannot get in; this is not a place to learn on the way down.
- **Waves and falling into the sea.** The Jogasaki Boulder Guide asks climbers to take great care not to fall into the sea (「海への転落に十分な注意をお願いします」). CLIMBING-net selected sectors where the base is on the rocky shore rather than over the water, which means some sectors do start above the sea. For tides, use the [Japan Meteorological Agency tide table for Ito](https://www.data.jma.go.jp/kaiyou/db/tide/suisan/suisan.php?stn=Z3): the station is in Futo, the same village as Funamushi Rock and Isaribi. The table is a prediction of astronomical tide, so it does not show big waves from typhoons or low-pressure systems. Sources say some problems and routes, such as the Akuma no Shita boulder and Nakashio Crack at Shiriidashi, get waves at high tide or can only be started at low tide.
- **Rockfall.** The Izu Peninsula Geopark [Kadowakizaki page](https://izugeopark.org/geosites/kadowakizaki/) warns that the cliffs collapse and rocks fall below them. [Mitsumine Sangaku-kai (February 2020)](https://www.mitsumine.gr.jp/kaiho/363/jogasaki.html) wrote that the top of Seaside is breaking up and rockfall is frequent (「上部の崩壊が進んでおり、落石が頻繁にある」). Tourist paths run along the cliff top; wear a helmet.
- **Approach footing.** The Isaribi approach is a steep descent that is especially slippery after rain; Hashidate has a section where you descend with a rope, which conflicts with Mountain Project's no-fixed-ropes rule, so go by what you find on site ([Isaribi](https://jogasaki.bouldering-guide.com/area/isaribi/), [Hashidate](https://jogasaki.bouldering-guide.com/area/hashidate/)).

In an emergency, call **119** for ambulance and fire or **110** for police ([City of Yokohama: Calling emergency services](https://www.city.yokohama.lg.jp/lang/residents/en/emergencies/20200221091958448.html)). For a fall into the sea or any accident at sea, call the Japan Coast Guard on **118** ([Japan Coast Guard: 118](https://www.kaiho.mlit.go.jp/doc/tel118.html)) and say when, where and what happened (「いつ・どこで・何があった」). The Jogasaki coast is long, so give the sector name (Seaside, Funamushi Rock, Family Crack) and the nearest car park rather than just "Jogasaki".

Also check with your insurer that **trad climbing, search and rescue, medical treatment and repatriation** are covered. A generic travel policy name is not enough; in Japan mountain rescue costs are in principle borne by the party rescued, subject to your policy and local rules.

Grades and route names in video titles describe only that video's version; choose routes from the source columns in the tables.

### Beginner: up to 6a (YDS 5.10a and below)

This band is almost all crack. Of ROCKTOPO's 105 sport routes, only 4 are below 5.10a. The easy cracks are concentrated in three sectors: Funamushi Rock (walkable from Futo station, 5 min from the car park), Family Crack (below the suspension bridge) and Akane-no-hama. CLIMBING-net calls Jogasaki fine for beginners and intermediates (「初心者・中級者OK」), assuming you can place your own gear or are climbing with someone who can.

Funamushi Rock and Family Crack are where clubs practise crack climbing, so ropes go up early at weekends. The hardest-feeling routes in this band are Flash Dance and Mumei Roof Crack. ## Where to look things up

- **[ROCKTOPO Jogasaki](https://www.rocktopo.net/areas/9)**: in Japanese; routes, grades, ascent counts and popular routes for each sector, car park sizes and locations, and rappel points. Offline data needs a login. In October 2026 direct access often returned 403, and some sectors could only be read through Wayback snapshots.
- **[CLIMBING-net Jogasaki](https://www.climbing-net.com/iwaba_detail/%E5%9F%8E%E3%83%B6%E5%B4%8E/)**: in Japanese; Yama-kei's crag overview, development history and parking rules by sector.
- **[Jogasaki Boulder Guide](https://jogasaki.bouldering-guide.com/)**: in Japanese; bouldering topos, walking times from stations, parking and approach photos, and [news](https://jogasaki.bouldering-guide.com/news/) such as broken holds.
- **[theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/japan/jogasaki)**: in English; route length, bolt counts, stars and logged ascents for each sector.
- **[Mountain Project](https://www.mountainproject.com/area/107001454/jogasaki)**: in English; a summary of the rules, easy and mid-grade routes in the popular sectors, and a classics list.
- **[JFA access-issue list](https://freeclimb.jp/activities/deal-with/issue-list/)** and the [JFA Jogasaki page](https://freeclimb.jp/tokai-hokuriku-area/%e9%9d%99%e5%b2%a1%e7%9c%8c%e3%83%bb%e5%9f%8e%e3%83%b6%e5%b4%8e/): in Japanese; access status, the post-accident agreement and new notices.
- **[JMA tide table for Ito](https://www.data.jma.go.jp/kaiyou/db/tide/suisan/suisan.php?stn=Z3)**: the station is in Futo; check high and low tide times before you go.
- **[Ito Tourism Association: Jogasaki coast](https://itospa.com/spot/detail_54002.html)**: in Japanese; Kadowaki car park size and fees, and transport.
- **Guidebooks**: Grades and route counts here come mainly from online databases; the guidebook is the authority.
  - [*Free Climbing Nihon 100 Iwaba 3: Izu & Koshin* (enlarged revised new edition)](https://www.yamakei.co.jp/products/2812180860%20.html) (ed. Makoto Kitayama, Yama-kei Publishers, 2012-04-20, 208 pp, Japanese): includes "51 East Izu Jogasaki", and the publisher calls Jogasaki Japan's representative sea cliff (「日本を代表するシークリフ・城ヶ崎」). An [ebook edition](https://www.yamakei.co.jp/products/2815120448.html) came out in 2016. There has been no new print edition since 2012.
  - [*Nihon Bouldering Area, vol. 2*](https://www.yamakei.co.jp/products/2814160170.html) (ed. Makoto Kitayama, Yama-kei Publishers, 2014-10-17, Japanese): a bouldering selection; the publisher says it newly adds the Jogasaki boulders (「新たに城ヶ崎のボルダーを追加」).
  - No dedicated printed Jogasaki topo turned up. For bouldering, the Jogasaki Boulder Guide website is the most current.
