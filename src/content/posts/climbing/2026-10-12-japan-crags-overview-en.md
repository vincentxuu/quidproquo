Each guide in this series covers one Japanese crag: rock type and style, the best seasons and why, how to get there by train or by car, and the local rules to know before you go. It does not cover itineraries, flights, or how to fit a trip into a holiday, because that goes out of date fast. A crag's seasons and rules change much more slowly.

Twenty-two crags are covered so far, across Hokkaido, Tohoku, Kanto, Koshin, Hokuriku, Izu, Tokai, Kinki, Chugoku, Shikoku, Kyushu, and Okinawa. Japan has far more than that: the prefecture pages on the crag database [ROCKTOPO](https://www.rocktopo.net/) list well over two hundred areas between them, and the series will fill in prefecture by prefecture.

## Picking a crag by season

Japan spans a wide range of latitudes, so crag seasons vary a lot. Roughly three kinds:

- **Winter crags**: south-facing, coastal, or far south. Warm on sunny winter days, too hot in summer. Jogasaki and Joyama in Izu, Yugawara Makuiwa in Kanagawa, the Odo Coast in Kochi, and Cape Hedo in Okinawa.
- **Summer crags**: high or far north, with snow or road closures in winter. Ogawayama in Nagano, Mizugakiyama in Yamanashi, Gozaisho in Mie, Zakkokudani in Toyama, and Akaiwa Seigankyo in Hokkaido.
- **Mostly spring and autumn, some year-round**: crags around Tokyo, Saitama, Tohoku, Kinki, Aichi, Okayama, and Fukuoka are climbed most of the year or in spring and autumn, but the rainy season and midsummer often bring seepage or humidity.

By access: Mitake Boulder (JR Ome Line, Mitake Station), Jogasaki (three Izukyu stations), Joyama (Izuhakone Railway, Ohito Station), Kasagi Boulder (JR Kansai Line, Kasagi Station), Fudoiwa (JR Fukuchiyama Line, Dojo Station), and Kitayama Park (Hankyu Koyoen Station) are reachable by train and a walk. Tenno-iwa, Yugawara, Ogawayama, Toyota, Gozaisho, Bitchu, and Hyugami need a bus as well. Akaiwa Seigankyo, Samuraihama, Aoba, Zakkokudani, Futagoyama, Mizugakiyama, the Odo Coast, and Cape Hedo effectively need a car, or have very few buses; see each guide.

## Where to look things up

Most information on Japanese crags is only in Japanese. Each guide's "Where to look things up" section links that crag's pages. The sources used throughout:

- **[ROCKTOPO](https://www.rocktopo.net/)**: a user-contributed crag database. Each crag has a route list, grade distribution, popular routes, parking pages, and approach notes, with current notices at the top of the page—for example, the Mitake Boulder page says 「ナイトボルダーは禁止です」 (no night bouldering). Offline downloads require a login.
- **[CLIMBING-net](https://www.climbing-net.com/iwaba/)**: run by the publisher Yama-Kei, whose site description says 「書籍『日本100岩場』掲載の岩場がすべて網羅されています」 (it covers every crag in the *Japan 100 Crags* books). Each page lists season, rock type, angle, route count, access, and parking rules. Some pages are old and say so.
- ***Free Climbing Japan 100 Crags* (フリークライミング日本100岩場)**: the standard guidebook series used by local climbers. [theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/japan)'s Japan page describes it as five volumes: Hokkaido & Tohoku, Kanto, Izu & Koshin, Tokai & Kansai, and Chugoku, Shikoku & Kyushu. Detailed topos are mostly in the guidebooks or in locally published topos.
- **[theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/japan) and [Mountain Project](https://www.mountainproject.com/area/106103884/japan)**: English-language sources with limited Japan coverage, but useful for classic routes and English approach notes.
- **[JFA access issues list](https://freeclimb.jp/activities/deal-with/issue-list/)** (in Japanese): the Japan Free Climbing Association's list of crag access problems—parking bans, usage rules, and how closed crags reopened. Check whether your crag is on it before going.
- **Local associations and managers**: for example the Ogano Climbing Association (Futagoyama), the Toyota Climbing Association, TCNet for the Takahashi River area (Bitchu), and the Shimukappu tourism association (Akaiwa Seigankyo). Current notices, contribution fees, and closures during rebolting are often only posted there.

## Rules that apply almost everywhere

Each guide's "Rules to know before you go" section covers that crag. A few things come up at nearly every crag:

- **Parking causes the most trouble.** The JFA list opens by saying 「ほとんどの岩場は潜在的なアクセス問題を抱えているといっても過言ではありません」 (it is no exaggeration to say almost every crag has a potential access problem). Tenno-iwa lost the parking lot across the road over parking problems, and residents have strongly objected to roadside parking at Toyota. Use only designated parking, and go elsewhere when it is full.
- **Night climbing, fires, and noise.** Mitake and Toyota ban night climbing; the local association at Futagoyama asks for no fires and no climbing after dark.
- **Registration and fees.** Some crags require registration or a fee. Akaiwa Seigankyo requires a forest entry form, the Ogawayama campground charges a day-use entry fee, and Yugawara charges park entry and parking during the plum festival. A guide that does not mention a fee is not saying there is none; it only reports what the sources say.
- **Closed sectors.** Several crags have sectors where climbing is not allowed. There are usually signs on site, and ROCKTOPO notes them at the top of the page.

## Finding climbing partners

Climbing communities in Japan connect mostly through gyms, local alpine clubs, and online groups. These were confirmed active on 2026-10-12:

**Facebook groups** (the author checked both while logged in; both had new posts that day)

- [Rock Climbing Japan](https://www.facebook.com/groups/37216626353/): a mostly English-language group for climbing in Japan; common questions are about gyms and finding partners.
- [Hiking, Climbing and Camping in Japan](https://www.facebook.com/groups/349592698536757/): a mixed hiking, climbing, and camping group.

**Meetup** (member counts and upcoming events as of 2026-10-12)

- [Climbing Tokyo](https://www.meetup.com/climbing-tokyo/): 3,821 members; runs gym lessons in English or Japanese and lists outdoor events in its description.
- [Tokyo Boulder Club](https://www.meetup.com/tokyoboulderclub/): 1,262 members; bouldering together on weekends.
- [Tokyo Climbers Coalition](https://www.meetup.com/tokyo-climbers-collective/): 240 members; uses Discord day to day, with a rope-climbing gym session as its next event.

All three currently schedule gym sessions, which makes them a good place to meet people before arranging outdoor days.

**Japanese partner boards**

- [Jimoty (ジモティー)](https://jmty.jp/all/com-kw-%E5%A4%96%E5%B2%A9) (in Japanese): search for 外岩 (outdoor rock) to find partner posts across Japan; new posts have appeared in recent months. Many posts set conditions, such as 「概ねグレード5.10台前半は登れる方」 (able to climb low 5.10s) and 「現地まで車など自力で来れる方」 (able to get to the crag on your own, e.g. by car). Read them before replying.

## Limits of this series

- Everything was checked on 2026-10-12. Bus timetables, campground seasons, parking fees, and forest-road closures change from year to year; each guide links the notice pages, so check again before you go.
- The author has not climbed at these crags. The guides compile public sources and contain no first-hand experience.
- Route counts vary a lot between sources (Ogawayama: 1,055 on ROCKTOPO, 365 on theCrag). That reflects different coverage, not that one source is wrong.
- Crags where climbing is prohibited are not included in this series.
