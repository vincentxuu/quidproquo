Austria, Germany and Italy share a post because none of them regulates guiding through a single national law. Austria's 9 states each have their own guiding act. In Germany only Bavaria regulates paid guiding. Italy has a national law but leaves training and exams to the regions. Before you apply, work out which state or region you are aiming for.

The three also have things in common: teaching is in German or Italian, fees fall roughly between €8,000 and €17,200, and the official documents say very little to non-EU citizens.

The shared IFMGA training framework is covered in [What you learn and how you are tested](/posts/climbing/2026-10-17-ifmga-training-and-exams-en). This post covers only what each country does.

## The three countries at a glance

| Country | Association / training body | Years of training | Approximate cost | Teaching language | Required by law? | Can foreigners apply? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Austria | [VÖBS](https://www.bergfuehrer.at/die-berg-und-skifuehrerinnen-ausbildung/) with the federal sports academy BSPA | 3 (new 2026 curriculum) | About €15,000 (2025–28 and 2026–29 cohorts) | German | Yes, under each state's law (e.g. Tirol) | No nationality requirement listed; German is the barrier |
| Germany | [VDBS](https://www.vdbs.de/werden/bergfuehrer-werden.html) with TU München | About 2.5–3 | Course fees about €17,200 (VDBS), plus a €1,700 state exam fee | German | Protected title; paid guiding regulated only in Bavaria | No nationality requirement in the regulations; spoken and written German required |
| Italy | [Conagai](https://www.guidealpine.it/diventare-guida-alpina.html) and the regional Collegi | About 4–5 (including at least 2 as aspirante) | Interregional pole unpublished; Lombardy AG1 €8,000 (2027) | Italian; German option in South Tyrol; Italian and French in Valle d'Aosta | Yes, national law L. 6/1989 | Most regions accept only Italian or EU citizens |

## Austria

### Training path

Training is run jointly by the national association VÖBS and the federal sports academy BSPA Innsbruck. The new 2026 curriculum on the [VÖBS training page](https://www.bergfuehrer.at/die-berg-und-skifuehrerinnen-ausbildung/) works like this:

1. **Entrance exam**: a 3-day winter part (skiing, steep ice) and a 2-day summer part (sport and trad climbing, plus a discussion of your climbing record). Both must be taken in the same year, winter first.
2. **Year 1**: VÖBS courses. The last day of the final course, "Hochtouren 1", is the Anwärterprüfung; pass it and you become a trainee guide (Anwärter).
3. **Years 2–3**: BSPA's state training plus compulsory guiding practice. The last course, "Hochtouren 2", is held in Chamonix, France.
4. **Licence**: after training you apply to the state government or district authority for an authorisation (Befugnis) under that state's law before you can work.

The new curriculum totals 89.5 course days over 3 years. Required practice varies by state: VÖBS says at least 14 days, while Tirol's law requires 6 weeks.

### Entry requirements

- Aged 18, with a sports-medical certificate under 6 months old.
- Pass the entrance exam; the standards are in the next section.
- Submit the VÖBS [climbing record form](https://www.bergfuehrer.at/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Tourenbericht_v28112023.pdf) (November 2023 version). Only list routes you were responsible for, led, or led in alternation:

| Category | Number | What the form recommends |
|---|---|---|
| Alpine rock | 15 different routes | Walls of at least 300 m, UIAA 5 or harder, not fully bolted |
| Ice or alpine routes | 10 | Including ice routes, mixed routes and 4000 m peaks |
| Icefalls | 5 | WI 4 or harder, at least 100 m long |
| Glacier ski tours | 15 | Mostly climbed under your own power, ideally 1,000 m or more each |
| Ski traverse | 1 | At least 5 days, moving to a new hut each day |

The [form's guidance notes](https://www.bergfuehrer.at/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Tourenbericht-Info_v28112023.pdf) spell out the details. Wall height must be continuous, so two 150 m routes do not count as one. For sport climbing, list your hardest redpoint on real rock; "a blue 7a in the gym does not count". The record has a legal basis, and false entries can get you removed from the exam or the training. Tirol's [implementing regulation T-BSFV](https://www.jusline.at/gesetz/t-bsfv/gesamt) sets out a longer statutory list. In practice the VÖBS form probably applies, since Tirol's law recognises the BSPA entrance exam (§26).

### How the entrance exam is assessed

The exam has two parts, taken in the same year, winter first. Fail the winter part and you cannot sit the summer part; pass winter but fail summer and your winter result is kept for 1 year.

| Part | Test | Standard |
|---|---|---|
| Winter (3 days) | Ski ascent | About 1,000 m carrying ski-touring kit, within 2 hours at most |
| Winter | Descent | Long turns, short turns, changes of rhythm; no kit changes between ascent and descent |
| Winter | Steep ice | Leading an icefall or mixed route, about WI 4–5, with protection already in place |
| Winter | Ice circuit | Up and down a 40° slope in alpine crampons, flat-footed traverse, front-pointing to 90°, and placing an ice screw from a climbing stance |
| Summer (2 days) | Sport climbing | UIAA VII+ (about 6b+) without falling |
| Summer | Alpine climbing | Placing your own nuts and cams on routes up to VI+ |
| Summer | Easy rock | Unroped climbing to grade III in stiff crampon-compatible boots |
| Summer | Climbing record | Discussed on the spot and counted in the assessment |

The standards come from the [VÖBS exam criteria](https://www.bergfuehrer.at/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/Pruefungskriterien-Info_v11102022.pdf) and the 2026 [ski](https://www.bergfuehrer.at/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Vorbereitungskurse_VOBS_Ski_2026.pdf) and [ice](https://www.bergfuehrer.at/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Vorbereitungskurse_VOBS_Eis_2026.pdf) preparation-course notes. The sources disagree on sport climbing: the exam criteria say redpoint, while the VÖBS website and Tirol's law say onsight. Steep ice is also judged on time, flow and rope management; you must bring two 60 m half ropes, and semi-automatic belay devices are banned.

There are two descriptions of how passing works:

- **VÖBS practice**: results come only after the whole part is finished. A test that falls a little short can be made up by another that goes very well; a clear fail cannot.
- **Tirol's law** (T-BSFV §1): the only outcomes are pass or fail. The exam board passes you by majority vote, and members may not abstain. A fail must be recorded with the main reasons, which the candidate may ask to see.

The number of resits allowed for the entrance exam is not published.

### Course exams and the final exam

After entry there are three more hurdles:

1. **Trainee guide exam**: on the last day of the "Hochtouren 1" course. The tasks and marking are not published.
2. **Course exams**: each course announcement lists what is tested. The [sport climbing course](https://www.bergfuehrer.at/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Ausschreibung-Sportklettern_2026.pdf), for example, tests a teaching session and a 6c+ technical route; [Rock 1](https://www.bergfuehrer.at/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Ausschreibung-Felskurs-1_ep2026_2026.pdf) tests rescue, teaching, personal climbing ability, and guiding and navigation. The [ski touring course](https://www.bergfuehrer.at/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Ausschreibung-Skitouren_2027.pdf) says it "is not an exam course".
3. **Final exam**: the guide annex of the federal [BSPA exam regulation](https://www.ris.bka.gv.at/GeltendeFassung.wxe?Abfrage=Bundesnormen&Gesetzesnummer=20007515) lists only the items below.

| Type | What |
|---|---|
| Certificate | A first-aid course certificate from the last 5 years |
| Oral | Training science; navigation and maps |
| Practical | Mountain rescue technique; a teaching session |

Climbing, ice and ski technique are probably tested in the course exams instead. The basis for this guess is that §9 of the regulation counts practical term exams towards the final exam, and the course announcements list technical tests. How many days the final exam takes and how many examiners attend are not published.

Tirol's law (T-BSFV §7–§8) sets these marking and resit rules:

- Marks run from 1 to 5, where 1 is best and 4 is a pass.
- A 5 (fail) in any subject fails the whole exam.
- A failed subject can be retaken at most twice, at the next exam session.
- For practical subjects other than mountain rescue, you must repeat that course before resitting.
- Once both resits are used up, you must repeat the whole training.

### Cost

- Training costs about **€15,000** for both the 2025 and 2026 intakes, paid in two instalments. It includes materials, insurance and instructors; travel, food, lodging and equipment are extra.
- Each entrance exam part costs €280.
- The €7,500 ([Styrian association page](https://bergsport-stmk.at/die-berg-und-skifuehrerinnen-ausbildung/)) and €4,000 ([Lower Austria government page](https://www.noe.gv.at/noe/Sport/Diplombergfuehrer1.html)) figures you may see online are out of date.

### Language

German. [Lower Austria's government page](https://www.noe.gv.at/noe/Sport/Diplombergfuehrer1.html) says EU citizens may train in Austria but need sufficient German, and that this is checked. [Tirol's guiding act TBSFG](https://www.jusline.at/gesetz/tbsfg/gesamt) also requires licence applicants to have "the German needed for client safety".

### Legal status

- Working as a guide requires a Befugnis granted by the state government, making it a state-level legal qualification.
- In Tirol, for example, commercially guiding mountaineering, ski mountaineering or sport climbing requires the licence, and an Anwärter may work only under a licensed guide's supervision. Alpine clubs leading their own members on a non-profit basis are exempt.
- The 9 states differ, so practice days and trainee rules depend on where you plan to work.

### Foreigners

- **Applying**: VÖBS lists only two entry conditions, being 18 and a sports-medical certificate. The nationality field on the form is free text, not limited to the EU. In practice German is the barrier.
- **Tirol licence**: Tirol's newer [online application](https://www.tirol.gv.at/buergerservice/e-government/formulare/antrag-zur-ausuebung-von-bergsportfuehrertaetigkeiten/) asks only for a passport or ID card, with no proof of nationality, which matches the lack of a nationality condition in TBSFG §4.
- **Short cross-border trips**: guides from non-EU countries may guide briefly in Tirol, but only with clients recruited in their home country and if that country grants Tirol guides the same rights ([TBSFG](https://www.jusline.at/gesetz/tbsfg/gesamt) §2a).
- The official pages do not say what residence or work status a Taiwanese trainee would need while training in Austria.

### Number of guides

The current [VÖBS "about us" page](https://www.bergfuehrer.at/ueber-uns/) says "knapp 1500 Mitglieder", just under 1,500 members. The page is undated.

## Germany

### Training path

The German title is "staatlich geprüfter Berg- und Skiführer" (state-certified mountain and ski guide), under Bavaria's [BayAPOFspl](https://www.gesetze-bayern.de/Content/Document/BayAPOFspl/true) regulation. TU München is responsible for training and runs the state exam; the teaching itself is done by the VDBS instructor team.

1. **Submit your climbing record** by 31 October each year. Once it is approved you are an applicant (Bewerber).
2. **Three entrance tests**: ice, skiing, and combined rock and ice. Pass all three and you become a candidate (Kandidat) and must join VDBS.
3. **Compulsory courses B101–B115**: passing all three graded courses makes you an Aspirant, allowed to start practice.
4. **Practice**: at least 36 days, with at least 12 in each of rock, ice/alpine and skiing.
5. **State exam**: 12 days in summer, 6 in winter and 2 of theory, run by TU München.

[VDBS](https://www.vdbs.de/werden/bergfuehrer-werden.html) says the fastest route is 2.5 years and about 3 is typical. Adding up the tables in the 2026 information handbook gives about 163 days, a total I calculated myself. You must register for the state exam within 6 years of passing the entrance tests.

### Entry requirements

- Aged 18; a first-aid course within the past year; a medical certificate and police clearance, both under three months old.
- BayAPOFspl §4 explicitly requires sufficient spoken and written German.
- The climbing record must cover at least 3 years, including, for example, 15 alpine rock routes on walls of at least 400 m, mostly without bolts.
- The sport-climbing entrance test requires a 6b+ redpoint in rock shoes.
- During the exam, examiners may interview you to check that your climbing record is genuine. If the interview confirms a problem, you can lose your place in the exam.

### Entrance exam and course exams

German marks always run from 1 to 6, where 1 is best, and usually two examiners each give a mark that is then averaged. These are the hurdles from entry to the state exam ([VDBS marking guide](https://storage.tramino.net/vdbs/1549937/infos-zu-efp-lehrgangsprfungen-gem-bayapofspl.pdf?r=1), [Bavarian ministry notice](https://www.verkuendung-bayern.de/files/baymbl/2021/217/baymbl-2021-217.pdf)):

| Stage | What is tested | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|
| Paper review | A climbing record covering at least 3 years | The training committee decides whether you may sit the exam |
| Entrance exam | Ice 2 days, skiing 2.5 days, combined rock and ice 8 days | The three guiding-style tests (ski touring, rock route, ice route) each average 4.0 or better; the others 4.5 or better |
| Course exams | Rock technique, flat-footed crampon technique, steep ice, ski technique, avalanche search | 4.5 or better in each |
| Graded courses | The three graded courses | A course mark of 4.0 or better |
| State exam | Guiding, risk-management skills, teaching, theory | See the next section |

For the scoring table of the uphill run, the technical circuit and the timed avalanche search, see [What you learn and how you are tested](/posts/climbing/2026-10-17-ifmga-training-and-exams-en); they are not repeated here.

What happens if you fail depends on the stage:

- **Entrance exam**: the VDBS guide says you may resit twice, and tests you have passed are kept.
- **Course exams**: you may resit twice, at the earliest with the next intake.
- **Graded courses**: failing means failing the whole course, and you may repeat it only once.

The course plan, from the [VDBS 2026 handbook](https://storage.tramino.net/vdbs/1544961/information-zur-berg-skifhrer-ausbild-ung-2026.pdf):

| Year | Code | Course | Days | Type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | B101 | Rock climbing and teaching methods | 7 | Course |
| 1 | B102 | Mountain rescue | 7 | Course, taken first |
| 1 | B103 | Alpine rock | 13 | **Graded course** |
| 1 | B104 | Theory I | 3 | Course |
| 2 | B105 | Avalanches | 8 | Course |
| 2 | B106 | Ski touring | 7 | Course |
| 2 | B107 | Icefall climbing | 5 | Course |
| 2 | B108 | Ski technique and teaching | 7 | Course |
| 2 | B109 | Off-piste skiing | 6 | **Graded course** |
| 2 | B110 | Personal technique exams (skiing, avalanche search, rock) | 3 | **Exam course** |
| 2 | B111 | Ice and mixed terrain | 10 | **Graded course** |
| 2 | B112 | Flat-footed crampon technique | 1 | **Exam day** |
| 2 | B113 | Theory II | 4 | Course |
| 3 | B114 | Winter mountaineering (including the steep-ice exam) | 7 | Pre-exam course |
| 3 | B115 | Ski mountaineering | 7 | Pre-exam course |

Since 2025 the personal technique exams have been taken out of the individual courses and grouped in B110 and B112. VDBS says this gives the courses a better learning atmosphere and gives trainees time to practise before the exam.

Graded courses are marked differently from one-off exams:

- Instructors give each trainee a mark **every day**, and the course leader combines them at the end. Aspects nobody had a chance to observe that day are not marked.
- The aspects are client care, risk management, technique, use of guiding technique, movement, and professionalism and teaching.
- Strengths in one aspect cannot offset weaknesses in another, and there is also an overall assessment.

### State exam

The state exam is run by TU München in four parts. The 2026/27 dates come from the [TUM registration form](https://www.mh.tum.de/fileadmin/w00ckv/mh/Studium/Sport-_und_gesundheitswissenschaftliche_Studiengaenge/Studierende/Nichtakad._Ausbildung/20260302_Anmeldung_EP_BSF_2026.pdf):

| Part | 2026/27 dates | Required before you register |
|---|---|---|
| Rock | 2026-08-29 to 09-05 | Aspirant, rock technique exam, summer practice |
| Theory | 2026-11-13 to 11-15 | Aspirant, Theory I and II |
| Winter ski mountaineering | 2027-04-18 to 04-24 | Aspirant, B115, all personal technique exams, winter practice |
| Ice and alpine | 2027-06-26 to 07-03 | Aspirant, B114, steep-ice exam, summer and winter practice |

Practice means at least 12 days in each of rock, ice/alpine and skiing, roughly half guiding and half teaching. The practice log must be signed by supervisors and handed to the exam chair on the first day. If your pre-exam course was more than 4 years ago, you cannot register.

What each part tests:

- **Guiding**: rock, ice/alpine and ski mountaineering. You brief "(fictitious) clients" and then guide them. The [TUM marking criteria](https://www.mh.tum.de/fileadmin/w00ckv/mh/Studium/Sport-_und_gesundheitswissenschaftliche_Studiengaenge/Studierende/Nichtakad._Ausbildung/190808_Staatliche_Pru__fungen_Beurteilungskrit_.pdf) ask you to solve complex situations with little gear; in their words, your harness and pack should not "look like a sports shop". Every task must be passed, and the average for the whole guiding part must be 4.0 or better.
- **Risk-management skills**: rope rescue tasks of 15–30 minutes each; the task list is in [part 2](/posts/climbing/2026-10-17-ifmga-training-and-exams-en). Every task must be passed.
- **Teaching**: 30 minutes to prepare and 30 minutes to teach. The fail conditions are in part 2.
- **Teaching task bank**: published in advance, with 20 [rock](https://www.mh.tum.de/fileadmin/w00ckv/mh/Studium/Sport-_und_gesundheitswissenschaftliche_Studiengaenge/Studierende/Nichtakad._Ausbildung/250508_Lehrproben_Fels.pdf) and 25 [ice](https://www.mh.tum.de/fileadmin/w00ckv/mh/Studium/Sport-_und_gesundheitswissenschaftliche_Studiengaenge/Studierende/Nichtakad._Ausbildung/250508_Lehrproben_Eis.pdf) tasks, from building belays to crevasse rescue. Each task states the learners' level and what they should learn.
- **Theory**: written, oral or both, with the format announced at the start. Subjects are teaching methods, sports biology, accident science and first aid, training science, snow and avalanches, weather, navigation, ecology, foreign-language communication, equipment, and belay theory.

To pass theory, all three conditions must hold:

1. The average across subjects is a pass.
2. No more than one subject gets a 6.
3. Snow and avalanches, weather, accident science and first aid, and navigation each get 4.5 or better.

A failed part or task can be retaken twice, at the next exam session. Resits carry a fee (BayAPOFspl §19):

| Resit | Fee |
|---|---|
| Guiding | €400 per task |
| Risk-management skills | €100 per task |
| Teaching | €100 per task |
| Theory | €50 per sitting, however many subjects |

A registration covers one year only, so a resit the following year needs a new registration. Withdrawing without good reason counts as a fail, and anyone who finally fails cannot re-enter the training.

### Cost

- VDBS says course fees alone come to about **€17,200**, with travel, food, lodging and the state exam fee on top.
- The state exam fee is €1,700.
- Adding up the [2026 price list](https://storage.tramino.net/vdbs/1590749/bgf-termine-2026-update-1025.pdf) myself (entrance tests, compulsory courses and the state exam) gives about €16,410; VDBS does not explain the gap.
- The [VDBS handbook](https://storage.tramino.net/vdbs/1544961/information-zur-berg-skifhrer-ausbild-ung-2026.pdf) says VDBS and the German Alpine Club (DAV) subsidise courses, so trainees pay roughly half the real cost.

### Language

German. The theory exam's "communication" subject includes English, French and Spanish.

### Legal status

- Only those who pass the exam may use the protected title "staatlich geprüfter Berg- und Skiführer".
- Bavaria's [BayBergSkiV](https://www.gesetze-bayern.de/Content/Document/BayBergSkiV/true) treats any paid mountaineering or ski-mountaineering instruction, including guiding, as a mountaineering school whose head must hold the state qualification. The authorities can close a school that breaks the rules.
- Germany's federal [foreign qualifications portal](https://anerkennung-in-deutschland.de/en/interest/finder/profession/393/profile) lists mountain guide as a regulated profession, but registers it only in Bavaria.

### Foreigners

- BayAPOFspl sets no nationality or residence condition; the requirement that matters most for foreigners is German.
- EU guides who are already qualified can seek recognition through the European Professional Card (EPC); non-EU guides are assessed case by case by [TU München](https://www.mh.tum.de/mh/studium/sport-und-gesundheitswissenschaftliche-studiengaenge/studierende/nichtakad-ausbildungen/fachsportlehrende/berg-und-skifuehrende/).
- The application form has a "country" field, which suggests applications from abroad are possible, though VDBS does not say so directly.

### Number of guides

[VDBS's "Über Uns" page](https://www.vdbs.de/ueber-uns/) gives about 700 state-certified mountain and ski guides.

## Italy

### Training path

Italy has no national school. The national law [L. 6/1989](https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn:nir:stato:legge:1989-01-02;6) sets the framework; the Collegi of the regions and autonomous provinces run training and exams, coordinated nationally by [Conagai](https://www.guidealpine.it/diventare-guida-alpina.html). There are currently five training poles: Valle d'Aosta, South Tyrol, Trentino, Lombardy and an interregional pole shared by seven regions.

The law has two levels only: aspirante guida (aspirant guide) and guida alpina (full guide). The usual path is:

1. **Selection**: CV review, interview and practical tests.
2. **Aspirante course and exams**: the interregional 2026/28 cycle runs 99 days, from May 2026 to April 2028; see the [course calendar](https://www.guidealpinepiemonte.it/res/download/pdf/969_it.pdf).
3. **Register on the albo and work as an aspirante for at least 2 years** (national law, art. 7).
4. **Full guide course and exams**: Piedmont describes 3 modules totalling about twenty days.

Regions differ:

- **Lombardy** splits the aspirante level in two. AG1 covers rock and mixed terrain with no ski test; AG2 adds skiing and ice.
- **South Tyrol**: [DLH 29/2024](https://lexbrowser.provinz.bz.it/doc/de/234560/dekret_des_landeshauptmanns_vom_15_november_2024_nr_29.aspx?view=1) lets an aspirante take the 11-day upgrade course after just 1 year on the register, shorter than the national 2 years.

### Entry requirements

Using the interregional pole's 2026 [application form](https://www.guidealpinepiemonte.it/res/download/pdf/1004_it.pdf) as the example:

- Aged 18 by 1 January 2026, lower-secondary schooling, and a competitive-sports medical certificate under three months old.
- Italian or EU citizenship.
- An Italian B2 language certificate.
- Route record: the "reference CV" on the [Veneto page](https://guidealpineveneto.it/corsi-per-aspirante-guida-alpina/) lists 31 classic rock and snow, ice and mixed routes, plus 2 multi-pitch sport routes, 2 icefalls and 15 ski-mountaineering tours, all led or swung leads in the Alps.
- Practical tests: under Piedmont's 2026/28 [rules](https://www.guidealpinepiemonte.it/res/download/pdf/981_it.pdf), the timed ski ascent is about 800 m at a minimum of 600 m an hour, and missing it means elimination.

### Cost

- **Interregional pole**: the selection fee is €400. Tuition for the 2026/28 cycle is not published; a Veneto regional resolution says the Collegio sets it according to the total number of students. Ask the Piedmont Collegio (formazione@guidealpinepiemonte.it). A third-party guide article puts the previous cycle at €18,600, without citing an original source.
- **Lombardy**: the [regional government page](https://www.regione.lombardia.it/cultura-turismo-e-sport/professioni-della-montagna/aspiranti-guide-alpine) and the Collegio's schedule give AG1 tuition of €8,000 for 2027, with travel, huts and hotels extra.
- **South Tyrol**: provincial law [LG 33/1991](https://lexbrowser.provinz.bz.it/doc/de/lp-1991-33/landesgesetz_vom_13_dezember_1991_nr_33.aspx?view=1) says the province pays all or part of course costs, with the trainee's share set by the provincial government.

### Language

- Interregional pole: Italian, with a B2 certificate required at application.
- South Tyrol: courses and exams in Italian or German, the candidate's choice (LG 33/1991, art. 8).
- Valle d'Aosta: registration requires passing exams in Italian and French, according to the [regional council's current text](http://www.consiglio.vda.it/app/leggieregolamenti/dettaglio?numero_legge=7%2F97&tipo=L&versione=V).

### Legal status

- Guiding clients in mountaineering, ski mountaineering or instruction on any terrain is reserved for guides registered on an albo (L. 6/1989, art. 2).
- Working without registration is punished under article 348 of the penal code.
- Registration lasts 3 years; renewal requires a medical check and continuing training.
- An aspirante may guide paying clients alone. Harder climbs are allowed only as part of a party led by a full guide.

### Foreigners

- **Most regions**: the national law and the documents of the interregional pole, Veneto, Lombardy and Trentino all say "Italian or EU citizen". On that basis a Taiwanese applicant generally cannot apply; this is an inference from those official conditions.
- **Valle d'Aosta is the exception**: article 7 of its current regional law lets non-EU citizens register as long as they hold legal residence in Italy, and article 11 applies the same conditions to selection, courses and exams. In other words, legal residence in Italy comes first.
- **South Tyrol**: the selection has no nationality condition, but registering on the albo requires proof of nationality and residence in South Tyrol, and non-EU citizens fall under national immigration law. Whether you can train without registering is something to ask the provincial government (tourismus@provinz.bz.it).
- **Guides already qualified abroad**: those bringing their own clients and working in Italy only occasionally do not need to register; regular work requires formal recognition first.

### Number of guides

Conagai's website gives **1,202 full guides and 176 aspirants**. The page is undated, and the [archive.org snapshot from April 2026](https://web.archive.org/web/2026id_/https://www.guidealpine.it/co-na-agai.html) still shows these figures.

## What this means for Taiwanese readers

Austria and Germany list no nationality requirement; the real barrier is German. Italy, apart from Valle d'Aosta and South Tyrol, mostly limits applicants to Italian or EU citizens. In all three you need to settle on a state or region first, because the rules follow the local authority. For how to choose overall, see [The path for Taiwanese climbers](/posts/climbing/2026-10-17-ifmga-taiwan-path-en).
