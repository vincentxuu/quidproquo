France and Switzerland share a post because both treat mountain guiding as a state-regulated profession. France issues a state diploma, Switzerland a federal professional certificate, and in both guiding for pay without one carries penalties. Both are founding members of the [IFMGA](https://ifmga.net/member-associations/member-associations), and both train well beyond the IFMGA [minimum of 94 days](https://ifmga.net/professional-mountain-guide/training-and-assessment).

The difference is who runs the training. France has one state school, ENSA. In Switzerland the guides' association, SBV, organises the federal exam. For a Taiwanese reader, neither country lists a nationality requirement for applicants; the real hurdles are language, cost, and working legally during the apprenticeship.

The shared IFMGA training framework is covered in [What you learn and how you are tested](/posts/climbing/2026-10-17-ifmga-training-and-exams-en). This post covers only what each country does.

## The two countries at a glance

| Country | Association / training body | Years of training | Approximate cost | Teaching language | Required by law? | Can foreigners apply? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| France | [ENSA](https://www.ensm.sports.gouv.fr/formation-de-guide-haute-montagne/) (ENSM's Chamonix campus); union [SNGM](https://www.guides-montagne.org/pages/le-sngm) | Officially at least 4 | €17,694.50 (official 2026 fee table) | French | Yes: state diploma plus professional card | No nationality requirement listed; the apprenticeship involves guiding, so you need the right to work |

## France

### Training path

The French qualification is the "diplôme d'État d'alpinisme – guide de haute montagne". The only training body is [ENSM](https://www.ensm.sports.gouv.fr/formation-de-guide-haute-montagne/) through its ENSA campus in Chamonix. The current rules are in the [arrêté of 19 December 2023](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/jorf/id/JORFTEXT000048881074).

1. **Probatoire (entrance exam)**: three parts, each eliminatory. Winter covers an interview, avalanche transceiver search and skiing in all snow; early summer covers navigation, sport climbing, crampon technique and mixed terrain; late summer is 5 days of independent movement in the high mountains.
2. **UF1**: 4 weeks on non-glaciated terrain. Pass it and a half-day EPMSP test and you become an aspirant 1, allowed to guide some activities for pay, such as crags and via ferrata.
3. **Common module FGCMEEESM**: 5 days of shared theory for mountain sports professions.
4. **UF2 to UF4**: ski mountaineering, summer alpine climbing, then winter climbing with ski mountaineering, moving you up to aspirant 2, 3 and 4. Each level widens the routes you may guide.
5. **UF5**: the final assessment, after which you receive the state diploma.

Other rules:

- **Tutors**: at least 2 during training (tutorat), plus a second personal route list to complete.
- **Total hours**: 728, spread over 21 weeks.
- **Years**: ENSM says "at least 4 years"; its [flowchart](https://www.ensm.sports.gouv.fr/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/CURSUS-GUIDE-1.pdf) spreads UF1 to the diploma across 4 calendar years.
- **Refresher**: every 6 years at ENSA after qualifying, or you lose the right to work.

### How each stage is assessed

[Part 2](/posts/climbing/2026-10-17-ifmga-training-and-exams-en) already tables how each probatoire test works. What it leaves out is who decides: an assessment commission sets and marks the tests, and a separate jury rules on pass or fail. The jury is chaired by the head of the national ski and mountaineering service. Its members include the ENSM director, representatives of the two climbing federations and the two guides' professional bodies, and ENSA guide instructors.

Once you are in training, every stage is assessed during the course and at its end. Tests may be adjusted for terrain, snow and weather, and the assessment commission tells you beforehand how they will run.

| Stage | Length | What is assessed |
|---|---|---|
| UF1 | 4 weeks | A 2-hour navigation paper: analyse a route on the map and combine GPS, altimeter, compass and map. Rescue someone stuck on a rock face, then send a clear, structured distress call in French. Teach a group of schoolchildren at a crag for up to 1 hour, then explain your teaching and safety choices aloud. Take a trainee up a one-day non-glacier climb |
| EPMSP | Half a day | Find checkpoints in the field by any means. Passing lets you guide for pay as an aspirant 1 |
| Common module FGCMEEESM | 5 days | Law, mountain economics, working with all kinds of clients, sports physiology, ending in a 3-hour written exam. Fail it and you cannot start UF2 |
| UF2 | 3 weeks | Dig and analyse at least one snow pit and explain avalanche mechanisms. Crevasse self-rescue, timed and done in a set way. Plan and lead a group on a one-day ski tour |
| UF3 | 4 weeks | Level-two rock rescue, one step up from UF1. A "special manoeuvre" of up to 1 hour. Brief the party, lead a rope team on a route of up to one day while coaching on the way, then debrief, including an assessment of your own performance |
| UF4 | At least 3 weeks | Winter rock rescue. A technical demonstration of up to 2 days. Brief, lead a winter climb or ski tour of up to 48 hours, then debrief |
| UF5 | 27–29 days | Present a "performance project" aloud, covering the clients' level, safety plan, budget and kit. A complex rescue: get the casualty out of an exposed spot, then hand over to a simulated rescue service. A foreign-language oral of up to 20 minutes, in German, English, Spanish or Italian |

Beyond the table, three more rules apply:

- From UF3 on, every stage adds a first-aid paper and a professional-knowledge paper.
- UF5 has a winter and a summer part; fail the winter part and you cannot take the summer part.
- After the UF4 assessment you must wait a full 12 months before starting UF5.

Between stages you complete your own [route list](https://www.ensm.sports.gouv.fr/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Declaration-liste-de-courses-Perfectionnement-personnel-et-tutorat-A-effectuer-entre-UF1-et-UF5.pdf), submitted before UF4 and UF5:

| Period | Personal routes | Tutored days and client work |
|---|---|---|
| UF1 to UF4 | Aid climbing, long rock routes, grade 5 icefalls; for example 3 rock routes of 300 m or more from TD+ | Crag, snowshoe, off-piste, ski touring and mountaineering, at least 10 days in total |
| UF3 to UF5 | Big limestone walls, set classic grandes courses, 8 routes from AD to D+ on typical guiding terrain | 25 paid client outings on at least 15 different routes, across rock, snow, ice, mixed and ski |

- **Tutored placements are unpaid**; only expenses can be reimbursed. Tutors may not count the trainee as an extra guide to take on more clients ([ENSM letter to tutors](https://www.ensm.sports.gouv.fr/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Lettre-aux-tuteurs.pdf)).
- **A third fail ends your training.** Fail the same assessment a third time and your training record is closed, along with any aspirant guiding rights you already hold.

### Entry requirements

- Aged 18 by 1 January of the exam year, with no upper age limit.
- PSC1 civic first aid, plus a medical certificate under 3 months old.
- The [2026 route list](https://www.ensm.sports.gouv.fr/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Liste-de-courses-2026-nouveau-cursus_V2.pdf): at least 55 routes spread over at least 3 years, all led or swung leads. It covers five categories: alpine, rock, snow and ice, ski mountaineering and icefalls; the icefalls, for example, must be WI5 or harder.
- The probatoire is hard. [ENSM's published figures](https://www.ensm.sports.gouv.fr/formation-de-guide-haute-montagne/) show a 26% pass rate for the 2025 summer part.

### Cost

ENSM's "Tarifs 2026" table adds up to **€17,694.50**. That covers registration, the three probatoire parts and hourly tuition for UF1 to UF5. Food and lodging are extra, and the common module is not in that table.

The "about €11,000" figure you will see online is a [2022 estimate from the French government's qualifications portal](https://web.archive.org/web/20220701012810/https://www.guichet-qualifications.fr/en/dqp/sport/alpine-mountain-guide.html) and is out of date. ENSM raised its hourly rate for 2026, so go by the 2026 table. French funding schemes such as CPF and France Travail can cover the fees.

### Language

ENSM never states "teaching is in French", but materials and written exams are in French, and a UF1 assessment item reads "transmit an alert message in French". In practice you need to study and sit exams in French. UF5 also includes an oral exam of up to 20 minutes in one foreign language: German, English, Spanish or Italian.

### Legal status

- Under article L.212-1 of the Code du sport, teaching or leading a sport for pay requires a registered qualification. For mountain guides that is this state diploma.
- Before starting work you declare yourself to the SDJES office of the département where you work and receive a professional card valid for 5 years.
- Working for pay without the qualification or declaration carries up to 1 year in prison and a €15,000 fine under [L.212-8](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000006547574); employers face the same penalty.

### Foreigners

- **Applying**: ENSM's entry conditions include no nationality or residence requirement. The only nationality-linked item is the JDC (defence and citizenship day) certificate, which the arrêté's annex limits to French candidates under 25.
- **Apprenticeship**: training includes real guiding with clients, so a non-EU citizen would first need the right to work in France. This is an inference from article 2 of the arrêté; ENSM publishes no guidance for foreigners.
- **Holding only a foreign IFMGA certificate**: you cannot work in France directly. According to [Bpifrance Création](https://bpifrance-creation.fr/activites-reglementees/guide-haute-montagne), a foreign diploma must be declared equivalent by the sports minister after an opinion from a recognition committee, and you need adequate French.
- **A precedent**: Chinese climber Yang Xiaohua took this route. [Xinhua](https://app.xinhuanet.com/news/article.html?articleId=2e9e7d8e869af09a1dcbe7088c998783) described her in 2026 as an aspirant guide at ENSA (in Chinese), meaning she had passed the probatoire but did not yet hold the diploma.

### Number of guides

France publishes no official count of diploma holders; the closest figure comes from the SNGM union, which says it covers almost every French mountain guide.

- **Active members**: 1,305, according to SNGM's [2025 press kit](http://guides-montagne.org/sites/default/files/2025_Dossier%20de%20presse_VF-bd_compressed.pdf) (counted in October 2024, aspirants included).
- **New diplomas**: few; [ENSM's statistics](https://www.ensm.sports.gouv.fr/resultats-statistiques-satisfaction-et-debouches-ensa/) show just 29 in 2023.

## Switzerland

### Training path

The Swiss qualification is the federal professional certificate "Bergführer/in mit eidgenössischem Fachausweis". SBV runs every module and organises the federal professional exam. Its [training regulations](https://sbv-asgm.ch/wp-content/uploads/2025_10_01-Reglement-Bergfuehrerausbildung-1.pdf) set out three phases:

1. **Entry evaluation**: skiing in January; rock, ice and navigation in September. Nobody is eliminated here; it tells you whether you stand a chance.
2. **Phase 1, aspirant course** (about 68 days): modules on medicine and rescue, avalanches and skiing, steep ice, Winter I, sport climbing, Summer I, leadership, and marketing and business, taken in order, each with its own exam. Passing earns the Aspirant certificate.
3. **Phase 2, practice period**: 30 supervised tours with clients, 10 hard tours of your own, and a "nature and environment" module. Each Aspirant chooses a mentor (Lehrmeister).
4. **Phase 3, guide course**: no earlier than the second calendar year after the Aspirant certificate. It covers Winter II, Summer II and the final exam.

SBV describes the whole programme as "about 3 years, alongside work". Adding up the days in the regulations gives about 96, a total I calculated myself.

### How each stage is assessed

The entry evaluation, how each module is tested, the final-exam stations and the grading scale are all covered in [Part 2](/posts/climbing/2026-10-17-ifmga-training-and-exams-en). This section adds the rules: who examines you, how many retakes you get, and what the last two modules ask for on top.

**Who examines you**

- At least two examiners agree each station's grade, and an observer keeps a written record ([SBV exam regulations](https://sbv-asgm.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023_03_09-PO-Bergfuehrer_revidiert_DE.pdf)).
- Examiners must stand aside if they are your technical director, a class instructor who taught you, a relative, or a current or former boss or colleague. Where an exception is made, such people may fill at most one examiner seat.
- On final-exam day the examiners hold a preliminary grading meeting, then the chair of the quality assurance committee runs the formal one, and **results are announced the same day** ([exam guidelines](https://sbv-asgm.ch/wp-content/uploads/2025_10_01-Wegleitung-Berufspruefung-Bergfuehrer-1.pdf)).

**The experience grade**

Throughout each module, class instructors keep an "experience grade". Any failing mark must come with written reasons and a talk with you. In the winter modules the experience grade covers 5 categories: the average must be at least 4, with no more than 1 category failed.

**How many retakes you get**

| Scope | Limit |
|---|---|
| A single module | Repeat at most 2 times |
| The whole training | Repeat at most 4 modules |
| Final exam | Retake up to 2 times, only the failed part |

Repeating a module usually means waiting a year.

**The last two modules**

The 30 supervised tours and 10 tours of your own from the practice period are checked in two batches, before each of these modules ([SBV training regulations](https://sbv-asgm.ch/wp-content/uploads/2025_10_01-Reglement-Bergfuehrerausbildung-1.pdf) 9.82, 9.83):

| Module | Complete before starting | What it assesses |
|---|---|---|
| [Winter II](https://sbv-asgm.ch/wp-content/uploads/9-Winter-II_2026-2.pdf) | 15 supervised ski tours plus 5 ski tours of your own at grade S or harder; also a valid Aspirant certificate, a cantonal permit and the "nature and environment" module | No more teaching, only performance. Three class instructors grade independently, with the same pass rule as the winter modules |
| [Summer II](https://sbv-asgm.ch/wp-content/uploads/10-Sommer-II_08.2026.pdf) | Pass Winter II; 15 supervised alpine or mixed tours plus 5 tours of your own at grade S or harder | More than Summer I: lead one or more rope teams, foresee where clients will struggle, pace a whole day, make decisions on your own, work with other guides and take an Aspirant along |

Fail the Summer II experience grade and you cannot enter the final exam; you repeat the whole module the next year. The final exam follows straight after Summer II.

### Entry requirements

- At least 18, a very strong skier, with at least 3 years of summer and winter mountaineering.
- The [tour list](https://sbv-asgm.ch/wp-content/uploads/A_Tourenliste_Vers.10.2026.pdf) must span 3 years or more and includes, for example, five 4000 m peaks at AD+ or harder, five sport routes at 6b+ or harder and five icefalls at WI4+.
- A recommendation letter from an SBV-recognised guide; just naming the referee is not enough.
- An oral foreign-language test at entry, waived if you hold a language certificate.
- Private liability insurance of CHF 5 million.
- The final exam also requires completed upper-secondary education (Sekundarstufe II) or equivalent.

### Cost

The reference prices in the [2026 module table](https://sbv-asgm.ch/wp-content/uploads/A_Moduluebersicht_2026_Vers.07.25.pdf) add up to about **CHF 31,230**, already including lodging and half board. SBV's own round figure is about CHF 30,000. Travel, equipment and insurance are extra.

There is a subsidy a Taiwanese trainee will probably not get. The [federal government refunds 50% of course fees](https://sbv-asgm.ch/wp-content/uploads/Flyer-HBB_SBFI_A5_2025_DT_web.pdf), capped at CHF 9,500, but only if your tax residence is in Switzerland when the exam decision arrives. A trainee living abroad would likely pay close to the full amount.

### Language

Modules are taught in German and French. Assessments and the final exam can be taken in any of the three official languages, German, French or Italian; to sit them in Italian you must apply at least a month before the module starts.

### Legal status

- The federal act on mountain guides and other risk activities, [RiskG](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2013/87/de), has since 2014 required a permit to offer alpine tours, ski tours, icefalls, multi-pitch climbing and similar activities.
- The canton of residence or business seat issues the permit. It is valid across Switzerland for 4 years, and renewal requires proof of safety and risk-management training.
- Guiding without a permit can bring a fine of up to CHF 10,000 for intentional cases. SBV says guiding counts as commercial "from the first franc earned", even when only travel costs are reimbursed.
- The [RiskV ordinance](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2019/124/de) requires professional liability insurance of at least CHF 5 million a year.
- Aspirants also need a cantonal permit and may guide only under a qualified guide's supervision.

### Foreigners

- **Applying**: SBV's regulations state that people living abroad apply on the same terms as Swiss residents, and no official document sets a nationality limit.
- **Modules taken abroad do not count**; you start again from the entry evaluation. Someone who already holds Aspirant status in another IFMGA country can pass an aptitude assessment and join phase 3 directly.
- **Right to work**: according to [SBV's guidance on guiding without a permit](https://sbv-asgm.ch/wp-content/uploads/SBV-2025-Wegleitung-Fuehren-ohne-Bewilligung-V08_Kurzfassung_02.2026.pdf), nationals of countries outside the EU and EFTA must first obtain a cantonal labour or immigration permit before working, and a RiskG permit does not replace a residence permit. The practice period requires guiding 30 tours with clients, so a Taiwanese trainee cannot avoid this step.
- **Already holding IFMGA**: you can apply for a Swiss practice permit, since an IFMGA certificate is treated as equivalent to the federal certificate.

### Number of guides

SBV's [2024 figures](https://sbv-asgm.ch/wp-content/uploads/8_Der-SBV-in-Zahlen_de_240130.pdf) count 1,580 guide members. The government's register counts something else: the [BASPO register](https://www.apps.baspo.admin.ch/riskv/CMS/riskvcms.asp?spr=d&art=P) listed 1,251 valid mountain guide permits on 17 October 2026. One counts association members, the other permits, so they are not directly comparable.

## What this means for Taiwanese readers

Neither France nor Switzerland bars foreigners from applying, but both require years of legally guiding clients there. France adds French; Switzerland adds German or French and a work permit that is hard for non-EU citizens to obtain. If you are aiming for either, sort out residence and language before technique. For how to choose overall, see [The path for Taiwanese climbers](/posts/climbing/2026-10-17-ifmga-taiwan-path-en).
