> 🌏 [中文版](/posts/climbing/2026-10-17-ifmga-hire-a-guide)

This post is for anyone planning to hire a guide abroad. You have found someone in Chamonix, Zermatt or Japan who calls themselves a "mountain guide". How do you check they are IFMGA, what does a day cost, which costs are missing from the quote, and should you tip at the end?

For what IFMGA is and how it differs from Taiwan's mountain guide license, see [Part 0: the overview](/posts/climbing/2026-10-17-ifmga-mountain-guide-overview-en).

## When people hire a guide

Judging by what guiding companies actually sell, the common situations are these (prices under "Prices by region" below):

| Situation | Example |
|---|---|
| Classic alpine routes | Mont Blanc, Grossglockner |
| Signature peaks | Matterhorn Hörnli ridge: all three outfitters found work 1:1, and ZERMATTERS requires a preparation climb with them first |
| Alpine rock ridges | Arête des Cosmiques on the Aiguille du Midi |
| Ice climbing | Chamonix icefalls, Canmore in Canada |
| Ski touring | Hut-to-hut tours in the Alps |
| Multi-day expeditions | Aoraki/Mount Cook, Mount Aspiring in New Zealand |

There is also a legal side: in some countries, **anyone who takes money to lead people in the mountains** must be qualified.

- Italy: leading clients in mountaineering, ski mountaineering or instruction, on any terrain and at any difficulty, is reserved for guides on the official register ([Law 6/1989, art. 2](https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn:nir:stato:legge:1989-01-02;6), in Italian).
- Switzerland: guiding without a license is fined up to CHF 10,000, and the Swiss guide association says it counts as commercial "from the first franc earned" ([SBV guidance on guiding without a license](https://sbv-asgm.ch/wp-content/uploads/SBV-2025-Wegleitung-Fuehren-ohne-Bewilligung-V08_Kurzfassung_02.2026.pdf), in German).

These rules govern the person being paid; they do not require you to hire a guide. A likely practical consequence for readers: paying an "experienced friend" to take you up in these countries may mean that person is working illegally.

## How to check a guide is IFMGA

### 1. Look for the badge and ask for the ID

[IFMGA's find-a-guide page](https://ifmga.net/professional-mountain-guide/find-a-ifmga-mountain-guide) says to look for the IFMGA/UIAGM/IVBV badge, and that every IFMGA guide holds an official membership card. IFMGA guide Eddie Schoen of [Rebel Alpine](https://rebelalpine.com/media/why-hire-a-fully-certified-mountain-guide) describes the badge as round (blue or white), with a silver pin version as well.

That page has no search function. It lists three ways to find a guide: national member association lists, local guiding companies or guide offices, and climbing schools that employ IFMGA guides.

### 2. Check national association lists

| Country | List | What to look for |
|---|---|---|
| New Zealand | [NZMGA Find a Guide](https://www.nzmga.org.nz/find-a-guide) | Each guide is marked "IFMGA Mountain Guide", "Ski Guide" and so on |
| Canada | [ACMG Member Directory](https://acmg.ca/member-search/) | Filter by qualification (Mountain Guide, Rock Guide, Ski Guide) |
| Japan | [JMGA active members](https://jfmga.jp/aboutus_guides.php) (in Japanese) | Only those marked 国際山岳ガイド are IFMGA; 37 as of October 2026 |

### 3. IFMGA's ID lookup

[searchguides.ifmga.info](https://searchguides.ifmga.info) is a "Check Mountain Guide by ID" tool that checks a qualification by member number. During research it required a login, so in practice ask the guide for the number and have the guide or their association confirm it.

Another official page, [ifmga.net/browse](https://ifmga.net/browse), has a search form, but queries returned empty results during research, and the page states the data comes from member associations and IFMGA does not guarantee it.

### 4. Titles that look similar

Being called a "mountain guide" does not make someone IFMGA:

| What you see | What it means |
|---|---|
| "AMGA trained" | Took one AMGA course (Rebel Alpine) |
| "AMGA certified" | May only be [SPI](https://www.amga.com/programs/climbing-instructor-programs/single-pitch-instructor-program), a single-pitch climbing instructor |
| AMGA Certified Rock Guide | One discipline; all three of Rock, Alpine and Ski are needed for IFMGA |
| Japanese 登山ガイド or 山岳ガイド | Not the same as 国際山岳ガイド (international mountain guide) |
| "IFMGA No Ski" | Wears the badge but cannot guide skiing in other countries |

In the US, [Colorado Mountain School](https://web.archive.org/web/20251022101508/https://coloradomountainschool.com/course/rock-climbing-guiding) headlines "Professional AMGA Guides" and does not say IFMGA. Before booking, ask directly: "Is the guide assigned to me IFMGA?"

Three things to keep in mind:

- **Not every row is confirmed IFMGA.** Exum's page does not say whether its guides are IFMGA; Alpine Ascents' Alps price list explicitly says IFMGA guide fees.
- **Japanese association rates are not IFMGA rates.** The [Kanagawa Mountain Guide Association](https://kmga.jp/request/) (in Japanese) charges ¥30,000 a day on ordinary trails as its standard for general guides.
- **Prices jump by year.** Compagnie has already switched to its 2027 price list, and Mont Blanc Premium is €85 more than in 2026; book now and you get the new price.

In Taiwan dollars: at the [Bank of Taiwan](https://rate.bot.com.tw/xrt?Lang=zh-TW) spot selling rate on 2026-10-17, €450 works out to roughly NT$16,500. Check again the week you travel.

## What quotes usually leave out

Treat the guide's fee as a starting point, not a total.

1. **The guide's hut and meals**: in the Alps the client usually pays. ZERMATTERS says the Hörnli hut half-board is paid for "you and the guide", about CHF 150 per person.
2. **The guide's lifts and trains**: Courmayeur states the client pays the guide's €25 Aiguille du Midi cable car.
3. **Your own lifts, huts and transport**: the Aiguille du Midi cable car is €81–83 per person (Courmayeur).
4. **Preparation climbs**: ZERMATTERS requires a preparation route before the Matterhorn, at an extra CHF 360–785 ([2024 official price PDF](https://web.archive.org/web/20240515053202/https://www.zermatters.ch/sites/default/files/media-document/2024_Preisvarianten%20Matterhorn_DE.pdf)).
5. **Gear rental**: about CHF 50–70 in Zermatt.
6. **Insurance**: Courmayeur asks for a European Health Insurance Card (EHIC) or personal health insurance. The [EHIC](https://ec.europa.eu/social/main.jsp?catId=559&langId=en) is issued by European national health systems; Taiwan's National Health Insurance is not part of it, so Taiwanese clients need their own policy that covers mountain activities.
7. **Taxes and fees**: Canada adds 5% GST (Yamnuska); Courmayeur charges a 5% admin fee.
8. **Helicopters in New Zealand**: Aspiring Guides charges NZ$1,500 extra for the helicopter approach, while [Adventure Consultants](https://adventureconsultants.com/guided-ascents/new-zealand-ascents/mount-aspiring) includes it on the same mountain.

Japan is no different: the Kanagawa association states the guide's transport, lodging and meals are charged on the day.

When comparing quotes, ask two questions first: is the price per person or per group, and who pays for the guide's hut and lifts?

## Client-to-guide ratios

Ratios are set by the route. You cannot squeeze in more people to save money.

| Activity | Typical ratio | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Matterhorn Hörnli ridge | 1:1 | ZERMATTERS, Mountains & Beyond |
| Mont Blanc normal route | 1:1 or 2:1 | Compagnie, [Simmons Mountain Works](https://simmonsmountain.works/termsconspolis/alps-rate-inclusions-and-exclusions) |
| Grossglockner | Up to 2–3:1, 2:1 when conditions are poor | Tauernguide, [MT Experts](https://www.mt-experts.at/hochtour/grossglockner-mit-bergfuehrer) |
| Ski touring, hut-to-hut | Commonly 3:1 | Simmons Mountain Works |
| Multi-pitch ice | Up to 2:1 | Yamnuska |
| Single-pitch rock or ice instruction | Up to 6:1 | Yamnuska |

Many European guides charge "one guide for one day" and the group splits it. Simmons Mountain Works gives the example of US$700 a day: two clients together each pay half.

## Tipping

| Region | Custom | Source |
|---|---|---|
| US | Common, about 10–20% of the trip price | [Mountain Trip](https://mountaintrip.com/how-much-should-i-tip-my-guide); [RMI](https://www.rmiguides.com/programs/mt-rainier-four-day-select-climb) says 10–15% |
| Canada | Listed as not included in the price | Yamnuska |
| Chamonix, France | Guides do not expect tips | IFMGA guide [Stuart Macdonald](https://stuartmacdonald.org/) |
| Europe overall | No fixed rule; appreciated if given | UK-based [Alpine Guides](https://www.alpine-guides.com/climb/planning-your-climbing-trip/travelling-from-the-usacanada/) |
| Switzerland | Voluntary, CHF 50–100 per day per group for a private full day (travel company advice) | [Swiss Epic Tours](https://www.swissepictours.com/en/articles/tipping-your-private-guide-in-switzerland-the-2026-etiquette-guide) |
| Japan | Tipping is generally not expected; if you do, use an envelope | [JNTO](https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/tipping-in-japan/) |
| New Zealand | Not expected for guided outdoor activities | [Tourism New Zealand](https://www.newzealand.com.au/info/frequently-asked-questions/is-it-expected-to-tip-during-guided-outdoor-activities-like-hiking-or-kayaking) |

Mountain Trip adds two points: tip even if you do not summit, because you are paying for judgment and care; with several guides, hand it to one of them to share.

The Japan and Switzerland entries come from general tourism or travel company sources; no guide-specific rule was found.
