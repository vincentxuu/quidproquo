In Alpine huts and on New Zealand guiding company websites you keep running into "IFMGA Mountain Guide" or "UIAGM", usually next to a round badge. This post answers three questions: what these guides do, who certifies them, and whether it is the same thing as Taiwan's "mountain guide" (山域嚮導) license.

The short version: IFMGA is a federation of national guide associations, and its standard is at least 94 days of training across four kinds of terrain. Taiwan is not a member. Taiwan's mountain guide license is a separate domestic system, and the two do not recognize each other.

## What an IFMGA guide does

The [IFMGA training standard](https://ifmga.net/professional-mountain-guide/training-and-assessment) requires one guide to cover four disciplines, not just one:

| Discipline | When you would hire them |
|---|---|
| Mountaineering | Glaciers, snow ridges, mixed alpine routes |
| Ski mountaineering | Ski touring, hut-to-hut tours, ski ascents |
| Rock climbing | Multi-pitch crags, alpine rock ridges |
| Ice climbing | Frozen waterfalls, alpine ice faces |

That is the main difference from single-discipline licenses in most countries. In the United States, for example, [AMGA](https://www.amga.com/programs/mountain-guide-programs) runs Rock, Alpine and Ski tracks that can be earned separately, but a Certified Rock Guide alone is not an IFMGA guide; you need all three. Training and exams are covered in [Part 2: What They Learn and How They Are Tested](/posts/climbing/2026-10-17-ifmga-training-and-exams-en).

## What the federation is

One organization, three names: IFMGA in English (International Federation of Mountain Guides Associations), UIAGM in French, IVBV in German. Its office is in Martigny, Switzerland.

- **Founded by**: the guide associations of Austria, France, Italy and Switzerland in 1965 ([IFMGA history page](https://ifmga.net/about-ifmga/history-of-ifmga)).
- **Role**: sets training standards and a code of professional ethics, promotes mutual recognition and guide mobility between member countries, and helps new associations join.
- **Size**: the history page says "more than 20 countries" and almost 7,000 guides, without a date.

### Associations issue the badge, not the federation

IFMGA does not train or test individuals. According to its [Become a Guide](https://ifmga.net/professional-mountain-guide/become-a-professional-mountain-guide) page, a national member association trains and assesses you and then issues the IFMGA badge. Section 3.3 of the federation's rulebook, the [IFMGA Platform](https://www.eemga.org/about-eemga/the-ifmga-platform/), says anyone who meets the standard can start training regardless of nationality, but IFMGA recognition comes "when they become a member of an IFMGA Member Association".

For a Taiwanese reader the implication is plain: a country without a member association has no local route to the badge.

## Who the members are

The [member associations page](https://ifmga.net/member-associations/member-associations) lists 28 member associations: 25 countries plus EEMGA, a cross-border Eastern European association (Romania, Bulgaria, Hungary, Greece, Cyprus, Ukraine and others). Italy alone has three associations.

| Region | Members |
|---|---|
| Alps | Austria, France, Germany, Italy, Switzerland, Slovenia |
| Rest of Europe | UK, Spain, Norway, Sweden, Poland, Czech Republic, Slovakia, EEMGA |
| Americas | USA, Canada, Argentina, Chile, Peru, Bolivia, Ecuador |
| Oceania | New Zealand |
| Asia and the Eurasian edge | Japan, Nepal, Kyrgyzstan, Georgia |

- Japan joined in 1991 and the US in 1997 ([IFMGA history PDF](https://ifmga.net/images/Documents/240212_History_IFMGA_update.pdf)).
- The [candidate associations page](https://ifmga.net/member-associations/candidate-associations) currently reads "No Pending Candidate Associations".
- **Taiwan, South Korea and China are not members and have no candidate association.**

### What No Ski means

The Peru, Bolivia, Nepal and Ecuador associations carry the "IFMGA No Ski" designation. Their guides wear the badge but have no ski guiding rights in other member countries, and must state No Ski in all external communication.

Switzerland shows what that means in practice. It treats an IFMGA-recognized guide certificate as equivalent to the federal one for licensing purposes, but a No Ski certificate does not qualify ([Swiss guide association SBV guidance on guiding without a license](https://sbv-asgm.ch/wp-content/uploads/SBV-2025-Wegleitung-Fuehren-ohne-Bewilligung-V08_Kurzfassung_02.2026.pdf)).

### Recognition is not a work permit

Mobility between member countries is a core IFMGA goal, but each country keeps its own paperwork. Two examples:

- Canada's [ACMG](https://acmg.ca/ifmga-mobility/): a visiting IFMGA guide may work up to 60 days a year, and guiding on public land needs a permit and liability insurance.
- Switzerland: nationals from outside the EU/EFTA need a cantonal work or immigration permit first; a guiding license does not replace a residence permit (same SBV guidance).

So "IFMGA guide" describes a competence standard and a recognition right. A work visa is a separate question.

## How it differs from Taiwan's mountain guide license

Taiwan's system is the Sports Administration's [Regulations for Mountain Guide Qualification Certification](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=H0120058) (in Chinese).

| | Taiwan mountain guide | IFMGA guide |
|---|---|---|
| Issued by | National certification under the Sports Administration | National member associations |
| Categories | Hiking, river tracing, rock, snow climbing, each separate | One certificate covering four disciplines |
| Training | 48 hours within 2 years plus 8 hours of first aid | At least 94 days, at least 84 of them in the field |
| Assessment | Written and practical tests (pass mark 70) plus a practicum | Aspirant course, supervised practice, final assessment |
| Validity | 4 years, renewal requires refresher training | At least 1 day of continuing professional development (CPD) every year |
| International recognition | None | Mutual recognition among member countries |

The two are on different scales and do not recognize each other. Holding all four Taiwanese licenses does not make you an IFMGA guide.

### Two common mix-ups

- **An old law mentioned an international federation**: the former [Mountain Guide Certification Regulations](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=H0120009) (in Chinese) let training recognized by an "international professional guide federation" count toward some subjects. That law was repealed on 22 February 2012, and the current regulations do not mention IFMGA.
- **A private title**: the Taiwanese outfitter [艾格探險](https://www.adventuretaiwan.com/mountain-guide-program/) (in Chinese) calls people who hold its hiking, rock and snow certificates "山域嚮導", saying it follows "the spirit of IFMGA". That is the company's own title, not an IFMGA qualification.
