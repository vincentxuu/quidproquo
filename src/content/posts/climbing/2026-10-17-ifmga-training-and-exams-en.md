This post answers two questions: what is the minimum training behind an IFMGA guide, and what do the exams actually test? If you are considering the path yourself, you will come away knowing what you need to build up before you even apply.

For what IFMGA is, see [Part 0: the overview](/posts/climbing/2026-10-17-ifmga-mountain-guide-overview-en). Country-by-country processes, costs and languages are linked at the end.

## The standard every member country shares

[IFMGA's training and assessment page](https://ifmga.net/professional-mountain-guide/training-and-assessment) sets the minimum that every member association must meet; each country's program is built on top of that floor.

### Four steps

1. **Entry requirements and exam**: submit a route list and pass a technical test.
2. **Aspirant Guide Course**: pass it and you become an aspirant guide.
3. **Supervised practice**: guide clients under the supervision of a senior IFMGA guide for at least 14 days.
4. **Mountain Guide Course**: final training and assessment; pass and you are qualified.

### How the 94 days are split

At least 94 days of training, at least 84 of them in the field:

| Area | Days |
|---|---|
| Mixed terrain (snow, ice, rock) | 32 |
| Skiing and winter | 32 |
| Rock climbing | 20 |
| Theory | 7 |
| Country-specific training | 3 |

The table adds up to exactly 94; the 14 supervised days come on top.

### Entry: 55 routes

Applicants must show at least 55 varied routes, covering rock climbing, ski mountaineering and ice climbing.

France spells this out most concretely: the [ENSM 2026 route list](https://www.ensm.sports.gouv.fr/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Liste-de-courses-2026-nouveau-cursus_V2.pdf) (in French) requires the 55 routes to span at least three years, with at least 24 done in the three years before the exam, all led or swinging leads. In other words, the build-up before entry alone takes years.

### After qualifying: annual CPD

Qualified guides need at least 1 day of continuing professional development (CPD) every year. In the US, certification stays valid only while membership and CPD are kept up ([AMGA old-site FAQ](http://old.amga.com/faqs/)).

## What the exams test: the US example

Each country slices the 94 days into its own courses and exams. The US [AMGA](https://www.amga.com/programs/mountain-guide-programs) split is the easiest to follow: three disciplines, Rock, Alpine and Ski, each running course → advanced course plus assistant exam → guide exam. Pass all three guide exams, add first aid and avalanche training, and you are IFMGA.

### The three Rock stages

The [Rock program page](https://www.amga.com/programs/mountain-guide-programs/rock-guide-program) lists the climbing resume required for each stage. It is the most direct answer to "what do they test": you must have climbed it yourself before you can learn to guide it.

| Stage | Days | Resume required before applying (excerpt) |
|---|---|---|
| Rock Guide Course (RGC) | 10 days (3:1) or 8 days (2:1) | In the last 5 years, 10 trad leads of 5.10a or harder; 50 multi-pitch trad routes; at least 5 years of climbing |
| Advanced Rock Guide Course & Assistant Exam (ARGC/AE) | 10 or 8 days | Since the RGC, 10 Grade IV trad routes and 20 Grade III routes guided or mock-guided |
| Rock Guide Exam (RGE) | 6 days | In the last 5 years, 10 leads of 5.11a; at least 20 guided routes, up to 15 of them mock-guided |

AMGA counts guided and mock-guided routes separately; from the term itself, a mock-guided route is presumably one done the way you would guide it but without a paying client. At the RGE you must lead 5.10+ consistently.

### How the titles step up

Per AMGA's [SOP v4.0](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1IJvCKAFRBrDEo8Zr0UPT5WVP7rOgGQ6P/view) (2024-11-15):

| Pass | Title |
|---|---|
| RGC | Apprentice Rock Guide |
| ARGC/AE | Assistant Rock Guide |
| RGE | Certified Rock Guide (one discipline, not IFMGA) |
| Assistant Exam in all three | Aspirant American Mountain Guide |
| Guide Exam in all three | American Mountain Guide / IFMGA Guide |

Alpine and Ski follow the same pattern, with the resume swapped for glaciers, ice, snow-slope angles and ski vertical. The [Ski program](https://www.amga.com/programs/mountain-guide-programs/ski-guide-program)'s entry course, for example, requires a skiing video and at least 20 ski tours on record.

### Prerequisites beyond climbing

Every course requires current AMGA membership, CPR, and an approved [WFR](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1n1AnL4sMr4q6r88iXkf27bTqZZk7fjtv/view) (at least 70 hours; fully online courses are not accepted). The later Alpine and Ski stages also require professional avalanche training, Pro 1 and Pro 2.

### How long and how much

- **Days**: AMGA's old site says "86+ days of training" ([old.amga.com](http://old.amga.com/ifmga-mountain-guide/)).
- **Years**: for Rock alone, [Golden Mountain Guides](https://goldenmountainguides.com/blog/so-you-want-to-become-a-climbing-guide) estimates most guides take 2–4 years; for all three, [Mountain School](https://www.mountainschool.com/content/main/amga-and-ifmga-certification) says about 5 years.
- **Tuition**: summing the 2026–27 course list gives about US$40,650 for all three disciplines, excluding first aid, avalanche courses, food, lodging and travel (this post's own sum of the course list). Mountain School puts the all-in cost at "upwards of $50,000".
- **Time windows**: most resume items must fall within the last 5 years, so stretching it out lets earlier routes expire.
- **If you fail**: a no-pass means retaking the whole course; a re-exam result means a make-up exam is arranged (AMGA old-site FAQ).

In 2025, 191 people held the AMGA/IFMGA certification ([AMGA 2025 Annual Report](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/674f3a589212e914d517c321/69c2f6ee9bb8a0619d1971d7_109e076586766d6068e53f56007d5a91_2025%20AMGA%20Annual%20Report%20Compressed.pdf)).

## Beyond technique: guiding people

Climbing well is not the same as guiding well. Every country's curriculum includes training for being in the mountains with clients:

| Country | What | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Switzerland | Aspirant course includes Leadership (about 2 days) and marketing and business (about 3 days); aspirants guide 30 supervised client tours | [SBV training regulations](https://sbv-asgm.ch/wp-content/uploads/2025_10_01-Reglement-Bergfuehrerausbildung-1.pdf) (in German) |
| Germany | The state exam tests guiding, teaching and theory; the theory subject "communication" covers English, French and Spanish | [VDBS 2026 training handbook](https://storage.tramino.net/vdbs/1544961/information-zur-berg-skifhrer-ausbild-ung-2026.pdf) (in German) |
| Austria | The curriculum includes "basic foreign language" so guides can talk to clients in other languages | [Tyrol T-BSFV](https://www.jusline.at/gesetz/t-bsfv/gesamt) (in German) |
| France | The final stage, UF5, includes a 20-minute oral exam in a foreign language (German, English, Spanish or Italian) | [ENSM guide training page](https://www.ensm.sports.gouv.fr/formation-de-guide-haute-montagne/) (in French) |
| Slovakia | A dedicated assessment of "the ability to guide clients under supervision" | [NAHVSR exam rules 2025](https://www.nahvsr.sk/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/skusobny-poriadok-2025.pdf) (in Slovak) |
| US | Courses run in English; fluency is not required, but you must understand instructions, especially on risk management | [AMGA Essential Eligibility Criteria](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1g3PHtitPkyrfiaBPHXsMFtW0PtRFFQrb/view) |

IFMGA also sets a code of professional ethics (see [Part 0](/posts/climbing/2026-10-17-ifmga-mountain-guide-overview-en)). For Taiwanese readers considering the path, the language column is the first thing to prepare for: most countries teach and examine in their own language.
