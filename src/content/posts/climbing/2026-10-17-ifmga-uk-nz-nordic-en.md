> 🌏 [中文版](/posts/climbing/2026-10-17-ifmga-uk-nz-nordic)

These four countries share a post for two reasons. First, none of them has a law saying "anyone who guides in the mountains must hold IFMGA certification": the UK licenses providers who sell adventure activities to under-18s, New Zealand regulates adventure activity operators, Norway regulates nothing, and Sweden relies on general advice from its consumer agency. Second, the UK and New Zealand teach in English, which makes them the easiest for Taiwanese readers to follow, while Norway and Sweden show two other Nordic models: a state-recognized vocational school (Norway) and a small association running its own scheme (Sweden, which had help from the UK's BMG when it started).

The common IFMGA standard (94 days of training, 55 routes to get in) is covered in [What You Learn and How You're Tested](/posts/climbing/2026-10-17-ifmga-training-and-exams-en). This post covers only the national differences.

## United Kingdom: BMG

### Training steps

[British Mountain Guides (BMG)](https://www.bmg.org.uk/become-a-guide/) runs the scheme in three stages:

1. **Registrant (entry):** a rock entrance exam in North Wales in September, a ski exam in the Alps in January, and a Scottish winter exam in March. Pass all three to be accepted.
2. **Training:** UK summer rock, UK winter, avalanche, ski touring, and summer Alpine courses, each with training and assessment.
3. **Aspirant:** two Alpine summer seasons and one ski season of supervised guiding, then the ski and Alpine assessments and a half-day final seminar.

The whole scheme takes at least three years. The [2022 Training Scheme Handbook](https://www.bmg.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/BMG_Training-Scheme-Handbook_2022-12-02_TB.pdf) also sets a "consolidation period" after rock and after winter, during which you build up summer rock and Scottish winter **working** experience in the UK.

### Entry requirements

Highlights from the [prerequisites page](https://www.bmg.org.uk/become-a-guide/prerequisites/):

| Area | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Rock | 50 multi-pitch routes at E1 5b or above |
| Snow and ice | 60 routes, 50 of them Grade III+ in the UK and more than 40 in Scotland |
| Alps | 4+ years of experience, at least 20 major peaks |
| Ski | 30 days of glacier ski touring plus 15 days off-piste |
| Other | 2 referees (one a qualified guide), medical certificate, mountain first aid |

The two official documents disagree on minimum age: the prerequisites page says 18, the 2022 handbook says 22. Applications close at the end of May and are accepted up to early August at the latest ([guidance notes](https://www.bmg.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/BMG-Guidance-notes-for-applicants-Training-and-assessment-scheme_May-2023-TB_v2.pdf)).

### Cost

**BMG does not publish total fees; contact BMG (guiding@bmg.org.uk).** The published figures are scattered:

| Item | Amount | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Registration fee | £75 | BMG guidance notes |
| Three entrance exams | Charged, amount not stated | Same |
| Summer Rock Assessment (Aug 2026) | £1,950 | [Mountain Training booking page](https://mt.tahdah.me/event/detail/1463314) |
| Ski Touring Assessment (Apr 2026) | £1,085 | [Booking page](https://mt.tahdah.me/event/detail/1523441) |
| Full scheme (third-party estimate) | About £10,000–11,000, travel and accommodation extra | [UKClimbing](https://www.ukclimbing.com/articles/skills/series/british_mountain_guide_scheme/), undated |

Course fees exclude food and lodging.

### Language

English. Not stated explicitly, but every document is in English (inferred).

### Legal status

We found no statutory personal "mountain guide" licence in the UK (inferred). What does exist is the [AALA adventure activities licence](https://hse.gov.uk/aala/general-information.htm): **providers** who sell climbing, trekking and similar activities to under-18s must hold one. It covers businesses, not individuals.

### Foreigners

BMG documents have no nationality or residency clause, and the [2026 application form](https://www.bmg.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/BMG-PDF-Application-Form-2026.pdf) asks for "Country of residency," so non-UK residents can apply.

The sticking point is that the consolidation periods during training require guiding work experience in the UK. The [Youth Mobility Scheme](https://www.gov.uk/youth-mobility) open to Taiwanese passport holders bans work as a professional sportsperson or coach, and the [Immigration Rules](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/immigration-rules/immigration-rules-introduction) count coaches whether paid or not. The rules do not name mountain guides, but BMG certification covers coaching lead climbing, so the risk of it being classed as coaching is high; the Home Office makes the final call.

### Number of guides

The IFMGA directory lists [75 BMG entries](https://ifmga.net/browse?search=&association=25&page=1) (counted 2026-10-17). It only includes guides with uploaded profiles, so it may not equal total membership.

## New Zealand: NZMGA

### Training steps

For the [New Zealand Mountain Guides Association (NZMGA)](https://nzmga.org.nz/become-a-guide/guiding-pathways/ifmga-guide-pathway/), IFMGA status means holding both the Level 2 Ski Guide and Level 2 Climbing Guide. The [2024 programme document](https://nzmga.org.nz/assets/Files/NZMGA-Training-Certification-Programme-Jun2024-v2406.pdf) has three stages:

1. **Entry:** first aid, avalanche, snow and ice training, ski and rock technical tests, guide training. 29 days in total.
2. **Development:** Level 1 ski and climbing assessments (14 days each), 14 days of avalanche, plus 28 days of supervised work.
3. **Assessment:** Level 2 ski and climbing assessments, 14 days each.

There are limits at both ends: at least three years after gaining Assistant Guide status, and no more than five years after the first 14-day assessment. Past five years you can still hold NZ qualifications, but not IFMGA.

NZMGA also lets you take a single discipline (ski, climbing, alpine trekking, hard ice) and switch to the IFMGA pathway later. [2026–27 course dates](https://nzmga.org.nz/become-a-guide/course-dates/) are published; the next climbing assessments run in March–April 2027.

### Entry requirements

From the [application requirements page](https://nzmga.org.nz/become-a-guide/how-to-apply/application-requirements/):

- Age 18 or over.
- New Zealand citizen or resident, or a current work permit (the form also lists Australian citizens).
- Medical certificate and a PHEC first aid certificate of 40+ hours.
- Level 2 needs two references from NZMGA guides who supervised you in the past 12 months, one of them an IFMGA guide.

### Cost

**NZMGA does not publish fees; contact NZMGA (admin@nzmga.org.nz).** The [course fees page](https://nzmga.org.nz/course-information/course-fees/) literally says to contact the Administration Officer. Small published amounts:

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Level 2 assessment deposit | NZ$500 |
| One-off levy on gaining IFMGA | NZ$50 ([certification page](https://nzmga.org.nz/course-information/assessment-and-certification/)) |
| Scholarship for the final IFMGA course | Up to NZ$1,000 ([Hiddleston-Scaife](https://nzmga.org.nz/about/scholarships/hiddleston-scaife-ifmga-scholarship), as of Oct 2020) |

Fees are due eight weeks before a course and are non-refundable.

### Language

English (inferred: all official documents are in English).

### Legal status

New Zealand's [2016 adventure activities regulations](https://legislation.govt.nz/regulation/public/2016/0019/latest/DLM6725629.html) require commercial adventure operators to pass a safety audit and register with [WorkSafe](https://worksafe.govt.nz/topic-and-industry/adventure-activities/documents-and-resources). The target is the operator, not an individual guide licence.

### Foreigners

Only citizens, residents, or work-permit holders are accepted, making NZMGA the only one of the four with an explicit residency rule. Taiwanese can apply for a [New Zealand Working Holiday Visa](https://www.immigration.govt.nz/visas/taiwan-working-holiday-visa/), but it lasts 12 months, while the IFMGA pathway takes at least three years.

### Number of guides

The IFMGA directory lists [22 NZMGA entries](https://ifmga.net/browse?search=&association=3&page=1) (counted 2026-10-17); the directory may be incomplete.

## Norway: NORTIND

### Training steps

The Norwegian guides' association [Norske Tindevegledere (NORTIND)](https://nortind.no/index.php?Itemid=117&id=71&lang=no&option=com_content&view=article) runs training through Norsk Fjellfagskole (NFFS), a state-recognized vocational college:

1. Written application; top-ranked applicants are invited to the entrance exam (about twice as many as there are places).
2. The entrance exam has a ski part in Hemsedal and a climbing part in Romsdal. Applications for the 2027 intake close in mid-March.
3. A three-year full-time programme, usually with at most eight students per intake.
4. After semester 5 you can apply for aspirant status (tindeveglederaspirant); after semester 6, for full guide (IFMGA).

The [NFFS study plan](https://www.nortind.no/images/stories/Nortind/utdanning/Studieplan_tindevegleder.pdf) lists 180 credits and 17 field sessions totalling 117 days, in Norway, Svalbard and the Alps, plus 10 weeks of practice. NORTIND's own page gives a [standard duration of three years, maximum five](https://www.nortind.no/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=70&Itemid=111&lang=no).

Skiing carries a lot of weight: half the entrance exam is skiing, and the ski subject is worth 60 credits (climbing is 75).

### Entry requirements

- Completion of the upper-secondary "bratt friluftsliv" (steep-terrain outdoor) programme, or age 23 that year with prior-learning recognition (realkompetanse).
- Minimum level: Norwegian grade 5+ in alpine boots, sport climbing 6+, WI4 ice, and able to ski any terrain in any snow.
- Six-day entrance exam with seven parts: cross-country skiing, ski technique, outdoor skills, alpine rock, sport climbing, ice climbing, and a reflection interview.
- Telemark and snowboards are not accepted for the ski exam.

### Cost

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Entrance exam | NOK 2,900 per day (2026) |
| Application fee | NOK 1,350 |
| Tuition before government subsidy | About NOK 215,000 |
| Including travel and accommodation | About NOK 300,000 |

The tuition figures come from a [2024 Friflyt article](https://www.friflyt.no/randone/offentlige-penger-til-ski-og-tindeveglederutdanningen) quoting the NFFS principal; the same article says public funding started in 2024/25, so the actual out-of-pocket cost is probably lower (inferred).

### Language

Taught in Norwegian only. There are two versions of the language requirement, so both are listed:

- [NFFS admission regulation § 2-4](https://lovdata.no/dokument/SF/forskrift/2023-08-30-2528): applicants from outside the Nordic countries must prove Norwegian at **B1**; Nordic school certificates are exempt.
- [NORTIND's English page](https://nortind.no/?Itemid=258&catid=52&id=196:guide-training-in-norway&lang=en&option=com_content&view=article): **B2**, or equivalent Swedish or Danish.

The regulation is the binding admission rule, so the legal minimum is B1; B2 may be the school's practical recommendation. To confirm what is actually accepted, write to nortind@nortind.no.

### Legal status

Not required. [NORTIND itself says](https://www.nortind.no/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=140&Itemid=255&lang=no) that in Norway anyone can call themselves a mountain guide and the authorities set no requirements; IFMGA certification is required in the Alpine countries.

### Foreigners

Yes, provided your Norwegian is good enough (see above).

### Number of guides

NORTIND's [list of certified guides](https://www.nortind.no/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=146&Itemid=113&lang=no) has 117 names (counted 2026-10-17).

## Sweden: SBO

### Training steps

The [Swedish Mountain Guide Association (SBO)](https://www.sbo.nu/cm/sidor/utbildning/) starts a new intake about every two years and only runs the entrance exam if there are at least six qualified applicants:

1. Apply by 31 March of the year before the course; written screening results by 15 April.
2. A three-day entrance exam in the Alps in early May (not in Sweden).
3. Two aspirant exams (ski and climbing) in year 2; aspirant status requires passing both plus at least 67 days of training.
4. Practice: 20 days in summer and 20 in winter, one-to-one with a supervisor who has been an IFMGA guide for three years or more.
5. Three final exams in years 3–4; full certification after at least one year as an aspirant.

The whole scheme is 134 days (94 days of group courses plus 40 days of individual practice), taking three to six years ([course plan](https://www.sbo.nu/cm/sidor/utbildning-kursplan/), [examinations](https://www.sbo.nu/cm/sidor/utbildning-examination/)).

### Entry requirements

[Prerequisites](https://www.sbo.nu/cm/sidor/utbildning-forkrav/) (revised June 2026):

| Area | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Age | 22 in the year the course starts |
| Experience | At least 5 years of climbing and skiing |
| Certificates | First aid course, UIAA-certified rock instructor exam |
| Reference | One letter from an IFMGA guide |
| Language | Proficient in English |
| Alpine routes | 50 (including 15 mixed, 15 alpine rock, 5 winter) |
| Other routes | 20 Scandinavian grade-6 trad routes, 5 multi-pitch 6a, 5 A2 aid, 10 ice routes, and more |

### Cost

**SBO does not publish fees; contact SBO (tc@sbo.nu).** None of SBO's training pages, course plan, application form or prerequisites document gives an amount. The only figure online is a 2004 forum guess, which we do not use.

### Language

SBO does not state the language of instruction, but the prerequisites require proficiency in English.

### Legal status

The Swedish Consumer Agency's [KOVFS 2008:5](https://www.sbo.nu/cm/nyheter/svenskt-regelverk-om-fjallsakerhet/) is "general advice," not a guide licensing law. It says that on glaciers and in alpine terrain with a fall risk, operators "should" ensure the leader is a qualified guide, meaning someone with IFMGA qualifications or judged to have equivalent training and experience.

It is enforced through [injunctions with fines](https://www.sbo.nu/cm/nyheter/forbud-med-vite-mot-icke-certifierade-guider/) issued case by case. The Norwegian NFFS study plan describes Sweden as legally requiring IFMGA, which overstates it.

### Foreigners

No nationality restriction is stated. SBO's list of certified guides includes addresses in Finland, Iceland, Denmark and Luxembourg, which suggests it does accept people from other Nordic countries (inferred).

### Number of guides

The [list of certified guides](https://www.sbo.nu/cm/sidor/medlemmar-auktoriserade-guider/) has about 75 names (counted by hand, including a few non-Swedes), and the [candidates page](https://www.sbo.nu/cm/sidor/medlemmar-kandidater/) lists 8. SBO was founded in 1990 and [became a full IFMGA member on 22 November 1997](https://www.sbo.nu/cm/sidor/om-sbo/), with help early on from BMG's training director ([Pure Ski Touring](https://pureskitouring.se/vagen-mot-ifmga-de-forsta-sju-kritiska-aren-for-svenska-bergsguideorganisationen-sbo/)).

## What this means for Taiwanese readers

The UK and New Zealand teach in English and look the most accessible, but both require working legally in-country: New Zealand asks for a work permit just to apply, the UK scheme requires local work experience during training, and the UK youth visa bans coaching work. Norway requires Norwegian; Sweden requires English, holds its entrance exam in the Alps, and does not publish fees. For how to choose among the options, read [The Path for Taiwanese Climbers](/posts/climbing/2026-10-17-ifmga-taiwan-path-en).
