The USA and Canada share a post because their systems look alike, and both differ from the Alps. In France, Switzerland and Italy, guiding clients for pay without a licence is an offence, and the licence comes from the state or a regional government (see [France and Switzerland](/posts/climbing/2026-10-17-ifmga-france-switzerland-en) and [Austria, Germany and Italy](/posts/climbing/2026-10-17-ifmga-austria-germany-italy-en)). The American [AMGA](https://www.amga.com/programs/mountain-guide-programs) and the Canadian [ACMG](https://acmg.ca/the-acmg-training-and-assessment-program-tap/) are associations, and the certificates are theirs.

US IFMGA guide Eddie Schoen writes on [Rebel Alpine](https://rebelalpine.com/media/why-hire-a-fully-certified-mountain-guide) that certification in the US is voluntary and the industry self-regulates; even National Park permits don't consider whether a guide is certified. ACMG's public documents likewise cite no law requiring its certificate, so Canada is probably also an association credential rather than a state licence. What actually constrains guiding work is public-land permits, insurance and the right to work.

The two countries share two more things. Both split training into rock, alpine and ski streams, and you can stop at one. Both teach in English and list no nationality requirement. For a Taiwanese climber these are the lowest language barriers in the IFMGA world; the sticking point is the work permit.

## USA

### Training path

The AMGA has three streams, Rock, Alpine and Ski, each with its own courses and exams, and you can hold a certificate in just one. Passing the Guide-level exam in all three, plus a WFR, CPR and the professional avalanche course Pro 2, makes you an American Mountain Guide, that is, an IFMGA guide.

| Stream | Sequence |
|---|---|
| [Rock](https://www.amga.com/programs/mountain-guide-programs/rock-guide-program) | RGC → ARGC/AE → RGE |
| [Alpine](https://www.amga.com/programs/mountain-guide-programs/alpine-guide-program) (one of the options) | RGC → AGC → IIC → AAGC/AE → AGE |
| [Ski](https://www.amga.com/programs/mountain-guide-programs/ski-guide-program) | SGC → SMSM → ASGC/AE → SGE |

- The middle step is now called the "Assistant Exam"; older material calls it the Aspirant Exam. Someone who has passed all three Assistant Exams is an Aspirant American Mountain Guide.
- A Certified Rock Guide alone is not an IFMGA guide; you need all three streams.
- Adding up scheduled course days gives about 83 across the three streams; the [old AMGA site](http://old.amga.com/ifmga-mountain-guide/) says "86+ days of training".
- The AMGA website shows no overall deadline for finishing, but each stage's résumé requirements must fall "within the past 5 years", so if you take too long earlier climbs drop out of the window.
- The third-party [Mountain School](https://www.mountainschool.com/content/main/amga-and-ifmga-certification) estimates the full process at about 5 years.

### Entry requirements

- Every course requires current AMGA membership, CPR and an approved WFR. The WFR must be at least 70 hours, 45 of them in person; fully online courses are not accepted.
- Rock Guide Course (RGC): at least 5 years of personal climbing, 10 trad leads of 5.10a or harder in the past 5 years, plus required numbers of multi-pitch trad routes.
- Ski Guide Course (SGC): from the 2026–27 season you can enrol directly. You need at least 4 years of personal ski touring and must submit a skiing video.
- For advanced courses and exams, the résumé must be built "since passing" the previous stage, and the exams require a set amount of real guiding; the RGE, for example, requires at least 5 guided routes.

### Cost

My sum of AMGA tuition on the 2026–27 schedule:

| Stream | Tuition (USD) |
|---|---|
| Rock (2027 3:1 sessions) | About 11,700 |
| Alpine (RGC already counted under Rock) | About 17,400 |
| Ski (cheapest US options) | About 11,550 |
| **All three** | **About 40,650** |

- Each course and exam also carries a $75 application fee.
- Membership: the Professional level costs $140 a year, rising once you are certified.
- Outside courses such as WFR, Pro 1 and Pro 2 are extra:
  - [NOLS WFR](https://www.nols.edu/courses/wm/wilderness-first-responder-wfr/): about $745–1,350.
  - [Silverton's Pro 1](https://www.avyschool.org/professional): about $1,799–2,399.
- Food, travel and all the climbing and skiing you do to build your résumé are not included either. Mountain School estimates the overall total at upwards of $50,000.

### Language

English. The AMGA's "[Essential Eligibility Criteria](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1g3PHtitPkyrfiaBPHXsMFtW0PtRFFQrb/view)" say courses are taught in English; fluency is not required, but you must understand instructions, especially on risk management.

### Legal status

- The AMGA joined the IFMGA in 1997.
- The AMGA's public pages cite no state law requiring certification to guide; the certificate comes from the association.
- What does constrain guiding is public land. [Joe Stock](https://www.stockalpine.com/about-the-amga-and-ifmga), an IFMGA and AMGA guide, writes that visas and permits are the two big problems for foreign guides in the US, and that business permits on national parks, national forests and other public land are hard to get. That is his personal view.

### Foreigners

- **Applying**: the current website, the EEC document and the membership page set no nationality, residence or work-permit condition. The [old AMGA FAQ](http://old.amga.com/faqs/) was explicit: "Our courses are open to all qualified students whether they are U.S. citizens or not".
- **Visa for courses**: the State Department's [9 FAM](https://fam.state.gov/fam/09FAM/09FAM040205.html) says attending non-credit professional seminars may fall under B-1. AMGA courses probably fit that category better, but the consular officer decides, so check with the American Institute in Taiwan (AIT) before you enrol.
- **Guiding in the US**: the [State Department](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/tourism-visit/visitor.html) says a B-1/B-2 visitor "is not permitted to accept employment or work in the United States". The rules do not say the real guiding days required for exams must be done in the US, so they can probably be built up elsewhere, but this has not been confirmed with the AMGA.

### Number of guides

The [AMGA 2025 Annual Report](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/674f3a589212e914d517c321/69c2f6ee9bb8a0619d1971d7_109e076586766d6068e53f56007d5a91_2025%20AMGA%20Annual%20Report%20Compressed.pdf) lists 191 AMGA/IFMGA certifications held in 2025.

## Canada

### Training path

The ACMG's Training and Assessment Program (TAP) also has three streams:

1. **Rock**: Guide Training Rock (9 days) → Apprentice Rock Guide exam (7 days) → (optional) full Rock Guide exam.
2. **Alpine**: you must already be an Apprentice Rock Guide. Guide Training Alpine (10 days) plus Guide Training Ice (6 days) → Apprentice Alpine Guide exam → full Alpine Guide exam.
3. **Ski**: ski descent screening → Guide Training Ski 1, 2 and 3 → Apprentice Ski Guide exam → full Ski Guide exam.

[The ACMG's definition](https://acmg.ca/mountain-guide/) is that holding both the Alpine Guide and Ski Guide certificates makes you an IFMGA Mountain Guide. **There is no separate Mountain Guide exam.**

Apprenticeship rules:

- **Length**: 1–3 years of industry experience under certified guides' supervision.
- **Supervised days**: full exams need supervisor letters covering 30 days each for Alpine and Ski (20 for Rock).
- **Deadline**: under the [ACMG timelines policy](https://acmg.ca/student-resources/), you must challenge the full exam within 3 calendar years of passing the apprentice exam.
- **Attempts**: at most 3 per exam.

The ACMG gives no overall length. Probably the fastest is about 3 years: rock training and the Apprentice Rock exam in summer of year 1, with ice and ski training that winter; alpine training plus the alpine and ski apprentice exams in year 2 (alpine training requires Apprentice Rock first, so it cannot fit into year 1); then at least a year of apprenticeship before the full exams.

### Entry requirements

- [Guide Training Rock](https://acmg.ca/rock-guide-certificate/): aged 19 or over; 3 years of climbing; 20 sport routes at 5.10c to 5.11a flashed in the past year; required numbers of trad and multi-pitch routes; a WFR.
- [Guide Training Alpine](https://acmg.ca/alpine-guide-certificate/): aged 19 or over; alpine routes at grade D or harder; already an Apprentice Rock Guide; avalanche rescue AvSAR Level 1; a WFR.
- [Guide Training Ski](https://acmg.ca/ski-guide-certificate/): 3 seasons of backcountry skiing; at least 75 days leading or co-leading in complex terrain; the descent screening certificate; AvSAR 1; a WFR.

### Cost

Counting only the required steps to IFMGA on the 2026–27 schedule, ACMG tuition totals **CAD 46,600–48,300**. That figure rests on several conditions:

- The Rock stream stops at Apprentice and excludes the full Rock Guide exam (CAD 3,525).
- It excludes the three optional exam-prep courses.
- The range comes from different Ski stream locations.
- It excludes WFR, AvSAR 1, [CAA Operations Level 2](https://avalancheassociation.ca/itp/avalanche-operations-level-2), membership, travel and living costs. CAA Ops 2 is the avalanche course both full Alpine and Ski exams require; the course alone costs CAD 2,674, with the assessment on top.

### Language

English; the whole programme is taught in English.

### Legal status

- ACMG certificates are association certifications. The ACMG timelines policy states that "There is no requirement to complete the Mountain Guide certification", so you can stop at a single-stream certificate.
- According to the [ACMG page for visiting IFMGA guides](https://acmg.ca/ifmga-mobility/), guiding on public land in Canada requires permits or business licences and commercial general liability (CGL) insurance. To join the ACMG Provincial Parks Permit Program, the policy must cover at least US$/€/£2,000,000 per occurrence.

### Foreigners

- **Applying**: the ACMG's TAP page, student resources and timelines policy contain no citizenship or residency requirement.
- **The apprenticeship needs a work permit**: Canada's immigration department, IRCC, [defines work](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/corporate/publications-manuals/operational-bulletins-manuals/temporary-residents/foreign-workers/what-is-work.html) to include unpaid internships. An ACMG apprenticeship means supervised guiding of real clients, so you need a work permit whether or not you are paid.
- **IEC is not enough**: Taiwanese citizens may take part in [IEC](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/work-canada/iec/eligibility.html) only once, for up to 12 months, far shorter than the ACMG path. IRCC says you can apply for a non-IEC work permit or a study permit before your IEC permit expires and stay in status.
- **The college route**: at [Thompson Rivers University](https://tru.ca/truworld/future-students/programs.html), neither the Adventure Guide diploma nor the certificate leads to a post-graduation work permit (PGWP); the 4-year Bachelor of Tourism Management (Adventure Studies) does.
- **CAA Ops 2 also requires work in Canada**: you need 100 operational days before you can enrol.
- **A Taiwanese example**: climber GW Chen is training in the ACMG system. A [2026 Hanchor profile](https://www.hanchor.com/news/台灣攀岩人物誌二十五-冠瑋wei-gw-chen) (in Chinese) says he holds Apprentice Rock Guide and plans to return to Canada for the full Rock Guide exam. He is not yet an IFMGA guide.

### Number of guides

The membership figures on the [ACMG home page](https://acmg.ca/) show 1,437 active members, 184 of them IFMGA guides.

## What this means for Taiwanese readers

The USA and Canada have the lowest language barriers, and the courses themselves do not shut out foreigners. What stops people is the guiding experience required during apprenticeship and before exams, which needs a work permit if done there. Plan how your visas will connect before you plan your course schedule. For how to choose overall, see [The path for Taiwanese climbers](/posts/climbing/2026-10-17-ifmga-taiwan-path-en).
