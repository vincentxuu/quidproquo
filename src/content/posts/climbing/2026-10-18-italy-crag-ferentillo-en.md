Ferentillo is a small town in the Valnerina valley in Terni province, southern Umbria. [Umbriatourism's town page](https://www.umbriatourism.it/en/w/ferentillo-en) says the Nera river splits it into two districts, Precetto and Matterella, and the crags sit right beside the town. Umbria's official tourism site, [Umbriatourism](https://www.umbriatourism.it/en/w/rock-climbing-in-ferentillo), calls it "one of the most popular Italian destinations for rock climbing".

This post covers what to decide before going: whether it suits you, which months, how to get there without a car, where to park for each area, and the local rules.

## What kind of crag is it

**The town paid for the bolts.** [Planetmountain](https://www.planetmountain.com/en/news/climbing/petzl-legend-tour-italia-climbing-at-ferentillo.html) writes that at the end of the 1980s, Luciano Santi and mountain guide Luigi Mario made Ferentillo the hub for local climbers, and that "Ferentillo in unique to Italian sport climbing as it was here that, for the first time ever, a local town council decided to bear the costs of bolting crags". [theTopo](https://thetopo.com/crags/ferentillo/description) still describes the routes as "all well-maintained by the local municipality".

**Rock and style.** It is all limestone. theTopo describes the climbing as "from slabs to steep overhangs with tufas", and the [Versante Sud](https://www.versantesud.it/it/negozio/catalogue/umbria-rock_1500/) Umbria guidebook singles out "gli strapiombi di Ferentillo" — the Ferentillo overhangs. [Alpinisti d'Appennino](https://www.alpinistidappennino.it/2024/12/06/falesia-balcone/) describes Balcone as running from overhangs on tufas (strapiombi a canne) to vertical or slightly steep walls on edges, routes that "richiedono una buona dose di resistenza" — need plenty of endurance.

**Three areas.** theTopo splits the crag into three:

| Area | Sectors | Aspect | Walk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ferentillo classica | L'Isola, Le Mummie, Il Balcone, Lu Strittu | S | 1–5 min |
| Gabbio | Gabbio, Curva di Gabbio | SW | 4–12 min |
| San Lorenzo | First sector, second sector, Masso, Torre Bianca | N, NE, E | 5–7 min |

theTopo's sector notes:

- **L'Isola**: no walk from the car park; nearly 50 routes, 4c–7c, plus a hard 8b+ freed by Mauro Calibani.
- **Le Mummie**: "an historic wall with almost 100 routes", mostly easy, with some multi-pitch.
- **Il Balcone**: an overhanging wall of reddish rock with lots of tufas; nearly 100 lines, 5c–8b+.
- **Lu Strittu**: a small sector, 15 lines, 4c–6c.
- **Gabbio**: a big cave behind the medieval hamlet of Gabbio, over 40 routes from 6a to around 8c, including some of the hardest at Ferentillo; Curva di Gabbio has 10 lines, 6b+–8b+.
- **San Lorenzo**: the first sector has 18 lines mostly in grades 4–6, with rock theTopo calls "not the best"; the second sector has 19 routes, 5c–7b+.

**Size.** Counts by site (checked 2026-10-18):

| Source | Count | Grades |
|---|---|---|
| [Falesia.it](https://www.falesia.it/crag/39/ferentillo.html) | 415 routes | 3b–9a |
| [Visit Ferentillo](https://visitferentillo.it/blog/portfolio/climbing/) | "oltre 400" (over 400) | — |
| [theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/ferentillo) | 397 routes, all sport | — |
| [27crags](https://27crags.com/crags/ferentillo) | 353 sport routes | — |

The top grade differs too: Falesia.it lists Il Gabbio up to 9a, while theTopo says Gabbio goes to "around 8c".

**Route length.** Visit Ferentillo and Umbriatourism both say routes run from 15 m to 120 m. Alpinisti d'Appennino recommends 16 quickdraws and a 70 m rope for Balcone.

**Who it suits.** Visit Ferentillo says Le Mummie "presenta le vie più semplici e adatte ai principianti" — has the easiest routes, suited to beginners — and that Balcone and Gabbio are the hardest sectors. So on one trip a beginner partner can climb at L'Isola and Le Mummie while anyone pushing grade 7–8 overhangs goes up to Balcone or Gabbio. Popular routes are slick: theTopo says many routes on Le Mummie are polished, and Alpinisti d'Appennino says some Balcone routes are "unti" (greasy).

## When to go

| Source | Season |
|---|---|
| theTopo | classica and Gabbio face south, "ideal for winter climbing"; San Lorenzo faces east with tree cover, "suitable for mid-season climbing" |
| Alpinisti d'Appennino (Balcone) | "da novembre a marzo" (November to March) |
| Versante Sud, *Umbria Rock* | Umbria's crags are "prevalentemente invernali, ad ogni modo è possibile arrampicare in tutte le stagioni" (mostly winter crags, but climbable in every season) |

**South-facing walls in winter, add San Lorenzo in spring and autumn.** theTopo says L'Isola, Le Mummie, Balcone and Lu Strittu get sun "All day long"; Gabbio gets it from mid-morning. In summer the only shade is east-facing, tree-covered San Lorenzo.

**Rain.** Alpinisti d'Appennino's Balcone page answers "Si scala con la pioggia" (can you climb in the rain) with "No".

## Getting there

| | Public transport | Driving |
|---|---|---|
| Start | Terni bus terminal (Bus Terminal Terni) | From Terni towards the Valnerina |
| Middle | Busitalia E621, via the Marmore Falls (Cascata delle Marmore) | SP209 into Ferentillo |
| Last leg | Get off at Ferentillo P.Garibaldi or Precetto | Follow the Precetto signs to each car park |

### Public transport

In the regional bus company Busitalia's [Terni-area extra-urban timetable](https://www.fsbusitalia.it/content/dam/fsbusitalia/documenti/umbria/orari/invernali_2026-2027/03_Extra_Terni_Ann_Bacino3.PDF) (winter 2026–2027), line E621 runs "Terni-S.Liberatore-Bv.Varcone-Arrone-Ferentillo-Scheggino". It leaves Bus Terminal Terni, passes Cascata delle Marmore, and stops at Precetto and Ferentillo P.Garibaldi.

The first morning bus leaves the terminal at 06:10 and reaches Ferentillo at 06:40, so the ride is about half an hour. The next one, 07:05 (arriving 07:33), runs on school days only; on non-school days the next bus is the 07:58, arriving 08:35.

Many runs carry day restrictions: in the timetable's key, FLS means Monday to Saturday, FE public holidays, SLS school days only, and NSLS non-school days only. Sundays and holidays have far fewer runs, so check the last bus back before you set out. The timetable index is on [Busitalia's extra-urban timetables page](https://www.fsbusitalia.it/it/umbria/orari-linee-umbria/orari-servizi-extraurbani-umbria.html).

### Driving

[27crags](https://27crags.com/crags/ferentillo/access) says you reach Ferentillo on the SP209 either from Terni to the west or from Spoleto to the north. Alpinisti d'Appennino's route from Rome: A1 to the Orte exit, SS675 to Terni Est, then SS79bis and the turn for the Valnerina, joining the SP209 to Ferentillo.

### Parking

27crags gives parking for all three areas:

- **Ferentillo classica**: leave the SP209 following signs for "Ferentillo (Precetto) / Monterivoso / Castellonalto" and drive 400 m to the L'Isola car park; the sector is right behind it. 300 m further on, on the left just before the narrow passage called "Lu strittu", is the car park for Balcone and Lu Strittu, with a steep path up from there.
- **Gabbio**: from the church in the town centre take Via Campania north, turn left after 260 m, and follow the narrow road 1.2 km to the hamlet of Gabbio; park on the right a few tens of metres past it.
- **San Lorenzo**: from the town centre head west, turn right onto Via Fosso Ancaiano just after the bridge, and park on the roadside after 350 m.

## Rules to know before you go

**San Lorenzo is on private land.** theTopo says this area is "on private land" and asks climbers to "respect the trails and crag base". Stick to the existing paths and don't spread out at the base.

**Gabbio's narrow road and fountain.** 27crags warns that the road to Gabbio is narrow ("be careful with large vehicles") and asks: "Please do not obstruct the passages and do not park by the fountain since it is located on private ground".

**Rockfall nets affect some routes.** theTopo says at L'Isola "Recently installed rockfall nets have made some multi-pitches unclimbable", and the first bolts on some routes may be high. Falesia.it says the works are finished, all sectors are accessible and the car park is roomy again, but some second pitches and multi-pitch routes on L'Isola and Le Mummie may have been made unclimbable by the nets; its photo gallery includes one captioned "Cantiere Gennaio 2023" (worksite, January 2023).

**Permits and fees.** None of the sources mention an entry fee or climber registration; 27crags marks it "No special access issues".

## Routes by level

YDS and V-grade conversions follow the [Mountain Project international grade comparison chart](https://www.mountainproject.com/international-climbing-grades) and are approximate; "approx." marks grades the sources give without a/b/c (such as 4 or 5+). The original grade is the one that counts.

I found no grade breakdown for the whole area; theTopo says Le Mummie's nearly 100 routes are "mostly easy", and when [verticalRock](http://verticalrock.blogspot.com/2013/11/falesia-ferentillo-gabbio.html) counted Gabbio's 33 routes in 2013, 10 were 8a or harder and none was below 6a. Repeat counts below come from Climbook's [Le Mummie](https://climbook.com/index.php/falesie/2/ferentillo-le-mummie) (updated March 2026), [Gabbio](https://climbook.com/falesie/10/ferentillo-gabbio) and [San Lorenzo](https://climbook.com/index.php/falesie/8781/ferentillo-san-lorenzo-secondo-settore-masso-primo-settore-torre-bianca/vie) pages; Balcone routes come from Alpinisti d'Appennino's route list.

### Beginner: up to 6a (YDS 5.10a and below)

Visit Ferentillo says Le Mummie "presenta le vie più semplici e adatte ai principianti" (has the easiest routes, suited to beginners). theTopo says L'Isola has a flat base right by the car park and "suits beginners"; San Lorenzo's first sector is mostly grade 4 to 6, though the rock is not the best. [Pro Loco Ferentillo](https://www.prolocoferentillo.it/ferentillo-verticale/) calls Lu Strittu "Ottima come falesia per arrampicatori di livello 5C/6A" (an excellent crag for 5c/6a climbers).

| Route | Grade | YDS | Length / pitches | Sector | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E' meio armedia | 5a | 5.7 | Single pitch, 20 m | San Lorenzo first sector | Climbook: 97 repeats |
| Fiocchi d'avena | 5b | 5.8 | Single pitch | Le Mummie | Climbook: 145 repeats |
| Mummie sprint | 5c | 5.9 | Single pitch | Le Mummie | Climbook: 100 repeats |
| Viaggio di nozze | 5c | 5.9 | Single pitch | Il Balcone | Alpinisti d'Appennino route list |
| Uràgano | 6a, 5c, 5b | hardest 5.10a | 3 pitches | Le Mummie | First pitch is the hardest; Climbook: 48 repeats of pitch 1 |
| Ciato spacciato | 6a | 5.10a | Single pitch | Le Mummie | The sector's most-repeated route on Climbook (182) |

theTopo says many Le Mummie routes are polished, but the rock on the multi-pitches is still good. Falesia.it warns that the rockfall nets may have made some second pitches and multi-pitches on L'Isola and Le Mummie unclimbable, so for multi-pitches like Uràgano check for netting above before you start.

### Intermediate: 6a+ to 6c+ (YDS 5.10b–5.11c)

theTopo describes San Lorenzo's second sector as a vertical wall covered in small tufas, 5c to 7b+. Gabbio is known for hard lines, but its most-repeated routes on Climbook are 6a to 7a.

## Where to find more information

- **Guidebooks:** route grades and route counts in this article come mainly from online databases such as theCrag and Mountain Project; where they disagree, go with the guidebook.
  - [*UMBRIA Rock*](https://www.versantesud.it/it/negozio/catalogue/umbria-rock_1500/) (Giuseppe Picone and Monica Delicati, Versante Sud, first edition 2023, Italian, ISBN 978-88-5547-064-3): sport climbing across Umbria, including Ferentillo's Isola, Mummie, Balcone, Gabbio and Attico sectors; Climbook's Ferentillo route data cites this edition.
  - [*Arrampica Roma Nord*](https://ideamontagna.it/libro/arrampica-roma-nord) (Riccardo Innocenti, Idea Montagna, 2016, Italian and English, ISBN 978-88-97299-74-5): 71 crags north of Rome, with Ferentillo in chapters 16–20; older than Umbria Rock.
  - Umbriatourism says local sporting goods stores also sell a guidebook, without naming it.
- **[27crags / theTopo](https://thetopo.com/crags/ferentillo/description)**: the three areas, aspect and walk-in time per sector, parking and approaches; topos are paid.
- **[Falesia.it](https://www.falesia.it/crag/39/ferentillo.html)**: route counts and grades for 15 sectors, and the state of things after the rockfall-net works.
- **[Alpinisti d'Appennino: Balcone](https://www.alpinistidappennino.it/2024/12/06/falesia-balcone/)**: the full Balcone route list, grades and gear advice.
- **[Visit Ferentillo](https://visitferentillo.it/blog/portfolio/climbing/)**: the town's tourism site, which lists climbing courses run by mountain guide Alvise Mario.
