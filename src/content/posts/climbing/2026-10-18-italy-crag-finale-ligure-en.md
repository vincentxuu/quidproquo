Finale Ligure is on the coast of Liguria in northwest Italy, between Genova and the French border. Behind the town, the edges of a series of limestone plateaus are lined with cliffs, from the sea all the way into the inland valleys. The local promotion body, [Finale Outdoor Region](https://finaleoutdoor.com/en/stories/climbing-in-the-finale-area-all-you-need-to-know), puts it this way: "Finale Ligure, with its almost 4000 routes, is one of the main climbing spots not only in Italy but in Europe".

This post covers the decisions to make before going: whether it suits you, which months, how to get there, where to park, and which walls are currently closed.

## What kind of crag is it

**Rock and style.** [theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/finale-ligure) labels it "Mostly Sport climbing" on "Limestone" and says "From Vado to Finale Ligure there is an enormous amount of crags and routes for any difficulty - mainly sport". Finale Outdoor Region adds that there are routes for beginners through experts, single-pitch and multi-pitch, and long routes too, such as [Bric Pianarella](https://finaleoutdoor.com/en/trails-walls/valle-aquila-bric-pianarella-paretone), the "big wall" of the Finale area.

**Size.** The figures each source gives (checked 2026-10-18):

| Source | Count | Original text |
|---|---|---|
| [Finale Outdoor Region (2025)](https://finaleoutdoor.com/en/stories/finale-outdoor-region-a-paradise-for-climbers-and-for-birds-of-prey) | 200+ sectors, 4,000+ routes | "more than 200 climbing sectors and over 4,000 routes" |
| [Finale Outdoor Region (2022)](https://finaleoutdoor.com/en/stories/climbing-in-the-finale-area-all-you-need-to-know) | nearly 4,000 routes | "almost 4000 routes" |
| [italia.it (Italian national tourist board)](https://italia.it/en/liguria/things-to-do/free-climbing-finale-ligure) | 3,500 routes, about 200 walls | "3,500 climbing routes and about 200 specially-equipped walls" |
| [theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/finale-ligure) | 4,353 routes | "Mostly Sport climbing 4,353 routes in crag" |

theCrag splits the area into eleven sub-areas. The biggest are Valle Aquila (667 routes), Rocca di Perti (525), Rocca Carpanea / Monte Sordo (517), Boragni / Valle di Nava (371) and Monte Cucco (315).

**History.** italia.it says "The first routes were established in 1968 by the brothers Gianluigi and Eugenio Vaccari". [Planetmountain](https://planetmountain.com/en/crags/dancing-dalle-finale.html) describes the sea-cliff route Dancing Dalle (7a) as "the historic climb first freed by Patrick Berhault and the first 7a sports climb at Finale".

**The classics are slick.** theCrag says it plainly: "Popular routes are more or less polished". Finale Outdoor Region's sector pages say the same of many crags, for example Rocca di Perti's [Placca dell'Oasi](https://finaleoutdoor.com/en/trails-walls/rocca-di-perti-placca-dell-oasi-1) ("is now rather polished") and Monte Cucco's [Anfiteatro](https://finaleoutdoor.com/en/trails-walls/monte-cucco-anfiteatro) ("unfortunately the rock is now polished").

**Who it suits.** Sport climbers who can spend weeks here with one rope and a set of quickdraws. The route count is huge and the grade range wide, and you can switch from a sea cliff to a forest crag inland in the same day. Multi-pitch climbers have routes of two to three hundred metres on Bric Pianarella. For the sea cliffs at the other end of Liguria, near La Spezia, see [Muzzerone](/posts/climbing/2026-10-18-italy-crag-muzzerone-en).

## When to go

| Source | Season |
|---|---|
| [Finale Outdoor Region](https://finaleoutdoor.com/en/stories/climbing-in-the-finale-area-all-you-need-to-know) | Winter is possible on the right sectors ("the grip is excellent"), summer on shady crags, but "the best seasons are still spring and autumn" |
| [theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/finale-ligure) | "Possible to climb year-round, just choose the suitable aspect"; high season (when parking fills up) is "February-March to October" |
| [Ecopoint Climbing](https://ecopointclimbing.com/ecopoint/climbing-trip-to-finale-ligure-by-train) | June was fine because some sectors are in the forest; spring or late summer may be better for temperature |

**In summer, look for shade.** Finale Outdoor Region's sector pages give aspect and season. Monte Cucco's [Canyon](https://finaleoutdoor.com/en/trails-walls/monte-cucco-canyon) (5b–8a) is "one of the few that can be visited in the summer. It remains shady and breezy"; Rocca di Perti's [Grotto di Perti](https://finaleoutdoor.com/en/trails-walls/rocca-di-perti-grotto-di-perti) (6c–8c+, north-facing) is "particularly suitable for the hottest months".

**Rain.** Every sector page has a "Possible when raining" field. Grotto di Perti says yes: "due to the angle of the wall, you can climb here on raining days".

**Nesting season.** Some crags are closed from 1 January to 31 July (see the rules below), so spring trips need to plan around them.

## Getting there

| | Public transport | Driving |
|---|---|---|
| To Finale | Train to Finale Ligure Marina (Genoa–Ventimiglia line) | A10 motorway, Finale Ligure or Feglino exit |
| To the crags | TPL Linea bus to Finalborgo or toward Feglino, then walk | Mountain roads, parking banned along most of them |
| Time | Depends on the sector | theCrag: crags up to about 45 minutes' drive from the sea; approaches from 5 minutes to 30–60 minutes |

### Public transport

The town's official tourism site, [Visit Finale Ligure](https://visitfinaleligure.it/en/how-to-get-there), says the station is on the "Genoa-Ventimiglia" line and "All trains have a stop at Finale Ligure station". For buses it lists [TPL Linea](https://welcome.tpllinea.it/en) "Line 40 Andora-Savona" and "line 40/ Finalborgo-Savona".

For the inland crags, [Ecopoint Climbing](https://ecopointclimbing.com/ecopoint/climbing-trip-to-finale-ligure-by-train) took the bus toward Feglino from outside Finale Ligure Marina station, through Finalborgo to Feglino, then walked about 2 km and 150 m up to the [BaseCamp Cucco](https://www.basecampcucco.com/en/camping-finale-ligure/) campsite below Monte Cucco. Twelve sectors with nearly 300 routes are within walking distance of the campsite, and bus tickets can be bought on board. For other crags, check TPL Linea's current timetables.

For airports, Visit Finale Ligure gives Genoa Cristoforo Colombo as the nearest at about 50 km, with bus or train connections; Nice is about 140 km away.

### Driving

Visit Finale Ligure: take the A10 and exit at Finale Ligure. For Bric Pianarella, Finale Outdoor Region's sector page uses the Feglino exit: turn right toward Finale Ligure, drive about 2 km and park opposite an abandoned chapel ([Bric Pianarella Paretone](https://finaleoutdoor.com/en/trails-walls/valle-aquila-bric-pianarella-paretone)).

### Parking

This is where most trouble in Finale starts. [Finale Outdoor Region](https://finaleoutdoor.com/en/stories/climbing-in-the-finale-area-all-you-need-to-know) says the roads to Perti, Monte Sordo, Rocca di Corno, Boragni and Bric Reseghe are drivable, but "parking is banned almost everywhere on these roads, as they are very narrow and the transit for emergency vehicles must always be guaranteed":

- The last 3–5 km of the Perti–Pian Marino road have length and weight limits that keep campers and vans out.
- On the road to Monte Sordo, parking is banned from Piazza Martiri Perticesi onward, and also along the Calvisio Vecchia road (Via Bedina and nearby).
- Each sector page names a designated parking spot; check it before you go.

theCrag also notes that approach times depend on "whether you find a parking spot on high season".

## Rules to know before you go

**Raptor-nesting bans.** According to Finale Outdoor Region, the bans date from 1994: after an agreement between climbers, WWF and LIPU, the Province of Savona marked out areas where climbing is prohibited to protect nesting Peregrine Falcons and Eagle Owls. There are two kinds: year-round bans and a seasonal ban from 1 January to 31 July. The list below follows the [January 2025 article](https://finaleoutdoor.com/en/stories/finale-outdoor-region-a-paradise-for-climbers-and-for-birds-of-prey); the [2022 article](https://finaleoutdoor.com/en/stories/climbing-in-the-finale-area-all-you-need-to-know) names the same seasonal crags, but its year-round list has Rocce del Borgo instead of Caprazzoppa.

| Crag | Ban | Extent (2025 article) |
|---|---|---|
| Caprazzoppa | Year-round | From the TV repeater to the sea cliff below Torre Colombara, east-south side |
| Pianarella | Year-round | After "Via Lunga" until San Bernardino; Via Lunga itself stays open |
| Casa del Vacchè | Year-round | Beyond "Peter Pan" to the Cornei bridge |
| Rocca di Corno | Year-round | Beyond "Il Futuro è un'Ipotesi" to near the Cornei bridge |
| Capo Noli | Year-round | The entire cliff above Via Aurelia |
| Monte Tolla | 1 Jan–31 Jul | |
| Bric Reseghe | 1 Jan–31 Jul | |
| Rocca degli Uccelli | 1 Jan–31 Jul | |
| Bastionata di Boragni | 1 Jan–31 Jul | Right and central sectors |

The 2025 article ends: "For the complete list of routes in the Finale Outdoor Region, updates on bans, and the status of the crags, visit the LIVE INFO section". Before you go, go by each crag's current status on [LIVE INFO](https://www.finaleoutdoor.com/en/live/climbing).

**No wild camping or sleeping in vehicles.** Finale Outdoor Region: "on the whole territory of the Municipality of Finale Ligure, outside the authorised areas, camping and overnight stays in vehicles is not permitted". theCrag: "Camping is allowed only on commercial camping sites".

**Maintenance funding.** Finale Outdoor Region runs the [FOR YOU CARD](https://www.finaleoutdoor.com/en/experience/trails-and-crags-in-top-notch-condition-buy-the-for-you-card): show it when paying at partner businesses and they donate a small share toward trail and crag maintenance. It is voluntary, not an access fee; none of the sources mention climbing registration or fees.

## Routes by level

YDS and V-grade conversions follow the [Mountain Project international grade comparison chart](https://www.mountainproject.com/international-climbing-grades) and are approximate; "approx." marks grades the sources give without a/b/c (such as 4 or 5+). The original grade is the one that counts.

The tables combine [theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/finale-ligure)'s three-star classics by grade, [Mountain Project's Classic Climbs](https://www.mountainproject.com/area/classics/107467705/finale-ligure) and Finale Outdoor Region's sector pages. "Not listed" in the sector column means theCrag's classics table gives no sector.

### Beginner: up to 6a (YDS 5.10a and below)

Most of the routes up to 6a on Mountain Project's classics list are on Monte Cucco, in Settore della Torre and Anfiteatro.

| Route | Grade | YDS | Length / pitches | Sector | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Miguel | 4c | 5.6 | 2 pitches (multi-pitch) | Monte Cucco (Settore della Torre) | Mountain Project classics |
| Cobrecesi | 5a | 5.7 | Not listed | Not listed | Three stars on theCrag |
| Rischia o Raschia | 5b | 5.8 | Single pitch | Monte Cucco (Anfiteatro) | Mountain Project classics |
| Smanie del ghiro | 5c+ | approx. 5.9+ | 2 pitches (5c 10 m, 6a+ 15 m) | Monte Sordo (Alveare) | Three stars on theCrag; pitches from [Finale Outdoor Region's Alveare page](https://www.outdooractive.com/en/poi/finale-outdoor-region/rocca-carpanea-alveare/51544822/) |
| La Torre | 6a | 5.10a | 2 pitches (multi-pitch) | Monte Cucco (Settore della Torre) | Mountain Project classics |
| Via Lunga | 6a (hardest pitch) | 5.10a | 320 m, 13 pitches (multi-pitch) | Bric Pianarella | See below |

**Via Lunga.** [Finale Outdoor Region's sector page](https://finaleoutdoor.com/en/trails-walls/valle-aquila-bric-pianarella-paretone) lists it at 320 m and 13 pitches, the hardest a 6a overhang on pitch 12, the rest mostly 3c–5c. Multi-pitches on Bric Pianarella Paretone are "on average 250 metres long and the bolting is overall good". Note that the wall from this route to San Bernardino is under a year-round ban.

### Intermediate: 6a+ to 6c+ (YDS 5.10b–5.11c)

On Mountain Project's classics list, the 6b–6c+ routes cluster in Grotta dell'Edera on Rocca Carpanea and in Monte Cucco's Anfiteatro.

| Route | Grade | YDS | Length / pitches | Sector | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mittelnaht | 6a+ | 5.10b | Single pitch | Tre Frati | Three stars on theCrag; also a Mountain Project classic |
| Bombolo | 6b | 5.10c | Single pitch | Rocca Carpanea (Grotta dell'Edera) | Three stars on theCrag; most-rated Mountain Project classic (21 ratings) |
| Stravolgimento progressivo | 6b+ | 5.10d | Single pitch | Monte Cucco (Anfiteatro) | Three stars on theCrag |
| Golden Lady | 6c | 5.11a/b | Not listed | Not listed | Three stars on theCrag |
| Coconut | 6c | 5.11a/b | Single pitch | Monte Cucco (Anfiteatro) | Mountain Project classics |
| Bagonghi | 6c+ | 5.11c | Not listed | Not listed | Three stars on theCrag |

### Advanced: 7a to 7c+ (YDS 5.11d–5.13a)

Monte Cucco Anfiteatro, known locally as Fenia, has about 60 routes, some multi-pitch. Finale Outdoor Region singles out "Waterfall, Turbo Diesel and Baci da Rio" as "well known and popular routes, 30 meters of endurance and technique"; the bolting is more spaced than on newer crags ([Monte Cucco - Anfiteatro](https://finaleoutdoor.com/en/trails-walls/monte-cucco-anfiteatro)).

| Route | Grade | YDS | Length / pitches | Sector | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dancing Dalle | 7a | 5.11d | Single pitch | Dancing Dalle sea cliff | See below |
| Waterfall | 7a | 5.11d | 35 m | Monte Cucco (Anfiteatro) | Named by Finale Outdoor Region |
| Vedo Nudo | 7a+ | 5.12a | Not listed | Not listed | Three stars on theCrag |
| Lubna | 7b | 5.12b | Single pitch | Rocca Carpanea (Grotta dell'Edera) | Three stars on theCrag; also a Mountain Project classic |
| Almalù L1 + L2 | 7c | 5.12d | 2 pitches | Not listed | Three stars on theCrag |
| Elfo scuro | 7c+ | 5.13a | Not listed | Not listed | Three stars on theCrag |

**Dancing Dalle.** On a sea cliff past Varigotti, just after the Malpasso tunnel. [Planetmountain](https://planetmountain.com/en/crags/dancing-dalle-finale.html)'s route list reads "Alessandro Grillo - 1982. RP Patrick Berhault"; the same cliff has 10 routes from 5c to 8a. Mountain Project files it under Capo Noli, and Finale Outdoor Region's year-round ban list includes the Scogliera di Capo Noli, so check LIVE INFO before you go.

### Expert: 8a and up (YDS 5.13b and up)

Finale Outdoor Region describes Alveare on Monte Sordo as "featuring some of the hardest routes of the whole Finale area": 5c–8c+, almost all overhanging, west-facing, and not climbable in the rain. Grotto di Perti on Rocca di Perti is 6c–8c+, north-facing, and climbable when it rains.

| Route | Grade | YDS | Length / pitches | Sector | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mosaiko | 8a | 5.13b | Not listed | Not listed | Three stars on theCrag |
| Bombay | 8a+ | 5.13c | 25 m | Monte Sordo (Alveare) | Finale Outdoor Region route list |
| Hyaena | 8b | 5.13d | 25 m | Monte Sordo (Alveare) | First ascent by Andrea Gallo, December 1986 ([Planetmountain](https://www.planetmountain.com/en/news/climbing/wafaa-amer-claims-hyaena-historic-andrea-gallo-testpiece-at-finale-italy.html)) |
| Dingo | 8c+ | 5.14c | 20 m | Monte Sordo (Alveare) | Finale Outdoor Region route list |

## Where to look things up

- **[Finale Outdoor Region LIVE INFO](https://www.finaleoutdoor.com/en/live/climbing)**: open/closed status and maintenance notices for each crag. Each sector also has its own page (for example [Grotto di Perti](https://finaleoutdoor.com/en/trails-walls/rocca-di-perti-grotto-di-perti)) with grades, aspect, rain suitability, phone signal, parking and approach, and some route lists.
- **Guidebooks:** route grades and route counts in this article come mainly from online databases such as theCrag and Mountain Project; where they disagree, go with the guidebook.
  - [*Finale Climbing*](https://versantesud.it/en/shop/catalogue/finale-climbing_1448) (Marco Tomassini, Versante Sud, 3rd edition 2022, separate Italian, German and English editions, 792 pages): the complete sport and multi-pitch guide, also in the iClimbing app. [UKC](https://www.ukclimbing.com/logbook/books/finale_climbing_2022-2710) says the third edition adds 21 crags, with aerial photos, QR codes for parking GPS and GPS tracks for every approach.
  - [*Finale 51*](https://www.sestogrado.it/it/prodotto/finale-51-rock-climbing-a-finale-ligure/) (Andrea Gallo, Idee Verticali, 9th edition 2019, Italian, English and German, two-volume box set, 752 pages): nearly 200 sectors and about 4,000 routes, with a Vertical-Life digital version. Sestogrado and Hoepli both list it as sold out or unavailable from the publisher.
  - Finale Outdoor Region names both books and says they are sold at the Infopoint Outdoor (Finale Outdoor Base) and in outdoor shops and bookshops in Finalborgo and Finale. Rockfax has no Finale guide.
- **[theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/finale-ligure)**: sub-area route counts, classics, accommodation notes.
- **Map**: Finale Outdoor Region recommends Fraternali Editori's "Map 20. Finalese" at 1:25,000; approaches through the woods are easy to lose.
