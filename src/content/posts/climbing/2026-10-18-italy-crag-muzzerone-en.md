> 🌏 [中文版](/posts/climbing/2026-10-18-italy-crag-muzzerone)

Muzzerone is a headland at the eastern end of Liguria, above [Porto Venere](https://www.visitportovenere.com/it/cosa-fare-a-portovenere/arrampicata), with a long line of white and grey limestone walls on its west side that fall straight into the sea. It lies inside the [Porto Venere Regional Natural Park (Parco Naturale Regionale di Porto Venere)](https://parconaturaleportovenere.it/palestra-di-roccia/), whose climbing page opens with: "Il Muzzerone e le sue falesie sono diventati uno dei luoghi più amati e frequentati da migliaia di arrampicatori provenienti da tutta Europa" — Muzzerone and its cliffs have become one of the best-loved and busiest spots for thousands of climbers from all over Europe.

This post covers what to decide before going: whether it suits you, which months, how to get up there, which sectors you can actually climb right now, and what to watch for when parking. For a bigger sport-climbing area in the same region, see [Finale Ligure](/posts/climbing/2026-10-18-italy-crag-finale-ligure-en).

## What kind of crag is it

**Rock.** The local climbing school, [Scuola di Arrampicata Muzzerone](https://www.scuoladiarrampicatamuzzerone.it/?p=12), calls it "un solido e ottimo calcare, in prevalenza bianco e grigio" — solid, excellent limestone, mostly white and grey. Most climbing is on vertical or slightly overhanging walls with small edges and water pockets (gocce d'acqua), "a volte dolorose" — sometimes painful. [Planetmountain](https://www.planetmountain.com/it/falesie/muzzerone-parete-centrale.html) describes the climbing as "molto tecnica ed elegante", with footwork the key skill. There is steep ground too: Polveriera is stamina climbing on tufas (canne), and Specchio di Atlantide needs finger strength; Planetmountain says the exposure below it "non ha nulla da invidiare a quello del Verdon" — nothing to envy the Verdon for.

**History.** The park website says the Navy special forces (incursori della Marina Militare) used these cliffs for training in the 1970s and bolted Parete Centrale first. Local climbers started adding routes in the late 1970s, and in the early 1990s "le due falesie più belle del Muzzerone" — the two finest walls on Muzzerone — were found and developed.

**Size.** The sites count very differently (checked 2026-10-18):

|---|---|---|

| [theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/area/450998862) | 406 routes | "18 separate limestone crags" |
| [Visit Portovenere](https://www.visitportovenere.com/it/cosa-fare-a-portovenere/arrampicata) | 300+ routes | 18 sectors, plus 2 on Palmaria island; 67 on Parete Centrale |
| [27crags](https://27crags.com/crags/muzzerone) | 294 sport routes | Most in grade 5 and 6a |

**Multi-pitch.** theCrag says multi-pitch routes go "up to 270m long. However most of the multi-pitch routes are 2 or 3 pitches long". For longer routes Planetmountain points to Parete Striata, "in un ambiente veramente selvaggio" — in a truly wild setting. theCrag lists Pilastro del Bunker at 160 m.

**Who it suits.** Grades run from 4 to 8b. The [Versante Sud](https://www.versantesud.it/en/shop/catalogue/muzzerone_1193) guidebook blurb says "The grades are never extreme", with multi-pitch routes "with bolts placed nearby and with short approach walks". Quickdraws and a 70 m rope (the length Planetmountain gives) cover most routes; if you want two- or three-pitch bolted routes over the sea, there is more choice here than at a plain roadside crag. Parete Centrale is so easy to reach that [Mountain Project](https://www.mountainproject.com/area/107498304/muzzerone) and Planetmountain both say the easier routes are polished.

## When to go

| Source | Season |
|---|---|
| Planetmountain | "si può arrampicare al Muzzerone tutto l'anno" (climbable all year) |
| Versante Sud | "It is possible to climb here all year round" |
| theCrag | "The best time to climb in Muzzerone is autumn and spring", winter also possible |
| Mountain Project | "from around September/October through May/June" |

**Aspect drives the season.** Planetmountain says every wall faces west and is "ottimamente riparate dai gelidi venti settentrionali" — well sheltered from cold north winds; theCrag and Mountain Project say most face south-west. Mountain Project warns that summer is "blazing hot". Planetmountain's summer advice is to skip the hottest hours and use morning shade and the cooler afternoon Mistral. Planetmountain's Atlantide Bassa page also recommends that sector for winter, while in summer it "heats up like an oven".

## Getting there

| | Public transport | Driving |
|---|---|---|
| Start | ATC bus from La Spezia | From La Spezia towards Porto Venere |
| Middle | To Piazza Bastreri in Porto Venere | Turn right uphill before you reach Le Grazie |
| Last leg | Trail no. 1 uphill; Planetmountain gives 30 minutes to the crags | About 4–5 km of mountain road to the parking spots |

### Public transport

The local bus company, [ATC Esercizio](https://www.atcesercizio.it/en/uncategorized/atc-for-tourism), says "ATC serves Portovenere with lines P and line 11". The park's [Track N.1 page](https://parconaturaleportovenere.it/track-n-1) says Piazza Bastreri in Porto Venere can be reached "by car or bus line 11 or P, or with the ferry from La Spezia or Lerici".

From Piazza Bastreri take the steps behind the castle up to the Cava Canese junction. Right leads via the hamlet of Mortea to the Rifugio Muzzerone hut; straight on climbs steeply to the Fortezza Muzzerone fort, from where, the park page says, "you can admire a fantastic view over the climbing area". Planetmountain and theCrag both give about 30 minutes from Porto Venere on path 1 or 1a. I did not find a first-hand timetable for frequencies or last buses; check the ATC website before you go.

### Driving

[27crags' access page](https://27crags.com/crags/muzzerone/access) has the most detail. Drive from La Spezia towards Porto Venere; about 600 m after entering Le Grazie, an uphill road goes off to the right with a brown "Palestra Rocciatori" sign, passing between two tooth-shaped rocks. Climb past a large quarry on your left, keep left at the first junction (1.5 km), then after another 1.6 km keep left again onto a slightly downhill road at the second junction. Planetmountain puts the mountain road at about 4 km, Falesia.it at about 5 km.

### Parking

27crags lists two spots:

- **Atlantide and Garimpo**: the second hairpin, just before the small quarry.
- **Central sectors and Cajenna**: a hairpin 700 m past the quarry. 27crags says you could once drive on to other car parks, but "now you have to stop here".

27crags adds a warning: do not park on the narrow sections, because "Heavy vehicles and trucks accessing the high quarry pass through frequently". Use only the wide spots and hairpins and leave the trucks room to turn.

theCrag gives the approach as "Steep hike 10-20 min". 27crags says Atlantide, Garimpo and Pilastro del Forte are reached down fixed ropes.

## Rules to know before you go

**The Parete Centrale ban.** The park's climbing page says that after a geological and geomechanical study found Parete Centrale unstable, the town of Porto Venere issued [Ordinance no. 2616 of 6 August 2014](http://www.parconaturaleportovenere.it/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/Ordinanza_Parete_Centrale_Muzzerone_2616_2014.pdf): "si vieta l'accesso e l'attività di arrampicata presso la "parete centrale" del Muzzerone" — access to and climbing on Parete Centrale are prohibited. The ordinance says the study found "blocchi più o meno grandi di roccia instabili distribuiti su tutta l'area indagata" — unstable blocks of various sizes across the whole surveyed area — and that the town meant to carry out work as soon as possible so the area could be used at an acceptable risk. Sources disagree on where things stand now:

| Source | What it says |
|---|---|
| Park website (checked 2026-10-18) | Still shows the 2014 ban |
| Planetmountain | "NOTA: Il 6/08/2014 il Comune di Porto Venere (La Spezia) ha vietato l'accesso e l'arrampicata alla Parete Centrale" |
| [Up-Climbing](https://www.up-climbing.com/falesia/news-falesia/muzzerone-chiusura-disgaggio-richiodatura-parete-centrale-2/) (2014-08-14) | The plan: geological survey, then scaling loose rock (disgaggio), then rebolting (richiodatura) |
| UKC | "The 2017 guidebook says it is closed but in April 2025 it was open again" |
| 27crags | Marks the whole crag "No special access issues" |

I found no notice from the town lifting the ban. At the crag, look for prohibition signs at the Parete Centrale entrance; if they are there, climb elsewhere.

**Climb only marked routes.** The walls sit inside the park's integral nature reserve RNI.1 "Falesie di Porto Venere – Muzzerone – Castellana". The ordinance cites article 5.4 of the park plan: climbing is allowed in this reserve "ma solo nelle vie già individuate e opportunamente segnalate" — but only on routes already identified and properly marked. Don't establish new lines or cut your own paths.

**Bolt condition.** theCrag says the 18 sectors "have recently been re-bolted", and Falesia.it lists spits, fixes and stainless-steel eyebolts. [Climbook](https://climbook.com/falesie/749/muzzerone-lintaglio-centrale)'s Muzzerone (L'intaglio, Centrale) page carries a warning that the crag has not been maintained for years, that the fixed gear is no longer reliable and the crag should be avoided, adding that the dangerous state of fixed gear on the sea cliffs applies to the whole of the eastern Ligurian coast. The same page says its data is "Agg. Dicembre 2019 (dati febbraio 2008)" — updated December 2019, data from February 2008. Bolts by the sea corrode, so check the bolts and lower-offs yourself before climbing.

**Where to stay.** Rifugio Muzzerone is on trail no. 1; theCrag says it is a 20-minute walk to the main crags. Planetmountain says the hut takes "al momento max 12 persone" — currently 12 people at most — with showers and hot water.

## Routes by level

YDS and V-grade conversions follow the [Mountain Project international grade comparison chart](https://www.mountainproject.com/international-climbing-grades) and are approximate; "approx." marks grades the sources give without a/b/c (such as 4 or 5+). The original grade is the one that counts.

Of 27crags' 294 sport routes, grade 5 and 6a are the most common; theCrag's per-sector counts are Parete Centrale 96, Cajenna 32, Atlantide 28, Pilastro del Bunker 27, Parete delle Meraviglie 26, Poveriera 15, Specchio di Atlantide 14, Garimpo 12 and Polveriera 12. Routes marked "see ban" are on Parete Centrale, so check the ordinance status in the previous section first.

### Beginner: up to 6a (YDS 5.10a and below)

Planetmountain calls Parete Centrale "il luogo ideale per i principianti e per le famiglie" (the ideal place for beginners and families), but that is the very wall that was closed in 2014. In the grade ranges collected by [Toscoclimb](https://www.toscoclimb.it/public/v2/modules/rockdocg/article.php?storyid=63), other easier sectors are Parete delle Meraviglie (3–7a), Poveriera (5b–6b+) and Pilastro di Mezzo (4c–6c).

| Route | Grade | YDS | Length / pitches | Sector | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Primi passi | 5a | 5.7 | Single pitch, 15 m | Parete Centrale | See ban; Planetmountain route list |
| Spigolo delle meraviglie | 5b max | 5.8 | Multi-pitch, 120 m | Parete delle Meraviglie | Multi-pitch recommended by [Bergführer Dolomiten](https://www.bergfuehrerdolomiten.it/it/vie-lunghe-liguria-muzzerone/) |
| Il corsaro | 5c | 5.9 | Single pitch, 15 m | Parete Centrale | See ban; one of Climbook's most-repeated routes |
| Via dell'allievo | 5c | 5.9 | Single pitch, 20 m | Parete Centrale, right side | See ban; one of Climbook's most-repeated routes |
| Bolscio | 5c | 5.9 | — | Cajenna | Planetmountain photo caption |
| Via Lidia | 5c, 6a | hardest 5.10a | 2 pitches, 35 m | Parete Centrale | See ban; Climbook lists Lidia L1 among its most-repeated routes |

### Intermediate: 6a+ to 6c+ (YDS 5.10b–5.11c)

For multi-pitch, go to Parete Striata (5c–7a per Toscoclimb). [RedClimber](https://www.redclimber.it/relazione/arrampicata/arrampicata-muzzerone/via-kimera/) calls Kimera "una delle più ripetute" (one of the most repeated) on the wall, but adds "per divertirsi conviene padroneggiare bene il 6a" (to enjoy it, you want to be solid at 6a).

| Route | Grade | YDS | Length / pitches | Sector | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rimedius | 6a+ | 5.10b | Single pitch, 15 m | Parete Centrale | See ban; one of Climbook's most-repeated routes |
| Oui je suis la plus facile | 6a+ | 5.10b | Single pitch, 15 m | Specchio di Atlantide | The name means "I'm the easiest one"; almost everything else in this sector is 7a and up ([Climbook](https://climbook.com/falesie/766/muzzerone-atlantide/vie) route list) |
| Kimera | 6b (hardest pitch 6b+) | 5.10c | 6 pitches, 180 m | Parete Striata | RedClimber: bolted throughout; pitch 5 is a link section along a fixed rope |
| Tramontana | 6b max, 6a obligatory | 5.10c | 6 pitches, 180 m + 70 m | Parete Striata | First ascent 2005 by Siliani and Vigiani; [Pro Rock](https://prorockoutdoor.it/service/muzzerone-parete-striata-tramontana/) says the easy, protectable sections are not bolted, so bring a set of friends and nuts |
| Angina Pectoris | 6c max | 5.11a/b | 7 pitches | Parete Striata | [RedClimber](https://www.redclimber.it/relazione/arrampicata/arrampicata-muzzerone/parete-striata-via-angina-pectoris/) calls it "una delle vie più belle" (one of the finest routes) on the wall; rebolted in 2016 |

[Gulliver](https://www.gulliver.it/itinerari/muzzerone-parete-striata-via-tramontana/) says that after a rockfall wiped out the approach on the right side of Parete Striata, the Pro Rock guides rigged an abseil approach of 4 rappels (70 m rope; the first is exactly 35 m). The alternative is a traverse along steel cables and fixed ropes in poor condition. Tramontana has a short section of conglomerate, so wear a helmet.

### Advanced: 7a to 7c+ (YDS 5.11d–5.13a)

Toscoclimb describes Garimpo (6c–8a+) as finger routes on an overhanging wall, and says Specchio di Atlantide (7a–8b) holds Muzzerone's hardest routes. Planetmountain says the right side of Atlantide Bassa is an overhanging red band, the left side sharp slabs.

| Route | Grade | YDS | Length / pitches | Sector | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ovomaltina | 7a | 5.11d | Single pitch, 15 m | Specchio di Atlantide | Climbook's most-repeated route in this sector |
| Siddharta | 7a, 6a+ obligatory | 5.11d | 6 pitches, 170 m | Parete Striata | [vielunghe.com](https://www.vielunghe.com/muzzerone-parete-striata-via-siddharta/) did pitch 5 on A0 (pulling on draws); first climbed ground-up on traditional pegs, now on fixed bolts |
| Supernova | 7a, 6b obligatory | 5.11d | 7 pitches, about 185–200 m | Parete Striata | The 7a is only on pitch 5; the rest is around 6a ([vielunghe.com](https://www.vielunghe.com/muzzerone-parete-striata-via-supernova/)); [Scuola Guido Della Torre](https://www.scuolaguidodellatorre.it/relazioni/Muzzerone-viaSupernova/724/) suggests 14 quickdraws and 60 m half ropes |
| Inox | 7a+ | 5.12a | Single pitch, 30 m | Atlantide Bassa | Planetmountain says some routes here are still fairly run out even after rebolting |
| Schake | 7b | 5.12b | Single pitch, 15 m | Specchio di Atlantide | Toscoclimb: "continuità di dita" (sustained finger strength) |
| Marmorea | 7c+ | 5.13a | Single pitch | Garimpo | Toscoclimb: "bloc di dita e resistenza dita" |

Climbook's Atlantide page carries the same gear warning as its Centrale page, also based on 2008 data.

### Expert: 8a and up (YDS 5.13b and up)

All on Specchio di Atlantide and Garimpo. Toscoclimb says almost all the hard lines on Specchio were bolted and first climbed by Roberto Vigiani in the late 1980s, and the hardest have all been rebolted.

| Route | Grade | YDS | Length / pitches | Sector | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gilgamesh | 8a | 5.13b | Single pitch, 25 m | Specchio di Atlantide | Toscoclimb: Muzzerone's first 8a |
| Cuor di Leone | 8a | 5.13b | Single pitch, 15 m | Specchio di Atlantide | Planetmountain photo caption |
| Fuga dal Buoux | 8a+ | 5.13c | Single pitch, 15 m | Specchio di Atlantide | Toscoclimb: finger strength plus one bouldery move |
| Sig. Doberman | 8a+ | 5.13c | Single pitch | Garimpo | Toscoclimb route list |
| No siesta | 8b | 5.13d | Single pitch, 15 m | Specchio di Atlantide | Bolted and first climbed by Vigiani in 1989 ([Versante Sud, *Up-Climbing*](https://www.versantesud.it/it/digital/up-climbing/up-climbing-10/no-siesta-8b/)); Toscoclimb says it was Muzzerone's first 8b |

## Where to find more information

- **Guidebooks:** route grades and route counts in this article come mainly from online databases such as theCrag and Mountain Project; where they disagree, go with the guidebook.
  - [*Muzzerone*](https://www.versantesud.it/en/shop/catalogue/muzzerone_1193) (Davide Battistella, Versante Sud, 2017, separate Italian, English and German editions): sport routes and bolted multi-pitches; this edition adds the Punta Bianca DWS area, and it is also in the iClimbing app. The publisher has no newer edition as of the check date.
