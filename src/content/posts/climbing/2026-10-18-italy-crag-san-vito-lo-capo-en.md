San Vito lo Capo is a small seaside town at the northwest corner of Sicily, in the province of [Trapani](https://westofsicily.com/en/useful-information/how-to-get-there/by-bus). West of town is a long run of limestone cliffs facing the sea. [theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/trapani) lists 1,306 routes here and opens by calling it "by far the most popular climbing area in Sicily"; Sicily's official tourism site [VisitSicily](http://visitsicily.info/en/climbing-and-dws-in-sicily) calls it "Sicily's sport climbing capital".

This post covers the decisions to make before going: whether it suits you, which months, how to get there without a car, where staying is easiest, and the local rules. For a season-and-region comparison of Italian crags, see the [series overview](/posts/climbing/2026-10-18-italy-crags-overview-en); another island limestone destination that suits winter is [Cala Gonone](/posts/climbing/2026-10-18-italy-crag-cala-gonone-en) in Sardinia.

## What kind of crag is it

**Rock.** theCrag describes "limestone in all shapes and forms": big overhanging walls, slabs, caves, tufas, jugs and crimps. [Climb Europe](https://climb-europe.com/pages/san-vito-rock-climbing) describes the main cliff as a mix of "good quality limestone and marble".

**Style.** theCrag says the "Majority is single pitch sport climbing". Routes are well bolted, and those that had quality problems in the past have "nearly all" been rebolted. For trad or routes over 100 m, go to Monte Monaco on the east side of town.

**Size.** Each source counts differently (checked 2026-10-18):

| Source | Count | Note |
|---|---|---|
| theCrag | 1,306 routes | "Mostly Sport climbing" |
| Climb Europe | "over 1,300 routes" | main cliff Salinella has "over 900 single pitch sport routes" |
| Sicily Rock guidebook | 2024 edition: 1,280 (theCrag listing) / 1,304 (Climb Europe crag page); 10th edition, October 2026: "over 1,300 sport routes" (Climb Europe shop page) | San Vito area only |
| Rockfax Sicily (2021) | "nearly 1,500 routes" | also covers Monte Pellegrino and Monte Gallo near Palermo |

**Sectors.** theCrag splits the area into several blocks; Climb Europe adds what each is like:

| Sector | theCrag routes | Character |
|---|---|---|
| Scogliera di Salinella | 868 | sea cliff west of town; Climb Europe says "nearly 4km long", grades F2a–F9a, with "over 700" at 6c+ and below |
| Monte Monaco | 142 | mountain walls east of town; Climb Europe says multi-pitch of 150–400 m, mostly 6b and up, needing trad gear and double 60 m ropes |
| Castelluzzo / Macari, Custonaci / Scurati | (not shown on theCrag page) | crags south of town; Climb Europe says routes from 7a up are more concentrated here, with plenty of tufas |
| Rocca Firriato | 38 | sport plus deep-water soloing; VisitSicily also mentions DWS above the sea caves at Cala Firriato |
| Valanga | 23 | all sport |

**Who it suits.** The main cliff runs from grade 2 to grade 9, with seven hundred-plus routes at 6c+ or easier; Climb Europe notes that "most of these routes are in the easy to mid-grade range". If you want to carry just quickdraws and hop between sectors by the sea, this is about as much choice as you get. For long routes, add Monte Monaco.

## When to go

| Source | Season |
|---|---|
| [theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/trapani) | "from October to May, in the summer it gets too hot" |
| [Climb Europe](https://climb-europe.com/pages/san-vito-rock-climbing) | "from October to mid-May" |

**Aspect decides hot or cold.** Climb Europe says most crags face south and west, while many walls around Monte Monaco face east and are "perfect for hot sunny days", so outside high summer you can climb almost year-round. theCrag route pages mark sun exposure; Soundgarden, for example, says "Afternoon sun West".

**Weather varies day to day.** theCrag warns that conditions in season "vary day to day": sometimes humid, sometimes rainy, and some days so windy that climbing is hard.

**Campground off-season.** The [El Bahira campground](https://www.elbahira.it/) says it is "aperto tutto l'anno" (open all year), but the bar, restaurant, market, pizzeria and pools "saranno chiusi da Novembre a Marzo" (closed November to March) — which is the heart of the climbing season.

## Getting there

| | Public transport | Driving |
|---|---|---|
| Start | Palermo airport or Trapani (Birgi) airport | Palermo airport or Trapani airport |
| Middle | AST bus from Trapani port, or Autoservizi Russo from Palermo | A29 motorway, exit Castellammare del Golfo, then SS187 |
| Last leg | from the town bus stop to the crag/campground on foot or by bike | park in town or at the campground |

theCrag says the closest airports are Palermo and Trapani, and "best way is to rent car, although buses also exist".

### Public transport

The bus page of [West of Sicily](https://westofsicily.com/en/useful-information/how-to-get-there/by-bus), the western Sicily tourist district, lists two lines:

- **Trapani side:** [AST](https://www.aziendasicilianatrasporti.it/) runs between San Vito and the port of Trapani; the page gives €4 one way / €7.50 return, about 1 h 30, 8–10 trips a day. A separate AST bus links Trapani airport to Trapani port: €4.90, about 20 min, hourly.
- **Palermo side:** [Autoservizi Russo](https://www.russoautoservizi.it/san-vito-lo-capo-2/) runs to and from Palermo; the page gives €9.40, 2–3 h, 1–2 trips a day. Russo's own destinations page lists Palermo, Palermo airport and San Vito.

The page puts the San Vito bus stop on Via Piersanti Mattarella near the beach. West of Sicily does not date the page, so check fares and times on the AST and Russo sites for the current season. [Wikivoyage](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/San_Vito_lo_Capo) notes San Vito has no railway station; the nearest is Trapani, about 40 km away.

Reaching town is not the end. The [Mountain Project](https://www.mountainproject.com/area/107611524/trapani-san-vito-area) area description warns that the campground sits below the sea cliff and the town above it, so "it'd be a few miles to walk into town because you have to go around the ocean cliff-face", and the author did not think there were groceries at the campground (El Bahira's own site lists a market, closed November to March). theCrag says bikes can be rented at El Bahira.

### Driving

Wikivoyage's route: take the Palermo–Trapani motorway (A29), exit at Castellammare del Golfo, then follow the SS187 to San Vito. The access page of the [Riserva dello Zingaro](https://riservazingaro.it/en/how-to-reach-us) also uses the SS187 to the San Vito junction; the reserve's north entrance is about 12 km from town, a good rest-day hike.

The author of that Mountain Project description rented a car in Palermo and drove to the campground in "about 1.5 hours", recommending a car for shopping and trips into town.

### Parking

None of the sources describe crag-specific parking rules. Wikivoyage warns that parking in the town centre is limited in summer and visitors often use paid lots outside it. If you stay at El Bahira, theCrag says most sectors are walkable from the campground.

## Rules to know before you go

**No new routes.** theCrag's Ethic field reads "As of 2020 it's forbidden to bolt new routes as re-bolting is still the main focus", and the note is inherited by every route page.

**Mind the cattle fences.** theCrag lists one access issue: reaching some sectors means crossing fences used for cattle — "be careful to not damage the fences".

**Rope and quickdraws.** The two sources roughly agree:

| Source | Single pitch | Multi-pitch |
|---|---|---|
| theCrag | at least a 60 m rope and 15 quickdraws; 70 m and 18 gets you almost everything | — |
| Climb Europe | most routes are 20–30 m; 35 m routes almost always have intermediate lower-offs; 60 m and 14–16 draws is plenty | double 60 m ropes and trad gear for Monte Monaco |

**Where to stay.** theCrag says El Bahira "is right under the main cliff", offers a climber discount in season, and tent campers can use the kitchen, showers and toilets. Climb Europe says the campground is above Salinella, with "the first routes being only a 1-minute walk away". Mountain Project mentions that some routes are on the campground's land, with flood lights for night climbing. There are also guesthouses in town and in Castelluzzo to the south.

## Routes by level

YDS and V-grade conversions follow the [Mountain Project international grade comparison chart](https://www.mountainproject.com/international-climbing-grades) and are approximate; "approx." marks grades the sources give without a/b/c (such as 4 or 5+). The original grade is the one that counts.

[Climb Europe](https://climb-europe.com/pages/san-vito-rock-climbing) says the main Salinella wall has over 900 single-pitch routes from F2a to F9a, more than 700 of them at 6c+ or below. [theCrag's Salinella page](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/trapani/area/351801099) says most routes are 15–30 m with "plenty of easier and moderate routes", and that in many cases "the grading seems to be a tad soft".

Sector names below follow [thetopo's Salinella North route list (S.V. Climbing)](https://www.thetopo.com/crags/scogliera-di-salinella/routelist) and [RockAroundTheWorld's 2025 trip report](https://rockaroundtheworld.co.uk/2025/02/16/san-vito-lo-capo-scogliera-di-salinella-part-1-north-to-campsite/). "Rating" is the average user rating on thetopo.

### Beginner: up to 6a (YDS 5.10a and below)

[sanvitoweb](https://www.sanvitoweb.com/en/climbing-on-the-cliffs) says The Zoo, just past the small beach at Cala Mancina, was "bolted primarily for beginner climbers", with a concentration of easy slabs, and that families with children are a common sight at the Salinella area by the campsite. RockAroundTheWorld warns that routes at The Zoo are "surprisingly upright for the grade".

## Where to look things up

- **[theCrag: San Vito lo Capo](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/trapani)**: theCrag describes its own coverage here as "very well maintained", with sectors, route pages, sun exposure and topos.
- **Guidebooks:** route grades and route counts in this article come mainly from online databases such as theCrag and Mountain Project; where they disagree, go with the guidebook.
  - [*Sicily-Rock*](https://gebro-verlag.de/gebro/sicily.htm) (Ulrich and Harald Röker, GEBRO Verlag, 10th edition September 2026, German, English and Italian, 320 pages, ISBN 978-3-938680-58-2): over 1,300 sport routes around San Vito, Castelluzzo and Custonaci, plus Monte Monaco multi-pitches and BIG CAVE bouldering, with rebolting and first-ascent years marked. theCrag calls it the main guidebook, regularly updated and on sale at El Bahira; [Climb Europe](https://climb-europe.com/products/sicily-rock-san-vito-sport-climbing) dates the 10th edition October 2026.
  - [*Sicily*](https://rockfax.com/climbing-guides/books/sicily/) (Daimon Beail, Rockfax, March 2021, English, ISBN 978-1-873341-68-1): San Vito plus Palermo, mostly sport, nearly 1,500 routes; Rockfax says a new edition is in preparation, and there is a Rockfax Digital version.
  - [*Di Roccia di Sole*](https://www.versantesud.it/it/negozio/catalogue/di-roccia-di-sole_1399/) (Massimo Cappuccio and Giuseppe Gallo, Versante Sud, 2022, separate Italian, German and English editions, 560 pages): single-pitch climbing across Sicily, including San Vito and Trapani, plus bouldering and DWS; also in the iClimbing app.
  - Versante Sud also has a single-sheet *San Vito Lo Capo* climbing map (3rd edition, 2015). It is old; use the three books above for route detail.
- **La Roccia di Sole**: single-pitch crags across Sicily; theCrag says it includes newer nearby crags such as Point Break and Antro dei ciclopi.
- **[West of Sicily bus page](https://westofsicily.com/en/useful-information/how-to-get-there/by-bus)**, **[AST](https://www.aziendasicilianatrasporti.it/)**, **[Autoservizi Russo](https://www.russoautoservizi.it/san-vito-lo-capo-2/)**: check this season's timetables before you go.
