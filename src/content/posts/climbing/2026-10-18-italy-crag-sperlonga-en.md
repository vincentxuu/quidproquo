> 🌏 [中文版](/posts/climbing/2026-10-18-italy-crag-sperlonga)

Sperlonga is a seaside town on the southern coast of Lazio, roughly halfway between Rome and Naples. When climbers say "Sperlonga" they don't mean the town. They mean the long band of limestone behind Sant'Agostino beach, on the road towards Gaeta. [Climbing Spot Factory](https://climbingspotfactory.com/index.php/en/climbadvisor/sperlonga/info_about) says so in its first paragraph: the walls actually lie in the municipalities of Itri and Gaeta, on the south side of Monte Vannellamare (257 m), and they close off the western end of Sant'Agostino beach.

This post covers what to decide before you go: whether it suits you, which months, how to get there, which sectors have bans, and how it differs from the neighbouring [Gaeta crags](/posts/climbing/2026-10-18-italy-crag-gaeta-en). For choosing among other Italian crags, see the [Italy crags overview](/posts/climbing/2026-10-18-italy-crags-overview-en).

## What kind of crag is it

**Mostly single-pitch sport, and a winter crag.** Climbing Spot Factory calls it "the crag of Lazio where the sport climbing has really grown more than any other". Climbers from Rome bolted heavily in the 1980s, and Bruno Vitale and others have kept opening new lines and replacing old gear with stainless steel and glue-in bolts. [Mountain Project](https://www.mountainproject.com/area/109659879/sperlonga) describes "more than 50 crags" and "100s and 100s of routes", from moderate seaside multi-pitch up to 5.13/14 in the cave.

**Rock.** [Planetmountain](https://www.planetmountain.com/en/crags/sperlonga-avancorpo-di-sinistra.html) says the vertical faces of Avancorpo di Sinistra are "littered with tiny pockets": not extremely hard, but they need finger strength and good footwork. [Oliunid](https://oliunid.com/blog/climbing-historic-limestone-sperlonga) writes that the limestone is often sharp, with edges, pockets and water-worn holds. The overhanging sectors such as Tetti Rossi are more powerful, and the vertical walls are more about balance and reading the rock. Stop when your fingertips split.

**One hillside, two labels.** Monte Moneta, directly behind Ristoro da Guido, is "Gaeta - Monte Moneta" on [falesia.it](https://www.falesia.it/crag/363/gaeta-monte-moneta.html). [Climbook](https://climbook.com/falesie/145/moneta-tetti-rossi-paretone) and [Alpinisti d'Appennino](https://www.alpinistidappennino.it/2025/02/21/falesia-tetti-rossi-moneta/) file it under Sperlonga. It uses the same parking and trailheads as the Vannellamare sectors, so it is covered here. The Gaeta post covers only Arenauta beach and Montagna Spaccata.

**Scale.** Each site divides the area differently, so the numbers can't be added together (checked 2026-10-18):

| Source | Coverage | Count |
|---|---|---|
| [theCrag: Sperlonga Cliffs](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/sperlonga-cliffs) | Vannellamare sectors | 424 routes, all sport |
| [falesia.it: Gaeta - Monte Moneta](https://www.falesia.it/crag/363/gaeta-monte-moneta.html) | 12 Moneta sectors (including both Avancorpi) | 206 routes, 4a–8a+, south-west facing |
| [Climbook: Moneta (Tetti Rossi, Paretone)](https://climbook.com/falesie/145/moneta-tetti-rossi-paretone) | Tetti Rossi and Paretone | 52 routes |
| [Planetmountain: Avancorpo di Sinistra](https://www.planetmountain.com/en/crags/sperlonga-avancorpo-di-sinistra.html) | One sector | 25 routes, 4c–6c+, south-east facing |

theCrag's two biggest sub-areas are Il pilastro di ponente (109 routes, with a warning flag on the page) and Parete del Chiromante (72). On falesia.it's Moneta page the biggest is Avancorpo di Destra (43 routes, 4a–6b), followed by Paretone (35, up to 8a+).

**Who it suits.** Climbers looking for winter sun and single-pitch sport. Mid-grade routes cluster on the two Avancorpi and Castello Invisibile. Italy's official tourism site [italia.it](https://www.italia.it/en/lazio/things-to-do/free-climbing-lazio) says Castello Invisibile has about twenty routes and suits beginners. The hard routes and historic lines are on Parete del Chiromante, Fascia Superiore and Tetti Rossi.

## When to go

| Source | Season |
|---|---|
| [Climbing Spot Factory](https://climbingspotfactory.com/index.php/en/climbadvisor/sperlonga/info_about) | "typically winter with the wall generally facing south"; best from October to May |
| [Mountain Project](https://www.mountainproject.com/area/109659879/sperlonga) | Autumn, winter, spring; "Summer must be high season for the beach but too hot for climbing" |
| [Alpinisti d'Appennino: Tetti Rossi](https://www.alpinistidappennino.it/2025/02/21/falesia-tetti-rossi-moneta/) | December to March |
| [Planetmountain: La Grande Muraglia](https://planetmountain.com/en/crags/la-grande-muraglia.html) | Autumn–winter |

**Summer.** Climbing Spot Factory suggests a few hours of afternoon climbing combined with a day on Sant'Agostino beach. italia.it says Castello Invisibile is in the shade and works in summer too.

**Rain and humidity.** italia.it says Palestra del Pueblo can be climbed on rainy days. Alpinisti d'Appennino says the main sector at Tetti Rossi sits under a big roof and stays climbable in rain, but being next to the sea it carries salt: friction is poor on humid scirocco days and much better on dry ones.

**October weather.** Mountain Project lists an October average high of 71°F (about 22°C), a low of 54°F (about 12°C), and 8 days of rain in the month.

## Getting there

| | Public transport | Driving |
|---|---|---|
| Start | Fondi-Sperlonga or Formia-Gaeta station on the Rome–Formia–Naples line | From Rome via the SS148 Pontina, or the A1 to the SR156; from Naples, A1 exit Cassino |
| Middle | Autoservizi Piazzoli bus from Fondi-Sperlonga station to Sperlonga town | Via Terracina or Formia onto the SS213 Flacca coast road |
| Last leg | No reliable direct service found from town to Sant'Agostino, see below | Ristoro da Guido at km 20.900 on the SS Flacca |
| Time | — | Mountain Project: about 1.5 hours' drive from Rome |

### Public transport

[Sperlonga Turismo](https://sperlongaturismo.it/en) recommends getting off at Fondi-Sperlonga station and taking the Autoservizi Piazzoli bus from the station square to Sperlonga. Tickets are sold on board or at the station bar. The station is in Fondi, inland, not in Sperlonga itself ([daytours.rome.it](https://daytours.rome.it/sperlonga-beaches)).

The problem is the last leg, because the crag is halfway between Sperlonga and Gaeta. [scoprigaeta.it](https://scoprigaeta.it/orari-autobus-gaeta/) builds its timetable from Cotral's open data: on 2026-10-18 it showed 0 direct runs from Sperlonga to Gaeta, and it describes direct runs as "pochi e non tutti i giorni" (few, and not every day). I found no stop or timetable for Sant'Agostino, so this post gives no schedule. A climber who went car-free in 2024 concluded on the [Mountain Project forum](https://mountainproject.com/forum/topic/125555168/gaeta-and-sperlonga-without-a-car) that renting a car is easiest and costs no more than taking cabs.

### Driving

Climbing Spot Factory's directions:

- **From the north and Rome:** take the A1 Milan–Naples motorway to the Ferentino exit, then the SR156 Monti Lepini. After about 20 km take the SA255 Priverno–Terracina road, join the SS7 bypass before Terracina, and after its tunnel get onto the SS213 Flacca towards Sperlonga/Gaeta. Past Sperlonga there are four tunnels, and the last one comes out onto the Sant'Agostino plain.
- **From southern Rome:** take the SS148 Pontina and join the same SS7 bypass before Terracina.
- **From Naples and the south:** take the A1 north to the Cassino exit, then the SS630 to Formia, through Gaeta and onto the Flacca towards Sperlonga. Sant'Agostino is about 6 km past Gaeta.

### Parking and the meeting point

The meeting point and trailhead is **Ristoro da Guido** ("the Mozzarellaro") at km 20.900 on the SS Flacca, the first restaurant on the left coming from the north (Climbing Spot Factory). italia.it says the owners offer climbers free parking, camper vans included, and showers. In 2025 Alpinisti d'Appennino described it as "aperto tutto l'anno" (open all year).

The trail depends on where you are going:

- **Avancorpo di Sinistra (Planetmountain):** walk the right-hand pavement of the Flacca towards Sperlonga, cross the viaduct, and take the steps on the right just before the tunnel.
- **Tetti Rossi (Alpinisti d'Appennino):** take a dirt road on the right a little way towards Sperlonga; about 40 minutes.
- **Moneta sectors (falesia.it):** walk back about 200 m and take the second lane on the right from the car park.

theCrag warns that the paths aren't very clear: allow extra time on your first visit, and wear long trousers.

## Rules to know before you go

**Seasonal bans and banned routes.** Three to note:

- Climbook's Moneta page: "DIVIETO di arrampicata dal 25 febbraio al 15 aprile (Solo parte sinistra fino ad Avatar)". No climbing from 25 February to 15 April on the left part of Tetti Rossi, as far as Avatar. The page gives no reason and is marked "Agg. dicembre 2018".
- [Climbook's Pilastro di Ponente page](https://climbook.com/falesie/50/sperlonga-pilastro-di-ponente) (updated January 2026): "ATTENZIONE! Divieto di arrampicata dal 25 febbraio al 15 aprile". No climbing anywhere on Pilastro di Ponente from 25 February to 15 April; again no reason is given.
- falesia.it's Moneta page: "Per il settore il Paretone alcune vie sono state vietate, e precisamente dalla 7 alla 19!" Routes 7 to 19 on the Paretone are banned.

**The park and the 2009 agreement.** [falesia.it's Montagna Spaccata page](https://www.falesia.it/crag/12/gaeta-montagna-spaccata.html) says that in 2009 the CAI, the mountain guides, the park authority and the region signed an agreement updating rules that "vietavano l'arrampicata in tutta Gaeta" (banned climbing throughout Gaeta), "comprese le falesie di S.Agostino". The park's signs were replaced, but many were later vandalised. The [Riviera di Ulisse regional park](https://parchilazio.it/rivieradiulisse-schede-32058-the_riviera_di_ulisse_regional_park) manages a natural monument along the Sperlonga coast (Villa di Tiberio–Torre Capovento–Punta Cetarola) and reports important peregrine falcon nesting sites there. None of the sources I found says whether the Sant'Agostino walls are inside that monument. If there's a sign at the crag, follow the sign.

**Bolt maintenance.** Climbing Spot Factory says a "green box" at Ristoro da Guido collects donations for replacing worn gear and maintaining routes. Some Climbook route notes report rust or missing bolts (for example, a rusty anchor chain on Nonno in carriola in spring 2024, and a missing last bolt on Avatar L1). A 2021 comment says many bolts replaced about ten years earlier now show discoloured resin, and some lower-offs are a single pigtail. Check recent comments before you go, and inspect the anchor yourself before climbing.

**Gear.** Alpinisti d'Appennino recommends 14–16 quickdraws and an 80 m rope for Tetti Rossi.

## Routes by level

YDS and V-grade conversions follow the [Mountain Project international grade comparison chart](https://www.mountainproject.com/international-climbing-grades) and are approximate; "approx." marks grades the sources give without a/b/c (such as 4 or 5+). The original grade is the one that counts.

I found no grade breakdown for the whole area; sector ranges suggest most routes are grade 5 to 6: falesia.it gives Avancorpo di Destra as 4a–6b, [Alpinisti d'Appennino](https://www.alpinistidappennino.it/2025/01/27/falesia-chiromante/) gives Parete del Chiromante as 3a–7b+ and says "mancano vie con difficoltà elevate" (hard routes are missing), and 7c and up is concentrated on Tetti Rossi. Grades on the historic routes are the ones given at the time and may since have changed; repeat counts come from each sector's Climbook page.

### Beginner: up to 6a (YDS 5.10a and below)

[ClimbFood](https://www.climbfood.it/falesia/sperlonga-castello-invisibile/) says that with so many grade-5 routes, Castello Invisibile "è molto usata nei corsi per principianti" (is used a lot for beginner courses). Alpinisti d'Appennino says [Pilastro di Ponente](https://www.alpinistidappennino.it/2023/01/20/falesia-pilastro-di-ponente/) suits everyone from beginners to advanced climbers, with a 5-minute approach, and that Chiromante's grades are "ideali per principianti e intermedi" (ideal for beginners and intermediates).

| Route | Grade | YDS | Length / pitches | Sector | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Viper | 4c | 5.6 | Single pitch, 15 m | Avancorpo di Sinistra | Planetmountain route list; Climbook: 32 repeats |
| Picchiami sulle bolle | 5b, 3a, 5c+ | hardest approx. 5.9+ | 3 pitches | Parete del Chiromante | [Climbook](https://climbook.com/falesie/41/sperlonga-parete-del-chiromante): 111 repeats of pitch 1 |
| III° via di gara L1 | 5c | 5.9 | Single pitch (pitch 2 is 6c) | Pilastro di Ponente | The sector's most-repeated route on [Climbook](https://climbook.com/falesie/50/sperlonga-pilastro-di-ponente) (225); seasonal ban, see below |
| Spigolo di Ferrante | 5c+, 4c, 5a | hardest approx. 5.9+ | 3 pitches | Parete del Chiromante | Historic route (5c per Climbing Spot Factory and Oliunid); pitch grades from Alpinisti d'Appennino |
| Messico e nuvole | 5b, 5c | hardest 5.9 | 2 pitches | Fascia Superiore | The sector's most-repeated route on [Climbook](https://climbook.com/falesie/42/sperlonga-fascia-superiore) |
| Inox | 6a | 5.10a | Single pitch, 22 m | Avancorpo di Sinistra | [Climbook](https://climbook.com/falesie/38/sperlonga-avancorpo-di-sinistra/vie): 73 repeats |

Climbook's Avancorpo di Sinistra page carries a warning: a large loose block sits between Rinaldik il terribile and Recinto ruspante, so don't climb those two routes or stand underneath. The Pilastro di Ponente page (updated January 2026) says "Divieto di arrampicata dal 25 febbraio al 15 aprile": no climbing from 25 February to 15 April.

Alpinisti d'Appennino describes Tetti Rossi as 5a to 8b+ on overhangs and roofs, with an approach of about 40 minutes; the main sector under the big roof can be climbed in the rain.

## How it differs from Gaeta

The two are only a few kilometres apart by car, and guidebooks often combine them, but they are different trips:

| | Sperlonga (this post) | [Gaeta](/posts/climbing/2026-10-18-italy-crag-gaeta-en) |
|---|---|---|
| Main sectors | Vannellamare and Monte Moneta sectors | Grotta dell'Arenauta, Montagna Spaccata |
| Style | Single-pitch sport, from vertical pockets to overhanging roofs | A steep cave (mostly 7a and up) and abseil-in sea-cliff multi-pitch |
| Access | Walk from Ristoro da Guido: Climbing Spot Factory gives 25 min on average, Tetti Rossi about 40 min | Private stairs down to the beach for the cave; abseil or boat for Montagna Spaccata |
| Key rules | Seasonal bans at Tetti Rossi and Pilastro di Ponente, some Paretone routes banned | Montagna Spaccata open only part of the year; Approdo dei Proci closed |

  - Every printed single-pitch guide is over ten years old, so check the edition before you buy.
