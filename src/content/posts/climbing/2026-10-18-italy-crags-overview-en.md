Each guide in this series covers one Italian crag: rock type and style, the best seasons and why, how to get there by public transport or by car, and the local rules to know before you go. It does not cover itineraries, flights, or accommodation prices, because that goes out of date fast. A crag's seasons and rules change much more slowly.

Seventy crags are covered so far, in 19 of Italy's 20 regions (all but Basilicata): from Piedmont, the Aosta Valley, Lombardy, Trentino-Alto Adige, Veneto, and Friuli in the north, through Liguria, Emilia-Romagna, Tuscany, and the central regions, to Campania, Puglia, and Calabria, and on to Sicily and Sardinia. Neighbouring crags in one area are sometimes combined into a single guide, for example the Sella Group, Val d'Ossola and Cadarese, Baunei and the Supramonte, and the Cortina single-pitch crags. Italy has far more than that: [theCrag's Italy page](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy) lists around 97,000 routes across 19 regions, about 15,600 of them in Trentino-Alto Adige alone.

## Picking a crag by season

Italy is long from north to south and runs from the Alps to Mediterranean islands, so crag seasons vary a lot. Roughly three kinds:

- **Winter crags: coastal limestone in the south and on the islands, plus a few low, south-facing crags in the north.** theCrag gives the season at [San Vito lo Capo](/posts/climbing/2026-10-18-italy-crag-san-vito-lo-capo-en) as October to May and says summer is too hot; [Rockfax's Sicily guide](https://uploads.rockfax.com/intros/Sicily-Intro.pdf) gives the main season as October to mid-May, with the best conditions from mid-November to the end of April. Elsewhere in Sicily, [Palermo](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-palermo-en), [Siracusa](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-siracusa-en), and [Ragusa and Modica](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-ragusa-en) are also autumn-to-spring crags. The Sardinian tourist board's [climbing page](https://www.sardegnaturismo.it/en/climbing-sardinia-best-walls-climb-island) says you can climb year-round but recommends spring, autumn, and winter; the local association at [Isili](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-isili-en) gives October to May as best, and [Domusnovas and Masua](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-domusnovas-en) suit winter best. On the mainland, the [Amalfi Coast](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-amalfi-coast-en) is an October-to-April crag, [Capo d'Uomo](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-capo-duomo-en) in Tuscany gets full sun on winter mornings, and [Grotti](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-grotti-en) in Lazio is best from November to March. The north has winter crags too: the south-facing side of [Val Rosandra](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-val-rosandra-en) near Trieste is a sunny-winter-day crag, and in [Val Pennavaire](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-val-pennavaire-en), inland from Finale, winter means the south-facing sectors on the north bank.
- **Summer crags: the high crags of the Dolomites, Mont Blanc, and the Apennines.** [Cinque Torri](/posts/climbing/2026-10-18-italy-crag-cinque-torri-en) sits at around 2,200 m, and [Planetmountain](https://www.planetmountain.com/en/crags/cinque-torri.html) lists its season simply as summer. The long Dolomite routes follow the huts and lifts: the [Tre Cime di Lavaredo](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-tre-cime-en) season runs from mid-June to early October, the [Catinaccio and Vajolet Towers](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-catinaccio-vajolet-en) from about mid-June to the end of September, and the lift used to descend from the [Marmolada South Face](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-marmolada-en) runs only 12 June to 27 September in 2026. There are summer sport crags too, such as [Frea](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-frea-en) below Passo Gardena (June to September) and [Laste](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-laste-en) in the Agordino (June to September). On the [Italian side of Mont Blanc](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-monte-bianco-en) the Skyway cable car sets the season, and you need glacier skills. Further south, [Gran Sasso](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-gran-sasso-en) in Abruzzo is a summer-to-early-autumn venue only, UKC calls [Caltavuturo](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-madonie-en) the best place in Sicily for summer climbing, and in Sardinia the summer favourite is [Ulassai and Jerzu](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-ulassai-en), at about 800 m with many sectors in shade all day (though spring and autumn are still the best seasons there).
- **Mostly spring and autumn, some year-round**: the local outdoor organization for [Finale Ligure](/posts/climbing/2026-10-18-italy-crag-finale-ligure-en) says that with the right sector you can climb in winter and summer, but spring and autumn are still best. The floor of [Val di Mello](/posts/climbing/2026-10-18-italy-crag-val-di-mello-en) is at 800–1,100 m; [27crags](https://27crags.com/areas/val-di-mello) calls it climbable almost year-round, with sunny sectors in winter and Bagni di Masino, at about 1,300 m, for summer. [Valle dell'Orco](/posts/climbing/2026-10-18-italy-crag-valle-dellorco-en) is listed for late May to early October, or spring and autumn. The granite and gneiss bouldering areas of the north are mostly spring and autumn too: [Valle Gesso](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-valle-gesso-en), [Champorcher](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-champorcher-en), and [Valle di Daone](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-daone-en) (move higher in summer to escape the heat). In central Italy, [Roccamorice](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-roccamorice-en) is best in spring and autumn and also climbable on sunny winter days, and [Gola della Rossa and Frasassi](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-frasassi-en) suit autumn to spring.

At some crags the season depends on closures as much as on weather: every source for [Collepardo](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-collepardo-en) gives a climbing ban from 1 January to 31 July, so in practice you can only plan for August to December; at [Statte](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-statte-en), the protected-area rules as written leave only September to December. Those crags and the others with current closures are listed in the next section.

The tables below run north to south by region and cover all 70 crags. The seasons come from the sources quoted in each guide. Where sources disagree, the individual guide lists all of them; the tables show a representative one.

## Check before you go: crags with current closures or restrictions

This list covers only what the individual guides already report (checked October 2026). Closures get lifted and new ones appear; the details and notice pages are in each guide's "Rules before you go" section, so check again before you travel. "Secondary" means the guide found only media or database reports and did not read the original order.

**Whole crag or large areas closed**

- [Andonno](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-andonno-en): from 1 October 2026 a Valdieri mayoral order bans access and climbing across the whole crag until maintenance works are finished, with no planned reopening date. After it reopens, the central sectors may close again in spring for nesting peregrines.
- [Tavolara](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-tavolara-en): in May 2025 the mayor of Olbia banned climbing and via ferrata on the island, with an exception only for mountain guides on research work; the ban was later set to run until 30 September 2025. The guide found no notice on whether it was renewed or replaced for 2026, so confirm with the Comune di Olbia before you go.
- [Frosolone](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-frosolone-en): the mayor banned climbing in August 2020; a May 2021 council resolution reopened the crag with conditions, closing it entirely from March to May that year for nesting cliff birds (secondary: Planetmountain, Montagna.TV and other reports; neither original order found). Planetmountain's crag page still shows a climbing ban, so check with the town hall first.
- [Cueva di Collepardo](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-collepardo-en): climbing is banned across the whole Collepardo area from 1 January to 31 July for nesting raptors (secondary: Climbook update of May 2026 and others; no 2026 primary notice found).
- [Statte](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-statte-en): the gorge lies inside Natura 2000 site IT9130007, whose management rules ban climbing during the bird-nesting period from 1 January to 31 August; databases and local sites still say it is climbable year round, so confirm before you go.

**Some sectors closed**

- [Val Pennavaire](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-val-pennavaire-en): the shady south-bank sectors Emisfero, Erboristeria and Cineplex fall inside the Province of Savona's permanent climbing ban of 2000 (the sector list is secondary, compiled by climbing media and blogs); in 2021 the administrative court annulled the region's trial opening, and no later opening decision could be found.
- [Lecco and the Grigne](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-lecco-grigne-en): Versasio was closed by order of the Comune di Lecco after rockfall at the end of 2022, and no notice lifting it could be found (secondary: Lecconotizie). Lagoland on the east shore was closed for stabilization works, with no reopening notice found since (secondary: reported by Larioclimb).
- [Arco](/posts/climbing/2026-10-18-italy-crag-arco-en): after a rockfall above Laghel on 25 August 2026, an emergency order from the town banned climbing on the crags and slabs above Laghel and use of the approach paths until expressly revoked; a 7 October notice repeated the closure.
- [Roccamorice](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-roccamorice-en): on 1 November 2025 a rockfall below the Betacam route in the Pancia d'Elefante sector killed a climber, and the accident area was placed under seizure (secondary: local media; no primary notice of the scope or of its lifting found). A town order also makes helmets mandatory.
- [Gola della Rossa and Frasassi](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-frasassi-en): park regulations allow climbing only on the routes listed in Annex 4; parts of the left bank of the Frasassi gorge, the Valle del Vernino and other areas are permanently off limits, and some routes have seasonal restrictions.
- [Orsomarso and Pollino](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-orsomarso-en): the park's 2016 interim climbing rules ban climbing in the Lao and Argentino valley areas, and Zone A is open only from September to December; some sectors at Frascineto are closed by the town.
- [Salento](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-salento-en): the Tetto Marino sector at Ciolo has been "closed to access since 2022" (secondary: Gulliver; original order not found). Separately, from 12 October 2026 a stretch of coast road at Ponte Ciolo is closed on weekday daytimes for about 30 days.

## Where to look things up

Each guide's "Where to look things up" section links that crag's pages. The sources used throughout:

- **[theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy)**: a user-contributed route database with around 97,000 routes in Italy, mostly sport. It is organized by region, and crag pages show a seasonality chart and grade distribution.
- **[27crags](https://27crags.com/countries/italy)**: another topo platform. Its Italy list includes Arco, Finale Ligure, Val di Mello, Valle dell'Orco, San Vito lo Capo, and several Sardinian areas. Many topos are behind its Premium paywall, and the list sometimes flags closures directly; Nago, near Arco, is marked "Closed for now".
- **[Planetmountain](https://www.planetmountain.com/en/crags)**: an Italian climbing and mountaineering outlet with a crag database. Each entry lists the grade range, height, number of routes, rock type, aspect, and access. Its news section often covers new routes and controversies.
- **[falesia.it](https://www.falesia.it/)**: an Italian-language crag site whose owner describes it on the forum as "praticamente il primo d'Italia" (practically the top in Italy) for crag information.
- **Guidebooks**: detailed topos are still mostly in print. [Versante Sud](https://versantesud.it/en/) is one of the main publishers, with regional books such as *Trento and Valsugana Rock*, which covers 64 crags. The local organization in Finale recommends two books: *Finale Climbing* (Versante Sud) and *Finale 51* (Idee Verticali). In English, Rockfax publishes guides to the Dolomites and Sicily.
- **[UKC Logbook](https://ukclimbing.com/logbook/books/finale_climbing-2710)**: shows which crags a guidebook covers, with route counts, rock type, and aspect for each—for example, the crag list for the 2022 third edition of *Finale Climbing*.
- **Local organizations**: current closures, maintenance, and parking rules are often only posted locally. [Finale Outdoor Region](https://finaleoutdoor.com/en/stories/climbing-in-the-finale-area-all-you-need-to-know), for example, has a "LIVE INFO" page for closures and maintenance.

**Grades**: theCrag's Italy page uses French grades (6a, 7b+, and so on) as its context; that is what sport climbing uses, and most of Planetmountain's crag entries are in French grades too. Trad routes in the Dolomites are different: the [Rockfax Dolomites guide](https://rockfax.com/wp-content/uploads/flipbooks/Dolomites_2014_intro/files/assets/basic-html/page60.html) says UIAA (IV, VI+, VII, and so on) is the standard system for trad routes throughout the Italian Dolomites, and that UIAA grades only technical difficulty, not objective dangers such as poor protection or rockfall.

## Rules that apply almost everywhere

Each guide's "Rules to know before you go" section covers that crag. A few things come up often in Italy:

- **Bird-nesting closures.** To protect potential nesting sites of eagle owls and peregrine falcons, [Finale Outdoor Region](https://finaleoutdoor.com/en/stories/climbing-in-the-finale-area-all-you-need-to-know) lists walls closed to climbing all year (for example the southern part of Bric Pianarella and Capo Noli) and walls closed every year from 1 January to 31 July (for example Monte Tolla and Bric Reseghe). [Up-Climbing](https://www.up-climbing.com/falesia/news-falesia/finale-attenti-ai-rapaci/) has published the same list. Other areas may have similar rules; check local notices before you go.
- **Parking and sleeping in vehicles.** The roads to crags around Finale such as Perti, Monte Sordo, Rocca di Corno, and Boragni are narrow, and parking is banned almost everywhere on them so emergency vehicles can always get through. Across the municipality of Finale Ligure, camping and overnight stays in vehicles are not allowed outside authorized areas.
- **Road closures.** Some approach roads close on a schedule. For Cinque Torri, the road from Cianzopè to Rifugio Cinque Torri: [Rifugio 5 Torri](https://rifugio5torri.it/en/5-torri.html) announces a summer closure from 6 June to 11 October 2026, with a shuttle only in August; Planetmountain's older note mentions only Sunday closures in August. See the Cinque Torri post for details.
- **How historic routes are protected.** Some areas in Italy care a lot about keeping the first ascensionists' protection. Valle dell'Orco is where the 1970s "Nuovo Mattino" (New Morning) movement began, and a [Planetmountain article](https://www.planetmountain.com/it/notizie/arrampicata/i-50-anni-del-caporal-in-valle-dell-orco-nuovo-mattino.html) recalls the British climber Mike Kosterlitz showing local climbers their first nuts and a clean style. Val di Mello has thousands of bolts and most of its walls are equipped, but its historic routes have traditionally kept their original protection. In May 2026 a climber publicly removed bolts and anchors that had been added to two historic routes on the Placche dell'Oasi; [Montagna.TV](https://www.montagna.tv/274231/val-di-mello-luca-schiera-sono-stato-io-a-schiodare-le-vie-vi-spiego-perche/) interviewed him, and the move reopened the debate between preserving history and adding safety. When a topo describes a route as trad or as keeping its original protection, prepare for trad and don't add bolts yourself.

**If something goes wrong**: Italy's official tourism site, [italia.it](https://www.italia.it/it/italia/informazioni-per-viaggiare/numeri-emergenza-e-assistenza), says the single emergency number is 112, dialed without any prefix; 118 is the medical emergency number and is also used for mountain and cave rescue; 1530 is sea rescue. Mountain rescue is mainly carried out by [CNSAS](https://www.cnsas.it/) (Corpo Nazionale Soccorso Alpino e Speleologico, the national alpine and cave rescue corps), a volunteer organization that is a national section of the Italian Alpine Club (CAI). In 2025 it ran 13,037 rescue missions and helped 9,624 people. CNSAS also offers a rescue app, GeoResQ.

**Rescue is not always free.** A [2023 summary by trekking.it](https://www.trekking.it/news/soccorso-alpino-pagamento-ticket/) explains that under a 1992 regulation, the national health service pays for rescue and transport only when the patient is admitted to hospital, and the regions along the Alps (except Liguria and Friuli Venezia Giulia) charge for rescues that don't lead to admission. The autonomous provinces of Trento and Bolzano charge a small fee even when the patient is admitted, and Veneto charges people injured in activities such as rock and ice climbing even if they are admitted. Rules vary by region and change, so check before you travel whether your travel or climbing insurance covers mountain rescue.

## Limits of this series

- The first 12 guides were checked on 2026-10-18; the 58 added on 2026-10-19 were checked in October 2026. Bird-closure areas, parking rules, bus timetables, and rescue fees change from year to year; each guide links the notice pages, so check again before you go.
- The author has not climbed at these crags. The guides compile public sources and contain no first-hand experience.
- Route counts vary a lot between sources. That reflects different coverage, not that one source is wrong.
- As a rule, crags where climbing is prohibited are not included. Andonno has been closed since 2026-10-01, but the order is a temporary closure until maintenance works are finished, and the 2026 status of Tavolara's ban is unclear; both guides are kept and listed under "Check before you go".
- Not included: Parete dei Militi (the wall is in France), Leano and Circeo (part of the Sperlonga–Gaeta coastal strip), Capri, Monte Amiata, and other crags with too little verifiable sourcing.
