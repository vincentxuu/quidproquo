Your familiar gym belay device may not handle every task on an overseas trip. One day uses a single rope for sport climbing; the next calls for two half ropes on a multipitch route. Threading a rope through a device does not establish a permitted combination.

Start with equipment matching, then compare four belay devices and representative carabiners and quickdraws. The goal is to state which rope, which mode, and which carabiner you will use, and identify techniques you still need to learn. Review [triple-rated ropes](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-dynamic-rope-triple-rating-en) first if necessary. This is purchase and training preparation, not complete rigging, load-release, or retreat instruction.

## Divide the work into three tasks

| Task | Question to answer | Common purchasing mistake |
|---|---|---|
| Belaying a leader or top-rope climber from the harness | Single or two-rope system? Compatible type and diameter? | Checking diameter without single/half/twin type |
| Belaying followers from the station | An approved anchor mode? One follower or two? | Treating ordinary and guide modes as the same operation |
| Rappelling and retreat | One strand or two? Braking, backup, and length arrangements? | Assuming a belay device supports the planned descent |

A multipitch day may involve all three. Resolve each task rather than buying something labeled multifunctional. If a column lacks training, record that as something to learn.

[REI's ATC-Guide page](https://www.rei.com/product/899108/black-diamond-atc-guide-belay-device) also shows US$54.95 and notes that an old listing may display the newest version instead. The same name does not establish identical specifications across generations. Locate the correct instructions when buying used or clearance stock.

You need not buy all four. For an entirely single-rope sport trip, begin with familiar single-rope options. When using two half ropes, a more expensive GRIGRI or NEOX cannot replace an appropriate two-rope device. NEOX's wheel design also is not field-test evidence that everyone will handle it better.

## Assisted braking still requires a brake hand

Both GRIGRI and NEOX product instructions require holding the brake rope while belaying. A device catching the rope does not permit letting go to chat or take photos, or gripping the mechanism in a way that defeats its braking during a fall. Practice feeding, taking in, and lowering with your own rope in an instructor-controlled setting before departure, rather than learning a new mechanism at an overseas stance.

The [REVERSO instructions](https://www.petzl.com/on/demandware.static/-/Sites-petzl-master-catalog/default/dw3ddbef35/media/product/technical-notice-REVERSO-5_068bK00000nDYPVQA4.pdf) distinguish ordinary belaying from assisted braking at the station. The latter depends on correct configuration and loading. Crossed thin ropes or one of two followers weighting their rope can affect the expected braking function.

The [ATC-Guide instructions](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/catsy.865/M10798_D_ATC-Guide_IS-WEB.pdf) likewise require gripping the brake rope and warn that one follower weighting a rope can affect locking on the other. **Guide mode is a specific belaying configuration, not a complete rescue system or evidence of rescue competence.** Load release, lowering, transfers, and retreat require additional instruction. If you need free hands, first complete the manufacturer's permitted securing/backup procedure; do not simply release the rope.

## Locking carabiners: match shape to device

Read the device instructions before choosing shape, dimensions, and locking system. Check major-axis loading with the gate closed and locked, and whether slackening and reloading can rotate the device, jam it, or press on the gate. Check complete closure each time with an auto-locker too.

The [ATC-Guide instructions](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/catsy.865/M10798_D_ATC-Guide_IS-WEB.pdf) specify EN 12275 Type H carabiners. In contrast, [Petzl's advice for attaching GRIGRI/NEOX to a harness](https://www.petzl.com/US/en/Sport/Choice-of-carabiner-for-attaching-a-GRIGRI-or-NEOX-to-the-harness) emphasizes rotation resistance and positioning, and warns that pear-shaped carabiners tend to rotate in that application. One device needing an HMS does not make it a universal choice.

## Draw length: sketch the rope path first

Short draws are compact. Longer ones can help the rope avoid features and reduce wandering where appropriate. Replacing everything with long draws is not automatically better: protection position, rope path, ground clearance, and terrain all matter. Discuss a multipitch rack with your partner using route requirements, including the longest pitch and additional needs. There is no universal guarantee that twelve draws suffice.

Keep each draw's bolt end and rope end distinct. The manufacturer's [quickdraw instructions](https://www.petzl.com/on/demandware.static/-/Sites-petzl-master-catalog/default/dwdefcef42/media/product/technical-notice-climbing-carabiner-sling-1_068Tx000004LnEjIAK.pdf) warn that anchor-generated notches can damage rope. Do not swap a bolt-used carabiner into rope service. Follow the specific model's instructions for rubber retainers rather than immobilizing both ends yourself.

Before traveling, lay the rack out on the ground and check webbing, stitching, gate return, sharp edges, and retainers. Only return clearly oriented, functioning items without unresolved concerns to the ready pile; isolate doubtful equipment. This sorting does not replace practical instruction on clipping direction, rope path, or loading against rock features.

## Find gaps with a compatibility sheet

| Field | Answer required before departure |
|---|---|
| Rope | Model, type, diameter, number of ropes, and condition |
| Device | Exact model/generation, intended mode, and manufacturer limits |
| Carabiner | Permitted shape/type for that mode, locking system, and positioning |
| People and descent | Both partners trained, shared-equipment responsibilities, complete descent equipment |

Consider a **constructed example**: two climbers bring two 8.0 mm half ropes but only a GRIGRI and the ATC-Guide listed above. GRIGRI fails the rope-type and single-rope-use check. This ATC-Guide's product range starts at 8.1 mm. Do not enlarge it yourself because the difference is only 0.1 mm. Reconsider the device or rope setup and check modes, carabiners, descent, and training; adding an arbitrary carabiner does not resolve the mismatch.

## Exercise: new equipment or a better understanding?

**Your partner says: “Buy the most expensive NEOX and an HMS and we can do everything. Guide mode locks, so I can just let go and get my phone.” What is wrong?**

<details>
<summary>Answer and criteria</summary>

NEOX remains a dynamic single-rope device, not a two-rope device. An HMS is neither optimal nor mandatory for every device; follow the exact model and mode. Anchor-assisted braking has loading requirements and failure conditions and does not permit simply releasing the brake rope. Secure/back up the system as instructed before freeing hands. The gaps are system understanding and relevant training, not the absence of the most expensive device.

</details>
