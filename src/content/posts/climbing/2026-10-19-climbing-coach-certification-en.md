In a Taiwanese gym class, the coach's vest or name tag often says "Level C coach". What did they have to pass to get that? How is it different from the belay card in your own wallet? And if you've climbed for a few years and want to start taking friends out, or teach a few classes at a gym, which certificate comes first?

In the previous post, [Kids and Adaptive Climbing](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-kids-adaptive-climbing-en), several answers came down to "ask the coach". This post looks at coaches themselves: how Taiwan's coaching certificates work, whether the law requires them, and how other countries divide things up.

## First, separate four things

Climbers call all sorts of cards "certificates", but they prove very different things:

| Type | What it proves | Taiwan example | Overseas example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Belay card | You can belay someone safely | Each gym's belay card, CTAA CBT | A gym belay test |
| Coaching certificate | You can teach and manage a group | CTAA Level C/B/A coach, CTCA recreational climbing coach | AMGA CWI, UK CWI, Foundation Coach |
| Mountain guide licence | You can lead clients in high mountains, on glaciers, on multi-pitch | Taiwan has no IFMGA member association | IFMGA guide |
| Competition coach pass | You can enter the athlete area at events | The legal purpose of the CTAA coaching certificate | USA Climbing L1/L2 |

How belay tests work is in [Learning to Top-Rope Belay in the Gym](/posts/climbing/2026-10-17-gym-top-rope-belay-en). Mountain guiding is a much longer road; see [How a Taiwanese Climber Can Become an IFMGA Mountain Guide](/posts/climbing/2026-10-17-ifmga-taiwan-path-en). This post covers coaching certificates only.

## Taiwan: the Alpine Association's Level C/B/A sport climbing coach

The Chinese Taipei Alpine Association (CTAA) is [Taiwan's member of World Climbing (formerly the IFSC)](https://www.worldclimbing.com/national-federations/nf-tpe/tpe-info) and runs the sport climbing coach examinations. Under its [2025 coach qualification plan](https://itsrcowebs.com/Files/archives/article/100015/c30784764e984a3288a4ca0fe4d60cad_043-114-%E6%95%99%E7%B7%B4-%E8%B3%87%E6%A0%BC%E6%AA%A2%E5%AE%9A%E5%8F%8A%E7%AE%A1%E7%90%86%E5%AF%A6%E6%96%BD%E8%A8%88%E7%95%AB-0313.pdf) (in Mandarin), eligibility for the three levels is:

| Level | Eligibility | Course hours | Climbing test standard |
|---|---|---|---|
| C | High school or above, valid CBT | At least 24 | 5.10a/b |
| B | Two years at Level C plus coaching practice, or a former national team athlete at the Olympics, Asian Games etc. | At least 32 | 5.11a/b |
| A | Three years at Level B plus coaching practice, or a national sports medal of the second grade, first class or above | At least 40 | 5.12a/b |

All three levels require you to be at least 20. At the end of 2024 the CTAA had 99 Level C coaches and 17 each at Levels B and A.

### What the Level C course actually tests

Take the CTAA's [Level C course at Neihu Sports Center in March 2026](https://itsrcowebs.com/Files/archives/article/100015/18f674967bcc4b66ba7d4c93392c0961_202603227-29-C%E6%95%99-%E5%85%A7%E6%B9%96-%E7%B0%A1%E7%AB%A0.pdf) (in Mandarin):

- **Eligibility**: valid CBT, actively climbing, at least 20, high school education, and a police criminal record certificate issued within one month.
- **Fee**: NT$4,000 for members, NT$4,500 for non-members, plus NT$300 for the certificate once you pass. The 2024 course at T-UP Zhonghe cost the same.
- **Size**: at most 30 people; cancelled if fewer than 10 sign up.
- **Course**: three days. Classroom topics are competition rules, sports injury care and first aid, climbing physiology, coaching science, training considerations for children and teenagers, anti-doping, gender equality education, climbing history and terminology. The rest is technical practice.
- **Tests**: a written exam and a practical test. Under Annex 5 of the 2025 plan, the written exam pass mark is 70; the practical covers climbing and belaying ability (pass mark 80, standard 5.10a/b) and a teaching demonstration (pass mark 60). You need to pass both. The 2026 course notice says the certificate is then issued by the Chinese Taipei Sports Federation; the 2025 plan and the coach qualification rules say the CTAA issues it.

The step before Level C is the CBT. [T-UP's 2023 notice](https://tupclimbing.tw/news/announcement/232) (in Mandarin) spells it out: top-rope belaying, lead belaying, one 8-metre lead at 5.7–5.8, and rope handling. The fee is NT$1,200 (NT$1,000 for members). The CBT is also the prerequisite for the Level C coach, judge and route setter courses.

### Certificates expire after four years

Under the [rules on coach qualification for specific sports bodies](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=H0120082) (in Mandarin), coaching certificates are valid for four years. To renew, you need 48 hours of professional development within those four years, with at least 6 hours each year. So when you look at a coach's certificate, check the expiry date as well as the level.

### Back to the vest: what "Level C coach" means

When a coach says Level C, you know:

- **They passed**: the CBT (can belay, can lead), a 24-hour coaching course, a written exam, a 5.10a/b climbing test and a teaching demonstration. When they applied, they submitted a police record certificate from the past month showing no record, such as assault, sexual offences or drug offences, that legally bars someone from coach certification.
- **What it doesn't prove**: how many students they've taught, whether they're good with kids, or whether they can lead outdoors. For that, look at their experience or just ask.

**Something you can do tonight**: at your next class, ask the coach, "Which body issued your certificate, and what level?" It isn't rude; good coaches are usually happy to answer.

## Taiwan: the Climbing Association's recreational climbing coach

The other route is the [Chinese Taipei Climbing Association](https://pa.org.tw/CTCA/04class/index.html) (in Mandarin) "Level C recreational climbing coach", run by Taipei Climbing School at the Liugong climbing base in Xindian. You need to be at least 20, have a high school education, and be able to lead and retreat from a route. The fee is NT$6,000, with 20 people per intake. It's a private association's certificate and a separate system from the CTAA's Level C/B/A. Despite the "Level C" in its name, it isn't the same certificate as the CTAA's Level C. Dates for the next intake (the 35th) haven't been announced.

## Do you need a certificate to teach in Taiwan?

This is the question people ask most, and the hardest to answer. Look at it in three layers:

**National law**: the Level C/B/A in the coach qualification rules are defined by competition role, for example Level C as a coach at government- or association-run events and Level B as an assistant national team coach. Neither the Sports Act nor the related rules contain anything saying you must be certified to teach in a commercial gym. A lawyer's 2026 [column](https://www.legis-pedia.com/columns/7/articles/1458) (in Mandarin) also notes that no regulation sets uniform specifications for climbing gyms, and that, judging from past rulings, courts mostly refer to the CTAA's *Sport Climbing Safety Manual*.

**Local inspections**: [Taipei's Department of Sports notice of 11 February 2026](https://industry.sports.gov.taipei/announcement/?K=286) (in Mandarin) added climbing and bouldering gyms to its inspection form for private sports venues. Two items concern coaches:

- During opening hours, at least one staff member on site must hold a relevant coaching licence and a first-aid certificate.
- Climbing coaches, judges and route setters on site must hold a Level C (or equivalent) or higher certificate from a recognised body, within its four-year validity.

These are Taipei's inspection items; other cities and counties may not have the same requirements.

**The gym's own rules**: many gyms don't allow outsiders to teach on their premises. [T-UP's entry rules](https://tupclimbing.tw/terms-and-conditions/78) (in Mandarin), for example, strictly forbid any private technical instruction. If you want to introduce a friend to climbing, the simplest thing is to sign them up for the gym's class.

The system may change. The [draft National Sports Act](https://www.ey.gov.tw/Page/9277F759E41CCD91/d0eb8192-299e-4522-a74d-23435408aed2) (in Mandarin) approved by the Executive Yuan in September 2026 adds a chapter on coaches and a lookup system for coaches found unfit. It's still before the Legislative Yuan; whether it affects commercial teaching won't be clear until the text passes.

## How other countries divide it: the US and the UK

Looking abroad shows how finely "coaching certificate" can be sliced.

### The US: AMGA, PCIA and USA Climbing

- **AMGA CWI (Climbing Wall Instructor)**: the American Mountain Guides Association's [climbing wall instructor](https://amga.com/programs/climbing-instructor-programs/climbing-wall-instructor-program) course, 2.5 days, age 18+, with a top-rope scope and a lead scope. Top-rope: 30 climbing days in the past 12 months and able to top-rope 5.7 comfortably. Lead: 50 days, top-rope 5.9, lead 5.8.
- **AMGA SPI (Single Pitch Instructor)**: an outdoor [single-pitch instructor](https://amga.com/programs/climbing-instructor-programs/single-pitch-instructor-program). A 27-hour course, then a 16-hour exam within two years; before the exam you need 40 traditional lead climbs (a multi-pitch route counts as one; 15 are needed to enrol in the course).
- **PCIA**: a separate organisation, the [Professional Climbing Instructors Association](https://pcia.us/programs), also offers a CWI and an SPI. Same names, different courses from AMGA's.
- **USA Climbing L1/L2**: USA Climbing's [coach certification](https://usaclimbing.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Coach-Certification.pdf) is really a competition pass, requiring membership, a background check and SafeSport online training. L1 is 16+, L2 is 18+ with first aid, and every team needs at least one L2. It isn't a teaching course.

### The UK: Mountain Training's ladder

The UK's [Mountain Training](https://www.mountain-training.org/qualifications/climbing/qualifications/climbing-wall-instructor) splits "instructors" (managing safety, leading groups) from "coaches" (teaching movement, improving performance):

- **Instructor track**: Indoor Climbing Assistant (16+, assists only under supervision) → Climbing Wall Instructor (indoor top-rope) → Climbing Wall Development Instructor (indoor lead) → Rock Climbing Instructor (outdoor single-pitch) → Rock Climbing Development Instructor (outdoor lead).
- **Coach track**: Foundation Coach → Development Coach → Performance Coach. Coaches who run top-rope sessions still need the matching instructor qualification.

Taiwan's coaching certificate combines both jobs in one card; the UK tests them separately.

## Becoming a coach: one possible order

A common path for gym coaches in Taiwan looks roughly like this:

1. **Be a good belayer first**: get a gym belay card, then take the CBT.
2. **Build your climbing level**: the Level C climbing test is 5.10a/b, and your leading should be solid.
3. **Sign up for a CTAA Level C course**: watch for the course notice; there are 30 places, and you often need to get the police record certificate sorted early.
4. **Teach part-time at a gym to build experience**: you need two years at Level C plus practical experience before applying for Level B.
5. **If you want to lead outdoors**: Taiwan has no statutory outdoor instructor certificate. To follow an overseas standard, use the AMGA SPI prerequisites as a guide and build your lead mileage and self-rescue skills.

On pay, a [January 2026 job-listing analysis by 104 Job Bank](https://blog.104.com.tw/104data-rock-climbing-recruit-salary/) (in Mandarin) shows full-time gym climbing coaches at about NT$32,000–40,000 a month and children's climbing teachers at about NT$300–600 an hour. These are ranges posted in job ads; actual income depends on hours and the gym.

**Something you can do tonight**: look up the CTAA's latest course notice and check which requirement you're still missing: CBT, climbing level or age.

## Practice: four scenarios

The answer is under each one. Think first, then look.

**1. A friend says: "I've got my CBT, so I can teach people to climb."**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

The CBT shows they can belay; it isn't a coaching certificate. A CTAA Level C coach needs at least 24 more hours of coursework on top of the CBT, and has to pass a written exam, a 5.10a/b climbing test and a teaching demonstration. Many gyms also forbid private teaching on their premises anyway.

</details>

**2. You want to take two friends top-roping at Long Dong and charge each of them NT$1,000. Which kind of certificate do you need?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

We couldn't find any Taiwanese rule requiring a certificate to guide paid outdoor climbing, and the CTAA coaching certificate is defined by competition role. But no legal requirement doesn't mean no responsibility: if something goes wrong, your training and decisions will be examined. The overseas comparison is the US SPI: a 27-hour course, 40 traditional leads, then a 16-hour exam. Taiwan's coaching certificate doesn't cover outdoor guiding either. If you want to charge, at minimum get first-aid training and self-rescue skills, and check your own and your friends' insurance.

</details>

**3. At a private gym in Taipei you ask a coach about certification. They say, "I've climbed for ages; I don't need one."**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

National law doesn't explicitly require it, but Taipei's 2026 inspection form requires climbing coaches on site to hold a Level C (or equivalent) or higher certificate valid within four years, and at least one person on site to have a coaching licence and first-aid certificate. If it matters to you, ask the gym directly about its coaches' qualifications.

</details>

**4. You've held a Level C certificate for three years and done only one 6-hour professional development session in that time.**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Certificates are valid for four years; renewal needs 48 hours of professional development in those four years, with at least 6 hours each year. You're short. If it expires without renewal, you'll have to retake the course and tests for your level.

</details>
