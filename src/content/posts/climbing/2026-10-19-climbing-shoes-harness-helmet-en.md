Shoes that feel slightly tight on day one may leave you desperate to remove them on day three. A harness can feel fine standing up and press painfully into your thighs at a hanging stance. For several consecutive climbing days abroad, consider how long you will wear each item, where you will use it, and what else you will be wearing.

Our **planning example** is a seven-day trip with travel and rest days, three sport-climbing days, and one multipitch day. Use it to practice choosing fit and features; this is not a field-tested ranking. Continue with the [travel-packing article](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-climbing-travel-packing-en) for luggage organization.

## Bring the multipitch day into the fitting room

Between sport-climbing attempts you can often remove shoes and put gear down. On a multipitch route, you may stand in your shoes for longer, while your pack shares space with your harness and helmet. Take this table to the shop and wear your intended climbing trousers and layers.

| Item | Sport-climbing check | Additional multipitch check |
|---|---|---|
| Shoes | Stable foothold contact, no obvious heel movement, acceptable removal and fastening | Sustained standing, intended rock style, and comfort through a whole pitch |
| Harness | Waist and leg fit, correctly tightened buckles | Suspension comfort, accessible gear loops, and pack interference |
| Helmet | Stability, clear vision, properly adjusted chinstrap | Looking up without hitting the pack; headlamp and hood compatibility |

This is a fitting plan. It does not replace the manufacturer's pre-use inspection or a partner check.

## Shoes: start with foot shape, not a two-size reduction

An EU 39 can fit differently across lasts, materials, and brands. [SCARPA's fit guide](https://us.scarpa.com/pages/size-chart-and-fit-guide) provides measuring tools and conversion information; use measurements to identify a starting point for trying shoes. A friend's downsizing formula is a poor basis for an international order.

Try both feet, fasten the shoes properly, and use the shop's approved foothold-testing facilities. Check for unnecessary toe space, heel movement, and whether you can load your feet effectively. Sharp pain, numbness, or having to endure an excessively curled position are reasons to try another size or last, rather than evidence of performance. Confirm tolerable wearing time through actual climbing before departure.

For long routes, start by trying flatter, supportive options. Steep climbing and small footholds may lead to different choices in shape and sensitivity. A flat shoe still needs to fit: excess movement inside it interferes with foot placement. Laces allow adjustment along the foot but can make removal and replacement more cumbersome than some strap designs.

## Harnesses: standing comfort is only the start

Measure your waist and thighs against the exact model's current chart. Once fitted, raise your legs, squat, and reach for equipment. Ask shop staff for a controlled suspension test using the shop's safe test system. [Petzl's sit-harness instructions](https://www.petzl.com/on/demandware.static/-/Sites-petzl-master-catalog/default/dwddc58faf/media/product/technical-notice-FOCUS-2_068Tx000002Px2AIAS.pdf) likewise include movement and suspension in a safe environment to check comfort and adjustment. Do not improvise an anchor on shop shelving or a doorframe.

During the test, check waistbelt position, pressure around the legs, and balance with gear attached. Add your intended small pack and check access to the necessary gear loops. Padding thickness is only one variable: it cannot compensate for poor load distribution or incompatible proportions.

Gear loops are not general-purpose personal attachment or tie-in points. Read your own model's diagrams to distinguish tie-in points, belay loop, and gear loops. Adjustable legs accommodate layering, but must still be adjusted correctly.

## Helmets: matching circumference is the first check

Adjust the headband and chinstrap as instructed, then look down, look up, and turn your head. Check stability and vision, and repeat with your headlamp, glasses, and intended hood. The [BOREO instructions](https://www.petzl.com/on/demandware.static/-/Sites-petzl-master-catalog/default/dw79a6bf57/media/product/technical-notice-BOREO-2_068Tx000001zLa1IAE.pdf) explicitly require another size or model if you cannot achieve a proper fit.

Neither construction promises continued protection after an impact. A helmet cannot protect against every impact, and a major impact can require retirement even without obvious exterior damage. Protect the shell and liner during travel; do not treat it as a compression-resistant container.

## Turn the itinerary into a purchase decision

In our example, keep existing shoes if they provide stable footwork and acceptable sustained comfort. Spend available money on a properly fitting helmet or necessary harness instead. If shoes hurt immediately, solve the last-and-fit problem first; avoid taking an untested new pair straight onto the trip.

Test the harness with your planned draws, belay device, and small pack. Record pressure points and inaccessible loops. Before buying features for one special day, check whether your current harness can do the job. Include training and route suitability in that decision; new equipment alone does not make the itinerary appropriate.

Check tax, delivery, cross-border restrictions, and return terms before checkout. Buying abroad works best when you can already establish version and fit.

## Exercise: which checks are still missing?

**Your shoes feel fine for five minutes at home. Your harness feels comfortable standing. The helmet matches your head circumference, but the pack stops you looking up. Is the setup ready for multipitch climbing?**

<details>
<summary>Answer and criteria</summary>

Not yet. Test shoes on footholds and over longer wearing periods before departure. The harness still needs a safe, controlled suspension test and a loaded-gear check. The helmet and pack already interfere: adjust the load or change to a compatible, properly fitting combination and repeat the test. Each item must support the intended movements; labels and standing comfort alone do not establish that.

</details>
