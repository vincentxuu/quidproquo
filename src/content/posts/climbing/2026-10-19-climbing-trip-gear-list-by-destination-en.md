Once the flights are booked, the first questions are usually these: is my 60 m rope long enough for Thailand? Do I need 12 quickdraws or 20? Can I even get a crash pad on the plane?

The answers depend on where you are going. The official Kalymnos climbing website says [70 m is the minimum standard](https://climbkalymnos.com/climbing), while the [Mountain Project page](https://www.mountainproject.com/area/106675927/seonunsan) for Seonunsan in Korea says 60 m is enough. In Tonsai a rope rents for 400 baht a day; in Japan, climbing schools and campsites mostly rent out shoes, harnesses and crash pads, not ropes.

This guide is for climbers who already own shoes, a harness and a belay device and are about to fly out of Taiwan with their gear. The first part lists gear by trip type, the middle covers the rope, and the second half goes through six destinations popular with Taiwanese climbers, ending with gear care on the road. By the end you should be able to write a bring / rent / leave-behind list for your own trip. For the decisions the whole series covers, see the [Climbing Travel Gear overview](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-climbing-gear-travel-overview-en); for how to choose each piece of gear, see the Climbing Gear series.

## Three things to check before you go

Work the list backwards from the crag information, not forwards from the gear you happen to own. Check three things:

1. **How long the longest route is.** Open [theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/) or the local guidebook, find the sectors you want to climb, and note the longest single pitch and its bolt count. Twice the route length plus some margin at the rope end is the rope length you need.
2. **What the bolts are made of and what state they are in.** Seaside limestone corrodes stainless steel bolts; Thailand and Kalymnos are both replacing them with titanium. Guidebooks and online databases mark which routes have been rebolted.
3. **What you can rent locally.** Ropes and quickdraws can be rented in Thailand and on Kalymnos. Shoes and harnesses can be rented in Thailand, on Kalymnos and in Japan, but size and fit are a gamble.

With those three answers, use the trip-type lists below.

## Lists for four kinds of trips

Shoes, harness, belay device, locking carabiners and helmet go on every trip, so they are not repeated below. These items have to fit, and a lost one is hard to replace with something that fits. Of the 30 post-2021 examples collected in [How Climbers Pack for Fly-In Trips](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-climbing-travel-packing-examples-en), about 17 kept shoes, harness and helmet in their cabin bag. One Mountain Project user put the reason simply in 2023: if the checked bag goes missing, [you can still usually climb with friends](https://www.mountainproject.com/forum/topic/123687187/travel-bags-to-fly-to-climbing-destination). How to split checked and cabin luggage is covered in [Flying with Climbing Gear](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-flying-with-climbing-gear-en).

### Sport climbing holiday (Thailand, Yangshuo, Kalymnos, Spain)

| Bring | Rent or buy there | Leave behind |
|---|---|---|
| One single rope (length depends on destination), 12–16 quickdraws, rope bag, chalk bag; knot the rope ends | Ropes and quickdraws can be rented in Thailand and on Kalymnos; shops near Siurana in Spain sell 80 m ropes | Cams, nuts, crash pad |

Base the quickdraw count on the bolt count of the longest route. Without a topo, [the official Kalymnos rule of thumb](https://climbkalymnos.com/climbing) is a useful reference:

| Route length | Quickdraws |
|---|---|
| 20–25 m | 10–12 |
| 25–35 m | 15 |
| 35–40 m | 20 |

### Bolted multi-pitch (Tonsai, Kalymnos, some Insubong routes)

Add to the sport list:

- 4–6 long slings to cut rope drag; the official Kalymnos site says the big Grande Grotta routes need [5–6](https://climbkalymnos.com/climbing)
- Locking carabiners for belays and a rappel setup; how to match them is in [Matching Rope, Belay Device and Carabiners](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-climbing-rope-belay-device-en)
- A headlamp and a windproof jacket
- A second rope: only when a rappel is longer than your rope doubled; see the exception section below

Every multi-pitch in Tonsai is a [sport route](https://bigwall.eu/?cat=70), so no trad gear is needed there.

### Trad (Ogawayama, Jogasaki, Insubong)

Add a full rack of cams and nuts to the sport list. On theCrag, the Insubong classic Chouinard A in Korea is listed as needing a [double rack](https://thecrag.com/en/climbing/south-korea/insubong/route/272137737). Neither of the two Tonsai shops lists cams on its rental price list ([Basecamp](https://basecamptonsai.com/climb-on-your-own), [The Rock Shop](https://justclimbthailand.com/climbing-equipment-sale-and-rental)), so assume you must bring your own trad gear.

### Bouldering (Ogawayama, Kasagi, Rocklands)

| Bring | Rent there | Leave behind |
|---|---|---|
| Shoes, chalk bag, brush, tape | Crash pads: at Ogawayama, [Kinpu Sanso](https://mawarime-daira.com/rental/) charges 2,000 yen a day, free for guests on the half-board plan; the [Kasagi Climbing Association](https://www.kasagiclimbing.com/post/rental) rents pads for 1,000 yen a day and shoes for 500 yen, booking required | Rope, quickdraws, harness |

A checked crash pad is charged as oversized baggage or sports equipment. A 2026 Rocklands webinar suggested renting locally if a pad at [about 150 euros a month](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CAVBVDsqTzc) costs less than the extra baggage. How airlines charge for pads is covered in [Flying with Climbing Gear](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-flying-with-climbing-gear-en).

## The rope: one single rope, length set by the destination

Most climbers flying abroad bring one single rope. In a 2021 [UKC thread](https://www.ukclimbing.com/forums/destinations/climbing_gear_on_a_plane-740523), one poster put it plainly: "Rope is the critical weight." It is usually the heaviest item in the bag, and its length directly affects your checked weight.

**Length.** Tonsai, Yangshuo, Kalymnos and Spain all start at 70 m. Kalymnos, Chulilla and Yangshuo have many long lines, so 80 m is more comfortable there. At Seonunsan in Korea and at most Japanese crags 60 m is enough, though at Ogawayama [some spots need a 60 m rope to get down](https://www.akatsuki-climbing.com/%E3%83%AB%E3%83%BC%E3%83%88%E7%B4%B9%E4%BB%8B%E5%B0%8F%E5%B7%9D%E5%B1%B1/). What 60, 70 and 80 m ropes are generally for is covered in [Matching Rope, Belay Device and Carabiners](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-climbing-rope-belay-device-en).

The most direct result of a rope that is too short is the end running through the belay device while lowering. Whatever length you bring, knot the ends.

**Diameter.** Tropical and Mediterranean limestone is often polished smooth and sharp in places. The official Kalymnos site recommends a single rope of [9–10.5 mm](https://climbkalymnos.com/climbing). For two weeks of lowering every day, a durable rope around 9.5 mm is the safer choice. Weights, sheath proportions and 80 m availability for current models are in [Choosing a Rope for a Climbing Trip Abroad](/posts/climbing/2026-10-23-climbing-travel-rope-en); check diameter against your belay device's range in the Climbing Gear article.

**Dry treatment.** Dry treatment mostly matters for ice and alpine climbing. All six destinations here are rock crags, so there is no need to buy a dry rope just for the trip. Wetness is the real concern: [Edelrid](https://edelrid.com/us-en/knowledge/knowledge-base/dangers-for-climbing-ropes) says a wet rope absorbs energy less well, so in humid places dry it out, as described in the last section.

### The exception: when you need a second rope

There is one case: a multi-pitch descent by double-strand rappel where the rappel is longer than your rope doubled. [Petzl](https://petzl.com/US/en/Operators/Multi-pitch-rappelling-with-a-single-rope) is clear that you can rappel on a single rope only when the rappel does not exceed half the rope's length; beyond that you need another rope.

Real examples:

- **Big multi-pitches in Tonsai**: a 2024 trip report recommends [one rope of at least 80 m and another of 60 m](https://bigwall.eu/?cat=70). The classic Humanality, though, can be rappelled on a single 70 m rope; with 60 m you add one short rappel ([theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/thailand/krabi/area/15687961)).
- **3 Stripes on Kalymnos**: to rappel down through the middle of Grande Grotta, the official site says you need [two 80 m ropes](https://climbkalymnos.com/3-stripes-a-nice-easy-new-multipitch-on-kalymnos).
- **Insubong, Korea**: [two 30 m rappels](https://thecrag.com/en/climbing/south-korea/insubong/route/272137737) down Pigeon Road on the south side reach the ground, so one 60 m single rope is enough.

The second rope can be another single rope, a pair of half ropes, or a thin tag line used only for retrieval. A tag line is lighter but only retrieves the rope and cannot be climbed on; half ropes are used as a pair. How to read the rope-end markings is in [Matching Rope, Belay Device and Carabiners](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-climbing-rope-belay-device-en).

## Six destinations

Start with the summary table, then read the details. Rope lengths and quickdraw counts are ranges from the sources; check them against the sectors you plan to climb.

### Railay / Tonsai, Thailand

The Railay peninsula is almost entirely [bolted sport routes](https://www.mountainproject.com/area/105894664/laem-phra-nang-railay-tonsai). Basecamp, a Tonsai climbing shop that also writes a guidebook, said in 2023 that if you bring your own gear, [a 70 m rope and 12 quickdraws will suffice](https://basecamptonsai.substack.com/p/climb-on-your-own-or-get-guided-or). Routes at nearby Ao Nang are longer, and a [2025 Climbing magazine piece](https://www.climbing.com/travel/ao-nang-thailand-climbing-wellness/) suggests 16 quickdraws.

Bolts matter most here. Salt, humidity and seeping limestone make stainless steel bolts fail within a few years, and the area has gone through [large-scale titanium rebolting](https://climbing.com/community/why-do-seaside-crags-around-the-world-still-have-so-many-bad-bolts). theCrag still has old stainless routes marked "Do not climb!", so buy a current guidebook before you go: the 2025 Basecamp guide [labels every route by bolt status](https://basecamptonsai.com/climb-on-your-own).

**Rent or bring.** Basecamp's daily rental prices (checked 2026-10-19; passport as deposit):

- Rope (60 or 70 m): 400 baht
- 12 quickdraws: 400 baht
- Full set for two: 1,800 baht

A couple who went in late 2023 wrote that they [regretted not bringing their own rope](https://mountaindiaries.com/posts/climbing-in-thailand-laos/); rope rental in Tonsai and Railay was about 10 euros a day. For a stay of a week or more, bringing your own rope usually works out cheaper. Basecamp also warns that [climbing shoes are hard to buy in Krabi](https://basecamptonsai.substack.com/p/climb-on-your-own-or-get-guided-or), so bring your own.

**Climate.** The dry season runs from [November to early March](https://thecragjournal.com/2023/02/01/climbing-in-thailand-a-journey-through-south-east-asia/), May to October is the rainy season, and it is hot all year. On rainy days, theCrag lists the roof at Tonsai Wall as [dry in the rain](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/thailand/krabi/area/15687961).

### Yangshuo, China

Karst limestone, mostly sport climbing. A writer who spent a month climbing in Yangshuo [found that](https://climbgrades.com/blog/yangshuo-rock-climbing) most routes are within 32 m, with Xin Jiang Black the longest at 45 m. So 70 m is the practical minimum, 80 m is more comfortable, and 16 quickdraws is the count to bring.

Thirty years of traffic have polished the popular lines. The rock is also loose in places, so wear a helmet.

**Bolts.** The same writer notes occasional rusty or spinning bolts, most often in caves. On theCrag, someone reported in February 2024 that the [third-to-last bolt on the classic Monkey Fur had broken](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/china/yangshuo/banyan-tree/route/130414185); it has since been fixed. The old bolts were left in place when the route was rebolted, so clip the new ones.

**Rent or bring.** Gear rental and route advice run through the climbing hostels, such as the long-established Climbers Inn; [do not count on buying replacement gear in town beyond the basics](https://climbgrades.com/blog/yangshuo-rock-climbing). The only Yangshuo rental prices available come from a hotel blog that does not say whether a rope is included, so plan to bring your own rope and quickdraws.

**Climate.** October and December are best, and November is good too; Golden Week (1–7 October) is the busiest. Summer is hot and humid; on rainy days go to Bamboo Cave or White Mountain.

### Ogawayama and Jogasaki, Japan

This site's [Japan Climbing Crags](/posts/climbing/2026-10-12-japan-crags-overview-en) series covers each crag; this section keeps only what affects your gear.

**Ogawayama** is a granite valley at about 1,600 m, with [plenty of sport routes, cracks and boulders](/posts/climbing/2026-10-12-japan-crag-ogawayama-en), including nearly two hundred crack lines. mont-bell's gear guide says [50 or 60 m](https://www.montbell.jp/generalpage/disp.php?id=711) ropes are standard in Japan, but at Ogawayama some spots need 60 m to get down. There is also a 70 m crack, [The Last Resort](https://kyokarakimiwa.com/the-last-resort-ogawa/), that needs a rope of 70 m or more to climb in one pitch. Ogawayama gets cold from mid-October, so count warm clothes in your luggage.

**Jogasaki** sits on the coast of the Izu Peninsula; of the 494 routes listed on ROCKTOPO, [255 are cracks](/posts/climbing/2026-10-12-japan-crag-jogasaki-en), so bring a full rack. The bolts are exposed to sea salt, and CLIMBING-net warns never to trust everything to a single bolt; Mountain Project says [many routes are now TR only and new bolting is prohibited](https://www.mountainproject.com/area/107001454/jogasaki). The south-facing walls are best in winter. In Okinawa, some routes at Cape Hedo [have been rebolted with titanium](/posts/climbing/2026-10-12-japan-crag-hedo-usahama-en).

**Rent or bring.** Rental in Japan centres on shoes, harnesses, helmets and crash pads; the outdoor courses of the NPO [Monkey Magic](https://www.monkeymagic.or.jp/school/rental), for example, rent only shoes, harnesses and helmets. Bring your own rope or buy one there: Kawakami village, where Ogawayama is, has the climbing shop [ROOF ROCK](https://roofrock.jp/english/), and [Kamoshika Sports](https://kamoshika.co.jp/honten) in Takadanobaba, Tokyo, has a deep climbing range.

### Seonunsan and Insubong, Korea

**Seonunsan**, in Gochang, North Jeolla, is a limestone sport area. Mountain Project recommends a [60 m rope and 10–15 quickdraws](https://www.mountainproject.com/area/106675927/seonunsan), and you can lower straight off the anchors; many routes from 5.12a up have fixed draws. Nearby Halmae-bawi has seen [a lot of rebolting with glue-in steel bolts](https://moredaysoff.wordpress.com/2020/07/01/the-south-korean-sport-climbing-of-seonusan-best-area-korea-and-halmae-bawi/), with 16 quickdraws recommended.

**Insubong**, in Bukhansan National Park in Seoul, is a granite dome over 200 m high, mostly slab and crack climbing with sparse bolts on the slabs. A Ukrainian climber who went in mid-November wrote that [all the multi-pitches have bolted anchors](https://alp.org.ua/en/archives/98218) and a 60 m single rope was enough for every rappel. In spring the national park suspends climbing for fire prevention and the thaw; the Seoul Mountaineering Federation's [2026 notice](https://saf.or.kr/forum_01/11188) listed Insubong among the closed walls, so check before a spring trip.

**Rent or bring.** The Seoul hiking tourism centre at Bukhansan rents [hiking boots, hiking clothes, poles and backpacks](https://korean.visitseoul.net/seoul-hiking/seoul-hiking-tourism-center-bukhansan/KON50nn0f), not climbing gear. Bring your full rope and rack.

**Climate.** Spring and autumn are best; summer is climbable but humid, like Southeast Asia.

### Kalymnos, Greece

A sport-only destination with no bouldering or trad, and routes averaging about 30 m. The official site, [climbkalymnos.com](https://climbkalymnos.com/climbing), sets the minimum kit at 15 quickdraws and a 70 m rope. Many 40–45 m routes have been opened in recent years, so bringing an 80 m rope is "always a good idea". The site also recommends 30–40 quickdraws for the trip, since a single extra-long pitch can take more than twenty. A calculation based on theCrag data finds that [a 70 m rope lowers off 80% of single pitches and an 80 m rope 90%](https://gearloop.app/crag/kalymnos/).

**Bolts.** After a fatal accident in March 2026 caused by ageing hardware, the official standard became certified titanium glue-in bolts. The volunteer group [Rebolt Kalymnos](https://reboltkalymnos.org/faq) prioritises routes with 304 stainless steel or unknown hardware, and [383 routes had been fully rebolted by September 2026](https://climbing.place/news/kalymnos-383-routes-rebolted-with-titanium-check-bolt-beta-before-you-climb-180). Sea air can crack 304 stainless from the inside with no visible sign, so check the Bolt Beta database before you go and favour sectors marked green.

**Rent or bring.** The main street in Masouri has six climbing shops; shoes rent for about 5 euros a day, and [ropes, quickdraws, belay devices, helmets and harnesses can all be rented](https://kalymnosclimbingadventure.com/post/kalymnos-hints-climbing-trip). A Finnish couple in 2024 brought [one 80 m and one 60 m rope](https://www.tuomasheinonen.com/blog/kalymnos-a-paradise-for-climbers-what-i-learned-on-my-first-visit-to-the-island/), checking just under 20 kg each; they found gear on the island pricier than in Finland, but anything broken or lost could be replaced there.

**Climate.** Mid-October to late November is usually best, with October the peak; spring is a close second, and in summer you climb only in the shade.

### Chulilla and Siurana, Spain

**Chulilla**, inland from Valencia, is a limestone canyon cut by the Turia river, with an [average route length over 30 m](https://climb-europe.com/products/chulilla-sport-climbing-guidebook). UKC's Chulilla North page lists routes up to 55 m and says [an 80 m rope is highly recommended](https://ukclimbing.com/logbook/crags/chulilla_north__pantano-30666). Based on theCrag data, [a 70 m rope lowers off only 58% of single pitches](https://gearloop.app/crag/chulilla/), and you want 18–21 quickdraws. Routes marked "(L1+L2)" are climbed from the ground to the second anchor in one push, so size your rope for the combined length.

**Siurana**, in Catalonia, is known for vertical, fingery routes on small holds, and needs less rope. The same calculation shows [a 70 m rope lowers off 90% of pitches and 15 quickdraws cover 90%](https://gearloop.app/crag/siurana/); only El Pati and three other sectors have 35–40 m pitches. Many older routes have been rebolted, but you will [occasionally find bolts from the 1980s and 1990s](https://climbingsiurana.com/en/information/siurana-general-information).

**Rent or bring.** Al Coxinillo, on Chulilla's main square, [sells and rents basic gear](https://valenciaclimb.com/climbing-areas/chulilla). In Cornudella, below Siurana, the specialist shop Goma 2 lists an [80 m, 9.6 mm single rope at 171.89 euros](https://goma2.net/en/cordes-simples/1360-siurana-s-rope-96-mm-x-80m-roca-orange.html) on its website (checked 2026-10-19). Most climbers in Taiwan own a 60 or 70 m rope; if you are heading to Chulilla and want to avoid extra baggage fees, consider buying an 80 m rope on arrival. A couple who moved to Europe for a year of climbing in 2021 did exactly that, [buying ropes once in Europe](https://helloholanihao.com/2021/09/21/packing-for-a-year/) to save luggage space.

**Climate.** Both areas climb best from autumn through spring, and summers are hot. Chulilla's winters are mild, and it is busiest over Christmas and Easter.

## Rope and hardware care on the road

On a climbing trip, care comes down to three problems: salt by the sea, humidity in the tropics, and no good place to dry gear in your room.

**Salt.** Railay, Jogasaki and Kalymnos are all on the coast. Black Diamond's [dynamic rope instructions](https://cdn.accentuate.io/7340393955491/1639550969347/Dynamic-Ropes-Instructions---M12493_E_Dynamic-Rope_IS-WEB.pdf?v=0) say to rinse with clean tap water and air-dry after contact with saltwater or salt air, and its [belay device instructions](https://blackdiamond-web.cdn.prismic.io/blackdiamond-web/36995969-6a90-4717-b988-86bf486bd2f2_M12534_C_ATC-Pilot_IS-WEB.pdf) say to rinse, dry and lubricate metal products. In practice: after a day at a sea cliff, rinse the rope, quickdraws and belay device in the shower, work the gates a few times, and once dry put a drop of lubricant on the springs.

**Humidity.** [Petzl](https://petzl.com/us/en/sport/tech-tips-by-product/product-information-and-specifications/how-to-protect-the-rope-/CORDES-precautions_SPO_a77w0000000KyvgAAC.html) says never store a rope wet; dry it in a ventilated place first, and do not leave it in a hot car. The rainy seasons in Thailand and Yangshuo are very humid, so take the rope out of its bag and spread it out each evening.

**Drying.** [Edelrid](https://edelrid.com/int-en/knowledge/knowledge-base/looking-after-your-climbing-rope) recommends spreading the rope on the ground somewhere cool and dark, not hanging it and not leaving it in the sun; [Mammut](https://mammut.com/us/en/support/product-support/care-instructions/ropes) says never tumble-dry it or put it on a heater. In a hotel, lay the rope loosely on the floor or in a shaded corner of the balcony and run a fan.

Back home, inspect any rope that took a lot of falls, was used by the sea or was dragged over sharp rock, following [Inspecting, Caring For and Retiring Climbing Gear](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-climbing-gear-inspection-retirement-en), and retire it if needed.

## Write your own list

1. Open theCrag or the guidebook, find the longest route on your trip, and double its length to set the rope length.
2. Count that route's bolts to set the quickdraw count; if you will stay at one crag for a while, bring a few extra to leave on a project.
3. Look up the local shops' rental prices and compare "days of rope rental × daily rate" with "the baggage fee for bringing your own rope".
4. Put shoes, harness, belay device and helmet on the cabin list, and everything else on the checked list.

If you are buying or replacing a rope for the trip, the next article, [Choosing a Rope for a Climbing Trip Abroad](/posts/climbing/2026-10-23-climbing-travel-rope-en), compares weight per meter, 80 m availability, sheath proportion and middle marks. Once the list is written and the total weight known, the next step is getting it through the airport: [Flying with Climbing Gear](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-flying-with-climbing-gear-en).
