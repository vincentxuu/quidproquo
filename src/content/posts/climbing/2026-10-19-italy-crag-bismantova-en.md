Pietra di Bismantova sits in the Apennine hills of Reggio Emilia province, Emilia-Romagna, next to the town of Castelnovo ne' Monti. It is an isolated flat-topped rock: [Montagna.TV](https://www.montagna.tv/225832/bismantova-la-lunga-storia-di-una-pietra/) gives it a length of 1 km, a width of 240 m and a height of 300 m above the plateau below, and Dante mentions it in the Purgatorio. The same article calls it "la più grande palestra di roccia dell'Emilia Romagna".

This post helps you decide whether sandstone sport climbing suits you, which months to go, how to get there from Reggio Emilia without a car, and which national park rules apply. For seasons and regions across Italy, see the [series overview](/posts/climbing/2026-10-18-italy-crags-overview-en).

## What kind of crag is it

**Rock.** Montagna.TV describes it as calcarenite sitting on marls, laid down in a shallow sea about 15 million years ago. [falesia.it](https://www.falesia.it/crag/109/la-pietra-di-bismantova.html) calls it "arenaria compatta, sovente levigata dall'acqua" (compact sandstone, often polished by water), with climbing that is mostly about balance and technique. [theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/la-pietra-di-bismantova/vecchie-gare) describes the Gare Vecchie sector as "Smeary climbing on slopers".

**Size.** The numbers each source gives (checked October 2026):

| Source | Number |
|---|---|
| [theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/la-pietra-di-bismantova) | 475 routes ("Mostly Sport climbing") |
| falesia.it (updated 4 September 2026) | 30 sectors, 452 routes, 2a to 8c |
| Montagna.TV | "oltre 300" (over 300) equipped routes |

theCrag calls it "The biggest and most iconic crag of the region"; falesia.it calls it "la più interessante e completa palestra di roccia" of Emilia-Romagna.

**Style.** Mostly bolted single pitches of about 20 m, spread over around thirty sectors on the boulders and walls around the rock. There are also 100–160 m short multi-pitches of 4–5 pitches that climb to the summit plateau, and a few A2 to A4 aid routes. Two via ferratas run up the rock: the Ferrata degli Alpini (1971) and the Ferrata Ovest (2017) (Montagna.TV).

**History.** From Montagna.TV and local climber [Alex Stecchezzini](https://www.alexstecchezzini.it/vie-lunghe-pietra-di-bismantova-raccolta/): in 1922 Voltolini soloed the first route, the Via degli Svizzeri (III+); aid climbing arrived in the 1960s; in the mid-1970s Levati reached the seventh grade on the Via del Centenario. Sport climbing took off in the 1980s, many old routes were rebolted, and some holds were chipped, which caused controversy at the time.

**Who it suits.** Climbers looking for sport climbing within an hour's drive of Parma, Reggio Emilia or Modena, and anyone wanting to practise changeovers on 100-odd-metre multi-pitches without going into the mountains. If you are used to small limestone edges, give yourself time to adapt to sandstone friction and rounded holds.

## When to go

**All year.** falesia.it says it is "frequentabile 12 mesi all'anno", "seguendo o evitando il sole" (following or avoiding the sun). The main aspect is east, but there are walls facing every direction; after 2 p.m. the north and north-east faces go into shade while the south-west faces still get sun. The crag is at about 930 m (falesia.it).

| You want | Go to | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Sun on a cold day | South-west faces; Gare Vecchie faces south-east and gets afternoon sun | falesia.it, theCrag |
| Shade on a hot day | North and north-east faces in the afternoon | falesia.it |

**Don't climb after rain.** theCrag marks it "Brittle when wet" and "Wet in rain". Sandstone weakens when it soaks up water, so wait until the rock has dried out.

**Crowds.** theCrag says the Piazzale Dante car park "gets full in the early morning", and falesia.it warns that weekends bring plenty of climbers to the base.

## Getting there

| | Public transport | Driving |
|---|---|---|
| To Castelnovo ne' Monti | SETA line 44 from Reggio Emilia | From Reggio Emilia on the SS63, about 45 km; from Parma on the SS513, about 60 km; from Modena on the SS486, about 64 km |
| To the foot of the rock | Walk about 3 km from town to Piazzale Dante | Drive to the Piazzale Dante car park (872 m) |
| To the sectors | 5–15 minutes from above the hut | Same |

### Public transport

From Reggio Emilia, take [SETA](https://www.setaweb.it/re/lineedyn-linea/306/Linea-44) line 44 (Reggio Emilia–Castelnovo Monti–Passo del Cerreto). Its timetable valid from 15 September 2026 has morning departures from the CIM stop in Reggio at 06:05, 07:11, 08:15, 09:45 and 10:30. From Castelnovo ne' Monti, Montagna.TV says it is about 3 km following the signs for the Pietra to Piazzale Dante. Timetables are reissued each period, so check SETA's current version before you go.

### Driving and parking

Road approaches are from falesia.it. The car park is Piazzale Dante (872 m, per Montagna.TV); theCrag says it fills early in the morning and that you can also park along the road. From the car park, take the steps and the paved road up to the hermitage (Eremo), where the paths start (falesia.it).

### Approach

falesia.it says every sector is 5 to 15 minutes from the hut above the steps, and the farthest sector in the woods below is 10 minutes. theCrag also gives 5–10 minutes.

**Food and lodging.** theCrag lists Rifugio La Pietra di Bismantova and Foresteria San Benedetto; falesia.it also mentions a guesthouse where you can sleep and a bar-restaurant.

## Rules to know before you go

**National park rules (secondary sources).** The rock is a Special Area of Conservation (ZSC) managed by the Appennino Tosco-Emiliano National Park. [Montagna.TV](https://www.montagna.tv/179047/pietra-di-bismantova-vietate-bike-e-sport-estremi-in-cima-solo-a-piedi/) reported in 2021 that the park banned extreme sports on the summit and access by mountain bike and e-bike, after BASE jumping (with a death in 2018) and e-bikes carving tracks into the grass. [Gazzetta di Reggio](https://www.gazzettadireggio.it/reggio/cronaca/2025/05/12/news/pietra-di-bismantova-scattano-i-controlli-dei-carabinieri-del-parco-nazionale-stop-a-guide-abusive-e-sport-estremi-1.100704840) reported in 2025 that the park's Carabinieri stepped up patrols enforcing park decision no. 10/2021. We did not find the park's original notice; check the park website before you go. Climbing itself is not banned.

**Guiding needs a licence.** The same Gazzetta di Reggio report says that under Italian Law 6/1989, leading people on the rock routes of the Pietra is reserved for registered mountain guides, and the Carabinieri check this too. Climbing with your own friends is not affected.

**Peregrine falcons and rockfall.** Montagna.TV says CAI path 697 runs under the overhanging walls where "nidifica il falco pellegrino" (the peregrine falcon nests), and that the path is exposed to rockfall. We found no breeding-season closure order for the walls. Watch for signs on site, and move to another sector if you see raptors active on the wall.

**Bolts and lower-off carabiners.** falesia.it says most routes have been rebolted with glue-in rings (ferle) and stainless chains, with some Fisher expansion bolts still in good condition, but warns "FATE ATTENZIONE AI MOSCHETTONI DI CALATA, potrebbero essere USURATI": the lower-off carabiners may be worn. Bring your own carabiners for top-roping instead of running the rope through worn ones.

**Loose blocks on the top edge.** falesia.it also notes unstable stones along the upper edges, so don't set up top ropes from above at weekends when people are climbing below.

**Soft rock on aid routes.** falesia.it's Danger in Trip page says "La roccia é la TENERA!! arenaria" (the sandstone is soft); aid routes need a full aid rack (hooks, tricams, wooden wedges and more).

**Rescue.** [CNSAS](https://www.cnsas.it/en/distress-call/), the Italian mountain rescue service, says to call 112 or the local rescue number.

## Routes by level

YDS and V-grade conversions follow the [Mountain Project international grade comparison chart](https://www.mountainproject.com/international-climbing-grades) and are approximate; "approx." marks grades the sources give without a/b/c (such as 4 or 5+). The original grade is the one that counts.

Single-pitch grades below are cross-checked between two databases: [thetopo](https://thetopo.com/crags/bismantova/routelist) (the same database as 27crags) and Climbook ([Gare vecchie sector](https://climbook.com/falesie/16331/pietra-di-bismantova-gare-vecchie-giorgione/vie), [Anfiteatro/Banana/Pilone giallo sectors](https://climbook.com/falesie/16341/pietra-di-bismantova-anfiteatro-banana-marchi-pilone-giallo/vie)), with both listed where they differ. "Ascents" are logged ascents on the database; lengths come from theCrag or montagneterne (a personal website).

### Beginner: up to 6a (YDS 5.10a and below)

The lower part of the Banana/Nino Marchi sector has a string of grade-5 routes, and Gare Vecchie is a row of 20 m slabs around 6a. theCrag says Gare Vecchie also has a "baby" area with short, closely bolted routes.

## Where to look things up

- **[falesia.it: La Pietra di Bismantova](https://www.falesia.it/crag/109/la-pietra-di-bismantova.html)**: route lists for 30 sectors, aspects, bolt condition, parking and approach (in Italian).
- **[theCrag: Pietra di Bismantova](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/la-pietra-di-bismantova)**: sector list in English, parking coordinates, via ferratas.
- **[thetopo (27crags)](https://thetopo.com/crags/bismantova/routelist)**: route lists per sector; most topos are paid.
- **Climbook**: Italian climbers' logged ascents and grade opinions ([Gare vecchie sector](https://climbook.com/falesie/16331/pietra-di-bismantova-gare-vecchie-giorgione/vie), [Banana/Pilone giallo sectors](https://climbook.com/falesie/16341/pietra-di-bismantova-anfiteatro-banana-marchi-pilone-giallo/vie)).
- **Guidebook:** Grades and route counts here come mainly from online databases; the guidebook is the authority.
  - [*Pietra di Bismantova – Vie e falesie*](https://www.versantesud.it/en/shop/catalogue/pietra-di-bismantova_1387) (Diego Filippi, Matteo Bertolotti, Versante Sud, 2nd edition 2021, Italian, ISBN 9788855470346): multi-pitch routes and single-pitch crags, with drone photos added in the new edition to help find the sectors. The publisher's page showed it available in October 2026.
- **[SETA line 44](https://www.setaweb.it/re/lineedyn-linea/306/Linea-44)**: current Reggio Emilia–Castelnovo ne' Monti timetable.
