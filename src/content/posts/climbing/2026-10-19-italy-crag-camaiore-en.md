Camaiore is a small town on the northern Tuscan coast, between Viareggio and Lucca. The valley north of town is lined with limestone sport crags, with the marble peaks of the Alpi Apuane behind. [Climb Europe](https://www.climb-europe.com/pages/rock-climbing-italy-tuscany) calls it "without doubt the best sport climbing area in Tuscany".

This guide centres on Candalla, the main crag in the valley, and adds one section on the best-known big wall of the Alpi Apuane: the north face of Pizzo d'Uccello. It should help you decide whether the area suits your level, which months to go, how to get there without a car, and what to watch out for. For a seasonal and regional comparison of Italian crags, see the [series overview](/posts/climbing/2026-10-18-italy-crags-overview-en); elsewhere in Tuscany are [Elba](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-elba-en) and [Capo d'Uomo on Monte Argentario](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-capo-duomo-en).

## What kind of crag is it

### The Camaiore valley

**Steep tufa.** Climb Europe says the valley has 25 limestone crags known for tufas and overhanging roofs, with routes mostly from 6b to 8c. The local association Versante Apuano describes the Candalla walls as old marble that water has slowly "calcarizzato", covering it in concretions and vertical columns, so that on some routes you feel you are climbing a cave wall.

**Upper and lower Candalla.** [falesiaonline](https://www.falesiaonline.it/falesia/candalla-alta.html) says Candalla has the most bolted pitches in the valley. The lower belt sits by the stream path at the valley floor; the upper belt is reached by an uphill path from the parking. Candalla Alta is "da molti considerato il più bel settore della Toscana" (considered by many the finest sector in Tuscany): a long line of steep, organ-pipe walls, with routes often 30–40 m long. Placche a mare, a small sector on the right of the approach path, suits climbers below 7a better.

**San Rocchino.** Above the village of Casoli. [Versante Apuano](http://www.versanteapuano.it/casoli-alto/159-san-rocchino.html) describes it as "Arrampicata atletica su strapiombi" (athletic climbing on overhangs). [Planetmountain](https://www.planetmountain.com/en/news/climbing/sport-climbing-not-only-adam-ondra.html) reported that in 2017 Adam Ondra made the first ascents of Naturalmente 9a+ and La Terza Età 9a here, calling Naturalmente "one of the best routes I can imagine".

**Who it suits.** Sport climbers who want long routes from 7a up and like pinching tufas. Below 7a the choice is narrower: Placche a mare and a handful of lines at the lower sector.

### The north face of Pizzo d'Uccello

**Tuscany's big wall.** Pizzo d'Uccello (1,781 m) is in the northern Alpi Apuane. The [Italian Wikipedia](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pizzo_d%27Uccello) gives the north face as "alta quasi 800 m di dislivello"; the guiding company [WB Guides](https://wbguides.com/salire-la-nord-del-pizzo-duccello/) gives 660 m of height and 850 m of climbing. Climb Europe also mentions Marathon, about 1,000 m in 24 pitches.

**The classic: Oppio-Colnaghi.** First climbed in 1940 by Nino Oppio and Serafino Colnaghi; Wikipedia calls it the most repeated route in the Alpi Apuane. WB Guides gives 18–20 pitches, mostly grade IV and V in chimneys and corners, with crux moves of V+, 7–9 hours of climbing and 10–12 hours car to car. The [REDclimber](https://www.redclimber.it/relazione/arrampicata/arrampicata-alpi-apuane/pizzo-d-uccello-oppio-colnaghi/) topo says protection is traditional pitons, close on the hard sections and sparse on the easy ones, and that you need cams.

**Who it suits.** WB Guides recommends being able to climb at least 5c at the crag, having done mountain multi-pitch routes of 8–10 pitches, and being able to abseil on your own. Camaiore is sport climbing; the north face is a long alpine-style trad route, and they suit different people.

**Rain.** Versante Apuano marks both upper and lower Candalla as climbable in the rain ("In caso di pioggia: Si").

**Temperature.** Planetmountain's 2021 report says the second and third ascents of Naturalmente both came after a sudden autumn cold snap, one of them thanks to a "freezing wind". Save the hard lines for cool days; on the north face, the rock is cold and damp in spring and autumn, so bring layers.

## Getting there

| | Public transport | Driving |
|---|---|---|
| Start | Viareggio or Lucca railway station (Camaiore has no station) | About 20 minutes from Pisa airport to Camaiore (Mapo Tapo) |
| Middle | Autolinee Toscane bus E2 to Camaiore P.za Romboni | In town, follow signs for Vado, Lombrici, Casoli uphill |
| Last stretch | Bus E33 to around Lombrici, then walk to the mill | Park on the road to the mill |

### Public transport

[Autolinee Toscane](https://www.at-bus.it/it/linee-e-orari/lucca-extraurbano-e2) line E2 links Camaiore, Viareggio and Lucca. According to the [timetable valid from 5 October 2026](https://rd-sftp-data.s3.eu-west-1.amazonaws.com/autolineeToscane/IVU/OUTPUT/TIMETABLES_WINTER/EXLU/E2.pdf), Camaiore P.za Romboni to central Viareggio takes about 30 minutes, roughly hourly on weekdays; the timetable differs between school terms and holidays, so check before you travel.

The bus up to Candalla is [line E33 (Casoli–Camaiore)](https://www.at-bus.it/it/linee-e-orari/lucca-extraurbano-e33), stopping at Vado, Lombrici and Casoli. The Autolinee Toscane page announces that the line passed to a new operator from 11 June 2026, and when checked in October 2026 E33 no longer appeared in its winter timetables. Before you go, ask the Comune di Camaiore or the tourist office who runs it now and where to find the times.

In summer there has also been a free council shuttle: [Toscana Today reported in 2024](https://www.toscanatoday.it/camaiore-navetta-camaiore-candalla-casoli-trescolli/) that it ran Friday to Sunday between Camaiore, Candalla (Emilio e Bona restaurant), Casoli and Trescolli, every 50–60 minutes, until 1 September. Ask whether it ran again in 2025 and 2026.

Pizzo d'Uccello is in the mountains above Minucciano, with no practical public transport; you need a car.

### Driving to Candalla

[Versante Apuano](http://www.versanteapuano.it/valle-del-lombricese/117-candalla-bassa.html): from Camaiore follow signs for Vado, Lombrici and Casoli; at the Emilio e Bona restaurant carry straight on and park on the road to the mill. There is drinking water at the mill.

- **Lower sector**: cross the footbridge and follow the valley path for about 10 minutes; when the big wall appears on your right, drop to the river, cross it and climb up to the sectors.
- **Upper sector**: before the mill take the dirt road on the right, pass some small houses and climb through the woods; after 30–35 minutes you reach Placche a mare, then in order Bao, Pancione Bianco, Le Villette and Penitenziario.

### Driving to San Rocchino

Versante Apuano: drive through Casoli toward Trescolli, where a private road begins; park before the barrier and take the path uphill.

### Driving to Pizzo d'Uccello

[Rifugio Orto di Donna](https://www.rifugioortodidonna.it/) gives the route: take the SS 445 (Lucca–Piazza al Serchio) to Minucciano, turn off for Val Serenaia before the tunnel, and park near Rifugio Val Serenaia at the hairpin; the tarmac ends at Rifugio Donegani. WB Guides says that from Donegani you follow CAI path 187 and then descend a via ferrata to the foot of the wall, about 1.5–2 hours; with two cars you can drop one at the Ugliancaldo quarries to shorten the approach.

## Rules to know before you go

**Parking at Candalla.** Versante Apuano asks climbers to respect the no-parking signs and the people who live here, and to leave room for the bus to turn.

**San Rocchino is reached by a private road.** Park before the barrier and do not drive in (Versante Apuano).

**Rockfall.** [LuccaInDiretta](https://www.luccaindiretta.it/cronaca/2025/08/27/alpinista-colpita-da-una-scarica-di-sassi-durante-la-salita-nella-falesia-di-candalla/473442) reported that on 27 August 2025 a climber was hit by falling rocks on a route at Candalla and was flown to hospital by the Querceta mountain rescue station and helicopter. Wear a helmet, and belayers should stand clear.

**Bolts.** Versante Apuano says Candalla's lower sector has been fully rebolted and cleaned by the local association Magicandalla. At another crag, Calamijeans, the last routes on the left still have old bolts; Versante Apuano says "da evitare" (avoid them).

**Pizzo d'Uccello sits between a park and the quarries.** 88% of the Alpi Apuane regional park is a Special Protection Area for birds (ZPS), and the [CAI Tuscany dossier](https://organizzazione.cai.it/gr-toscana/wp-content/uploads/sites/66/2025/05/CRTAM_dossier-Apuane_042023.pdf) notes that the Oppio-Colnaghi can be reached by quarry roads and by crossing a quarry. Quarries are work sites: stay on CAI paths and keep out of the working areas. The park website lists no climbing restriction for the north face; the nearby Solco d'Equi wall was closed for years for nesting golden eagles and in 2024 changed to opening for part of each year (secondary: reader comments on [GognaBlog](https://gognablog.sherpa-gate.com/arrampicata-e-rapaci-2/); the original order was not found).

**Rescue.** The [Tuscan mountain and cave rescue service (SAST)](https://www.sast.it/richiedi-aiuto/) says to call 112 in the mountains and ask explicitly for mountain rescue (CNSAS); say where you are and which phone you are using, and keep the line free afterwards so the centre can call back.

## Routes by level

YDS and V-grade conversions follow the [Mountain Project international grade comparison chart](https://www.mountainproject.com/international-climbing-grades) and are approximate; "approx." marks grades the sources give without a/b/c (such as 4 or 5+). The original grade is the one that counts.

Many Candalla routes have two pitches (L1, L2): the first ends at a lower-off, and the extension above is usually harder. The tables give the first pitch; extensions are in the notes. [Mapo Tapo](https://www.mapotapo.com/blog/italy-camaiore) says Candalla runs from 5+ to 8c, mostly in the 7th grade.

### Trad and multi-pitch (UIAA grades)

The Pizzo d'Uccello classics use UIAA grades and are protected mainly by pitons; bring cams and slings. The usual descent is the normal route along the east ridge to Foce di Giovo and back to Donegani.

| Route | Original grade | YDS | Length / pitches | Wall | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Via dei Genovesi | IV+ | 5.5 | 700 m | North face, left | First climbed 1922, the first route on the north face; popular as a winter mixed route (Wikipedia) |
| Oppio-Colnaghi | V+ | 5.7 | 850 m, 18–20 pitches | North face, centre | First climbed 1940; WB Guides, REDclimber; a 10–12 hour day |
| Diretta dei Pisani | V+ | 5.7 | 660 m | North face, right of centre | 1965; continuous V and V+, harder than the Oppio-Colnaghi (Wikipedia) |
| Gran Diedro Nord | VI | 5.9 | multi-pitch | North face, right | 1969; grades V and VI (Wikipedia) |
| Diedro Sud | V | 5.6 | 200 m | South face | Often linked with Via Tiziana for 400 m, max V+; WB Guides suggests it as preparation for the north face |

## Where to look things up

- **[Versante Apuano](http://www.versanteapuano.it/)**: the local association's crag pages, with topos, approaches, seasons and rebolting news for each sector.
- **[theCrag: Camaiore](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/central-and-southern-italy/lucca-area/area/9466016841)**: route counts and logged ascents across the valley.
- **[Climbook: Placche a mare](https://climbook.com/falesie/212-candalla-alta-placche-a-mare/vie)**: repeat counts, to see which lines are most climbed.
- **[falesiaonline: Candalla](https://www.falesiaonline.it/falesia/candalla-alta.html)**: what each sector is like, plus users' recent ascents.
- **[Autolinee Toscane lines and timetables](https://www.at-bus.it/it/linee-e-orari/lucca-extraurbano-e2)**: stops and notices for E2 and E33.
- **[SAST: requesting help](https://www.sast.it/richiedi-aiuto/)**: what to say when calling for rescue in the mountains.
- **Guidebooks**: Grades and route counts here come mainly from online databases; the guidebook is the authority.
  - [*Toscana e Isola d'Elba*](https://www.versantesud.it/it/negozio/catalogue/toscana-isola-elba_1650/) (Raffaele Giannetti, Versante Sud, new edition 2025, Italian, 672 pp, ISBN 978-88-55471-24-4): 116 crags across Tuscany from the Alpi Apuane to Argentario, including Camaiore; the new edition adds 50 sectors.
  - [*Arrampicare in Toscana: Camaiore e Sant'Anna di Stazzema*](https://www.falesiaonline.it/guida/24/arrampicare-in-toscana-2015.html) (Lorenzo Abbarchi, Toni Lonobile, Roberto Vigiani, Toscoclimbstore Editing, first edition May 2015, Italian and English, 170 pp): the dedicated Camaiore guide by the local bolters; [one review](https://ptithotel.com/arrampicare-a-camaiore/) gives 785 routes. There has been no new edition since 2015; for newer routes use Versante Apuano.
  - [*Alpi Apuane Multipitch*](https://www.versantesud.it/it/negozio/catalogue/italiano_5240/) (Gregorio Pedrini, Versante Sud, 2026, Italian, 480 pp, ISBN 978-88-55472-77-7): multi-pitch and big walls of the Alpi Apuane, with Pizzo d'Uccello as the first chapter; the [GognaBlog review](https://gognablog.sherpa-gate.com/alpi-apuane-multipitch/) says it covers 345 routes and came out in June 2026.
  - [*Toscana Pareti*](https://www.versantesud.it/it/negozio/catalogue/toscana_1379/) (Raffaele Giannetti, Versante Sud, 2021, Italian, also in English and German, ISBN 978-88-55470-37-7): 221 multi-pitch routes in Tuscany, including the Pizzo d'Uccello north face; the publisher's page shows it as "Non disponibile" (out of stock).
