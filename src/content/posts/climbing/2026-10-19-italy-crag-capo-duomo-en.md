Capo d'Uomo is on the Monte Argentario promontory at the southern tip of Tuscany, between the harbours of Porto Santo Stefano and Porto Ercole. The wall is a triangle of limestone rising out of the sea, topped by the ruins of a medieval watchtower. [Planetmountain](https://www.planetmountain.com/it/falesie/capo-d-uomo.html) calls it "una delle più belle falesie affacciate sul mare d'Italia" (one of the most beautiful sea-view crags in Italy).

This guide covers what to decide before you go: whether the grades suit you, which months to go, how to get there without a car, and what to watch for on the walk in. For a seasonal and regional comparison of Italian crags, see the [series overview](/posts/climbing/2026-10-18-italy-crags-overview-en); elsewhere in Tuscany are [Camaiore](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-camaiore-en) and [Elba](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-elba-en).

## What kind of crag is it

**Technical vertical limestone.** Planetmountain describes a vertical slab of grey-white limestone with drip pockets, holes and concretions, demanding technique and stamina. A [2025 Montagna.TV report](https://www.montagna.tv/249787/ampliata-la-storica-falesia-di-capo-duomo-allargentario/) quotes the climber Carmela Malomo: this sea limestone hardly polishes, and the old routes still have excellent friction.

**Two sectors.** [theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/central-and-southern-italy/firenze-florence-area/area/957418218) says the single-pitch sport sector is close to the approach path; further down the path toward the sea is the multi-pitch sector, whose routes all finish below the watchtower.

**Scale.** Figures from each source (checked October 2026):

| Source | Figure | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| [Planetmountain](https://www.planetmountain.com/it/falesie/capo-d-uomo.html) | 15 routes, 5c–7a | The single-pitch list from the 2012 rebolt, 30 m high |
| [Montagna.TV](https://www.montagna.tv/249787/ampliata-la-storica-falesia-di-capo-duomo-allargentario/) | 18 original routes plus 10 new | Old routes mostly 5c–6c+; new ones 19–38 m, up to 8a |
| [theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/central-and-southern-italy/firenze-florence-area/area/957418218) | 38 routes | Single-pitch and multi-pitch combined |

**History and bolts.** From theCrag and Montagna.TV: the walls have been climbed since the 1960s and 70s, and from 1985 the locals Giancarlo Rustici and Paolo Pozzi bolted them. Sea air rusted the old bolts; in 2012 the mountain guide Eraldo Meraldi, with help from the CAI Grosseto section, rebolted them in stainless steel and opened new lines, later adding several multi-pitch routes. In spring 2022 and winter 2025 Lorenzo Masala rebolted the crag again with 316 stainless bolts and chains, paying himself with donations from climbers.

**Who it suits.** Climbers from 5c to 6c have the most choice, and there are easy fully bolted multi-pitch routes for a first long route by the sea. Above the 7th grade there are only a few new lines.

## When to go

**A winter crag.** Montagna.TV says the wall faces east to south-east: in winter it gets sun all morning and is good until early afternoon, and the sea keeps the temperature from dropping as soon as the sun goes behind the hill. In spring and autumn you can climb all day; in summer only in the late afternoon. The multi-pitch routes face different ways and suit winter only on calm days, especially on the arête sections.

| Season | Single-pitch sector (Montagna.TV) | Multi-pitch sector |
|---|---|---|
| Winter | Morning to early afternoon | Calm days |
| Spring, autumn | All day | All day |
| Summer | Late afternoon only | Not stated in the sources |

**Busy at weekends.** Montagna.TV quotes Malomo: when the weather is bad elsewhere in central Italy it is often sunny here, and at weekends the crag fills with climbers from Lazio.

Planetmountain's page simply says "Andateci in tutte le stagioni" (go in any season).

## Getting there

| | Public transport | Driving |
|---|---|---|
| Start | Train to Orbetello-Monte Argentario (Tirrenica line) | theCrag: about 40 minutes from Grosseto, about 2 hours from Rome |
| Middle | Autolinee Toscane bus to the port of Porto Santo Stefano | Drive to the port of Porto Santo Stefano |
| Last stretch | About 3.5 km uphill from the port to the parking, no bus | Drive up the Vallone del Campone to the parking |

### Public transport

[Orbetello-Monte Argentario station](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stazione_di_Orbetello-Monte_Argentario) is on the Tirrenica line between Rome and Pisa, served by regional and InterCity trains. Buses outside the station are run by [Autolinee Toscane](https://www.at-bus.it/en/linee-e-orari/grosseto-extraurbano-0o1); line 0O1 and others run from Orbetello Stazione FS to Molo Garibaldi and Lungomare Navigatori in Porto Santo Stefano.

From the port to the crag parking there is still about 3.5 km uphill along the Vallone del Campone with no bus; you have to walk, take a taxi or cycle.

### Driving and parking

[Planetmountain](https://www.planetmountain.com/it/falesie/capo-d-uomo.html): at the first roundabout at the port of Porto Santo Stefano turn left and drive up the Vallone del Campone for about 3.5 km; take the left fork and park just after the col with a sea view. theCrag gives the parking coordinates as 42.40102579598612, 11.11294566340444.

From the parking walk down the dirt road on the right for about 300 m to a barrier, which is there to stop cars; walkers can go round it. Continue to a big bend, take the path on the left along the ridge toward the tower, and near the tower drop down a short steep section to the base of the wall. It takes 15–20 minutes to the single-pitch sector and about 30 minutes to the start of the Spigolo Bonatti; [Planetmountain's route page](https://www.planetmountain.com/en/routes/spigolo-bonatti-capo-d-uomo-argentario.html) warns about the loose scree on the way down.

## Rules to know before you go

**No cars on the dirt road.** theCrag's access note says "Cars are not allowed on the dirt road"; leave the car at the tarmac parking.

**Bolts.** The single-pitch sector was rebolted by Masala in 2022 and 2025 with 316 stainless bolts and chains (theCrag); Montagna.TV says the sport multi-pitch routes have also been re-equipped with 12 mm 316L stainless anchors. The work was paid for by Masala himself and by voluntary donations from climbers. Some of Meraldi's multi-pitch routes are "da integrare" (you add your own protection), so bring a set of cams for those.

**Summer wildfire period.** The [Comune di Monte Argentario](https://www.comune.monteargentario.gr.it/en-us/servizi/ambiente/prevenzione-incendi-boschivi-392-71-1-276c16e1eff9e43d8145306d9a06c92a) publishes the Tuscan regional wildfire-risk period; in 2025, for example, it ran from 21 June to 21 September, with a total ban on burning vegetation across the region. The whole approach runs through Mediterranean scrub: light no fires and drop no cigarette ends, and report any fire on 800 425 425.

**Rescue.** The [Tuscan mountain and cave rescue service (SAST)](https://www.sast.it/richiedi-aiuto/) says to call 112 and ask explicitly for mountain rescue (CNSAS); give your location and phone number, and keep the line free afterwards.

## Routes by level

YDS and V-grade conversions follow the [Mountain Project international grade comparison chart](https://www.mountainproject.com/international-climbing-grades) and are approximate; "approx." marks grades the sources give without a/b/c (such as 4 or 5+). The original grade is the one that counts.

Single-pitch routes and stars come mainly from theCrag (up to three stars); lengths of the older routes are cross-checked against Planetmountain's list. Montagna.TV says Masala graded the new lines to the same standard as the old ones, so grades should be consistent between them.

**Spigolo Bonatti.** From [Planetmountain's route page](https://www.planetmountain.com/en/routes/spigolo-bonatti-capo-d-uomo-argentario.html): facing south-south-east, 240 m of climbing over 350 m of height, 7 pitches, 61 10 mm stainless bolts plus 16 on the belays. Bring 15 quickdraws and a helmet. Pitch 5 is on friable rock, and the upper section has an exposed traverse; from the start of pitch 4 you can escape easily to the left, and pitch 6 can be avoided by walking left of the arête. The route is named after Walter Bonatti and finishes below the watchtower.
