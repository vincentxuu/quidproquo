Cornalba is a small village in Val Serina, a side valley north of Bergamo, and the white limestone wall above it is called Corna Bianca. From the 1980s it was one of the centres of hard Italian sport climbing, and in 2010 Adam Ondra made the first ascent of a 9a+ here. Fewer people come now, for reasons that have to do with the style of climbing.

This post helps you decide three things: whether the wall suits your level, whether you can reach it without a car, and which bans to check before you go. For other crags in Lombardy see [Val di Mello](/posts/climbing/2026-10-18-italy-crag-val-di-mello-en) and [Lecco and the Grigne](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-lecco-grigne-en); for a region-and-season comparison see the [series overview](/posts/climbing/2026-10-18-italy-crags-overview-en).

## What kind of crag is it

**Technical finger walls.** The [comune's crag page](https://www.comune.cornalba.bg.it/citta/sport-e-tempo-libero/palestra-di-roccia/) calls it "il paradiso dei muri tecnici a dita" (a paradise of technical finger walls): routes do not need much stamina, the difficulty sits in a few very hard moves. [theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/cornalba) describes slightly overhanging face climbing on crimpy, technical routes; on [8a.nu](https://www.8a.nu/crags/sportclimbing/italy/cornalba/routes) the top style tags are Technical, Crimps and Cruxy. The wall is about 200 m high, and the comune says some routes run to 5 pitches.

**Scale.** Figures by source (checked October 2026):

| Source | Figure |
|---|---|
| [Comune di Cornalba](https://www.comune.cornalba.bg.it/citta/sport-e-tempo-libero/palestra-di-roccia/) | 100+ routes: 4 at grade 5, 46 at grade 6, 50 at grade 7, 38 at grade 8 and up |
| [VisitBrembo (official Val Brembana tourism site)](https://www.visitbrembo.it/elemento/arrampicare-in-corna-bianca/) | 132+ routes, very few below grade 6 |
| [theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/cornalba) | 119 routes in 8 sectors |
| [8a.nu](https://www.8a.nu/crags/sportclimbing/italy/cornalba/routes) | 124 routes, 1,334 logged ascents |

**Who it suits.** The comune says plainly: "Il livello richiesto all'arrampicatore è almeno il 6c" (you need to climb at least 6c). The few grade-5 routes are "spesso relegati in posti poco accessibili", often in awkward spots. It suits climbers who want 7a and up and like small holds and footwork; beginners have very little to choose from.

**Why it is quieter now.** [Planetmountain's crag page](https://www.planetmountain.com/it/falesie/cornalba.html) quotes the view that Cornalba is "ora un po' in disuso" (now a bit out of use), because slightly overhanging slab-style climbing has gone out of fashion and strong climbers want steep, sustained routes, "come testimoniano le poche ripetizioni" (as the few repeats show); yet the big central wall "avrebbe ancora un potenziale notevolissimo" (still has huge potential). On [Climbook](https://climbook.com/falesie/922/cornalba-corna-bianca-fotonica/vie) most routes show between zero and a handful of logged repeats, which tells the same story.

**History.** VisitBrembo says Bruno Tassi "Camòs" and Gianandrea Tiraboschi began bolting in 1980. Planetmountain credits Camòs with freeing grade-8 classics such as Apache, Peter Pan and Jedi, after which Simone Moro, Dallona, Alippi and others pushed the grades up to 8c+. The key recent routes are Goldrake, first climbed by Ondra, and Extrasistole galoppante, first climbed by Luca Bana; grades and years are in the route tables below.

## When to go

| Source | Season |
|---|---|
| [Comune di Cornalba](https://www.comune.cornalba.bg.it/citta/sport-e-tempo-libero/palestra-di-roccia/) | 1,100 m, south and east aspects plus a local microclimate make it "accessibile in ogni stagione", except on the coldest winter days |
| [VisitBrembo](https://www.visitbrembo.it/elemento/arrampicare-in-corna-bianca/) | Recommends spring and summer |
| [Planetmountain: Extrasistole galoppante](https://www.planetmountain.com/en/news/climbing/luca-bana-frees-extrasistole-galoppante-9a-at-cornalba-italy.html) | Luca Bana writes that the face "basks in the sun all day", which is bad for the tiny crux crimps; he went in November and December |

**How to choose.** The comune says you can climb year-round and the tourism site suggests spring and summer; for hard crimpy routes, Bana's experience is to wait until temperatures drop in late autumn and early winter. In December and early January, check first for a ban like the one in 2024 (see the rules below).

## Getting there

| | Public transport | Driving |
|---|---|---|
| Start | Train to Bergamo, walk to the Arriva bus station on Piazza Marconi | A4 Milan–Venice motorway, Dalmine exit |
| Middle | [Arriva line B](https://www.bergamotrasporti.it/percorsi/invernale/linea-b.pdf) to Zogno, change for Cornalba | Towards Val Brembana on the SS40; after Zogno turn right at Ambria for Zambla-Cornalba into Val Serina |
| Last leg | Get off at Cornalba Chiesa and walk into the village | After about 10 km turn right for Cornalba, right again 1 km later, then follow "palestra di roccia" signs in the village |

### Public transport

At [Arriva Bergamo](https://bergamo.arriva.it/en/bus-terminal/)'s Piazza Marconi bus station, Val Brembana services leave from shelters 1 to 5. Line B runs from Bergamo to Zogno, and the timetable says "A ZOGNO cambiare bus per … CORNALBA": you change at Zogno.

Taking the winter timetable checked in October 2026 (14 September 2026 to 8 June 2027) as an example: only a few runs a day reach Cornalba Chiesa, and many are marked "SCO" (school days only); the Sunday and holiday pages only go as far as Zogno, with no runs to Cornalba. Whether you can get there and back on a non-school weekday or a Saturday has to be worked out run by run from the timetable, or by calling the Arriva contact centre on 035 289000. In practice a day trip without a car is hard; renting one is the simplest option.

### Driving

The route from the [comune](https://www.comune.cornalba.bg.it/citta/sport-e-tempo-libero/palestra-di-roccia/) and Planetmountain: leave the A4 at Dalmine, turn left for Val Brembana, join the SS40 after 11 km and stay on it towards Val Brembana; after Zogno, at Ambria (about 22 km from the exit), turn right for Zambla-Cornalba, drive up the Val Serina gorge, and after about 10 km turn right for Cornalba, then right again 1 km later.

### Parking

[Planetmountain](https://www.planetmountain.com/it/falesie/cornalba.html) says there are 3 car parks within about a hundred metres of Bar-Ristorante Vico; from behind the bar a road soon becomes a path, and the base of the wall is 10 minutes away. [theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/cornalba) also says to park by Bar Vico, with a 5–10 minute walk.

## Rules to know before you go

**The comune issues climbing bans.** The wall is managed by the Comune di Cornalba, and the whole crag has been closed twice in recent years:

| Period | Reason | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 19 June to 31 July 2019 | Crag improvement works (lavori di valorizzazione); the Cornabusa path and the right side of the wall were also closed | [Comune ordinance no. 1/2019 (Ordinanza n. 1/2019)](https://www.comune.cornalba.bg.it/news-e-iniziative/novita/Ordinanza-interdizione-pratica-arrampicata-e-divieto-accesso-dal-18-06-2019-al-31-07-2019-per-lavori-di-valorizzazione-falesia-Cornalba.) |
| December 2024 to 10 January 2025 | A giant Christmas-tree light installation on the wall | Second-hand: December 2024 reports by [Up-Climbing](https://www.up-climbing.com/falesia/news-falesia/cornalba-inagibile-per-albero-di-natale/), [Val Brembana Web](https://www.valbrembanaweb.com/redazionale/in-evidenza/cornalba-lalbero-di-natale-sulla-falesia-divide-i-climber-e-viene-vandalizzato/) and [Montagna.TV](https://www.montagna.tv/247707/albero-di-natale-falesia-di-cornalba/); the ordinance is not on the comune's website |

In 2024 the mayor, Luca Vistalli, told Val Brembana Web that volunteers had first cleared trees from the wall because they got in the way of the helicopter winch during rescues, and the lights came afterwards. Reports differ on the size (Val Brembana Web says 170 m tall, Montagna.TV 120 m), but all give the ban as running to 10 January. Climbers were split over it, and some of the lights were vandalised. Before a winter visit, check the [comune's news page](https://www.comune.cornalba.bg.it/news-e-iniziative/novita/) for new ordinances.

**Bolts.** The comune says "Chiodatura ingaggiante, ma ormai quasi sempre ottima, a fix o resinati": spacing is bold, but the bolts are now almost always good expansion or resin bolts.

**Emergencies.** The emergency number in Italy is 112. [AREU Lombardia](https://www.areu.lombardia.it/web/home/servizionue112) says calls to 112, 113, 115 or 118 all reach the same centre, and the province of Bergamo is handled by the Varese centre; calling through the "112 Where ARE U" app sends your GPS position automatically. Mountain rescue (CNSAS) is dispatched through that centre.

## Routes by level

YDS and V-grade conversions follow the [Mountain Project international grade comparison chart](https://www.mountainproject.com/international-climbing-grades) and are approximate; "approx." marks grades the sources give without a/b/c (such as 4 or 5+). The original grade is the one that counts.

Grades come mainly from [Climbook](https://climbook.com/falesie/922/cornalba-corna-bianca-fotonica/vie)'s sector lists, with first ascents and grades at 8a and above cross-checked against Planetmountain. Climbook shows very few repeats (mostly 0 to 7), so the tables only give a count where one exists. Climbook lists multi-pitch routes pitch by pitch (L1, L2…); the tables combine them into one line with each pitch's grade. Sector names follow Climbook; theCrag's 8 sectors (Tempi nuovi, Apache, Fotonica, La vie en rose, La dolce vita, Boce, Figli del vento, Jedi) roughly match.

### Beginner: up to 6a (YDS 5.10a and below)

There is very little here. The comune says only 4 routes are grade 5, often in awkward spots; what there is sits mainly in the Fotonica and La Dolce Vita/Boce sectors.

| Rotolando | 5c | 5.9 | Single pitch | La Dolce Vita, Boce | 2 repeats on Climbook; single source |
| Via dei gastù | 6a | 5.10a | Single pitch | Fotonica | Most repeated in this sector on Climbook, 7; single source |
| Bella frittella | 6a | 5.10a | Single pitch | La Dolce Vita, Boce | 4 repeats on Climbook; single source |
| Via del caminone | 5c, 5b, 6a | hardest 5.10a | 3 pitches | Figli del Vento | Listed as three pitches on Climbook; single source |

### Expert: 8a and up (YDS 5.13b and up)

The comune counts 38 routes at grade 8 and up, and they are what Cornalba is known for. First ascensionists in the table come from Planetmountain's crag page and news reports.

| Route | Grade | YDS | Length / pitches | Sector | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Apache | 8a | 5.13b | Single pitch (upper extensions are projects) | Apache | Freed by Camòs (Planetmountain); three stars on theCrag |
| Peter Pan | 8a+ | 5.13c | Single pitch | Figli del Vento | Freed by Camòs (Planetmountain, Climbook) |
| Jedi | 8b | 5.13d | Single pitch | Jedi | Freed by Camòs; Goldrake is just to its right |
| Settimo senso | 8c (Planetmountain) / 8b+ (Climbook) | 5.14b | Single pitch | Tempi Nuovi | Freed by Dallona; the two sources differ by half a grade |
| Les sindacalistes | 8c+/9a (Planetmountain) / 8c+ (Climbook) | 5.14c | Single pitch | Figli del Vento | First ascent by Dallona; bolted by Giuseppe Bonfanti "Ciusse" ([Up-Climbing](https://www.up-climbing.com/luoghi-verticali/cornalba-e-camos-raccontati-da-simone-moro/)) |
| Extrasistole galoppante | 9a | 5.14d | Single pitch (reach the small ledge belay by an easier first pitch) | The heart-shaped block visible from the village | Bolted by Yuri Parimbelli in 2010, first ascent by Luca Bana on 26 December 2021; three stars on theCrag ([Planetmountain](https://www.planetmountain.com/en/news/climbing/luca-bana-frees-extrasistole-galoppante-9a-at-cornalba-italy.html)) |
| Goldrake | 9a+ | 5.15a | Single pitch | Jedi (east face) | Bolted by Camòs, first ascent in 2010 by 17-year-old Adam Ondra ([Planetmountain](https://www.planetmountain.com/en/news/climbing/adam-ondra-goldrake-9a-at-cornalba.html)); later repeated by Gabriele Moroni (2014), Stefano Carnati, Stefano Ghisolfi (2016, 4th ascent, [UKC](https://www.ukclimbing.com/news/2016/04/goldrake_9a+_by_ghisolfi-70377)) and Luca Bana (2019) |

**Vento nei capelli.** Planetmountain's 2010 Goldrake report says Ondra had already climbed Vento nei capelli 8c+/9a at Cornalba, but Climbook now lists it as a project (Progetto). The two do not match; go with the guidebook.

[Oliunid](https://www.oliunid.com/versante-sud-valli-bergamasche-climbing-guide) also lists a German edition, which theCrag shows as *Kletterführer Valli Bergamasche*.
