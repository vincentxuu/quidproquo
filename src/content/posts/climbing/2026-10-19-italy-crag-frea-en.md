Frea sits at the very top of Val Gardena in South Tyrol, beside the road down from Passo Gardena towards Selva, on the edge of the Sella massif (Val Gardena tourist board). [UKC](https://ukclimbing.com/logbook/crags/frea-10802) calls it "one of the most renowned sports crags in the Dolomites"; [Planetmountain](https://www.planetmountain.com/en/crags/frea.html) says that driving up from Val Gardena, the cliff of "particularly compact dolomite" on your right just below the pass is Frea.

This post covers what to decide before you go: whether the grades suit you, which months, how to get there without a car, and where to park. The Sella Towers, Cansla and Città dei Sassi on the other side of the pass are in the [Sella post](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-sella-en), and over the pass in Val Badia there's [Sass Dlacia](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-sass-dlacia-en); for a season-by-season and region-by-region comparison of Italian crags, see the [series overview](/posts/climbing/2026-10-18-italy-crags-overview-en).

## What kind of crag is it

**Rock and style.** All bolted sport climbing. Planetmountain says it's mostly vertical rock and partly slabs with huecos and pockets; [theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/dolomites/frea) says "mostly slab climbing".

**Tiers.** The [Val Gardena tourist board](https://www.val-gardena.com/en/summer/climbing-val-gardena-dolomites/sport-climbing/) says the crag is arranged on "floors", so you can climb several pitches one after another. theCrag splits it into three lower sectors (Sonnentau, Mondviole, Bittersüss und Fingerkraut) and two upper ones (Himmelsleiter, Rechter Pfeiler). Planetmountain says the two upper sectors can only be reached by a short via ferrata or by climbing two pitches up from the lower sectors.

**Size.** Numbers from each source (checked October 2026):

| Source | Routes | Grade range |
|---|---|---|
| [Val Gardena tourist board](https://www.val-gardena.com/en/summer/climbing-val-gardena-dolomites/sport-climbing/) | 113 | 4a–7c |
| [Planetmountain](https://www.planetmountain.com/en/crags/frea.html) | 117 | 4a–7c+ |
| [UKC](https://ukclimbing.com/logbook/crags/frea-10802) | 108 | — |
| [theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/dolomites/frea) | 49 | 4–7b+ |

**Who it suits.** Planetmountain says half the routes are French grade 6, about 20 are grade 7, and there are some multi-pitches. It suits people climbing 6a to 6c; if you climb grade 5, theCrag's lower Bittersüss sector has a few routes at 4 to 5a. The tourist board says Frea "captivates with its alpine character" and gives a 10-minute approach; walking down from the pass takes 20–30 minutes (theCrag).

## When to go

**Altitude and months.** Planetmountain gives "June - September" and about 2,200 m; the tourist board says 2,100 m, and theCrag puts the wall at 2,002–2,229 m. The tourist board lists May to October as the ideal months for sport climbing in Val Gardena, but also says spring and autumn are noticeably cold.

**Aspect.** The tourist board and theCrag say northwest; UKC and Planetmountain say west. The tourist board says the sun only arrives in the evening, so "you can climb here even if you are not an early riser"; summer mornings are in the shade.

**Seepage.** theCrag says that being north-facing it's "a bit green in places", with a fair amount of seepage. After heavy rain, have a look before committing.

## Getting there

| | Public transport | Driving |
|---|---|---|
| To Passo Gardena | Bus 473 from Ortisei and Selva, or from Corvara and Colfosco (summer) | Val Gardena side: A22, exit Chiusa or Ponte Gardena, via Selva; Val Badia side via Corvara |
| Pass to the wall | Walk down the SS243 towards Selva for 15–20 min, then 5–10 min on a path | Same (park at the pass) |

### Public transport

[Line 473 (PDF)](https://www.suedtirolmobil.info/fileadmin/pdf/2026/473_20260607.pdf) on South Tyrol's [südtirolmobil](https://www.suedtirolmobil.info/en/my-journey/timetables) network runs Val Gardena–Passo Gardena–Val Badia daily from 7 June to 11 October 2026. From the Plan de Gralba lift station there's roughly an hourly bus up to the pass, with a few running through from Ortisei and Selva; from the other side, buses come up from Corvara via Colfosco about hourly too. Get off at Grödner Joch / Passo Gardena.

On 19 October [fahrplan.it](https://www.fahrplan.it/en/473-groeden-groednerjoch-gadertal) showed "Line 473 is currently not in service", so from mid-October to early June there's no bus to the pass. Coming from Val di Fassa, the Val di Fassa tourist board's [Canazei–Passo Gardena (PDF)](https://www.fassa.com/02-file-documenti-APT/PIANIFICA/COME%20ARRIVARE/Orari-Timetables-suedtirolmobil_Canazei-Passo-Gardena-Corvara_27.06.2026-09.09.2026.pdf) shows connections via Passo Sella, changing to line 473 at Miramonti.

### Driving

[Planetmountain](https://www.planetmountain.com/en/crags/frea.html): from Chiusa or Ponte Gardena drive into Val Gardena, through Ortisei, Santa Cristina and Selva towards Passo Sella/Passo Gardena, and turn left at the fork for Passo Gardena. From Val Pusteria, go through Val Badia to Corvara and up to the pass. UKC says the crag is about 500 m below the pass towards Selva, on the left as you drive down, and easy to see from the road.

Traffic plans: local site [val-gardena.net](https://www.val-gardena.net/en/questions-and-answers.asp?id=1652) (August 2026) says the trial restriction planned for Passo Gardena from 1 September 2026 became a "virtual" digital monitoring test, with no physical road blocks or fines, and arrangements for summer 2027 are undecided (second-hand: no original provincial notice found). Check before a 2027 trip.

### Parking

This is where Frea trips go wrong. Planetmountain says the roadside parking bay (at the end of the flat section) is "Many times... occupied". A UKC comment from 29 June 2026 says "You can't park where the rockfax guidebook suggests anymore", and theCrag says the trailhead parking in the Rockfax book "no longer exists".

theCrag's suggestion: park at Passo Gardena, walk southwest down the SS243 round the hairpins for 15–20 minutes to a path at a break in the wall, then 5–10 minutes up. theCrag warns that "there is no sidewalk" on this stretch of road, so it doesn't feel very safe; a marked path cuts out some of the road walking.

## Rules to know before you go

**Helmets and the cable.** Planetmountain says the two upper sectors sit directly above two of the lower ones, so wear a helmet; anyone climbing in the upper sectors should stay clipped into the via ferrata cable. theCrag says the upper tier is reached by the cable in the middle of the Bittersüss sector.

**Parking and the road walk.** See Parking above. The parking spot in the older Rockfax guide can no longer be used; go by the signs on the ground.

**Closures.** As of October 2026, no closure or climbing ban for Frea was found.

**Rescue.** South Tyrol mountain rescue ([Bergrettung / CNSAS Alto Adige](https://www.bergrettung.it/en/raising-the-alarm-2.html)) is reached on 112, staffed 24 hours; [CNSAS South Tyrol](https://www.soccorsoalpino.org/en/safety) says 118, 115 and 113 are also routed to the emergency centre in Bolzano.

## Routes by level

YDS and V-grade conversions follow the [Mountain Project international grade comparison chart](https://www.mountainproject.com/international-climbing-grades) and are approximate; "approx." marks grades the sources give without a/b/c (such as 4 or 5+). The original grade is the one that counts.

Routes and stars come mainly from [theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/dolomites/frea)'s five sector pages, cross-checked against Planetmountain's recommended routes. For multi-pitches theCrag only gives the overall grade.

### Expert: 8a and up (YDS 5.13b and up)

Not listed in the sources; Frea tops out at 7c+. The tourist board mentions the 45 m Menhir (8b) on a separate wall near Passo Gardena, which is not part of Frea.

## Where to look things up

- **[theCrag: Frea](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/dolomites/frea)**: routes and stars in all five sectors, plus the current parking and walk-in notes.
- **[UKC Logbook: Frea](https://ukclimbing.com/logbook/crags/frea-10802)**: English overview and user comments (parking changes show up here first).
- **[Planetmountain: Frea](https://www.planetmountain.com/en/crags/frea.html)**: recommended routes, approach, helmet and cable advice.
- **[Val Gardena tourist board: Sport Climbing](https://www.val-gardena.com/en/summer/climbing-val-gardena-dolomites/sport-climbing/)**: route counts, aspects and approach times for Val Gardena's crags.
- **[südtirolmobil timetables](https://www.suedtirolmobil.info/en/my-journey/timetables)**: current PDF for line 473.
- **Guidebooks:** Grades and route counts here come mainly from online databases; the guidebook is the authority.
  - *Sportklettern in Südtirol / Arrampicata sportiva nell'Alto Adige* (Thomas Hofer, Benjamin Kofler, Egon Larcher; Vertical Life; 3rd edition 2023; German and Italian; about 700 pages; ISBN 978-88-98495-22-1): 105 sport crags, with Frea in the Eisacktal/Gröden chapter; includes a Vertical-Life app unlock code (listings at [Panico](https://www.panico.de/vertical-life-sportkletterfuehrer-suedtirol.html) and [freytag & berndt](https://www.freytagberndt.com/en/sportklettern-in-sudtirol-1.html)).
  - [*The Dolomites: Rock Climbs and Via Ferrata*](https://rockfax.com/climbing-guides/books/the-dolomites-rock-climbs-and-via-ferrata) (James Rushforth; Rockfax; 2nd edition 2019; English): the Val Gardena chapter covers Frea, Tridentina and Eiszeit. The parking it shows can no longer be used (UKC and theCrag comments). The publisher's page says it's out of stock; the full guide is on Rockfax Digital.
  - *Sportclimbing in the Dolomites* (Vertical-Life; 2013; German, Italian and English): 54 Dolomite crags, listed by Planetmountain as the reference for Frea; it's older, so go with the first book.
