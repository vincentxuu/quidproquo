Frosolone is a mountain village in the province of Isernia in Molise. On the Colle dell'Orso ("Bear Hill") plateau outside the village sits a scatter of grey limestone blocks, the biggest of which is called Morgia Quadra. When climbers say "Frosolone", "Colle dell'Orso" or "Morgia Quadra", they mean the same place. [Planetmountain](https://www.planetmountain.com/it/falesie/colle-dell-orso.html) calls it "storicamente la più importante del Molise" (historically the most important crag in Molise).

The tricky part here is not the routes, it is the current status. The mayor banned climbing in 2020 and the council reopened it with conditions in 2021, but we could not find either original document, and Planetmountain's crag page still carries the ban notice. This post covers what to decide before you go: whether it suits your level, which months, how to get there without a car, whether you can climb there now, and which months you can't. For Abruzzo, one region north, see [Pennadomo](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-pennadomo-en); for a season-and-region comparison of Italian crags, see the [series overview](/posts/climbing/2026-10-18-italy-crags-overview-en).

## What kind of crag is it

**A field of big blocks on pasture land.** Planetmountain's write-up is by local climber Pietro Radassao. The rock outcrops on this upland are mostly clusters of limestone blocks, "grigio ed impegnativo calcare di Frosolone" (Frosolone's grey, demanding limestone), mainly smooth slabs and flared pockets. Constant strong wind has carved the faces into sloping holes and pockets. The blocks face every direction; [Gulliver](https://www.gulliver.it/itinerari/frosolonecolle-dellorso/) labels them Blocco A, B, C and so on, with routes 10–35 m long.

**On the hard side.** Planetmountain says about three quarters of the routes are 6b or harder; roughly a hundred sit between grade 4 and 6, and there are around 70 eighth-grade routes and projects. Development began in the early 1980s; the [publisher's blurb for *Molise Rock*](https://www.versantesud.it/it/negozio/catalogue/molise-rock_1336/) says the first climbers drawn here came from Rome.

**Scale.** The sites disagree widely (checked October 2026):

| Source | Count |
|---|---|
| [theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/colle-dellorso) | 189 routes, 1,080–1,300 m altitude |
| [Planetmountain](https://www.planetmountain.com/it/falesie/colle-dell-orso.html) | 400 routes, 4a–8c+/9a |
| [Climb Europe](https://climb-europe.com/pages/rock-climbing-italy-potenza-and-south-italy) | 405 routes, 1,200 m |
| [Planetmountain 2020 news](https://www.planetmountain.com/it/notizie/arrampicata/frosolone-la-chiusura-della-storica-falesia-di-arrampicata.html) | "quasi 500 vie" (nearly 500) |
| [GoFundMe page](https://www.gofundme.com/f/frosolone-rebolt-project-mettiamo-in-sicurezza-la-falesia) (2026) | "oltre 500 vie" (over 500) |

**Who it suits.** Single-pitch sport climbers at 6b and up who like technical slabs and pockets. There are around a hundred routes at 6a and below, but this is not a beginners' crag.

## When to go

| Source | Season |
|---|---|
| [Planetmountain](https://www.planetmountain.com/it/falesie/colle-dell-orso.html) | Over 1,200 m and very windy: best in spring and summer after the snow melts; the blocks face different ways, so you can find a good face all day |
| [Gulliver](https://www.gulliver.it/itinerari/frosolonecolle-dellorso/) | Climbable most of the year except winter; fine in summer thanks to the wind and choosing the right aspect; in strong wind it can be almost impossible to climb |
| [Climb Europe](https://climb-europe.com/pages/rock-climbing-italy-potenza-and-south-italy) | Spring through summer |

**Two things that change the plan.** First, March to May: the 2021 reopening set those months as a bird-nesting closure (see the rules below), and we found nothing on whether later years repeated it, so ask before any spring trip. Second, wind: this is an open plateau, and Gulliver warns that on strong-wind days you can hardly climb. In practice the safest window is July to October.

## Getting there

### Public transport

[Autoservizi Scarano](https://www.scaranobus.it/orarilinee/) runs two bus lines from Frosolone village: Frosolone–Isernia and Acquevive–Frosolone–Campobasso, with timetables as PDFs on its site (checked October 2026; the Frosolone–Isernia PDF link did not open, so ask the operator). The bus only goes to the village. The crag is out on the plateau several kilometres further towards Sessano, with no bus. Without a car you'll need to stay in the village and arrange a lift.

### Driving

Gulliver: take the A1 Rome–Naples motorway, exit at Cassino and follow signs to Isernia and Frosolone. From Frosolone follow signs for Colle dell'Orso–Sessano del Molise to the fork by the church (hermitage) of Sant'Egidio, then continue 2–3 km towards Sessano.

### Parking

Planetmountain says to park by a small stone building on the right of the road; Gulliver describes it as an animal shelter on the right near a parking spot. From there walk down to the drinking trough you can see from the road and follow the path, about 15 minutes to the walls. To reach blocks A–Z you take a flat path through pasture gates; **always close them behind you**.

A 2025 town order (see the rules below) bans parking on the road between the Colle dell'Orso car park and the Belvedere. We could not tell from the order's map whether the roadside spot climbers use is inside that stretch, so follow the signs on site.

## Rules to know before you go

**The August 2020 ban (secondary: Planetmountain and Montagna.TV reports from 2020; original order not found).** [Planetmountain](https://www.planetmountain.com/it/notizie/arrampicata/frosolone-la-chiusura-della-storica-falesia-di-arrampicata.html) and [Montagna.TV](https://www.montagna.tv/165958/molise-chiude-la-falesia-della-morgia-quadra-climbers-in-rivolta/) reported that on 18 August 2020 the mayor, Felice Ianiro, issued an order banning "attività spontanea dell'arrampicata sportiva" (unorganised sport climbing) on Morgia Quadra until the necessary works were done. The order cites council resolution no. 77 of 12 August. Planetmountain says the crag lies inside an EU SIC (Site of Community Importance) and is also under environmental and landscape protection; the mayor said the activity could not take place without prior authorisation.

**The conditional reopening in May 2021 (secondary: UP Climbing 2021 report; original resolution not found).** [UP Climbing](https://www.up-climbing.com/falesia/news-falesia/frosolone-riaperta-la-falesia-2/) reported that the council adopted a resolution on 25 May 2021, announced on 31 May, in three stages:

| Period (2021) | Rule |
|---|---|
| March to May | No climbing anywhere on Morgia Quadra, so cliff-nesting birds can occupy nests and lay eggs |
| May to June | Climbing only outside the designated "aree di rispetto" (protected zones) |
| From July | Whole crag open |

UP Climbing also noted that a further document was still awaited to say which blocks were protected zones. We found no notice of whether this closure has been repeated in later years.

**Where we actually looked (October 2026).** Searching the [town website](https://comune.frosolone.is.it/) for "arrampicata", "falesia" and "morgia" returns nothing. The [online notice board](https://servizionline.comune.frosolone.is.it/urbi/progs/urp/ur1ME002.sto?DB_NAME=wt00037281&w3cbt=S) and transparency section can search orders and resolutions back to 1997, but only through a web form our tools could not submit, so we did not read the 2020 ban or the 2021 reopening resolution. Planetmountain's crag page still showed the "DIVIETO ARRAMPICATA" (climbing ban) notice in October 2026; that notice predates UP Climbing's reopening report.

**Is anyone climbing there now?** There are signs of it. In April 2026 local climber Pietro Radassao launched the "Frosolone Rebolt Project" fundraiser ([isNews](https://www.isnews.it/2026/04/16/falesia-di-frosolone-una-raccolta-fondi-dal-basso-per-proteggere-un-patrimonio-dellarrampicata/) reported over €2,000 raised by then). The [fundraiser page](https://www.gofundme.com/f/frosolone-rebolt-project-mettiamo-in-sicurezza-la-falesia) says the crag "continua ad accogliere ogni anno appassionati" (still welcomes climbers every year), and makes two safety points: many bolts and anchors date from older standards, and some routes are "meno affidabili e, nei casi peggiori, non più utilizzabili" (less reliable and, at worst, no longer usable). [Climbook](https://climbook.com/falesie/328/colle-dellorso-blocco-n-p-e-q-morgia-quadra) also updated its route data in March 2025 from the 2020 guidebook.

**How to check before you go.** Call the town hall on +39 0874 890435 (the switchboard number on the town website in October 2026) and ask two things: whether climbing on Morgia Quadra is currently allowed, and whether any zones are closed for nesting birds between March and June that year. Don't make this your main stop in March–May.

**The 2025 order for the Colle dell'Orso tourist area (primary).** Town [order no. 17 of 2025](https://comune.frosolone.is.it/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Ordinanza-Sindacale-n-17-2025.pdf) (signed 21 May 2025, posted on the town website on 12 August 2026) applies from 30 July 2025 "fino a nuove disposizioni" (until further notice):

- No parking on the road between the Colle dell'Orso car park and the Belvedere.
- Camping only in the areas marked on the [attached map](https://comune.frosolone.is.it/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/ALLEGATO_A_3.pdf); no bivouacking elsewhere.
- No driving on grass, under the trees or in picnic areas.
- Fines of €25 to €500.

The order does not mention climbing, so it says nothing about whether climbing is now authorised, but it does govern where you park and sleep around the crag.

**Rescue.** Italy's official tourism site [italia.it](https://www.italia.it/it/italia/informazioni-per-viaggiare/numeri-emergenza-e-assistenza) says to call 112 in an emergency; 118 is the medical emergency number and also covers mountain rescue. The 2020 fall was evacuated by helicopter to hospital in Campobasso.

## Routes by level

YDS and V-grade conversions follow the [Mountain Project international grade comparison chart](https://www.mountainproject.com/international-climbing-grades) and are approximate; "approx." marks grades the sources give without a/b/c (such as 4 or 5+). The original grade is the one that counts.

Routes and stars come mainly from Planetmountain's list of classics (all 5 stars); repeat counts come from Climbook's Blocco P and Q (Morgia Quadra proper) page; block letters follow Gulliver. Climbook updated its data in 2025 from *Molise Rock* (2020), and a few routes came down half a grade from older topos; both grades are shown.

### Beginner: up to 6a (YDS 5.10a and below)

Planetmountain puts about a hundred routes in this range, spread across the blocks; in Gulliver's list the easy routes cluster on Blocco E, H and I.
