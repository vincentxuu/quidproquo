> 🌏 [中文版](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-gran-sasso)

Gran Sasso d'Italia is a limestone massif in Abruzzo, between the provinces of L'Aquila and Teramo. Its highest summit, Corno Grande, at 2,912 m, is the high point of the entire Apennine chain. [Guillaume Dargaud](http://www.gdargaud.net/Climbing/GranSasso.html) writes that Francesco De Marchi reached the top of Corno Grande in 1573, and "The birth of Italian mountain climbing may be here". The whole massif became part of the Gran Sasso and Monti della Laga National Park in 1991.

Climbers come for two things: multi-pitch routes on Corno Piccolo and Corno Grande, whose rock is compared to the Marmolada south face in the Dolomites ([SummitPost](https://www.summitpost.org/corno-piccolo/288292)); and summer bouldering and short sport routes around the Campo Imperatore plateau, at 1,700 to 2,400 m. This post covers what to decide before you go: which kind suits you, which months, whether the lifts are running, and how to get there without a car. For alpine multi-pitch elsewhere in the series, compare [Cinque Torri](/posts/climbing/2026-10-18-italy-crag-cinque-torri-en); for a season-and-region comparison of Italian crags, see the [series overview](/posts/climbing/2026-10-18-italy-crags-overview-en).

## What kind of crag is it

**Two sides, two gateways.** On the north side of the massif (province of Teramo) the gateway is Prati di Tivo, for the north and east faces of Corno Piccolo and its three shoulders (Le Tre Spalle), and for the [Rifugio Franchetti](https://www.rifugiofranchetti.it/) hut at 2,433 m. On the south side (province of L'Aquila) the gateway is Fonte Cerreto and the Campo Imperatore plateau, for the south face and west summit of Corno Grande and the boulders at the edge of the plateau.

**Corno Piccolo: the home of multi-pitch.** At 2,655 m, SummitPost says it has "some of the hardest and beautiest climbing routes of all central-southern Italy". The north face is smooth and cut by couloirs; the east face is vertical with cracks and overhangs, "very dolomitic"; the three shoulders on the west ridge are known for perfect slabs; the south-west face is more broken and carries the normal route. Dargaud says Corno Piccolo has the best rock and the shortest approaches.

**Corno Grande and the Paretone.** The north-east face of Corno Grande's east summit is the Paretone, which Dargaud calls "a mile high face"; on [Planetmountain](https://www.planetmountain.com/it/itinerari/vie-di-arrampicata?gruppo=Gran+Sasso), the Paretone Express route climbs about 850 m of wall before joining the Canale Jannetta. This is a remote, serious big wall, not a short day out.

**Protection.** Most routes are trad: Dargaud writes that the only bolted routes are those within 15 minutes of the lift, and everything else needs a full rack of cams and nuts on top of the occasional fixed peg. Planetmountain's description of Spigolo a destra della crepa says "I chiodi ci sono tutti ma alcune soste sono da attrezzare con dadi e friend" (the pegs are all there, but some belays have to be built with nuts and cams). Newer routes such as The Trooper (completed in August 2026) have stainless bolted belays.

**Bouldering and sport.** [Versante Sud](https://www.versantesud.it/en/shop/catalogue/magia-di-calcare_1127/)'s bouldering guide *Magia di Calcare* covers five limestone areas, Monte Aquila, Vado di Sole, Val Maone, Vallone delle Cornacchie and Valle del Monte (plus the Poggio Umbricchio sandstone), with "hundreds of problems of any difficulties", says the publisher. [Oliunid](https://www.oliunid.it/blog/fare-boulder-al-monte-aquila-oliunid-is-bloc/) counts about 70 problems at Monte Aquila, at about 2,000 m; Vado di Sole has about 30 boulders next to a sport crag in a beech wood ([Alpinisti d'Appennino](https://www.alpinistidappennino.it/2024/07/03/falesia-vado-di-sole/) lists 32 routes, 6a–9a+).

**Scale.** There is no single count for the multi-pitch routes; [theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/central-and-southern-italy/laquila-area/area/2901664905) lists 311 routes for the whole national park (checked October 2026), far fewer than the guidebooks.

**Who it suits.**

- Multi-pitch climbers with trad experience who can build belays and abseil: the shoulders and east face of Corno Piccolo.
- People looking for a first alpine multi-pitch: the Chiaraviglio-Berthelet on Corno Piccolo's south ridge (PD+, grade III) and the Traversata delle Tre Vette on Corno Grande, ideally with a mountain guide.
- Boulderers and strong sport climbers: Monte Aquila, Val Maone and Vado di Sole in summer, where most problems and routes are grade 7 and up.

## When to go

| Source | Season |
|---|---|
| [SummitPost](https://www.summitpost.org/corno-piccolo/288292) | High summer is best for rock; depending on the year and aspect, early October and late May can work, but watch for verglas in the shade; in early summer you may need axe and crampons for leftover snow; fog is common even in summer |
| [Dargaud](http://www.gdargaud.net/Climbing/GranSasso.html) | 45 km from the Adriatic, the weather changes fast and clouds always build on summer afternoons; start early and carry a rain jacket |
| [Alpinisti d'Appennino](https://www.alpinistidappennino.it/2024/07/03/falesia-vado-di-sole/) | Vado di Sole sport: June to September, 1,700 m, west-facing, shaded all morning |
| [Oliunid](https://www.oliunid.it/blog/fare-boulder-al-monte-aquila-oliunid-is-bloc/) | Monte Aquila bouldering: about 2,000 m and often windy, so climbable even in high summer; best in June and October |

**When you can actually go.** Multi-pitch and bouldering both cluster in June to September. The lifts and huts also only run in that window (see below); after October the walks get longer, and north-facing gullies and shady spots start to ice up.

## Getting there

| | Teramo side (Corno Piccolo, Rifugio Franchetti) | L'Aquila side (Corno Grande south side, Campo Imperatore bouldering, Vado di Sole) |
|---|---|---|
| Driving | Leave the A24 at San Gabriele-Colledara, SS491 to Montorio al Vomano, then the SS80 to the Pietracamela / Prati di Tivo turn-off | Leave the A24 at Assergi and take the SS17bis via Fonte Cerreto up to Campo Imperatore |
| Bus | TUA from Teramo via Montorio to Prati di Tivo, very few buses | AMA line M6 from the Terminalbus in L'Aquila to the Fonte Cerreto lift station |
| Lift | Prati di Tivo – La Madonnina gondola (2,007 m), summer 2026 season over | Funivia del Gran Sasso (Fonte Cerreto – Campo Imperatore), closed for maintenance from 21 September 2026 |

### Public transport

**Teramo side.** On [TUA](https://www.tuabruzzo.it/orari-del-sabato-provincia-di-teramo-corse/108.html?linea=36)'s Prati di Tivo – Teramo line, taking the Saturday timetable checked in October 2026 as an example, buses only reach Prati di Tivo at 15:35 and 16:15 in the afternoon, and the only bus down is at 06:10 in the morning to Montorio al Vomano. You need to stay at Prati di Tivo or Pietracamela to fit in any climbing; a day trip is not possible.

**L'Aquila side.** [AMA L'Aquila](https://www.ama.laquila.it/linee-e-orari/elenco-linee-ama/)'s line M6 runs from the Terminalbus in town via Paganica and Assergi to the lower lift station (Base funivia); line M6F runs on public holidays. Timetables change by season, and AMA changed weekday services again from 19 October 2026, so download the current PDF before you go. While the cable car is closed, the only ways from Fonte Cerreto to Campo Imperatore are driving or walking.

### Driving

- **Teramo side**: [SummitPost](https://www.summitpost.org/corno-piccolo/288292) says to leave the A24 at the San Gabriele-Colledara exit, take the SS491 to Montorio al Vomano, then the SS80 to the Pietracamela / Prati di Tivo turn-off; about 70 km from L'Aquila and 30 km from Teramo.
- **L'Aquila side**: leave the A24 at Assergi and take the SS17bis via Fonte Cerreto up to Campo Imperatore (*[Magia di Calcare* preview](https://cdnc.heyzine.com/files/uploaded/v3/a32cbd8172daec0eea1c7f1fd4bd6d62d46f9014.pdf)). The lift operator, the [Centro Turistico del Gran Sasso](https://www.ilgransasso.it/), says the SP17bis stays open while the cable car is closed. For Vado di Sole, continue east across the plateau and turn left towards Farindola after the Mucciante restaurant, about 2 km (Alpinisti d'Appennino).

### Lifts (checked October 2026)

- **Funivia del Gran Sasso** (Fonte Cerreto 1,115 m – Campo Imperatore 2,130 m): the operator announced 20 September 2026 as the last day of service, and "Da lunedì 21 settembre 2026 la Funivia verrà chiusa per lavori di ordinaria manutenzione" (from Monday 21 September 2026 the cable car is closed for routine maintenance), with no reopening date given.
- **Prati di Tivo – La Madonnina gondola** (for Corno Piccolo and the Franchetti hut): the [operator's site](https://www.pdtx.it/it/impianti.php) gives the summer 2026 season as daily from 15 August to 6 September, then only 12, 13, 19 and 20 September, 8:00 to 17:00 with the last run at 17:00; on 19 October its status was "Chiuso". The local outlet [emmelle.it](https://www.emmelle.it/2026/09/03/prati-di-tivo-seggiocabinovia-aperta-anche-nei-week-end-di-settembre/) notes that the lift has been shut for long periods in the past because of management disputes.
- **Without the lifts**: the Franchetti hut gives about 2 hours 30 minutes and 780 m of ascent from Piana del Laghetto; Planetmountain gives 45 minutes to an hour from Cima Alta to the top station at La Madonnina. Allow another 30 to 40 minutes from there to the starts on the shoulders.

### Parking and where to stay

- Monte Aquila boulders: the *Magia di Calcare* preview says to park in the small space at the last hairpin before the Campo Imperatore car park, which takes only 2 or 3 cars, then walk about 600 m.
- Vado di Sole: park by the road next to the crag and walk 5 minutes. Oliunid says campervans can stay in several nearby car parks.
- **Rifugio Franchetti** (2,433 m, CAI Rome section): its site gives the summer season as early June to the end of September, with October to May only for pre-booked groups of 15 or more; a notice of 1 October 2026 says "Siamo chiusi, la stagione estiva 2026 é volta al termine" (we are closed, the 2026 summer season is over), opening only on weekends in October when the weather allows. It has 23 beds and is often full at weekends.
- SummitPost says wild camping is not allowed in the national park without a permit from the park.

## Rules to know before you go

**National park.** The whole massif lies in the Gran Sasso and Monti della Laga National Park. The park's 2019 draft regulation ([Bozza Regolamento del Parco](https://www.gransassolagapark.it/albOnline/2019/PNGSLdocumento50629-allegato1.pdf)) says mountaineering and bouldering are "consentito liberamente" (freely allowed) throughout the park, but the park can close areas, paths and climbing routes for conservation (for example during breeding seasons) through formal notices with maps; bouldering must not damage plants, disturb wildlife or strip vegetation from the boulders; new sport routes may only be bolted on walls near paved roads, and near huts only with park permission. This is a draft; whether it has come into force could not be confirmed. Checked October 2026, no closure notices were found for Corno Piccolo, Corno Grande or the bouldering areas; check the notices on the [park website](https://www.gransassolagapark.it/) before you go.

**Rockfall and weather.** The gullies on Corno Grande and the Paretone see rockfall, and snow lingers into early summer; SummitPost notes that the old Ferrata Brizio is no longer maintained and is closed after many fatal accidents. Afternoon cloud and fog are normal in summer, so start long routes early.

**Rescue.** [CNSAS](https://www.cnsas.it/richiesta-di-soccorso/) says that for an accident in the mountains you call 118 or the European emergency number 112 and ask the operator to activate mountain rescue; the call is free.

## Routes by level

YDS and V-grade conversions follow the [Mountain Project international grade comparison chart](https://www.mountainproject.com/international-climbing-grades) and are approximate; "approx." marks grades the sources give without a/b/c (such as 4 or 5+). The original grade is the one that counts.

The four grade bands hold routes with French grades: mainly the Vado di Sole sport crag (from [Alpinisti d'Appennino](https://www.alpinistidappennino.it/2024/07/03/falesia-vado-di-sole/)'s route list) plus The Trooper, a new multi-pitch on Corno Piccolo's east face. The classic multi-pitch routes on Corno Piccolo and Corno Grande use UIAA grades and go in the trad table further down; bouldering has its own table.

### Beginner: up to 6a (YDS 5.10a and below)

Vado di Sole has a single 6a. The real entry-level climbs are the grade III ridges in the trad table (Chiaraviglio-Berthelet, Traversata delle Tre Vette), but those are alpine terrain: you need to place gear or go with a guide.
