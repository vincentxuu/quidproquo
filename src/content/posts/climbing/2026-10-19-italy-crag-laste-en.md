> 🌏 [中文版](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-laste)

Laste is in the Agordino valley in the north of the province of Belluno, Veneto, in the municipality of Rocca Pietore, near Caprile and the mouth of the Val Pettorina. The crag wraps around four big blocks next to the hamlet of Ronch, looking straight at the north-west face of the Civetta. [CAI's Lo Scarpone](https://www.loscarpone.cai.it/dettaglio/fuga-dal-caldo-cinque-falesie-per-arrampicare-nelle-dolomiti-orientali/) calls it "probabilmente la falesia più emblematica delle Dolomiti Venete" (probably the most emblematic crag of the Veneto Dolomites).

This post helps you decide whether this footwork-heavy slab climbing suits you, which months to go, whether the 2026 roadworks affect you, and how to get there without a car. For a season-and-region comparison of Italian crags, see the [series overview](/posts/climbing/2026-10-18-italy-crags-overview-en).

## What kind of crag is it

**Four blocks of smooth, compact Marmolada limestone.** The [Dolomiti Bellunesi tourist board (DMO)](https://www.visitdolomitibellunesi.com/it/cosa-fare/escursionismo/falesie-attrezzate/falesia-laste) says the crag is made up of four blocks, Sass de Ròcia, Sass de la Murada, Sass de la Gusela (also spelled Gujela) and Sass de l'Aghinèl, of "calcare liscio e compatto della Marmolada" (smooth, compact Marmolada limestone). The biggest, Sass de Ròcia, is about a hundred metres high with routes on every side, some with second and third pitches ([Alpinisti d'Appennino](https://www.alpinistidappennino.it/2026/08/12/falesia-laste/)).

**Style: footwork and balance.** The tourist board says that apart from Aghinèl, which has overhanging routes, everything needs technique and footwork, and "anche le vie di grado basso possono risultare severe al primo approccio" (even the low grades can feel severe the first time). CAI says the climbing is mostly vertical, on crimps and balance, and "non potrai andare via solo di forza" (you can't get by on strength alone); [Climbook](https://climbook.com/falesie/1659-laste-sass-de-la-guesla) says the easy routes are technical too, and the hard ones need technique plus finger strength.

**Scale.** Counts by site (checked 2026-10-19):

| Source | Count |
|---|---|
| [DMO Dolomiti Bellunesi](https://www.visitdolomitibellunesi.com/it/cosa-fare/escursionismo/falesie-attrezzate/falesia-laste) | 200+, grade 4 to 8c |
| [La Rivolta](https://www.larivolta.it/falesie/laste) (local climbing club) | 215; just over 120 in 2005 |
| [theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/dolomites/laste) | 188 (Sass de Rócia 112, Murada 45, Aghinèl 23, Gusela 8) |
| [falesia.it](https://www.falesia.it/crag/958/laste.html) | 167, 5a–8b+, 8 sectors |

**Sectors.** falesia.it splits the blocks by aspect: Sass de Rócia north 41 routes (5a–8a+), south 39 (6b–8b+), east 17 (7a+–8b+); Murada south 20 (6b–7b+) and two north sectors with 18 between them (7a–7c+); Aghinèl 23 (5a–8a+); Gusela 9 (5c–6b+). La Rivolta notes that sectors are sometimes hundreds of metres apart.

**Bolts.** Alpinisti d'Appennino says the bolting is consistently good, with the odd longer gap that is never dangerous; bases are mostly roomy for belaying, and moving between sectors is easy.

**Who it suits.** CAI is blunt: "non rappresenta un'ottima idea per chi arrampica da poco" (not a great idea for people who have only just started). It suits climbers who already lead confidently and want to work on footwork and reading routes, and anyone who wants to escape the summer heat lower down.

**Nearby.** A watchtower on top of Sass de la Murada was destroyed around 1390 (La Rivolta); the Pian de le Stelle bivouac on top of Sass de Ròcia is reached by a short via ferrata (tourist board).

## When to go

| Source | Season |
|---|---|
| [Alpinisti d'Appennino](https://www.alpinistidappennino.it/2026/08/12/falesia-laste/) | June to September; all aspects |
| [Climbook](https://climbook.com/falesie/1659-laste-sass-de-la-guesla) | Summer, late spring, not-too-late autumn; choose sun or shade |
| [La Rivolta](https://www.larivolta.it/falesie/laste) | A summer crag; south faces possible in other seasons |
| [Consorzio Turistico Marmolada Rocca Pietore](https://www.outdooractive.com/it/route/arrampicata/rocca-pietore/falesia-laste/52883838/) | Sass de Rocia's south face works on mild winter days |

**Pick your face by aspect.** With routes on every side, Climbook says you can choose between sun and shade. When it's hot, go to the north faces of Sass de Rócia or Murada; when it's cold, the south face of Rócia. falesia.it gives the altitude as 1,560 m, and CAI includes Laste among its five "Fuga dal caldo" (escape the heat) crags.

## Getting there

| | Public transport | Car |
|---|---|---|
| Start | Train to Belluno | From the Veneto via Agordo to Caprile; from the north via the Pordoi or Falzarego passes or Alleghe |
| Middle | Dolomiti Bus to Agordo and Caprile | Caprile → Saviner di Laste → right towards Digonera |
| Last leg | Line 4 Caprile → Laste (one morning and one midday run, school days only) | Before Digonera, hairpin left up to Laste di Sopra, then left to Ronch; park and walk about 10 minutes |

### Public transport

The [Consorzio Turistico](https://www.outdooractive.com/it/route/arrampicata/rocca-pietore/falesia-laste/52883838/) says Laste is about 58 km from Belluno by Dolomiti Bus. Belluno to Agordo and Caprile is Dolomiti Bus's main Agordino corridor ([winter lines page](https://dolomitibus.it/it/linee-extraurbane-invernale-2026)).

The last leg is Line 4. **In summer 2026 (8 June to 9 September) the Laste runs were suspended**: the [summer timetable](https://dolomitibus.it/files/orari/estivi/Linea04_E26_v2.pdf) says "Servizio per Laste sospeso a causa dell'interruzione stradale dovuta ai lavori in corso lungo la SP 35 "di Laste"" (service to Laste suspended because of the road closure for works on the SP35). The [winter timetable](https://dolomitibus.it/files/orari/invernali/Linea04_S26_v1.pdf) (10 September 2026 to 8 June 2027) brings Laste back, but only Monday to Saturday on school days: departures from Caprile at 7:00 and 12:50, 20 minutes to Laste; back from Laste at 7:20 and 13:10. Arriving in the morning and leaving at midday gives you half a day; whether summer service returns will only be clear from the 2027 summer timetable.

### Driving

[falesia.it](https://www.falesia.it/crag/958/laste.html) says you can come via the Pordoi or Falzarego passes or Alleghe, aiming for the village of Digonera. Alpinisti d'Appennino gives more detail: from Caprile go to Saviner di Laste, turn right towards Digonera, and just before the village take a hairpin left up to Laste di Sopra; at the village turn left again for Ronch.

**The SP35.** The "SP 35 di Laste" in the Dolomiti Bus timetable is the provincial road to Laste, closed for works in summer 2026; check the latest notices from Dolomiti Bus or Veneto Strade before you go.

### Parking

falesia.it says there is a car park at Laste di Sopra; from there walk along the tarmac road to Ronch, where a path starts on the right behind the fountain; keep left for the main sector. Alpinisti d'Appennino says there are several places to park along the way, the last by the fountain; Climbook says to leave the car at Ronch. La Rivolta gives the approach as 10 minutes; Climbook says the loop path round the whole crag takes about 20.

## Rules before you go

**Closures and bans.** As of October 2026, theCrag shows "Access issues: None", and no climbing ban for the crag was found. For the roadworks, see above.

**Respect the place.** La Rivolta specifically asks for "il massimo rispetto dei luoghi" (the utmost respect for the place); the crag is right by the hamlet of Ronch, so don't park where you block residents.

**Rescue and costs.** Call 112; 118 is medical and mountain rescue ([italia.it](https://www.italia.it/it/italia/informazioni-per-viaggiare/numeri-emergenza-e-assistenza)). [trekking.it's 2023 summary](https://www.trekking.it/news/soccorso-alpino-pagamento-ticket/) (second-hand) says Veneto charges for rescues from sports such as rock climbing even when you are admitted to hospital (regional decision 1411 of 2011). Check that your insurance covers mountain rescue.

## Routes by level

YDS and V-grade conversions follow the [Mountain Project international grade comparison chart](https://www.mountainproject.com/international-climbing-grades) and are approximate; "approx." marks grades the sources give without a/b/c (such as 4 or 5+). The original grade is the one that counts.

Routes and lengths come mainly from falesia.it's sector pages ([Sass de Rócia north](https://www.falesia.it/sector/2306/laste/sass-de-rcia-north.html), [south](https://www.falesia.it/sector/2308/laste/x.html), [Aghinèl](https://www.falesia.it/sector/2304/laste/x.html)); Gusela routes and repeat counts come from Climbook and Alpinisti d'Appennino. The tourist board warns that even low grades are "severe", so the same grade here usually feels harder than you're used to; on day one, drop half a grade to a grade.
