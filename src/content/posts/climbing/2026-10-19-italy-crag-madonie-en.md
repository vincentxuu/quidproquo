Caltavuturo is a small inland town in Palermo province on the western edge of the Madonie mountains, below a limestone ridge called Rocca di Sciara. Most Sicilian crags are by the sea, good in winter and too hot in summer; [UKC's Sicily guide](https://www.ukclimbing.com/articles/destinations/sicily-14498) says the Caltavuturo district is the most mountainous part of the island and "the most suitable during the hot Sicilian summers".

This post covers what to decide before you go: whether it suits your level, whether it really is cooler in summer, how to drive there, and the rules inside the nature park. For other Sicilian crags see [Palermo](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-palermo-en), [San Vito lo Capo](/posts/climbing/2026-10-18-italy-crag-san-vito-lo-capo-en) and [Rocche del Crasto](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-rocche-del-crasto-en); for a season comparison of Italian crags, see the [series overview](/posts/climbing/2026-10-18-italy-crags-overview-en).

## What kind of crag is it

**Three single-pitch sport crags.** [AllClimb](https://allclimb.com/en/guides/sicily/caltavuturo/) says that besides the walls with mountaineering routes, Caltavuturo has three sport-climbing areas:

- **Gazzara:** a big wall at the mouth of a gorge, split into sectors A, B and C. [theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/central-and-southern-italy/sicily/area/517758468) says sectors A and B on the left (east) get the most traffic, and routes nobody climbs get overgrown with lichen.
- **Cabeci:** upper and lower sectors with routes of all grades and plenty of greenery.
- **Rocca di Sciara:** behind the town's sports ground; AllClimb calls it "Simple routes on slabs". The [Madonie park's points-of-interest page](https://www.parks.it/parco.madonie/pun_dettaglio.php?id_pun=2232) describes Rocca di Sciara as a geosite with a natural climbing wall equipped on its south-east side, and the [park leaflet](http://parcodellemadonie.it/wp-content/uploads/2010/04/SentieroRoccadiSciara_INGLESE.pdf) says the ridge drops about 300 m above the town.

**Size.** Counts by site (checked October 2026):

| Source | Gazzara | Cabeci | Rocca di Sciara | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/central-and-southern-italy/sicily/area/644885418) | 134 routes (A 42, B 47, C 45) | 51 routes | 23 routes | 208 routes, 4a to 8a |
| [Climbook](https://climbook.com/aree/2546/madonie) | 135 routes | 49 routes | 23 routes | — |

The routes are short: theCrag gives average heights of 20 m at Gazzara, 19 m at Cabeci and 13 m at Rocca di Sciara.

**Who it suits.** People visiting Sicily in summer who want somewhere cooler for single pitches. Most of the most-repeated routes on Climbook are 5b to 6a+, so there is plenty for lower and mid grades; most routes of 7a and up have no repeats logged at all. Nearby, Monte d'Oro at Collesano and Passo Scuro at Castelbuono are other small Madonie crags (Climbook lists 32 and 20 routes).

## When to go

| Spot | Source | Season and aspect |
|---|---|---|
| Caltavuturo district | [UKC](https://www.ukclimbing.com/articles/destinations/sicily-14498) | The best place in Sicily for the hot summers; across the island, winter (December to March) is cold inland |
| Caltavuturo town | [theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/central-and-southern-italy/sicily/area/644885418) | 635 m above sea level, "the place to cool down if it gets too hot at the coast" |
| Gazzara | [AllClimb](https://allclimb.com/en/guides/sicily/caltavuturo/), [theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/central-and-southern-italy/sicily/area/517758468) | Faces north-east; the base of the wall is at about 620–720 m |
| Cabeci | [AllClimb](https://allclimb.com/en/guides/sicily/caltavuturo/) | Faces north-east, lots of greenery, "it's nice to climb in the heat" |
| Rocca di Sciara | [parks.it](https://www.parks.it/parco.madonie/pun_dettaglio.php?id_pun=2232) | South-east side |

Gazzara and Cabeci face north-east, so they are out of the sun in the afternoon and are the first choice in summer; Rocca di Sciara faces south-east and gets the morning sun. That is worked out from the aspects; the sources don't give sun hours for each crag.

## Getting there

| | Public transport | Driving |
|---|---|---|
| Start | Palermo or Catania airport | A19 Palermo–Catania motorway |
| Middle | We found no operator timetable to Caltavuturo | Tremonzelli exit, then towards Caltavuturo |
| Last stretch | — | Gazzara: park by the crucifix; Cabeci: uphill from the SS120; Rocca di Sciara: behind the sports ground |

### Public transport

UKC says overland public transport in Sicily involves long journeys and long waits and recommends renting a car. We did not find an operator timetable for a Palermo–Caltavuturo bus, and [SAIS's Palermo routes page](https://www.saisautolinee.it/en/sicily-tpl-lines/palermo) does not list Caltavuturo. If you want to try by bus, ask the Caltavuturo tourist office (phone 0921-541759, from the park leaflet).

### Driving

The park leaflet says: leave the A19 Palermo–Catania motorway at the Tremonzelli exit and head for Caltavuturo; in town, go past the sports field and turn right at the junction to reach the start of the Rocca di Sciara geological trail. AllClimb says the Rocca di Sciara crag is just behind the sports ground.

### Parking and approach

- **Gazzara:** AllClimb gives 10–15 minutes from the parking by the crucifix: down the path into the gorge, across the river, then up the slope. theCrag also says you walk to the gorge and cross the river. After heavy rain the river may be higher (our guess; the sources don't say), so ask locals in the wet season.
- **Cabeci:** AllClimb says to turn off the SS120 and climb a vegetated slope for 15–20 minutes.
- **Rocca di Sciara:** behind the sports ground, with almost no walk.

## Rules to know before you go

**Madonie nature park.** Caltavuturo is one of the 15 municipalities covered by the Madonie regional nature park ([Sicily Hiking](https://www.sicilyhiking.it/riserve-e-parchi/parco-delle-madonie-le-regole-per-visitare-e-rispettare-il-parco/), second-hand), and AllClimb places Gazzara in the "park Gazzara-Caltavuturo". The park rules summarised by Sicily Hiking include no camping or bivouacking, no collecting rocks or fossils, and no unauthorised motor vehicles. The park has not published any climbing-specific rules; if in doubt, ask the Madonie park office (Petralia Sottana, phone 0921 684011, from the [park leaflet](http://parcodellemadonie.it/wp-content/uploads/2010/04/SentieroRoccadiSciara_INGLESE.pdf)).

**Climbing on Rocca di Sciara is park-approved.** The park's page says outright that on the south-east side "è stata attrezzata una parete naturale d'arrampicata" (a natural climbing wall has been equipped).

**Lichen and bolts on quiet routes.** theCrag says unclimbed routes at Gazzara get overgrown with lichen; on Climbook, more than ten of Rocca di Sciara's 23 routes are still marked "Progetto" (project) and none has a logged repeat. No source covers the state of the bolts on little-climbed routes, so judge them yourself.

**Rescue.** All of Sicily uses the single emergency number 112 ([Ministry of the Interior, 2019](https://www.interno.gov.it/it/notizie/attivo-anche-palermo-numero-emergenza-unico-europeo-112)).

## Routes by level

YDS and V-grade conversions follow the [Mountain Project international grade comparison chart](https://www.mountainproject.com/international-climbing-grades) and are approximate; "approx." marks grades the sources give without a/b/c (such as 4 or 5+). The original grade is the one that counts.

The tables come from the most-repeated lists and route lists on Climbook's crag pages (Climbook says it updated them in January 2024 from the 2022 edition of *Di Roccia di Sole*). Repeat counts are in single figures, so treat them only as relative popularity; where Climbook's "suggested grade" differs from the original, it is in the notes.

## Where to look things up

- **[theCrag: Caltavuturo](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/central-and-southern-italy/sicily/area/644885418)**: route counts, heights and logged ascents for the three crags.
- **[Climbook: Madonie](https://climbook.com/aree/2546/madonie)**: route lists and most-repeated lists for the crags at Caltavuturo, Collesano and Castelbuono.
- **[AllClimb: Caltavuturo](https://allclimb.com/en/guides/sicily/caltavuturo/)**: aspects, approaches and paid topos (app); also mentions the town's annual climbing festival.
- **[Madonie park: Rocca di Sciara](https://www.parks.it/parco.madonie/pun_dettaglio.php?id_pun=2232)** and the **[park leaflet](http://parcodellemadonie.it/wp-content/uploads/2010/04/SentieroRoccadiSciara_INGLESE.pdf)**: the geology trail, directions and phone numbers.
- **Guidebooks:** Grades and route counts here come mainly from online databases; the guidebook is the authority.
  - [*Di Roccia di Sole*](https://www.versantesud.it/it/negozio/catalogue/di-roccia-di-sole_1399/) (Massimo Cappuccio, Giuseppe Gallo, Versante Sud, 2022, separate Italian, English and German editions, 560 pages, Italian ISBN 978-88-85475-83-0): single-pitch climbing across Sicily; Climbook's Caltavuturo data was updated from this 2022 edition. It is the only printed guide we found that covers Caltavuturo.
