> 🌏 [中文版](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-marmolada)

The Marmolada is the highest mountain in the Dolomites; its main summit, Punta Penia, is over 3,300 m, on the border between Veneto and Trentino. The north side is glacier and ski runs. The south side is one huge limestone wall that Italians call the "Parete d'argento" (silver wall). [Planetmountain](https://www.planetmountain.com/it/notizie/alpinismo/la-sud-della-marmolada-arrampicare-sulla-parete-d-argento-della-regina-delle-dolomiti.html) describes it as "1000 metri d'altezza per tre chilometri di favoloso calcare", about an hour from the car park at Malga Ciapela.

This post helps you decide whether your level and speed are up to a 20-plus-pitch alpine route, which months the hut and the cable car are open, how to reach Malga Ciapela without a car, and what the 2022 serac collapse means for the descent. For seasons and regions across Italy, see the [series overview](/posts/climbing/2026-10-18-italy-crags-overview-en). For short multi-pitch practice in the same area, see [Cinque Torri](/posts/climbing/2026-10-18-italy-crag-cinque-torri-en). For a north-west-facing big wall with looser rock, see the [Civetta north-west face](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-civetta-en).

## What kind of crag is it

**The wall.** The south face runs from Punta Penia in the west, past Punta Rocca, east to Marmolada d'Ombretta and Monte Serauta. Planetmountain notes a big halfway ledge (the cengia mediana) that splits most routes in two, and says most lines are "difficili o molto difficili". [theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/dolomites/agordino/area/5101994994) marks it "All day sun"; the whole face looks south.

**Size.** The numbers each source gives (checked October 2026):

| Source | Number | Original wording |
|---|---|---|
| [Versante Sud guidebook](https://www.versantesud.it/en/shop/catalogue/marmolada_1202/) | about 200 routes | "approximately 200 routes" |
| [UKC Logbook](https://ukclimbing.com/logbook/crags/marmolada-2645) | over 200 routes | "over 200 known routes on the Marmolada's south face alone" |
| theCrag | about 200 routes, about 5,000 pitches | "about 200 routes", "ca. 5000 pitches" |
| [Rifugio Falier](https://www.rifugiofalier.altervista.org/) | over 180 routes | "oltre 180 vie" |
| Planetmountain | over a hundred | "più di un centinaio di vie" |

**Style.** This is alpine trad, not a sport crag. theCrag says you should expect to place your own gear on most pitches and to back up some anchors, with the hard sections usually protected by pegs left by earlier parties: "pitons are often favoured over bolts". UKC also notes that the in-situ pegs keep changing, so it sticks with the guidebooks' UIAA grades. Most routes are 550–900 m long, roughly 20 to 30 pitches (see the route table).

**Who it suits.** Climbers who already move efficiently on 10-plus-pitch trad routes in the Dolomites or other mountain areas, can build anchors and know how to retreat. If this would be your first time on dolomite or your first alpine multi-pitch, shorter routes such as those at Cinque Torri are a more realistic start.

**History.** Planetmountain dates the first ascent of the face to 1901 (Bettega, Zagonel, Tomasson); over the following century the wall tracked the development of climbing in the Dolomites and the Alps.

## When to go

**Summer, set by the hut and the lift.** Two things decide the real season:

| Item | 2026 | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Rifugio Falier (hut below the face, 2,074 m) | opened 10 June | [hut website](https://www.rifugiofalier.altervista.org/) (no closing date given; theCrag says "20 june - 20 sept") |
| Marmolada cable car (Malga Ciapela–Serauta–Punta Rocca) | 12 June to 27 September; first ride up 09:00 from Malga Ciapela, last ride down 16:30 from Punta Rocca | [Funivie Marmolada](https://www.funiviemarmolada.com/en/opening-prices/) |

Once the lift has stopped, routes that top out near Punta Rocca leave you only the glacier or the ski runs for the descent (see "Getting there").

**Afternoon storms.** Planetmountain puts it plainly: "è una parete severa". Go only in good weather with a stable forecast, because the main summit is over 3,300 m and the north-side glacier helps build storms that are often very violent.

**Wet exits and verglas.** The same article warns that early in the season the exits are often wet from glacier melt, and sometimes the rock is coated in a thin film of ice (vetrato).

## Getting there

| | Public transport | Driving |
|---|---|---|
| To the Agordino area | Train to Belluno, then Dolomiti Bus line 1 to Agordo and line 3 to Alleghe/Caprile | From Agordo via Cencenighe, Alleghe and Caprile towards Passo Fedaia, or from Canazei over Passo Fedaia |
| To Malga Ciapela | Dolomiti Bus line 54 in summer (ran until 9 September in 2026); essentially no service in other months | On the Passo Fedaia road |
| To the face | Path 610, about 1 h 15 min to Rifugio Falier | Same; or drive the campsite road to the no-entry sign and park |

### Public transport

[Dolomiti Bus](https://dolomitibus.it/it/linee-extraurbane-invernale-2026) runs line 1 Belluno–Agordo and line 3 Agordo–Caprile–Arabba in the Agordino area. The line 3 [timetable](https://dolomitibus.it/files/orari/invernali/Linea03_S26_v1.pdf) valid from 10 September 2026 has a first weekday bus from Agordo at 06:02, via Alleghe to Caprile.

Between Caprile and Malga Ciapela there is a bus only in summer. [Autobusweb](https://www.autobusweb.com/dolomiti-bus-servizio-estivo-dolomiti-val-cordevole/) (secondary: a transport news site reporting Dolomiti Bus's announcement) says line 54 Falcade–Alleghe–Caprile–Sottoguda–Malga Ciapela–Pian di Fedaia returned in summer 2026, with five return trips on weekdays and three on holidays, running until 9 September. The autumn/winter [line 4 timetable](https://dolomitibus.it/files/orari/invernali/Linea04_S26_v1.pdf) (from 10 September 2026) lists Malga Ciapela only among the optional stops, and none of its departures go there. Summer timetables are published fresh each year, so check the Dolomiti Bus seasonal lines page for the current version; out of season you need a car or a local taxi.

### Driving and parking

Planetmountain's route pages give two road approaches: leave the A22 motorway at Ora, drive through Val di Fiemme and Val di Fassa to Canazei, and cross Passo Fedaia down to Malga Ciapela; or come up from Agordo via Cencenighe, Alleghe and Caprile.

theCrag's parking note: from Malga Ciapela, follow the road to the campsite and the agriturismo up to the "accesso divieto" no-entry sign, where it turns into a dirt road, and park there. It is the closer start for routes on the eastern half of the face.

### Approach: Rifugio Falier

[Planetmountain's Pesce page](https://www.planetmountain.com/it/itinerari/via-attraverso-il-pesce-weg-durch-den-fisch-marmolada-d-ombretta.html) gives about 1 h 15 min from the Malga Ciapela car park to Rifugio Falier on path 610; theCrag says the path passes Malga d'Ombretta (1,904 m). From the hut to the starts, Planetmountain's Don Quixote page says about an hour. For the Punta Penia routes, continue on path 610 towards Rifugio Contrin.

**Where to stay.** Rifugio Falier belongs to the CAI Venice section. Its 2026 notice says booking is "sempre raccomandata" and compulsory for groups of more than 5–6, and warns that the hut's phone line has problems, so email is the better way to reach it. theCrag also lists the Bivacco Marco dal Bianco at Passo Ombretta (9 beds).

### Descent

This is the part people forget when planning. theCrag gives two options:

1. Take the cable car from Punta Rocca or the Serauta mid-station back to Malga Ciapela. In 2026 the last ride down from Punta Rocca was at 16:30 (Funivie Marmolada).
2. Walk down towards Serauta, turn onto the ski runs and follow them to Passo Fedaia, then get back to Malga Ciapela (theCrag suggests two cars or hitchhiking).

Several Planetmountain route pages describe the same descent: two 50 m abseils to the glacier, then the ski runs to the mid-station. Planetmountain's overview adds that if you miss the last cable car, the alternative is walking down the glacier, "magari in scarpette" (possibly still in climbing shoes).

## Rules to know before you go

**The 2022 serac collapse and the north-side "attention area".** On 3 July 2022 a serac near Punta Rocca collapsed and killed 11 people. The mayor of Canazei closed the north side, and lifted the closure in November 2022. The [press release](https://www.ufficiostampa.provincia.tn.it/Comunicati/Marmolada-revocata-la-zona-rossa) from the Autonomous Province of Trento says that, at the same time, a new "area di attenzione" was defined on the north side: the ice cap and its two main tongues, down to Lake Fedaia. The province keeps monitoring it and can impose a new access ban there if long spells of high temperatures return. The same release says the two north-side huts, Capanna ghiacciaio Marmolada and Pian dei Fiacconi, cannot be used, and that the summit paths would be rerouted away from the most critical zone.

Why it matters on the south face: routes that top out on Punta Rocca or Punta Penia descend over the north-side glacier or ski runs when the lift is not running. As of October 2026 there was no new ban. Before a summer trip, check the Comune di Canazei or the Province of Trento for notices, plan around the last cable car, and do not treat the glacier as your default way down.

**Gear.** Planetmountain recommends two ropes of at least 60 m and a rack of cams and nuts. The Pesce page adds pegs, 5–6 Kevlar cords for threading the rock's hourglass holes (clessidre) and some long slings. UKC, quoting Rockfax, says Pesce's steep slabs are notoriously hard to protect, with few fixed pegs of dubious quality, and that most parties benefit from a couple of skyhooks.

**Retreat is hard.** UKC says that abseiling off Pesce from the fish-shaped niche or the halfway ledge is possible but "a major undertaking", and most parties go to the top. The same applies to other long lines: past the halfway ledge, topping out is often more practical than going down, so set your turnaround time before you start.

**Rescue.** [CNSAS](https://www.cnsas.it/en/distress-call/), the Italian mountain rescue service, says to call 112 or the local rescue number; the [CNSAS Veneto](https://www.cnsas.veneto.it/contatti) page says "IN CASO DI EMERGENZA CHIAMARE IL 118".

**Registration and fees.** None of the sources mention a climbing registration or an access fee. The cable car and hut nights are paid; see their websites for prices.

## Routes by level

YDS and V-grade conversions follow the [Mountain Project international grade comparison chart](https://www.mountainproject.com/international-climbing-grades) and are approximate; "approx." marks grades the sources give without a/b/c (such as 4 or 5+). The original grade is the one that counts.

Almost every route on the face is graded in UIAA, and neither the guidebook nor Planetmountain gives a French-grade breakdown. The four French-grade bands therefore hold only the few modern routes that have French grades; the rest are in the trad table at the end. The grade is only the hardest pitch: the length of 20-plus pitches, placing your own gear and the alpine setting are the real barrier.

### Beginner: up to 6a (YDS 5.10a and below)

Not listed in the sources. The face has no French-graded beginner routes; for UIAA IV+ to V classics, see the trad table below.

### Intermediate: 6a+ to 6c+ (YDS 5.10b–5.11c)

Not listed in the sources. For UIAA VI to VII classics (Don Quixote, Schwalbenschwanz, Vinatzer), see the trad table below.
