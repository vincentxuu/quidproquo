The Italian side of Mont Blanc (Monte Bianco in Italian) is at Courmayeur, at the far western end of the Aosta Valley. Climbers come here in two very different ways. One is to ride the [Skyway Monte Bianco](https://www.montebianco.com/en/info/cableway-access-schedules) cable car to Punta Helbronner at 3,466 m and cross the glacier to the row of red granite towers on the south-east side of Mont Blanc du Tacul. The other is to drive into Val Ferret and walk up the moraines into the cirques for long routes.

This post helps you decide three things: whether your grade and alpine experience are enough, which months the cable car runs, and how to get there without a car. This is not a single-pitch crag; glaciers, rockfall and weather are risks you do not meet at a normal crag. For a season comparison of Italian crags, see the [series overview](/posts/climbing/2026-10-18-italy-crags-overview-en); for what a mountain guide is and how to find one, see the site's [IFMGA series overview](/posts/climbing/2026-10-17-ifmga-mountain-guide-overview-en).

## What kind of crag is it

**Rock.** All Mont Blanc granite: cracks, corners and steep towers. SummitPost describes [Grand Capucin](https://www.summitpost.org/grand-capucin/150273) as a red granite tower rising out of the Géant glacier, with faces no higher than 490 m, climbing that is close to vertical, "something quite exceptional on granite".

**Two areas.**

- **Tacul satellites above the Skyway:** Grand Capucin (3,838 m), Pic Adolphe Rey, Roi du Siam near Petit Capucin, Trident du Tacul, and Rognon Vaudano below the Dent du Géant. From Rifugio Torino it is 25 minutes to 1.5 hours across the glacier. The area sits on the Italy–France border; [Planetmountain's route database](https://www.planetmountain.com/it/itinerari/vie-di-arrampicata?gruppo=Monte%20Bianco) files most of the satellites under France, but the Skyway from the Italian side is the shortest way in.
- **Val Ferret cirques:** a row of small cirques north of the valley floor, such as Mont Greuvetta and Tour des Jorasses. Publisher JMEditions notes that each cirque has a small bivouac hut and only the Triolet cirque has a staffed hut, the Dalmazzi ([Rock+Run](https://rockrun.com/products/mont-blanc-granite-volume-6)). Approaches are two hours or more from the valley.

**Scale.** There is no reliable total route count. [theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/northern-italy/val-daosta/area/349563810) lists only 214 routes under "Mont Blanc" (checked October 2026), clearly incomplete; the 2025 guidebook *Mont Blanc Granite* volume 6 has 248 pure rock multi-pitch routes in Val Ferret alone ([Climb Europe](https://climb-europe.com/products/mont-blanc-granite-guidebook-topo-val-ferret)).

**Who it suits.** Mountain Project says of Grand Capucin: "No route easier than sustained 5.10" ([Mountain Project](https://www.mountainproject.com/area/113485557/grand-capucin)). Classic routes on the satellites are mostly 6a to 7c and 200–300 m long, with gear you place yourself, and you need basic use of crampons and an ice axe. The Courmayeur guides' Grand Capucin trip lists ice axe, crampons and mountain boots in its kit ([Società delle Guide di Courmayeur](https://www.guidecourmayeur.com/en/scheda_valle.php?id_prg=60&id_stag=1)). If you have only climbed single-pitch sport and never travelled on a glacier, go with a guide, not on your own.

## When to go

**The cable car sets the season.** The Skyway is the only practical way up to the satellites, so its timetable is the outer limit of the rock season:

| Period (Skyway site, checked October 2026) | Status |
|---|---|
| 23 May 2026 | Reopened after maintenance; the second stage (Pavillon–Punta Helbronner) only from around midday ([La Stampa](https://www.lastampa.it/aosta/2026/05/20/news/riapre_skyway-15629032/)) |
| 14 September–15 November 2026 | First car 8:30, last descent 15:30 (16:30 at weekends) |
| 16 November–4 December 2026 | Closed |
| 19 April–21 May 2027 | Closed |
| 26 June–29 August 2027 | First car 6:30, last descent 17:30 (18:00 in August) |

The [Skyway timetable](https://www.montebianco.com/en/info/cableway-access-schedules) is the authority, and the site notes that runs can change for technical or weather reasons. Only in high summer does a 6:30 first car make a day trip realistic; before late June the first car is 7:30 or later, and most parties stay at Rifugio Torino. The Skyway page says the hut is "closed until end of May" ([Skyway: Rifugio Torino](https://www.montebianco.com/en/activity/rifugio-torino)).

**The actual rock season.** CAI Seregno's record of the Salluard route gives the period as "Estate" (summer) ([CAI Seregno](https://www.caiseregno.it/relazione/pic-adolphe-rey-salluard)). South-facing towers warm up only around midday on a sunny day; Planetmountain notes the sun reaches Camalot on Rognon Vaudano only "dopo le 11:00", after 11 a.m. ([Planetmountain](https://www.planetmountain.com/it/itinerari/camalot-rognon-vaudano-monte-bianco.html)).

**Weather.** SummitPost warns that the weather changes quickly and that being caught on the summit in a storm is "a great and serious danger". Before going, check the conditions reports from [Fondazione Montagna Sicura](https://www.fondazionemontagnasicura.org/en/mountain-conditions), a free service run by Courmayeur's safety foundation with the Chamonix high-mountain office.

## Getting there

### Public transport

**The railway is currently closed.** The Aosta–Ivrea line has been shut since January 2024 for electrification, with replacement buses. A 2026 notice from the regional government says Ivrea–Pont-Saint-Martin should reopen first on 11 January 2027, with trains to Aosta by spring 2027 ([Regione Valle d'Aosta](https://trasporti.regione.vda.it/notizie/2026/riapertura-della-tratta-ferroviaria-ivrea-chivasso-2); checked October 2026). Local paper CityNext reported in October 2026 that the rail users' association says there is still no date for the Aosta section ("non esiste una data") ([CityNext](https://citynext.it/2026/10/03/treni-ivrea-aosta-tre-anni-di-stop-da-gennaio-2027-riparte-solo-la-tratta-fino-a-pont-saint-martin/)).

**To Courmayeur and the Skyway.** Buses around Courmayeur are run by Arriva ([regional list of bus operators](https://trasporti.regione.vda.it/trasporto-su-gomma/trasporto-pubblico-locale/orari-autobus)). The Skyway site says Courmayeur has buses to Aosta and to the big cities of northern Italy, with Autolinee SAVDA as the contact ([Skyway: How to reach](https://www.montebianco.com/en/info/how-to-reach-skyway-monte-bianco)). The Skyway valley station is outside Courmayeur near the tunnel entrance, not in the town centre. Geneva airport is about 106 km away through the Mont Blanc Tunnel.

**To Val Ferret.** The Aosta Valley tourist board says summer buses run to Planpincieux, Lavachey and Arp Nouvaz from June to September ([lovevda](https://www.lovevda.it/it/scoprire/aree-turistiche/monte-bianco/val-ferret)).

### Driving

Take the A5 motorway (Torino–Aosta–Courmayeur) or the SS26. From France or Switzerland, use the Mont Blanc Tunnel or the Great St Bernard Tunnel.

### Parking

**Skyway.** The valley station has 300 covered spaces plus outdoor parking; the covered car park opens half an hour before the first car. Rates: first 30 minutes free, 1–5 hours €2 per hour, 13–24 hours €15, multi-day stays €15 per day ([Skyway: Parking](https://www.montebianco.com/en/info/parking-rates-and-useful-information); checked October 2026).

**Val Ferret.** In summer cars can go only as far as Lavachey, using the three paid public car parks at Planpincieux, Pra Sec and Lavachey, 260 spaces in total, charged 9:00–19:00; when they are full you cannot get in. Vehicles are banned beyond Lavachey to Arp Nouvaz (lovevda). From 22 May 2026 the Planpincieux–Lavachey stretch reopened; parking on the roadside is banned around the clock along the valley and offending cars are towed ([AostaSera](https://aostasera.it/notizie/societa/val-ferret-e-val-veny-riaperta-la-viabilita/)).

## Rules to know before you go

**Glacier and crevasses.** From Punta Helbronner to the Combe Maudit below Grand Capucin you cross a glacier; Mountain Project warns of "significant crevasse-fall hazard". Rope up, carry crampons and an axe, and know crevasse rescue. CAI Seregno's Salluard record says the approach has to avoid the "grossi ed evidenti crepacci" (big, obvious crevasses).

**Skyway rules.** Ordinary visitors may stay at Punta Helbronner for only 1.5 hours; climbers are exempt (Skyway timetable page). On 18 October 2026 the Skyway alerts page said the only routes starting there are mountaineering routes, unmarked, unchecked and unprotected against rockfall or avalanches; the same day it posted that the Thoula staircase is closed and its use forbidden ([Skyway: Alerts](https://www.montebianco.com/en/info/alerts)).

**Rockfall and loose blocks.** SummitPost says rockfall on Grand Capucin's routes is "very dangerous". A concrete example: Planetmountain's Calcul du Roi (Roi du Siam) page records that on 15 July 2024 climbers found a huge loose slab poised above the third belay, and strongly advises against this route and the one immediately to its left ([Planetmountain](https://www.planetmountain.com/it/itinerari/calcul-du-roi-roi-du-siam-petit-capucin.html)). Planetmountain's Via degli Svizzeri page also says to check the abseil anchors, especially early in the season.

**Rescue.** The emergency number in the Aosta Valley is 112; the Soccorso Alpino Valdostano (mountain rescue) also has a free line, 800.800.319. When you get through, say where you are, who you are and your mobile number, and what happened ([Regione Valle d'Aosta](https://www.regione.vda.it/protezione_civile/centrale_unica_soccorso/chiederesoccorso_i.aspx)).

**Rescue costs (secondary source).** Outdoor magazine Outdoortest, summarising a Senate document in January 2026, writes that the Aosta Valley charges €120 per flight minute, capped at €3,500, for an "inappropriate" helicopter call-out, and the full cost for a completely unjustified call ([Outdoortest](https://outdoortest.it/quando-si-devono-pagare-i-soccorsi-in-montagna/); the regional decision itself was not found). Genuine injuries taken to hospital are not charged this way. Before going, check that your insurance covers alpine climbing and helicopter rescue.

**Hire a guide.** The Courmayeur guides' Grand Capucin Swiss-route trip cost €1,008 for one person when checked in October 2026, excluding hut half board (€65) and cable car tickets; it can also be done in a day with the first car. Without glacier experience, this is the realistic option.

## Routes by level

YDS and V-grade conversions follow the [Mountain Project international grade comparison chart](https://www.mountainproject.com/international-climbing-grades) and are approximate; "approx." marks grades the sources give without a/b/c (such as 4 or 5+). The original grade is the one that counts.

Everything here is alpine multi-pitch, and the grade column gives the hardest pitch; "obligatory" is the minimum you must climb free without aid or pulling on draws. Grades and lengths below come mainly from Planetmountain route pages (many written by local mountain guides) and Mountain Project. Sources often differ by a full grade on the same route; go with the guidebook.

### Beginner: up to 6a (YDS 5.10a and below)

There are almost no rock routes of 6a and under on the satellites. Planetmountain lists the north-west ridge of the Aiguille Croux on the Val Veny side, a mountaineering-style ridge. If you want a first taste of granite above a glacier, the Dent du Géant normal route (see the UIAA table below) is another option, but it is a full alpine climb, not a crag.

| Route | Grade | YDS | Length / pitches | Sector | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Aiguille Croux Cresta NW | 5b | 5.8 | Ridge | Val Veny | Planetmountain gives 5b/4c; "poco sostenuta", superb granite on the upper part |

### Intermediate: 6a+ to 6c+ (YDS 5.10b–5.11c)

This is the busiest grade band on the satellites. The closest is Rognon Vaudano, 25–30 minutes from Rifugio Torino; Grand Capucin and Pic Adolphe Rey are about 1.5 hours across the glacier.

| Route | Grade | YDS | Length / pitches | Sector | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Camalot](https://www.planetmountain.com/it/itinerari/camalot-rognon-vaudano-monte-bianco.html) | 6a+ (obligatory 5c) | 5.10b | 200 m, 7 pitches | Rognon Vaudano | Planetmountain: opened 2024; single 60 m rope, cams 0.3–2; abseil the route |
| [Bettembourg-Thivierge](https://www.planetmountain.com/it/itinerari/bettembourg-thivierge-pic-adolphe-rey.html) | 6b (obligatory 5+) | 5.10c | 250 m | Pic Adolphe Rey, south face | First ascent 1975; Planetmountain: "forse la via più bella dei satelliti", perhaps the finest satellite route at this grade; cams to #3.5 |
| [Via degli Svizzeri](https://www.planetmountain.com/it/itinerari/via-degli-svizzeri-grand-capucin.html) | 6b (hardest pitch) | 5.10c | 300 m, 12 pitches | Grand Capucin, south face | First ascent 1956; Courmayeur guides give max 6b; Mountain Project 6a (with the O Sole Mio exit, 14 votes); Planetmountain ED-, max 7b, obligatory 5+; the easiest way to the summit |
| Cric e Croc | 6b+ | 5.10d | About 150 m | Rognon Vaudano | Planetmountain: cracks and technical slab below the Dent du Géant |
| [Dromi](https://www.planetmountain.com/it/itinerari/dromi-petit-greuvetta.html) | 6b+ (obligatory 6a) | 5.10d | 600 m | Petit Greuvetta (Val Ferret) | First ascent 1996; 12 mm bolts on the lower slabs, sparse protection in the upper cracks; 2 hours from Lavachey |
| Bonne Ethique | 6c+ (hardest pitch) | 5.11c | Multi-pitch | Trident du Tacul | Planetmountain ED; the 6c+ fourth pitch depends on finger size |
| [Calcul du Roi](https://www.planetmountain.com/it/itinerari/calcul-du-roi-roi-du-siam-petit-capucin.html) | 6c (or 6a+/A0), obligatory 6a | hardest 5.11a/b | 300 m, 10 pitches | Roi du Siam (Petit Capucin) | ⚠️ Loose slab found above belay 3 in July 2024; Planetmountain strongly advises against it |

### Advanced: 7a to 7c+ (YDS 5.11d–5.13a)

The historic routes on Grand Capucin's east face are in this band. Planetmountain warns that retreat from Eau et gaz à toutes les étages is practically impossible above the big roof.

| Route | Grade | YDS | Length / pitches | Sector | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Astro Zap | 7a | 5.11d | 1 pitch | Trident du Tacul | Planetmountain: opened 2013 by Bassanini and Camurri, the crack the Bonatti direct avoids |
| C'era una volta il West | 7a+ | 5.12a | Multi-pitch | Rognon Vaudano | Planetmountain: crack line, last two pitches "incredibile" |
| Elixir d'Astaroth | 7a+ (plus A2 aid) | 5.12a | Multi-pitch | Grand Capucin | Planetmountain ED+; the most direct line on the face, not every belay fixed; Mountain Project 7b+ |
| Bonatti-Ghigo | 7b | 5.12b | 15 pitches | Grand Capucin, east face | Mountain Project classic; first ascent July 1951 by Bonatti and Ghigo (SummitPost) |
| Cenerentolo | 7b | 5.12b | Multi-pitch | Aiguille de la Brenva, south-east face | Planetmountain: opened ground-up; needs full command of the obligatory grade and trad gear |
| Empire State Building | 7c (hardest pitch) | 5.12d | Multi-pitch | Clocher du Tacul | Planetmountain ED+, more technical than athletic; the crack pitch is hard without jamming skills |
| Eau et gaz à toutes les étages | 7c (hardest pitch) | 5.12d | Multi-pitch | Grand Capucin | Planetmountain ED+, very exposed; no retreat above the big roof |

## Where to look things up

- **Guidebooks:** Grades and route counts here come mainly from online databases; the guidebook is the authority.
  - [*Mont Blanc. The complete rock climbing guide. Italian Side*](https://www.versantesud.it/en/shop/catalogue/mont-blanc_1334/) (Fabrizio Calebasso, Matteo Pasquetto, Versante Sud, 2020, English, 447 pages): the whole Italian side, from Val Veny and Brouillard-Freney through the central massif and the Tacul satellites to Val Ferret ([book details](https://libridimontagna.net/guide/libri-arrampicata-e-alpinismo/piemonte-e-valle-d-aosta-3/mont-blanc-rock-climbing-guide-italian-side-9788885475816)).
  - [*Mont Blanc Granite* volume 6, Val Ferret](https://rockrun.com/products/mont-blanc-granite-volume-6) (François Damilano, Julien Desecures, Louis Laurent, JMEditions, June 2025, English, 276 pages): 248 pure rock multi-pitch routes in the Val Ferret cirques, the last volume of the series. theCrag also lists the 2023 Val Veny volume of the same series (116 routes, including the Skyway area).

- **[Fondazione Montagna Sicura conditions](https://www.fondazionemontagnasicura.org/en/mountain-conditions):** current glacier and route conditions.

- **[Mountain Project: Grand Capucin](https://www.mountainproject.com/area/113485557/grand-capucin)**, **[theCrag: Mont Blanc](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/northern-italy/val-daosta/area/349563810):** English-language route ratings and approach notes.
