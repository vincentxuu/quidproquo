The Pale di San Martino rise above the Primiero valley on the eastern edge of Trentino. The [San Martino di Castrozza tourist board](https://www.sanmartino.com/en/territory/the-italian-alps-spectacles-in-nature/the-dolomites/pale-di-san-martino) calls it "the largest mountain group in the Dolomites": a ring of summits around a limestone plateau of about 50 km² at roughly 2,700 m. The same page names the Solleder on Sass Maor, the Velo and Sass d'Ortiga arêtes and the Buhl on Cima Canali among the most famous classics in the Alps.

This post helps you decide what level these historic routes need, which hut to stay in, how to reach the trailheads without a car, and which valley crags to look at if you want single pitches. For seasons and regions across Italy, see the [series overview](/posts/climbing/2026-10-18-italy-crags-overview-en); for classic alpine towers elsewhere in Trentino, see [Catinaccio and the Vajolet Towers](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-catinaccio-vajolet-en).

## What kind of crag is it

**Two kinds of climbing.** First, the big dolomite walls of the group itself: long alpine multi-pitches where route-finding is hard and the descent is often worse than the ascent. Second, the single-pitch crags in the valleys. The [tourist board's climbing page](https://sanmartino.com/en/what-to-do/outdoor/adventure-sports/climbing) describes granite, porphyry and limestone, with "more than 1,000 routes across 53 cliffs" around the Pale, Passo Rolle, Val Canali and Val Noana; easier routes are concentrated at Passo Rolle and on the Sasso di Castel Pietra.

**Three signature classics.**

- **Spigolo del Velo** (NW arête of Cima della Madonna): [Planetmountain](https://www.planetmountain.com/en/routes/spigolo-del-velo-cima-della-madonna.html) gives the first ascent to Gunther Langes and Erwin Merlet in 1920, 430 m, "one of the best in the Dolomites"; despite countless repeats the rock is still excellent, with plenty of threads.
- **Solleder** (east face of Sass Maor): [Planetmountain](https://www.planetmountain.com/it/itinerari/solleder-sass-maor.html) gives Emil Solleder and F. Kummer, 1926, 700 m, TD; two exposed traverses avoid the steepest part of the great yellow corner. The [1935 CAI-TCI guide text reprinted by CAI Vicenza](https://organizzazione.cai.it/sez-vicenza/wp-content/uploads/sites/175/2026/09/2016-2-06-solleder.pdf) calls it sixth grade (sesto grado), about 600 m from the start.
- **Fessura Buhl** (west face of Cima Canali): [Planetmountain](https://www.planetmountain.com/it/itinerari/fessura-buhl-cima-canali.html) gives Hermann Buhl and H. Erwing, 1950, 550 m, TD+, "una tra le più grandi classiche delle Dolomiti", and warns that the descent is complex and needs careful study of the description in fog.

**Who it suits.** The big-wall classics suit climbers who have already done a few IV–V long routes in the Dolomites and can find their own way and abseil. The paths and via ferratas to Rifugio Velo della Madonna make the approach straightforward; the length and the descent are the hard part. The valley crags cover all levels, and the tourist board points beginners to the local guides (Aquile di San Martino).

## When to go

**The big walls follow the huts.** [Rifugio Pradidali](https://rifugiopradidali.com/eng/Home.html) is open "from mid June to the end of September". [Rifugio Velo della Madonna](https://rifugiovelodellamadonna.it/en) (2,358 m) belongs to CAI-SAT; its home page does not give the season's dates, so contact the hut. Once the huts close, approaching and retreating from the long routes gets harder.

**The valley crags are almost year-round.** The tourist board says the foot of the Pale "offers climbing almost all year round". Tognazza at Passo Rolle sits at about 2,000 m and faces SE ([Planetmountain: Pinne gialle](https://www.planetmountain.com/en/routes/pinne-gialle-tognazza.html)), so in practice it is a summer and autumn venue.

## Getting there

| | Public transport | Driving |
|---|---|---|
| To San Martino or Fiera di Primiero | Trentino Trasporti: San Martino–Fiera di Primiero–Feltre, San Martino–Fiera di Primiero–Trento | SS50 (Feltre–Fiera–San Martino–Passo Rolle) |
| Spigolo del Velo, Sass Maor | Walk from San Martino to Malga Zivertaghe and up | SS50, then a 3 km track to Malga Zivertaghe (1,375 m); path 713 to Rifugio Velo della Madonna in 2.5 hours |
| Cima Canali and the Val Canali walls | Sources list no bus into Val Canali | From Fiera di Primiero into Val Canali; path 709 up to Rifugio Pradidali |
| Tognazza (Passo Rolle) | Fiera–San Martino–Passo Rolle–Predazzo line | SS50 towards Passo Rolle; park in a layby or at Malga Fosse, 20-minute walk |

### Public transport

The [tourist board's public transport page](https://sanmartino.com/en/plan-your-mountain-holiday-in-italy/mobility/public-transport) lists three Trentino Trasporti routes: San Martino di Castrozza–Fiera di Primiero–Feltre, San Martino–Fiera di Primiero–Trento, and Fiera di Primiero–San Martino–Passo Rolle–Predazzo, with timetables to download on the same page. Guests with the Trentino Guest Card ride free. Feltre has a railway station.

### Driving and trailheads

- **Rifugio Velo della Madonna**: Planetmountain says to take the SS50 (Fiera di Primiero to Passo Rolle), turn onto the track to Malga Zivertaghe (3 km), then follow path 713 to the hut in 2 hours 30 minutes. The hut's website says it can also be reached from Val Canali.
- **Rifugio Pradidali** (2,278 m): park around the Cant del Gal restaurant in Val Canali (paid) and take path 709, about 2 hours (secondary: the Sass Balòss climbing blog, updated 2016).
- **Tognazza**: Planetmountain says to drive about 6 km up the SS50 from San Martino towards Passo Rolle and, where the road flattens out with the pass in view, look for a layby, or park at Malga Fosse; cross the meadows and the stream and walk up the scree to the base in about 20 minutes.

## Rules to know before you go

**Closures and bans.** The sources checked show no closure or climbing ban (October 2026).

**The descent is the crux.** For the Spigolo del Velo, Planetmountain describes following the summit ridge to the Camino Winkler, then several 20–40 m abseils and grade I–II downclimbing back to the hut, and recommends two 55 m ropes; the Solleder descent also goes round to the col between Sass Maor and Cima della Madonna and down the same gully. On Cima Canali, Planetmountain says the descent is marked with red dots and cairns throughout but needs careful study in fog; a climbing blog reports that local guides set up the Buhl belays for abseiling in 2012 (secondary).

**Rescue.** [Soccorso Alpino Trentino](https://soccorsoalpinotrentino.it/en/prevention): call 112 and give the exact location, the activity, the number of people and their condition, the weather and the number you are calling from.

## Routes by level

YDS and V-grade conversions follow the [Mountain Project international grade comparison chart](https://www.mountainproject.com/international-climbing-grades) and are approximate; "approx." marks grades the sources give without a/b/c (such as 4 or 5+). The original grade is the one that counts.

The group's classics are graded UIAA and sit in the trad table at the end. The four French-grade bands list modern routes from [Planetmountain's route list](https://www.planetmountain.com/it/itinerari/vie-di-arrampicata/pagina/1?gruppo=Pale+di+San+Martino&tipologia=38), mostly with bolted belays. Route-by-route data for the 53 valley crags is not compiled in the online sources checked; use the guidebook. The grade shown is the hardest pitch.

### Beginner: up to 6a (YDS 5.10a and below)

The sources list no French-graded routes. The tourist board says the easier single pitches are at Passo Rolle and on the Sasso di Castel Pietra; for a first long route, see the Sass Maor normal route and the Spigolo del Velo in the trad table.

## Where to look things up

- **[San Martino tourist board climbing page](https://sanmartino.com/en/what-to-do/outdoor/adventure-sports/climbing)**: overview of the valley crags and contacts for the local guides.
- **[Planetmountain: Pale di San Martino route list](https://www.planetmountain.com/it/itinerari/vie-di-arrampicata/pagina/1?gruppo=Pale+di+San+Martino&tipologia=38)**: topos and first-ascent details for classic and modern multi-pitches.
- **Guidebooks:** Grades and route counts here come mainly from online databases; the guidebook is the authority.
  - [*Pale di San Martino*](https://www.versantesud.it/it/negozio/catalogue/pale-di-san-martino_1229/) (Renzo Corona and Igor Simoni, Versante Sud, 2018, Italian, ISBN 9788898609864): 124 selected classic and modern multi-pitches; there is an [English product page](https://versantesud.it/en/shop/catalogue/pale-di-san-martino_1229) and a German edition, *Palagruppe*.
  - [*IV Grado Dolomiti Occidentali vol. 1*](https://ideamontagna.it/libro/iv-grado-dolomiti-occidentali-vol-1-3a-ed) (Emiliano Zorzi, Idea Montagna, 3rd edition, Italian, 400 pages, ISBN 978-88-85468-86-3): the [table of contents preview](https://ideamontagna.it/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/IV-Grado-Dolomiti-OCC.-1.pdf) covers Sass Maor (normal route, Castiglioni-Detassis, Solleder), Cima della Madonna (Spigolo del Velo) and Cima Canali.
  - [*Dolomiti Falesie*](https://www.versantesud.it/it/negozio/catalogue/dolomiti-falesie_1384/) (Guido Colombetti, Versante Sud, 2021): 105 sport crags across the Dolomites; the publisher's blurb names Primiero among the areas covered; listed as out of stock.
  - The chapter list of [Rockfax's *The Dolomites: Rock Climbs and Via Ferrata*](https://rockfax.com/climbing-guides/books/the-dolomites-rock-climbs-and-via-ferrata) runs from Catinaccio to the Tre Cime and does not include the Pale di San Martino.
- **[Rifugio Velo della Madonna](https://rifugiovelodellamadonna.it/en)**, **[Rifugio Pradidali](https://rifugiopradidali.com/eng/Home.html)**: opening dates and bookings.
- **[Tourist board public transport page](https://sanmartino.com/en/plan-your-mountain-holiday-in-italy/mobility/public-transport)**: Trentino Trasporti timetables to download.
