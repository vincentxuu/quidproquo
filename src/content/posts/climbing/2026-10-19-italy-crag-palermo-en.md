> 🌏 [中文版](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-palermo)

Palermo, the capital of Sicily, has two limestone mountains on its northern edge. Monte Pellegrino is inside the city itself, with rock walls all along the road to Mondello beach. Further north, Monte Gallo rises straight out of the sea and ends in the Capo Gallo headland. [Mountain Project](https://www.mountainproject.com/area/107611547/palermo-area) calls Pellegrino "the most important rock climbing area in southern Italy" and says Gallo is "similar to the Dolomites".

Many people climbing in Sicily stay at [San Vito lo Capo](/posts/climbing/2026-10-18-italy-crag-san-vito-lo-capo-en) and only pass through Palermo for the airport, but the city is worth a few days of its own. This post covers what to decide before you go: whether it suits your level and style, which months, how to get there without a car, and which areas are under rockfall orders or closed roads. For a season-and-region comparison of Italian crags, see the [series overview](/posts/climbing/2026-10-18-italy-crags-overview-en).

## What kind of crag is it

### Monte Pellegrino: single-pitch sport in the city

**A mountain inside a city.** Mountain Project says it is over 600 m high and runs several kilometres north to south, with limestone walls on every side. Most of the climbing is on the west side, across 8 major crags, single-pitch and multi-pitch up to about 200 m. [theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/central-and-southern-italy/sicily/area/2362171629) says "nine climbing sectors".

**Where climbing on the island began.** [Climbing Spot Factory (CSF)](https://www.climbingspotfactory.com/en/climbadvisor/valdesi/info_about) says Valdesi and Schiavo are the cradle of Palermo climbing, where the mountaineering schools and local climbers started after the Second World War. The west face of Valdesi averages 60 m high and is compact limestone, grey on the vertical sections and red or yellow on the steep ones, cut by cracks, corners, chimneys and small holes. [UKC Logbook](https://www.ukclimbing.com/logbook/crags/mount_pellegrino_-_roccia_dello_schiavo-12025/) says Roccia dello Schiavo is where Sicilian climbing started in the 1940s. It used to be long multi-pitch with little fixed gear. These days people mostly climb the lower pitches, and the upper sections are an unknown quantity. The lower pitches have been re-bolted, but some old gear remains.

**Steep caves and multi-pitch.** Parete dei Rotoli, above the cemetery on the north-east side, is a steep red wall full of stalactites. [Planetmountain](https://www.planetmountain.com/en/news/climbing/pompa-funebre-new-route-by-larcher-giupponi-and-sartori-on-monte-pellegrino-palermo.html) reported Rolando Larcher's team opening the 150 m Pompa Funebre here in 2011 (8b max, 7a+ obligatory), and wrote that it is steep enough to keep climbing in the rain.

### Monte Gallo: sea-cliff multi-pitch and Bauso Rosso

Mountain Project describes Monte Gallo as an isolated mountain made of several peaks, with walls up to 400 m; every route is multi-pitch, with bolted big-wall routes up to 370 m. The modern lines on Planetmountain are mostly on the seaward north and west faces, such as the 325 m Kaos and the 360 m Mona Lisa. [theCrag's Bauso Rosso page](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/bauso-rosso) calls it a "Long overhanging red cliff with excellent climbing on tufas": single pitch, 105 routes, mostly 6a and up.

The whole mountain lies in the [Capo Gallo nature reserve](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riserva_naturale_orientata_Capo_Gallo), managed by the regional forestry agency (Azienda Foreste Demaniali).

**Who it suits.** The easier and mid-grade single pitches are concentrated at Valdesi, Schiavo and Mariella. Bauso Rosso and the Pellegrino caves lean towards 7a and up. The long routes on Monte Gallo are for multi-pitch climbers who can change over, abseil and place their own gear.

| Mona Lisa (Monte Gallo) | [Planetmountain 2025](https://www.planetmountain.com/en/news/climbing/mona-lisa-added-monte-gallo-sicily.html) | Exposed to coastal weather; pick a day with a southerly wind and "avoid northerlies at all costs" |
| All of Sicily | [UKC Sicily guide](https://www.ukclimbing.com/articles/destinations/sicily-14498) | Spring and autumn are best; July and August are too hot; most rain falls in late autumn and from late winter to early spring |

**Ticks in summer.** Planetmountain's [Vento d'estate route page](https://planetmountain.com/Rock/vie/itinerari/scheda.php?id_itinerario=956&id_tipologia=38&lang=ita) warns that in hot weather there are ticks all over Monte Gallo and suggests light-coloured long trousers.

**Wait after heavy rain.** The rockfall ordinance bans entry to the rockfall zones for 24 hours after heavy rain; see the rules below.

## Getting there

| | Public transport | Driving |
|---|---|---|
| North side of Pellegrino (Valdesi, Mariella) | [AMAT](https://www.amat.pa.it/percorsi-e-orari/) bus 806 from the centre through Parco della Favorita towards Mondello; get off around Valdesi | Through Parco della Favorita towards Mondello |
| Monte Gallo (Kaos, Mona Lisa) | To Mondello, then walk | Planetmountain (2009) says drive into the reserve and park at the lighthouse |
| Monte Gallo west face (Vento d'estate) | To Sferracavallo, then walk | Park on the Sferracavallo seafront and walk Via Barcarello to the reserve gate |

### Public transport

The [UKC Sicily guide](https://www.ukclimbing.com/articles/destinations/sicily-14498) says Palermo, Catania and Trapani all have international flights, but trains and other overland public transport on the island are "not recommended"; if you want to move around, renting a car is essential. If you are only climbing in Palermo, city buses reach some of the crags.

CSF says you can reach Valdesi on bus 806. [AMAT's 806 page](https://www.amat.pa.it/linea-estiva-806-dall8-aprile-corse-per-mondello/) gives the route from piazza Don Sturzo via viale della Libertà, viale Diana, via Margherita di Savoia and piazza Valdesi to via Teti in Mondello. AMAT calls the 806 a summer line; that page says it first runs at weekends in spring and daily from May, but does not say which year. AMAT's diversion notice for mid-October 2026 still lists the 806, so it is running in autumn. Check AMAT's route and timetable page on the day.

We did not find bus information for Sferracavallo this time; search the AMAT route page before you go.

### Driving

- **Valdesi:** CSF says to cross Parco della Favorita towards Mondello, go down Viale Regina Margherita, turn right at the roundabout with the petrol station onto Via Monte Ercta, and Valdesi Centrale is on your right after the first left-hand bend. **This is a closed road**; see the rules below.
- **Kaos, Mona Lisa:** [Planetmountain's Kaos page](https://www.planetmountain.com/en/routes/kaos-monte-gallo.html) says to enter the Monte Gallo reserve from Mondello and park at the lighthouse, then follow the road and a path along the sea cliff to the base of the wall: about 25 minutes from the lighthouse, 45 from Mondello. It mentions a €5 fee to drive into the reserve; that information is from 2009.
- **Vento d'estate:** Planetmountain says to park in Sferracavallo, walk the whole of the Via Barcarello seafront to the Capo Gallo reserve entrance, turn right about 100 m past the gate onto the "Sentiero del Gabbiano", and reach the start in about 40 minutes.
- **Bauso Rosso:** theCrag gives a 5–10 minute walk-in and says the last part of the road is only passable with an off-road vehicle. [Escursionismo.it](https://www.escursionismo.it/escursioni/escursione-rno-monte-gallo-e-bauso-rosso/) says hikers park at via Tolomea in Mondello; Bauso Rosso is on the right of the road up to the forestry gate of the reserve.

### Parking

CSF says you can park on the pavement by the road at Valdesi, with room for about 30–40 cars. In June 2026 the city banned parking on both sides of the affected stretches, around the clock, for works to stabilise the rock walls above Vergine Maria and Addaura ([Ordinanza 1040/2026](https://www.comune.palermo.it/novita/mobilit-lavori-consolidamento-pareti-rocciose-area-monte-pellegrino-ordinanza-divieto-di-sosta/)); check whether the works are still going on before heading to the Addaura sectors.

## Rules to know before you go

**Entry limits in the rockfall zones (first-hand: Palermo city ordinance).** Both mountains are high-risk rockfall areas. The mayor's [Ordinanza Sindacale 157/OS](https://protezionecivile.comune.palermo.it/protezione-civile-documenti-dettaglio.php?id=17) of 25 November 2020 covers Monte Pellegrino and Monte Gallo. Its annex says that in the P3 and P4 rockfall zones mapped in the hydro-geological plan (PAI), "È VIETATO L'ACCESSO PER LA FRUIZIONE E PER LE ATTIVITÀ DI TEMPO LIBERO" (entry for recreation and leisure activities is banned):

1. while the Sicilian regional civil protection has an orange or red hydro-geological weather alert in force;
2. immediately after exceptional rainfall and for the following 24 hours;
3. immediately after a felt earthquake confirmed by the national geophysics institute (INGV) and for the following 24 hours.

The ordinance is valid until the "cessate esigenze" (until no longer needed); as of October 2026 we found no notice revoking it. A separate [Ordinanza 80/2019](https://www.comune.palermo.it/novita/regolamentazione-delluso-dei-sentieri-e-delle-aree-del-parco-della-favorita-per-caduta-massi/) covers the P4 zones of Parco della Favorita along Viale Diana and Viale Regina Margherita and restricts use from a yellow alert upwards; in October 2026 the reserve's notice page still lists it. Which sectors fall inside P3/P4 can only be read off the PAI maps attached to the ordinance. In practice: check the day's weather alert on the [city civil protection's regional alerts page](https://protezionecivile.comune.palermo.it/protezione-civile-avvisi-regionali.php) before you go, and don't plan Pellegrino or Gallo for the day after heavy rain.

**Via Monte Ercta is a closed road.** This road from Mondello up to the Santa Rosalia sanctuary has been closed for rockfall since the end of 2011, according to [a 2020 la Repubblica report](https://palermo.repubblica.it/cronaca/2020/08/11/news/sei_crolli_e_una_frana_in_pochi_anni_cosi_si_sgretola_monte_pellegrino-264379300/); the [reserve's notice page](https://riservamontepellegrino.it/news-e-avvisi/) lists Ordinanza Dirigenziale 407 of 1 April 2019, which bans pedestrian and vehicle traffic. The Valdesi approach CSF describes uses exactly this road, and CSF adds that "il divieto è ignorato da tutti" (everyone ignores the ban). That doesn't make the order any less valid; ask local climbers on the spot what the current legal approach is.

**Pavement and path closures towards Mondello.** [PalermoToday reported in December 2025](https://www.palermotoday.it/cronaca/caduta-massi-monte-pellegrino-limite-30-km-orari.html) that rock fell twice from Colle d'Allaura onto viale Margherita di Savoia, on 14 October and 14 November 2025. The mayor closed the mountain-side pavement and cycle path between via Mater Dolorosa and via Monte Ercta, set a 30 km/h speed limit, and closed the Piano Landolina path inside the reserve (Ordinanza 233/2025). Walking from the 806 stop to Valdesi takes you through this stretch; use the pavement on the other side.

**Reserve rules.** Monte Pellegrino is a regional nature reserve run by Rangers d'Italia ([reserve website](https://riservamontepellegrino.it/)). The [reserve's visitor code](https://orbs.regione.sicilia.it/images/decalogo_riserva_monte_pellegrino.pdf) says there is no medical post, water or toilets inside; camping, bivouacking and fires are banned; and in thunderstorms, strong wind or heat you should avoid passing under the rock walls. For Ferragosto 2026 (13–16 August), the mayor also banned picnics, fires and tents in the Favorita and the Pellegrino reserve ([Amica News](https://www.radioamica.it/palermo-ferragosto-2026-scatta-lordinanza-anti-bivacco-per-parco-della-favorita-monte-pellegrino-e-spiagge/), second-hand).

**Bolts and protection on Monte Gallo.** Planetmountain's route pages say Kaos has bolts that are sometimes far apart, with obligatory moves in exposed sections; Vento d'estate needs a full set of cams and nuts; Mona Lisa was bolted ground-up (S2/3), and a set of Totem cams helps on pitch 5.

**Rescue.** All of Sicily now uses the single emergency number 112; calls to 113, 115 and 118 are routed to the 112 centre too ([Ministry of the Interior, 2019](https://www.interno.gov.it/it/notizie/attivo-anche-palermo-numero-emergenza-unico-europeo-112)). Calls are free and the service is multilingual.

## Routes by level

YDS and V-grade conversions follow the [Mountain Project international grade comparison chart](https://www.mountainproject.com/international-climbing-grades) and are approximate; "approx." marks grades the sources give without a/b/c (such as 4 or 5+). The original grade is the one that counts.

The single pitches below come from the most-repeated lists on each Climbook sector page (Climbook says it updated them in January 2024 from the 2022 edition of *Di Roccia di Sole*); the multi-pitch routes come from Planetmountain and Gulliver route pages. Repeat counts are small, so treat them only as relative popularity.

### Beginner: up to 6a (YDS 5.10a and below)

Valdesi has the most routes at this level; the ten most repeated routes there on Climbook are all below 6b. Schiavo and Mariella have some too.
