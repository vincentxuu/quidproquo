Rocca Sbarua sits above Pinerolo, near Turin: a string of granitic gneiss buttresses and towers on the south side of Monte Freidur, in the municipality of Frossasco. [Versante Sud](https://www.versantesud.it/en/shop/catalogue/rocca-sbarua_1479/) notes that "Sbarua" means fear in Piedmontese dialect. Rifugio Melano, the hut at the foot of the walls, is open all year and is where everyone meets.

[Turismo Torino](https://turismotorino.org/en/visit/things-to-do-and-things-to-see/outdoor/climbing-in-turin-and-in-the-piedmont) says that this crag and the Parete dei Militi at Bardonecchia were "the gyms on which the great post-war mountaineers tried, from Boccalatte to Gervasutti to Guido Rossa". This guide covers what you need to decide before you go: what the bolts are like now, whether it suits your level, which months to go, and how to get there without a car. For a comparison of seasons and regions across Italy, see the [series overview](/posts/climbing/2026-10-18-italy-crags-overview-en); the other crag near Turin, famous for trad crack climbing, is [Valle dell'Orco](/posts/climbing/2026-10-18-italy-crag-valle-dellorco-en).

## What kind of crag is it

**Short multi-pitch on granitic gneiss, mostly rebolted.** [falesia.it](https://www.falesia.it/crag/131/rocca-sbarua.html) describes the rock as "Gneiss granitoide molto solida e rugosa" (very solid, rough granitic gneiss), with cracks, corners, friction slabs, vertical walls and roofs. [theCrag](https://thecrag.com/it/arrampicata/italy/sbarua) gives route lengths of 20 to 200 m and grades from 3+ up to unclimbed projects thought to be 8a or harder. The sectors include the central Sbarua wall (Normale, Placche Gialle), Sperone Rivero, Sperone Cinquetti, Torre del Bimbo and Freak Street.

**History.** In a [Planetmountain article](https://www.planetmountain.com/it/notizie/arrampicata/rocca-sbarua-come-preservare-larrampicata-e-la-storia-nelle-falesie.html), climber Maurizio Oviglia traces the routes. In 1929 Ettore Ellena climbed the Spigolo Ellena, an unprotected 5 m boulder move he graded V. The Gervasutti-Ronco followed in 1937, and the 1949 Spigolo Bianciotto is considered the first grade VI in Piedmont. In 1957 Guido Rossa used expansion bolts for the first time, on the Placche Gialle. The first sport routes came in the 1980s, and the 1990s brought a wave of easy-going "plaisir" routes.

**The bolts today, and the debate.** The same article says that in the 1990s "Furono rinchiodate a spit quasi tutte le classiche" (almost all the classics were rebolted), one of the few exceptions being the Via Beuchod on the Torre del Bimbo, and that on most of them the original line was also changed. Gervasutti climbed the all-crack Gervasutti-Ronco with 6 pitons; today it has far more fixed protection. Some proposed restoring about ten historic routes to their original commitment, others objected, and the hut keepers pointed out that most visitors come precisely to repeat these routes. The upshot: most classics can now be clipped like sport routes, but bolt spacing varies from route to route. Guiding company [Escape Outdoor](https://www.escapeoutdoorguides.com/blog/2020/03/20/rocca-sbarua-gems/) also warns that bolting and grades vary by route and are sometimes harder than written.

**Who it suits.** Beginner to intermediate climbers who want to practise multi-pitch, building belays and descending; also anyone who wants to climb classics from Italian mountaineering history.

## When to go

| Source | Season |
|---|---|
| [falesia.it](https://www.falesia.it/crag/131/rocca-sbarua.html) | Climbable practically all year, except briefly after heavy snowfall |
| [theCrag](https://thecrag.com/it/arrampicata/italy/sbarua) | South-facing; the rock gets wet when it rains |
| [Escape Outdoor](https://www.escapeoutdoorguides.com/blog/2020/03/20/rocca-sbarua-gems/) | Best in the shoulder seasons and in winter, with good sun |
| [Frossasco town council](https://www.comune.frossasco.to.it/ita/ana_luogo.aspx?id=4) | The hut faces south and enjoys a mild climate for most of the year |

The walls face south at about 1,100 m, so clear winter days are a good bet.

## Getting there

| | Public transport | Driving |
|---|---|---|
| Start | SFM2 train from Turin to Pinerolo | Pinerolo |
| Middle | Arriva urban line 3 Pinerolo–San Pietro Val Lemina–Talucco | SR23 towards Sestriere, then right to San Pietro Val Lemina and Talucco |
| Last stretch | Walk from Talucco to Rifugio Melano | Park at Dairin, Brun or Crò and walk 30–40 minutes to the hut |

### Public transport

Line SFM2 runs from Turin to Pinerolo; [sfmtorino](https://www.sfmtorino.it/le-linee-sfm/sfm2-pinerolo-chivasso/) says weekday trains run every 30 minutes at peak times and hourly otherwise. From May to 26 July 2026 parts of the line were replaced by buses for engineering work ([Pinerolo town notice](https://www.comune.pinerolo.to.it/media/3547)); that has ended. From Pinerolo up the hill, [Arriva](https://torino.arriva.it/en/area/pinerolo/) lists urban line 3 to San Pietro Val Lemina and Talucco; check its timetable on the Arriva site. From the bus you still have to walk to one of the parking spots before the 30–40 minute path.

### Driving

The route given by [Frossasco town council](https://www.comune.frossasco.to.it/ita/ana_luogo.aspx?id=4) and the [CAI hut page](https://www.prenotarifugi.cai.it/en/detail/Rifugio%20Melano%20Casa%20Canada/?id=6013): from central Pinerolo take the SR23 towards Sestriere, turn right following signs for San Pietro Val Lemina, then continue to Talucco; after the bridge over the Lemina stream there are three ways to park (below). Alternatively, from the Val Noce side drive from Cantalupa to the end of the tarmac at Borgata Scrivanda and walk about 1 hour 30 minutes.

### Parking

| Parking | How | Walk to the hut |
|---|---|---|
| Località Brun | Turn right after the bridge for about 1.5 km, via Colle Ciardonet | about 40 minutes |
| Borgata Dairin | Towards Crò, turn right before the last bends; do not use the space reserved for residents | about 30 minutes |
| Località Crò | Park near the osteria, take the dirt road behind it and then the path | about 40 minutes |

From the hut to the walls, theCrag gives 10–20 minutes. There is no road to the hut; it is reachable only on foot or by mountain bike.

## Rules to know before you go

**No closures found.** As of October 2026 there is no closure, climbing ban or raptor nesting notice on record for Rocca Sbarua.

**Parking.** At Borgata Dairin, do not park in the space reserved for residents (Frossasco town council).

**Bolts and gear.** Most classics have been rebolted, but bolt density varies (see above). A 2017 personal trip report on [On-Ice.it](http://www.on-ice.it/onice/onice_view_report.php?id=5304&type=3) used a 60 m rope on the Gervasutti-Ronco and found cams and nuts "non indispensabili" (not essential); the few routes that were not rebolted (such as the Via Beuchod) need a trad rack.

**The hut.** [Rifugio Melano Casa Canada](https://www.prenotarifugi.cai.it/en/detail/Rifugio%20Melano%20Casa%20Canada/?id=6013) belongs to the CAI Pinerolo section, sits at 1,060 m, is open all year and has 24 beds; book overnight stays on the CAI booking page. There is a helicopter landing area next to it (Frossasco town council).

**Rescue.** Call 112, say it is a mountain accident and ask for the Soccorso Alpino ([CNSAS](https://www.cnsas.it/en/distress-call/)).

## Routes by level

YDS and V-grade conversions follow the [Mountain Project international grade comparison chart](https://www.mountainproject.com/international-climbing-grades) and are approximate; "approx." marks grades the sources give without a/b/c (such as 4 or 5+). The original grade is the one that counts.

Most routes here are multi-pitch of 2 to 6 pitches, so the grade column gives the pitches or the hardest one. Pitch grades come from [Climbook](https://climbook.com/falesie/27921/rocca-sbarua-sbarua-centrale-normale-placche-gialle/vie), overall lengths and grades from [Escape Outdoor](https://www.escapeoutdoorguides.com/blog/2020/03/20/rocca-sbarua-gems/), and popularity from [8a.nu](https://www.8a.nu/crags/sportclimbing/italy/rocca-sbarua/routes). Records often differ by a grade or more (Climbook itself lists two versions), so go by the guidebook.

### Beginner: up to 6a (YDS 5.10a and below)

Most beginner multi-pitches are on the Settore Normale of the central wall and on the Sperone Rivero and Cinquetti.

| Route | Grade | YDS | Length / pitches | Sector | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Via normale | 4b, 4b, 4b, 4c, 3b | hardest 5.6 | 5 pitches | Central wall (Normale) | Climbook's other record gives 3b–3c per pitch |
| Gervasutti-Ronco | 5c, 4b, 5b, 5b, 5c | hardest 5.9 | 120 m, 5 pitches | Central wall | 1937; the most-logged route on 8a.nu (17); the last pitch is a crack climbed by dülfer (On-Ice report); Climbook's other record gives 5a, 3b, 4b, 4c, 5a |
| Via Rivero | 5c | 5.9 | 140 m | Sperone Rivero | Escape Outdoor: slabs, roofs, cracks and overhangs |
| Cinquetti | 5c | 5.9 | Multi-pitch | Sperone Cinquetti | 8 ascents on 8a.nu |
| Il Tao della Sbarua | 5c | 5.9 | 400 m (three routes linked) | Torre del Bimbo → Sperone Cinquetti → Torre Rubinetto | Escape Outdoor: links Osteria del viandante, Analcolica and Tupac Amaru, finishing on the last pitch of the Diretta Carbone |
