> 🌏 [中文版](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-sass-dlacia)

Sass Dlacia sits at the southern end of Val Badia in South Tyrol (Südtirol / Alto Adige), on the road from San Cassiano up to Passo Valparola, at the edge of the forest near Armentarola. In a piece on five summer crags in the eastern Dolomites, the Italian Alpine Club's *Lo Scarpone* writes: "Sass Dlacia rappresenta senza dubbio il non plus ultra per l'arrampicata in falesia" ([CAI Lo Scarpone](https://www.loscarpone.cai.it/dettaglio/fuga-dal-caldo-cinque-falesie-per-arrampicare-nelle-dolomiti-orientali/)), meaning the best single-pitch crag in Val Badia.

This post covers what to decide before you go: whether the grades suit you, which months, how to get there without a car, and how long a rope to bring. For a season-by-season and region-by-region comparison of Italian crags, see the [series overview](/posts/climbing/2026-10-18-italy-crags-overview-en).

## What kind of crag is it

**Rock and style.** Everything is bolted sport climbing. The main wall (sector A) is one big face, with several large boulders and smaller sectors around it. Local guide Enrico Maioni of [Guide Dolomiti](https://www.guidedolomiti.com/en/crags-in-cortina-dampezzo/) describes the main wall as "mostly technical and sustained climbing, often fingery and endurance-based", while the smaller sectors are steeper and more physical. [falesia.it](https://www.falesia.it/crag/889/scassiano-sass-dlacia.html) mentions "tacche, buchi e svasi" (crimps, pockets and slopers). [UKC](https://www.ukclimbing.com/logbook/crags/sass_dlacia-10591/) calls it "A large, steep and imposing sports crag" with bolts "plentiful and currently in good condition" (page updated June 2026).

**Sectors.** CAI counts seven main sectors; two big boulders, Sorcini and Sorci Verdi, can be climbed on all four sides. falesia.it splits it further into 16 sectors from A1 to H, including a dedicated Multi-Pitch sector. The outlying H sector (Simsalabim) is reached by driving towards Passo Valparola and parking after the third hairpin.

**Size.** Sources disagree a lot (checked October 2026):

| [falesia.it](https://www.falesia.it/crag/889/scassiano-sass-dlacia.html) | 272 | 4a–8a |
| [UKC](https://www.ukclimbing.com/logbook/crags/sass_dlacia-10591/) | 227 | 5a–8a+ |
| [theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/dolomites/sass-dlacia) | 209 | — |

falesia.it says "negli ultimi anni questa falesia è diventata la più grande dell'Alto Adige", and theCrag's tagline is "The biggest Sportclimbing Area in the Dolomites!".

**Who it suits.** The Alta Badia tourist board rates it "technically demanding", with about nine in ten routes (169 of 189) between 6a and 7c; UKC also says "upper 6's and low 7's predominating". The count per grade band is under Routes by level below.

Easy routes are scarce, so it suits climbers who are solid at 6a and up. If you climb grade 5, [Sass de Stria](https://www.alta-badia.org/en/leisure-activities/climbing-and-mountaineering/climbing-gardens/) at Passo Valparola (grade 4 to 6a), on the same tourist board list, is a better fit.

**Route length.** Guide Dolomiti gives 15–35 m; CAI says some reach 35 m and adds "raccomandiamo la massima attenzione nella scelta della corda". UKC notes several multi-pitch routes, mostly three pitches, and says a clipstick helps because the first bolt is high on some routes.

## When to go

**Altitude.** Sources give different heights: falesia.it 1,730 m, CAI about 1,700 m, theCrag a range of 1,686–2,118 m, UKC 2,125 m.

**Season.** CAI says the altitude and surrounding forest make it "fruibile quasi esclusivamente in estate" (usable almost only in summer). Guide Dolomiti says "from late spring to autumn; particularly pleasant in summer".

**Aspect.** falesia.it lists the aspect as "360°" and CAI says the routes are "suddivisi su tutte le esposizioni", so you can follow the sun or the shade between sectors. theCrag marks the main wall as getting afternoon sun.

**Crowds and parking.** CAI warns that Val Badia is busy in summer and traffic and parking can be hard work.

## Getting there

| | Public transport | Driving |
|---|---|---|
| To Val Badia | Train to Brunico/Bruneck, then bus 460 to La Villa | A22 motorway, exit Bressanone/Brixen, via Val Pusteria into Val Badia |
| To below the crag | Bus 465 to Sciaré or Armentarola | Past San Cassiano and Armentarola, turn left at the "Capanna Alpina" sign |
| To the wall | Car park to the picnic area, then 5–20 min to each sector | Same |

### Public transport

The [line 465 timetable (PDF)](https://www.suedtirolmobil.info/fileadmin/pdf/2026/465_20251214.pdf) of South Tyrol's public transport network [südtirolmobil](https://www.suedtirolmobil.info/en/my-journey/timetables) has two parts:

- **Summer timetable** (17 June–11 October 2026): Passo Falzarego–Passo Valparola–Sciaré–Armentarola–San Cassiano–La Villa–Corvara, roughly every 30–60 minutes during the day.
- **Winter timetable** (12 October–12 December 2026): only Sciaré to La Villa/Corvara, about hourly during the day, not up to the passes.

[Cortina Delicious](https://cortinadelicious.it/EN/p97-Bus-timetables) lists SAD as the operator of this Falzarego–San Cassiano–Corvara line. At La Villa you can change to line 460 for Brunico station. The [Alta Badia tourist board](https://www.altabadia.org/it/poi/cosa-fare-in-alta-badia/dettaglio/poi/parcheggio-capanna-alpina) says the Capanna Alpina car park is about 1 km from the bus stop.

From Cortina d'Ampezzo: take [Dolomiti Bus](https://dolomitibus.it/it/linee-stagionali-estivo-2026) seasonal line 52 to Passo Falzarego (details in the [Cinque Torri post](/posts/climbing/2026-10-18-italy-crag-cinque-torri-en)), then line 465 down to Sciaré. [Cortina Delicious](https://cortinadelicious.it/EN/p97-Bus-timetables) says line 52 ran until 4 October in 2026, and line 465's summer timetable ends on 11 October, so from mid-October neither line crosses the passes and you have to come in from the Val Badia side.

There is also summer [line 468 (PDF)](https://www.suedtirolmobil.info/fileadmin/pdf/2026/468_20260617.pdf), three runs a day from 17 June to 6 September 2026, from Passo Falzarego via San Cassiano to Badia and Piccolino. Timetables are reissued every season, so check the current PDF before you go.

### Driving

[falesia.it](https://www.falesia.it/crag/889/scassiano-sass-dlacia.html): from Brunico drive up Val Badia towards La Villa and on to San Cassiano; past the hamlet of Armentarola, turn left at the "Capanna Alpina" sign. UKC describes driving to the Armentarola plain and the Rio Sare bridge, then up a rough track alongside the river to its end.

From Cortina or Arabba, cross Passo Falzarego/Valparola and drop down to Armentarola.

### Parking

The Capanna Alpina car park is gravel; the [Alta Badia tourist board](https://www.altabadia.org/it/poi/cosa-fare-in-alta-badia/dettaglio/poi/parcheggio-capanna-alpina) lists "In estate: € 10,00 al giorno" and free in winter (checked October 2026). The [Alta Badia climbing page](https://www.alta-badia.org/en/leisure-activities/climbing-and-mountaineering/climbing-gardens/) says the parking is along a dried-up river bed; cross the river bed to the picnic area, then 10–20 minutes to each sector. CAI notes that almost all car parks in the area charge a fee and may fill up in high season.

[theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/dolomites/sass-dlacia)'s approach notes: 5–10 minutes from the car to the picnic area directly below the main wall; for sector A take the path on the left or straight ahead, 5 minutes steeply uphill; for sectors B, C and D take the path on the right, with sector D a further 5–10 minutes of steep uphill. Guide Dolomiti puts it at 15–30 minutes.

## Rules to know before you go

**Rope length.** This is the most consistent warning across sources. James Rushforth writes in [UKC's Dolomites destination guide](https://ukclimbing.com/articles/destinations/the_dolomites-2708): "A 70m rope is useful for Tridentina crag and Sass Dlacia". Routes often run 30–35 m, so a 60 m rope won't get you down some of them; tie a knot in the end.

**Nature park.** The Capanna Alpina car park is at the entrance to the [Fanes-Senes-Braies nature park](https://www.altabadia.org/it/poi/cosa-fare-in-alta-badia/dettaglio/poi/parcheggio-capanna-alpina) and is a trailhead for hikes into it. None of the sources say whether the crag itself lies inside the park, and no climbing ban or seasonal restriction turned up.

**Closures.** As of October 2026, no closure notice for Sass Dlacia was found; UKC's June 2026 page says the bolts are in good condition.

**Rescue.** South Tyrol mountain rescue ([Bergrettung / CNSAS Alto Adige](https://www.bergrettung.it/en/raising-the-alarm-2.html)) is reached on 112, staffed 24 hours. [CNSAS South Tyrol](https://www.soccorsoalpino.org/en/safety) says 112 was introduced in South Tyrol on 17 October 2017, and 118, 115 and 113 are routed automatically to the central emergency centre in Bolzano; the "112 Where ARE U" app can send your location.

**Supplies and accommodation.** [Camping Sass Dlacia](https://campingsassdlacia.it/en/activities/rock-climbing) sits below the crag and sells the local guidebook in its shop; the Capanna Alpina restaurant is next to the car park.

## Routes by level

YDS and V-grade conversions follow the [Mountain Project international grade comparison chart](https://www.mountainproject.com/international-climbing-grades) and are approximate; "approx." marks grades the sources give without a/b/c (such as 4 or 5+). The original grade is the one that counts.

Route names and grades come mainly from the sector lists on [falesia.it](https://www.falesia.it/crag/889/scassiano-sass-dlacia.html) (updated 9 August 2024) and star ratings on [theCrag's Main Wall page](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/dolomites/sass-dlacia/area/716542398). The multi-pitch routes here are bolted sport multi-pitches with French grades, so they go straight into the four bands below.

### Beginner: up to 6a (YDS 5.10a and below)

Easy routes are few. The Alta Badia tourist board counts only 4 at grade 4 and 11 at 5b–5c, mostly on the left side of sector A1 on the main wall and in sector H.
