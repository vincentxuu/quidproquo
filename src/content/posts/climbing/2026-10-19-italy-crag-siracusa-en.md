Siracusa sits in the south-east corner of Sicily. Climbers come for two things. One is the canyon country around Canicattini Bagni, inland on the Monti Iblei plateau, where rivers have cut deep gorges into the limestone and almost everything is single-pitch sport climbing. The other is deep water solo (DWS: climbing ropeless above the sea and falling into it) on the city's coast.

This post helps you decide whether the canyons suit your level, which months to go, whether you can get there without a car, and what to avoid around the Plemmirio marine protected area if you want to try DWS. The other canyon area in south-east Sicily is covered in the [Ragusa and Modica post](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-ragusa-en); for a season-and-region comparison across the series, see the [series overview](/posts/climbing/2026-10-18-italy-crags-overview-en).

## What kind of crag is it

**Single-pitch sport in canyons.** [theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/brucoli-beach/area/2662762029) describes Canicattini Bagni as rolling limestone country cut by several canyon systems. The climbing is mostly in narrow gorges with compact faces, caves and sculpted walls, and routes tend to follow natural lines, "emphasising movement and technical climbing rather than pure power". Every route is fully bolted.

**Scale.** Counts by site (checked October 2026):

| Source | Coverage | Count |
|---|---|---|
| [theCrag: Siracusa](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/brucoli-beach) | Whole province | 913 routes |
| [theCrag: Canicattini Bagni](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/brucoli-beach/area/2662762029) | Canyons around the town | 626 routes |
| [Climb Europe](https://climb-europe.com/pages/rock-climbing-italy-sicily) | Canicattini | "over 400 routes", canyons up to 200 m deep |

theCrag splits Canicattini into five areas: Cava Bagni Alta (194 routes and the busiest, with 1,305 logged ascents), Contrada Alfano (155, where local climbing started), Contrada Cavadonna (115), Cava Bagni Centrale (93) and Cava Bagni Bassa (40), plus Cugno Lupo. The [Cava Bagni Alta page](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/brucoli-beach/area/12980966541) says that in 2017 Stefan Löw saw the potential of Cava Bagni and, with local climber Christian Leube and Christoph Stahr, developed many new sectors in a short time. Others joined later, and more than 350 routes have gone up across Cava Bagni since 2017.

**Who it suits.** Grades run from 5a to 8b, but the areas lean different ways: sectors like Belvedere have plenty of easy and mid grades, while Contrada Alfano and Cavadonna are steeper and harder (see Routes by level). If you want sea views and a scene, [San Vito lo Capo](/posts/climbing/2026-10-18-italy-crag-san-vito-lo-capo-en) in western Sicily fits better; Canicattini is quiet.

**DWS.** Sicily's official tourism site [visitsicily](https://www.visitsicily.info/en/climbing-and-dws-in-sicily) calls Siracusa "the leading location for lovers of Deep-Water Soloing", with DWS fans from across Europe coming every year to climb above the coves and the sea caves of the Plemmirio marine protected area. [UKC's Sicily guide](https://www.ukclimbing.com/articles/destinations/sicily-14498) (Massimo Cappuccio, 2022) says British climber Neil Gresham climbed the first hard DWS lines in Syracuse. No source gives a total number of DWS problems.

## When to go

| Source | Season |
|---|---|
| [UKC Sicily guide](https://www.ukclimbing.com/articles/destinations/sicily-14498) | Climbable all year; spring and autumn best; July and August too hot; rain mostly in late autumn and from late winter into early spring |
| [Climb Europe](https://climb-europe.com/pages/rock-climbing-italy-sicily) | Autumn through to spring; often shorts and T-shirt in winter |

**Pick sectors by sun.** theCrag's sector pages are detailed. On [Belvedere](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/brucoli-beach/area/11195422287) the right wall is shaded until about midday and the left wall longer, which makes it the first choice on warm mornings. [Jungle Book](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/brucoli-beach/area/5813728410) stays shaded until about 1 p.m. [Fuchsberger](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/brucoli-beach/area/3878818380) faces east to south-east and gets the morning sun, so on warm days go there in the afternoon. [B4](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/brucoli-beach/area/11193850836) is shady until noon. On a hot day: shady sectors in the morning, then move to the walls that had the morning sun.

**DWS is the summer option.** An [Up-Climbing report from 2014](https://www.up-climbing.com/en/boulder/news-boulder/deep-water-solo-siracusa-neil-gresham-daniel-jung-epic-tv-video/) says the local Gruppo Roccia Siracusa took up DWS precisely because summer made the canyons too hot.

## Getting there

| | Public transport | Driving |
|---|---|---|
| Into Sicily | The nearest airport is Catania | Same; hire a car at the airport |
| To Siracusa | Trenitalia regional trains and Interbus coaches from Catania | E45 / SS114 towards Siracusa |
| To Canicattini | AST bus into town | Near Siracusa, take the SP14 to Canicattini |

### Public transport

theCrag says you can reach Siracusa from Catania airport by public transport and change for Canicattini, "but a car is strongly recommended" because the canyons are spread out. The Siracusa province [tourist information point](https://www.infopointprovinciasiracusa.it/trasporti-interprovinciali/) lists [AST](https://www.aziendasicilianatrasporti.it/) as the bus operator for Canicattini Bagni, and [Trenitalia](https://www.trenitalia.com/content/dam/trenitalia/regionale/treni-garantiti/Regionale%20Sicilia_sito.pdf) runs a Messina – Catania – Siracusa regional line. Timetables change, so check with the operators before you go. The UKC guide is blunter: trains and buses in Sicily mean long waits, so hire a car if you want to move around freely.

A compromise without a car: stay in Canicattini. theCrag says Cava Bagni Alta is the canyon closest to town and can be reached on foot or by bike down Via Bagni, the old road into the canyon.

### Driving

All approaches start from Canicattini (theCrag sector pages):

- **Cava Bagni Alta:** take the SP74 towards Floridia for about 1 km; after the bridge over Cava Bagni the main car park (by the solar plant) is on the right. Or drive down Via Bagni from town to the wastewater treatment plant and walk into the canyon.
- **Contrada Cavadonna:** SP74 towards Floridia for about 4 km, then right onto the SR15.
- **Cava Bagni Bassa:** SP14 towards Siracusa for about 2 km, then left onto a small parallel road for about 400 m.

### Parking

theCrag repeats it on every page: don't block gates, other cars or turning areas. The town has no facilities for camper vans, so use campsites and don't sleep overnight in sensitive spots. There's no climbing shop in Canicattini; buy gear at the Vertical Climbing Center or Decathlon in Siracusa.

## Rules to know before you go

**Canyon etiquette.** theCrag's Canicattini page asks climbers to stay on established paths, keep approaches discreet and **close every gate**. New routes and rebolts should use stainless-steel glue-in bolts only (such as Inox 316L). For the state of the bolts or development in a given sector, theCrag suggests contacting the local developers. The Belvedere page also carries a notice from the local group Friends of Canicattini (checked October 2026): about 20 bags of rubbish were dumped at the foot of the wall near the route "Laura", and they are arranging for it to be removed.

**Closures flagged on theCrag.** The Siracusa province page marks Sortino as "Closed" and puts warnings on Grotta dei Pipistrelli and La Pagoda. None of these are in Canicattini; I couldn't read the warning text, so treat it as a database flag and check before you go.

**Plemmirio marine protected area.** Under the [founding decree](https://www.mase.gov.it/portale/documents/d/guest/plemmirio_dm_15-09-2004-pdf) (Ministry of the Environment, 15 September 2004), the area has three zones. Zone A, off Capo Murro di Porco, is a "riserva integrale" (strict reserve) where only rescue, patrols, research and authorised guided dives are allowed; swimming (balneazione) is permitted only from zone B outwards. The [European MSP Platform](https://maritime-spatial-planning.ec.europa.eu/practices/zoning-marine-protected-areas-case-plemmirio-mpa) gives the sizes as 80 ha for zone A, 1,470 ha for B and 950 ha for C, and says "bathing and swimming is only permitted in zones B and C". DWS always ends in the water, so **don't climb the sea caves in zone A**. The decree also prohibits damaging geological formations. The managing body's [annual supplementary rules](https://www.plemmirio.eu/wp-content/uploads/2025/Disciplinare_Integrativo_Plemmirio_2025_MASE.pdf) (2025 edition) contain nothing specific to climbing or DWS, so the zoning decides where you can go in the water. Check the map at [Consorzio Plemmirio](https://www.plemmirio.eu/area-marina/zonazione-e-regolamento/) to see which zone you're in.

**DWS on the city coast.** The 2014 Up-Climbing report describes the three main DWS spots at the time on roughly 6 km of city sea cliff, all reachable along the cycle path without a boat: a sea cave reached by the "Bar Tunisi" traverse (15–17 m, deep water); an arch at the end of the cycle path (some starts have rocks breaking the surface, and the report says to avoid those lines); and Cannone, a few tens of metres north of the arch and the only one with afternoon sun (6a to 8a). The report also stresses not going alone and having someone with you who can help both on the rock and in the water. It is a secondary source from 12 years ago, so confirm spots and water depth with local climbers.

**Rescue.** [CNSAS Sicilia](https://www.cnsas.sicilia.it/come-chiamare-soccorso/) says that in Sicily you call 112 after a mountain accident and specifically ask for the Soccorso Alpino e Speleologico.

## Routes by level

YDS and V-grade conversions follow the [Mountain Project international grade comparison chart](https://www.mountainproject.com/international-climbing-grades) and are approximate; "approx." marks grades the sources give without a/b/c (such as 4 or 5+). The original grade is the one that counts.

Online route data for Canicattini comes in two forms: the "Nearby Icons" on each theCrag area page (the top-starred routes) and the [Planetmountain page for Antro dell'Eco](https://www.planetmountain.com/en/crags/antro-dell-eco.html) (all 21 routes, bolted by M. Flaccavento and A. Tidona). theCrag stars and ascent counts are database figures, and some routes have only one or two logged ascents.

theCrag singles out [Belvedere](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/brucoli-beach/area/11195422287) (20 routes, 5a–7b+) as suited to beginners, families and mixed-ability groups.

## Where to look things up

- **[theCrag: Canicattini Bagni](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/brucoli-beach/area/2662762029):** routes, parking coordinates, sun and shade by sector, and warnings; the local developers keep it up to date.
- **[Planetmountain: Antro dell'Eco](https://www.planetmountain.com/en/crags/antro-dell-eco.html)** and **[Gole della Stretta](https://www.planetmountain.com/en/crags/gole-della-stretta.html):** canyon crag pages with route-by-route tables.
- **[Consorzio Plemmirio](https://www.plemmirio.eu/area-marina/zonazione-e-regolamento/):** zoning map, decree and the annual supplementary rules for the marine protected area.
- **[AST](https://www.aziendasicilianatrasporti.it/):** buses from Siracusa to Canicattini and service notices.
- **Guidebooks:** Grades and route counts here come mainly from online databases; the guidebook is the authority.
  - [*Di Roccia di Sole*](https://www.versantesud.it/it/negozio/catalogue/di-roccia-di-sole_1399/) (Massimo Cappuccio and Giuseppe Gallo, Versante Sud, 5th edition 2022, separate Italian, German and English volumes plus an iClimbing digital version, 560 pages): single-pitch sport climbing across Sicily including Canicattini, plus bouldering and DWS. theCrag and Climb Europe both name it as the only guidebook that covers Siracusa.
  - Rockfax's *Sicily* and GEBRO's *Sicily-Rock* cover western Sicily only.
