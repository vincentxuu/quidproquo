Val Rosandra (Slovene: Dolina Glinščice) is on the south-eastern edge of Trieste, in the municipality of San Dorligo della Valle – Dolina: a karst canyon cut by the Rosandra stream, which rises just over the border in Slovenia. [Trieste's official tourism site](https://discover-trieste.it/live/natural-wonders/rosandra-valley-reserve) calls it "one of the most valued rock climbing areas in Europe" and the place where Emilio Comici "first learnt his craft".

This post helps you decide whether a valley of small scattered crags suits you, which months to go, how to get there without a car, and what to watch for in the reserve. The last section covers Napoleonica, on the other side of the city, with its own century of climbing history. For a season-and-region comparison of Italian crags, see the [series overview](/posts/climbing/2026-10-18-italy-crags-overview-en).

## What kind of crag is it

**A puzzle, not one big wall.** [Planetmountain](https://planetmountain.com/en/crags/val-rosandra.html) says Val Rosandra "doesn't offer a single, magnificent crag" but a set of very different sectors dotted across the valley, like a puzzle that loses its sense if one piece is missing. The rock is limestone, "mainly crimps, some pockets and tufas", mostly vertical with a few steep overhangs; take a 60 m rope.

**Two different sides.** Planetmountain says the sunnier right-hand side has sectors such as Canarini, Giardini d'Inverno, Oci de Moccò, 12 Vie, Piccola Ferrovia, Falchi, Vergini, Montasio, Concave, Amicizie and Altari; the other side has the shady Crinale and the Formai slab near the little church of Santa Maria in Siaris.

**Scale.** Counts by site (checked 2026-10-19):

| Source | Coverage | Count |
|---|---|---|
| [theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/val-rosandra-glinscia) | Whole valley | 541 routes, 2,835 ascents; Crinale 82, Vergini 57, Piccola Ferrovia 49, Falchi 44 |
| [UKC Logbook](https://ukclimbing.com/logbook/books/climbing_without_frontiers_adriatic_coast_-_istria-910) | In the guidebook | 80 routes, all aspects |
| [falesia.it](https://www.falesia.it/crag/192/val-rosandra.html) | The crag reached from Hervati along the old railway | 67 routes, 4a/8b, faces south |

**Bolts.** [theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/val-rosandra-glinscia) says almost all routes have resin bolts and anchors, but some anchors lack lower-off karabiners, so you need to know how to thread the rope; the best equipped are Parete di Bagnoli, Giardini d'inverno, Caprette, Bedrock–Cartoonia–Il pergolo, Piccola Ferrovia, Vergini, Altari and most of Crinale.

**Multi-pitch and teaching walls.** [Trieste tourism's climbing page](https://discover-trieste.it/live/outdoor-activities/climbing/seaside-mountaineers) says the valley has a few classic multi-pitch routes, "almost always fully or partially bolted", to be used with nuts and cams; the stand-outs are La Grande, five pitches to the top of Monte Stena, and Spigolo Verde. Concave, Amicizie, Montasio, Bianca, Falchi and Altari are often used by the CAI for teaching and may be "difficult from a safety perspective" for sport-only climbers.

**History.** Planetmountain calls the valley the "memory - historical and tangible" of the great alpinism between the two World Wars. Montasio, a 25 m wall named after the Julian Alps peak, is "the most admired limestone architecture in the valley", and carries old routes named after Comici: Strapiombi de Comici, Naso de Comici, Via de Comici. theCrag also lists the 140 m Cippo Comici.

**Who it suits.** People based in Trieste looking for a half day of winter climbing, and anyone who wants to combine sport and trad multi-pitch on one trip. If you want to tick through one wall, the sectors may feel too scattered.

## When to go

| Source | Season |
|---|---|
| [Planetmountain](https://planetmountain.com/en/crags/val-rosandra.html) | "Sunny winter days"; the right-hand side is sunnier |
| [falesia.it](https://www.falesia.it/crag/192/val-rosandra.html) | Faces south, "impraticabile" (unclimbable) in summer |
| [theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/val-rosandra-glinscia) | "Climbable all year as you will find cliffs in all expositions" |

**South faces in winter, north faces in summer.** On sunny winter days go to the south-facing sectors on the right-hand side; in summer you're left with shady walls like Crinale, or early and late sessions. [Trieste tourism](https://discover-trieste.it/live/natural-wonders/rosandra-valley-reserve) mentions the famous "Bora Saddle" at the valley, where Trieste's bora wind speeds up, so check the wind when picking a winter day.

## Getting there

| | Public transport | Car |
|---|---|---|
| Start | Trieste railway station (largo Città di Santos) | Into Trieste on the A4 |
| Middle | [Trieste Trasporti](https://www.triestetrasporti.it/it/trasporto-pubblico/linee-orari/linea-40) bus 40 towards Dolina, get off at Bagnoli | To San Dorligo della Valle / Bagnoli della Rosandra, or via Basovizza and Hervati from above |
| Last leg | Walk in from Bagnoli; theCrag gives 10–20 minutes to the sectors | Same; you can also come in from Osp on the Slovenian side (theCrag) |

### Public transport

[Trieste Trasporti line 40](https://www.triestetrasporti.it/it/trasporto-pubblico/linee-orari/linea-40) runs from the railway station to Dolina via Bagnoli. The [winter timetable from 14 September 2026](https://www.triestetrasporti.it/sites/default/files/attachments/triestetrasporti_linea_40_invernale_14SET26.pdf) shows a bus roughly every 40 minutes to an hour on weekdays; for example, Bagnoli 9:29 reaches the station at 10:03, so about half an hour each way. The reserve visitor centre is at Boljunec 507 in Bagnoli ([tourism site](https://discover-trieste.it/live/natural-wonders/rosandra-valley-reserve)), and after climbing you can stop at the Premuda hut in Bagnoli, which the tourism site describes as the lowest CAI hut in continental Italy.

### Driving

[falesia.it](https://www.falesia.it/crag/192/val-rosandra.html) describes the approach from above: leave the motorway at Trieste, follow the expressway to the Basovizza junction, then S. Antonio in Bosco and the village of Hervati; past the village, follow the old railway and take the path uphill to the crag. The old railway is now the Giordano Cottur walking and cycling path, running all the way to Hrpelje in Slovenia (tourism site).

### Parking

The reserve does not publish dedicated parking for climbers (checked October 2026). Taking bus 40 to Bagnoli and walking in saves hunting for a space; if you drive in from above, follow falesia.it's directions past Hervati and along the old railway. Once in the valley, stay on marked paths (see the rules below).

## Rules before you go

**This is a nature reserve.** [Turismo FVG](https://turismofvg.it/en/carso/itinerari/val-rosandra-reserve?LangSetCMS=en) says the Val Rosandra Regional Nature Reserve was set up in 1996, covers about 750 ha and is run by the municipality of San Dorligo della Valle. [inItaly's July 2026 report](https://initaly.it/en/articolo/val-rosandra-un-ecosistema-da-proteggere-tra-tutela-e-fruizione-28379) relays the municipality's position: the aim is not to ban access, and the reserve is "suitable for hiking, climbing and nature observation", but visitors should stay on marked paths, not bathe in the stream (home to the protected white-clawed crayfish), carry out their waste and keep noise down; spray-paint vandalism on the rocks is under investigation.

**Closures and bans.** As of October 2026 no climbing ban or nesting restriction was found. theCrag marks the Falchi wall with a "Warning" whose content isn't visible; check theCrag or with local climbers before going to Falchi.

**Wear a helmet; watch for loose rock.** The tourism site is explicit: on the multi-pitch routes and cliffs, "always wear a helmet", because "the rock in this area is quite crumbly and unexpected events occur frequently".

**Check the anchor before lowering.** theCrag says some anchors have no lower-off karabiners, so you'll need to thread the rope; practise beforehand.

**Rescue.** Call 112; 118 is medical and mountain rescue ([italia.it](https://www.italia.it/it/italia/informazioni-per-viaggiare/numeri-emergenza-e-assistenza)). [trekking.it's 2023 summary](https://www.trekking.it/news/soccorso-alpino-pagamento-ticket/) (second-hand) says Friuli Venezia Giulia does not charge for rescue.

## Nearby: Napoleonica

**A sea-view crag above the city.** Napoleonica is on the other side of Trieste, on the edge of the Karst plateau along the Strada Vicentina, the old path between Opicina and Prosecco. [Gravità Zero](https://www.gravitazerotrieste.it/news/napoleonica/) calls it "una delle falesie storiche triestine" (one of Trieste's historic crags): a training ground for alpinists since the 1930s, where Comici made his "prime prove su passaggi ancora esistenti" (first attempts on moves still there today), and now more than 200 routes from grade IV to 8b+. [UKC's](https://ukclimbing.com/logbook/books/climbing_without_frontiers_adriatic_coast_-_istria-910) guidebook listing gives 270 routes, facing south.

**Aspect and season.** [Planetmountain](https://www.planetmountain.com/it/falesie/napoleonica.html) says all sectors face south/west and get sun from 11 or 12 until sunset, and recommends spring, autumn and winter; the Sopra la Napoleonica sector isn't sheltered from the bora.

**Sectors.** The main sector runs along the paved path, from grade 4 to 8b, with long traverses (up to 8a); Planetmountain says it takes "un bel giuoco di piedi" (good footwork) and some stamina. Opposite the main sector are three small towers (20 routes, 6a–7a+, with abseils), and below is a small grotto (13 overhanging routes, 6b–8a+).

**Bolt maintenance.** Gravità Zero says the local association Progetto Verticale Trieste replaced every anchor in the Vedetta Italia sector in January 2024, but plenty of old routes still need work.

**Getting there.** Planetmountain's driving directions: take the A4 to its end, continue on the expressway, exit at Prosecco, follow signs for the Monte Grisa sanctuary, turn left at the roundabout, and park near the fountain at the start of the closed Strada Vicentina on the left. [Turismo FVG](https://www.turismofvg.it/it/active/strada-napoleonica-o-vicentina) says the start of the path is also easy to reach by public transport; check times on the Trieste Trasporti site.
