> 🌏 [中文版](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-varazze)

Varazze is a small seaside town on the western Ligurian coast, between Genova and Finale Ligure. The bouldering is not by the sea but on the hillsides behind town, up the Teiro valley towards Alpicella and Faie, 15–20 minutes by car. One boulder here is famous well beyond Italy: Gioia in the Potala area, first climbed by Christian Core in 2008 and later upgraded to 8C+.

This post helps you decide whether the area suits your level, which months to go, how to get up the hill without a car, and which landowner rules to respect. For limestone sport climbing on the same coast, see [Finale Ligure](/posts/climbing/2026-10-18-italy-crag-finale-ligure-en) and inland [Val Pennavaire](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-italy-crag-val-pennavaire-en); for a season-and-region comparison of Italian crags, see the [series overview](/posts/climbing/2026-10-18-italy-crags-overview-en).

## What kind of crag is it

**Boulders in hillside woods, with the sea below.** [topoguru](https://topoguru.com/en/content/varazze) puts the bouldering at 400–600 m on south-facing slopes with a view of the sea and town; [InfoBoulder](https://www.infoboulder.com/varazze.php?mod=boulders) gives 300–1000 m. The biggest area is Potala, split into nine sectors along a stream. The others include La Cava, Lilliput, Eden, Futurama, Harem, La Collina and Area 51, 5–10 minutes apart by car.

**Rock.** Sources describe it differently. [theTopo](https://thetopo.com/areas/varazze) lists metamorphic rocks such as serpentinite, metabasite and metagabbro; InfoBoulder says "Varia, con prevalenza Serpentinite" (mixed, mostly serpentinite); [MadBoulder](https://www.madboulder.org/varazze) calls it gneiss. All agree the rock is compact with a coarse surface and good friction, with mostly slopers and edges and few pockets. topoguru warns that your skin suffers if you climb several days in a row, so go easy on dynamic moves.

**Size.** Counts by site (checked 2026-10-19):

| Source | Scope | Count |
|---|---|---|
| [InfoBoulder](https://www.infoboulder.com/varazze.php?mod=boulders) | Whole area | About 1,180 problems |
| [Planetmountain](https://www.planetmountain.com/en/crags/varazze.html) | In the guidebook | Over 1,100 problems (text by Marco Bagnasco) |
| [theTopo / 27crags](https://thetopo.com/areas/varazze) | 9 areas | 1,067 problems |
| [theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/area/2263888143) | Whole area (including the 54-route Alpicella sport crag) | 1,041 routes |
| [8a.nu](https://www.8a.nu/crags/bouldering/italy) | Logged problems | 842 problems and 6,849 ascents, 2nd among Italian bouldering areas |

Problems per area on 27crags: Potala 441, Lilliput 222, Eden 113, Area 51 74, La Collina 69, La Cava 61, Futurama 39, Harem 32.

**History.** The Planetmountain crag page is written by the developer, Marco Bagnasco. In 1999 he found the first sector, La Cava, out walking with his dog Carlotta, then brought in his old friend Christian Core (a former bouldering world champion) and Stella Marchisio to clean and climb the problems.

**Who it suits.** There is everything from easy problems to 8C+, but the 27crags list is concentrated between 6A+ and 7C+ (see the levels below). Landings at Potala are mostly flat, and topoguru says one or two pads are enough for most problems. The Baby Druidi sector at Potala has many problems for children ([theTopo Potala](https://thetopo.com/crags/potala-druidi-alto-18374/description)). If you want roped sport climbing, there is a small 3–6c crag at Alpicella (see Routes by level).

## When to go

| Source | Season |
|---|---|
| [theTopo](https://thetopo.com/areas/varazze) | September to May; summer usually too hot |
| [topoguru](https://topoguru.com/en/content/varazze) | Locals say late September to May; close to the sea, so good in winter too |
| [InfoBoulder](https://www.infoboulder.com/varazze.php?mod=boulders) | All year, best in spring, autumn and winter |
| [MadBoulder](https://www.madboulder.org/varazze) | Autumn and winter are best |

**Sun by sector.** From [theTopo's Potala description](https://thetopo.com/crags/potala-druidi-alto-18374/description): Graal is in shade in winter; Antro dei Druidi, Druidi basso, Dimora degli Elfi and Terra di Mezzo get morning sun and afternoon shade; Druidi alto and Baby Druidi get sun from morning to early afternoon, so go there on the coldest days. You can climb in summer, but wait until after 5 p.m. topoguru describes La Cava as a small sunny sector, good in winter.

**Rainy days.** Tana del Tasso at Potala is a large overhang under a roof and beside a high stone wall; theTopo says it stays climbable even in heavy rain.

**Ticks in summer.** theTopo warns that some sectors get overgrown in summer and, with roe deer around, are full of ticks.

## Getting there

| | Public transport | Driving |
|---|---|---|
| Start | Varazze railway station | A10 motorway: exit Varazze coming from Genova, Celle Ligure coming from Ventimiglia |
| Up the hill | [TPL Linea](https://www.tpllinea.it/linee-e-orari-invernali-in-vigore-dal-14-09-2026/) bus 22, Varazze Stazione → Pero → Alpicella, or → Faie | Up the Teiro valley towards Alpicella and Faie |
| Get off / park | Pero (for Potala), Alpicella (for La Cava and the Alpicella crag) | Each area has its own parking; at Potala the boulders are 2–3 minutes from the car |

### Public transport

The [RFI page for Varazze station](https://www.rfi.it/it/stazioni/varazze.html) shows a bus stop at the station. The bus up the hill is TPL Linea route 22. Taking the [winter timetable valid from 14 September 2026](https://www.tpllinea.it/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/22-%E2%80%93-URBANO-VARAZZE-%E2%80%93-VARAZZE-STAZIONE-%E2%80%93-ALPICELLA-%E2%80%93-CAMPOMARZIO-%E2%80%93-FAIE-Feriale-e-Festivo-2.pdf) as an example (checked October 2026):

| | Up (from Varazze Stazione via Pero and Alpicella) | Down (from Alpicella) |
|---|---|---|
| Weekdays | 9:00, 12:35, 17:30 (plus route 23 at 6:00) | 11:10, 13:00, 15:00, 16:25, 17:55 |
| Holidays | 13:30 | 9:50, 17:35 |

Few combinations actually work: on weekdays, up at 9:00 and down at 15:00 or 17:55; on holidays, only up at 13:30 and down at 17:35.

Part of the 15:40 weekday run is on demand and has to be booked two hours ahead on 800 808 288. More runs go towards Faie (the Eden and Lilliput side); check the PDF for which branch each run takes. No source gives walking distances from the stops to the areas, and with crash pads on a hill road most people drive.

### Driving

[Planetmountain](https://www.planetmountain.com/en/crags/varazze.html) gives this route: in Varazze, follow the seafront to the fork for Alpicella, pass the town hall, go straight over the roundabout and follow signs for Alpicella down a narrow one-way road beside the river. At the end turn right for "Eremo del deserto - Faie" and climb the valley past a small petrol station.

- **Eden, Hollywood, Lilliput, Up-there:** 200 m after the petrol station, turn right for "Faie".
- **Potala, Harem, La Cava, Futurama:** continue to the village of Il Pero, to the fork for "Alpicella" and "Scavi archeologici", then follow the approach for each area. theTopo's Potala page adds: after the small bridge at Pero, do not go towards Alpicella but carry straight on up two hairpins.

### Parking

theTopo says each Potala sector has its own parking, with two parking spots for the main sector; a path links the Antro dei Druidi parking to Terra di Mezzo in 10 minutes. InfoBoulder gives the Antro dei Druidi parking at 44.3978, 8.53653. topoguru says you can park right next to the boulders at La Cava; Lilliput is a 10–15 minute walk on a forest road, and you need a good topo to find the boulders.

## Rules to know before you go

**Potala is on private land.** From [theTopo's Potala description](https://thetopo.com/crags/potala-druidi-alto-18374/description): "The whole area of Potala is situated on a private property… The owners of the area allow us access as long as there's no damage." Tana del Tasso is also marked as private land. The Alpicella sport crag is in private woodland too; [montagnapertutti](https://www.montagnapertutti.it/roccia/arrampicate/liguria/alpicella/alpicella.php) says the owner gave permission for the cleaning and bolting.

**Beigua park: bouldering is exempt from climbing seasons.** theTopo places the bouldering inside the [Beigua Regional Nature Park](https://www.parcobeigua.it/pagina.php?id=164). Article 27.4 of the park's [management rules](https://static.parcobeigua.it/PianoParco/Piano_Integrato_Parco_Beigua/QuadroProgettualePrescrittivo-QP/documenti/BEIG.PIDP_REG-VAR.pdf), approved by the regional council on 28 October 2025, sorts cliffs into three classes for nesting birds: class 1 open all year, class 2 from 1 August to 30 April, class 3 from 1 August to 30 November. Clause 27.4.7 reads "L'attività di bouldering non è soggetta alle limitazioni di cui al presente articolo" (bouldering is not subject to the limits in this article).

The same article bans chipping and adding artificial holds, and new bolted routes need an environmental assessment. A [2019 park notice](https://www.parcobeigua.it/dettaglio.php?id=53163) puts fines at €1,000 to €10,000.

**Whether the Alpicella sport crag has a season is unclear.** The rules' annex lists "Sant'Anna Alpicella" as class 3 (climbing only from 1 August to 30 November) but does not say whether that is the Monte Castellaro crag; montagnapertutti says the crag sits next to the park boundary. If you plan to go between December and July, ask the park office first.

**Don't open new problems or routes.** In 2006 Renato Berruti, who bolted the Alpicella crag, asked on the [Quotazero forum](https://www.quotazero.com/forum/viewtopic.php?t=887) that nobody add routes: the owners agreed because they knew the developers personally, and too much traffic could get access withdrawn.

**Start marks.** theTopo says problems have black arrows at the start, but rain and stream water fade them.

**Rescue.** The emergency number in Liguria is 112; calls to 113, 115 and 118 are also answered by the 112 centre, which has a multilingual service ([Regione Liguria](https://www.regione.liguria.it/homepage-salute/item/14818-112.html)).

## Routes by level

YDS and V-grade conversions follow the [Mountain Project international grade comparison chart](https://www.mountainproject.com/international-climbing-grades) and are approximate; "approx." marks grades the sources give without a/b/c (such as 4 or 5+). The original grade is the one that counts.

Varazze is mainly bouldering. The four bands below follow the series, with boulder problems placed in the band with the same Font letters (Font 6A+ to 6C+ goes in Intermediate, for example). Font boulder grades and French sport grades are different scales, so the V-grade column is the better guide. Problems are picked by number of logs on [27crags](https://27crags.com/crags/potala-druidi-alto-18374/routelist) (checked 2026-10-19), and the "Sector" column gives the area and the boulder. The eight areas on 27crags hold 1,051 problems: 65 at Font 6A and below, 357 from 6A+ to 6C+, 363 from 7A to 7C+, 84 at 8A and up, and 182 ungraded.

The only roped sport climbing is the small Monte Castellaro crag at Alpicella: [theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/area/396846465) lists 54 routes, 12 to 90 m, graded 3 to 6c; [Gulliver](http://www.gulliver.it/itinerario/47868/) says route names are written at the base but without grades. No route-by-route grades are available online, so each sport table below says "not listed in sources".

### Beginner: up to 6a (YDS 5.10a and below)

**Sport.** Renato Berruti, who developed the Alpicella crag in 2006, wrote on the Quotazero forum that about 70% of routes are below 6a, with some 2–4 pitch routes up to 90 m; a single 60 m rope is enough. montagnapertutti says the easiest routes are 3+/4 and recommends a helmet because the crag is young.

| Gioia | 8C+ | V16 | Potala (Antro dei Druidi) | See below |

**Gioia.** In his [first-ascent piece on Planetmountain](https://www.planetmountain.com/en/news/climbing/gioia-fb8c-at-varazze-by-christian-core.html), Core places it in the small cave right of the well-known "Chiavi del regno", on the first big boulder of the Antro dei Druidi sector: sit start on the right, then a diagonal line up and right on small edges with poor feet. He graded it 8C when he climbed it in March 2008; it was later upgraded to 8C+, and [Planetmountain](https://www.planetmountain.com/en/news/climbing/stefano-ghisolfi-sends-gioia-boulder-varazze.html) calls it the world's first boulder at that grade. Repeats: Adam Ondra (2011), Nalle Hukkataival (2014), Niccolò Ceria (2020), Elias Iagnemma (2021), and Stefano Ghisolfi in February 2026.

- **[theCrag: Falesia di Alpicella](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/italy/area/396846465):** sectors of the sport crag.
- **[TPL Linea winter timetables](https://www.tpllinea.it/linee-e-orari-invernali-in-vigore-dal-14-09-2026/):** PDF timetables for routes 22 and 23; the files change with each season.
- **[Beigua park: how to behave in the park](https://www.parcobeigua.it/pagina.php?id=164):** climbing-period rules and the management-rules download.
- **Guidebooks:** Grades and route counts here come mainly from online databases; the guidebook is the authority.
  - [*BluBloc – Bouldering a Varazze e Triora*](https://www.ibs.it/blubloc-bouldering-a-varazze-triora-libro-vari/e/9788895224138) (Christian Core, Stella Marchisio and Marco Bagnasco, Idee Verticali, 2013, Italian and English, ISBN 978-88-95224-13-8): bouldering at Varazze and Triora; InfoBoulder reported its release in March 2013. Swiss bookshop [Piz Bube](https://pizbube.ch/en/shop/blubloc-bouldering-a-varazze-e-triora/) lists it as "out of print" and says a new edition was expected in February 2019, but no new edition can be found.
  - [*VarazzeBlock*](https://www.planetmountain.com/it/libri/varazzeblock.html) (Bagnasco, Marchisio and Core, Antersass, 2008, Italian, 240 pages): the first complete Varazze bouldering guide; the Planetmountain crag page now lists only BluBloc in its bibliography.
  - The Alpicella sport crag has a small local topo. theCrag says the café in Alpicella sells it; Gulliver (revised 2023) says the proceeds go to new gear but it is hard to find.
  - There has been no new printed bouldering guide since 2013; the most complete current data is on 27crags.
