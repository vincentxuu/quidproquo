A day on granite sport routes need not mean leaving Busan for Ulsan. Daeryuk-bong has upper and lower walls on Geumjeongsan, giving a city-based trip a short-route option rather than another multipitch summit.

This guide helps you decide whether its grades suit you, approach from Oncheonjang, and separate older route information from current park management.

## What kind of crag

**Daeryuk-bong (대륙봉)** is on **Geumjeongsan (금정산)** in Busan. This article concerns the two climbing walls, not every crag on the mountain or the hiking summit alone.

The [2012 guide *Climb*](https://product.kyobobook.co.kr/detail/S000001582823) describes southeast-facing granite sport climbing, with the lower wall on the left and generally shorter climbs than the upper wall. Its index lists **25 routes, YDS 5.10a–5.13a**. These describe the book's coverage, not a current inventory of maintained, accessible lines.

It suits climbers with outdoor leading experience who want a sport day while staying in Busan. The listed easier routes begin at 5.10a. If you need several 5.7 or 5.8 warm-ups, short routes alone do not make this a beginner venue. Both walls offer intermediate grades: match names to the topo before settling your gear at one wall.

## When to go

The southeast aspect comes from [*Climb*](https://product.kyobobook.co.kr/detail/S000001582823). Morning sunshine can help on cold days and become uncomfortable on warm ones. Include the belayer's resting place and water in your plan, rather than assuming the mountain will always feel cooler than the city.

For a first visit, compare dry spring and autumn days. The [Korea Meteorological Administration](https://www.kma.go.kr/neng/biz/climate/climate-of-korea.do) describes summer monsoon rain and high humidity, with relatively drier autumn conditions. This is climate background, not a forecast of dry rock. Winter requires a wind and temperature check; short climbs do not remove summer storms or a slippery approach.

Check Busan's forecast and reassess wind, rock and the ground when you arrive. If the belay stance requires crossing a slippery slope, changing the plan remains a sensible option.

## Getting there

### Public transport

The [official Geumjeong tourism guide](https://www.geumjeong.go.kr/download/tour/gj_guidebook.pdf), consulted in October 2026, lists **subway Line 1 to Oncheonjang (온천장), then bus 203 toward the East Gate entrance or Sanseong Pass (동문입구 / 산성고개)**. Use the [Busan bus information system](https://bus.busan.go.kr/) for departures and the return journey; do not carry forward an old guide's frequency or stop sequence.

The 2012 climbing approach heads south from the Sanseong road area before dividing for the walls. Official visitor transport directions reach the mountain entrance, not the final climbing junction. Save **대륙봉 암장**, your selected wall and the return stop offline. Meet a local partner at a specific stop rather than sharing only a summit pin.

### Driving

Locate the Sanseong Pass road area and an authorized entrance, then confirm the walking approach. A navigation pin for Daeryuk-bong is not a drive-up belay stance. Do not improvise a descent from the hiking summit to the cliff.

### Parking

Use designated spaces and leave roads, paths and emergency access clear. A bus stop is not parking permission for climbers. Confirm where you may park and for how long; otherwise plan around subway and bus, allowing time for the walk back.

## Rules before you go

The [KNPS Geumjeongsan page](https://juwang.knps.or.kr/front/portal/visit/visitCourseMain.do?menuNo=7020102&parkId=122800) now identifies this mountain as a national park. **For an October 2026 trip, check current park notices for the particular cliff and approach.** Camping, spring-water and path descriptions in the 2012 book do not establish present permission.

An open hiking trail does not establish access to every climbing branch or wall. Respect signs and cordons, ask the managing authority when uncertain, and follow its fire, camping and rubbish rules.

Old route tables do not certify bolts or anchors. Confirm the finish, descent and appropriate equipment before leaving the ground, and tie a stopper knot in the rope end. A listed route height alone does not determine a suitable rope setup. If hardware is doubtful, choose another line and report it to local maintainers.

Call **119** in an emergency ([National Fire Agency](https://www.nfa.go.kr/eng/ems/service/)). Your team should know the Korean crag name and which wall it is using. Confirm outdoor sport climbing, rescue, medical treatment and repatriation with your insurer.

## Routes by level

YDS and V-grade conversions follow the [Mountain Project international grade comparison chart](https://www.mountainproject.com/international-climbing-grades) and are approximate; "approx." marks grades the sources give without a/b/c (such as 4 or 5+). The original grade is the one that counts.

These tables retain original YDS grades and lengths from [the 2012 *Climb*, printed page 11](https://product.kyobobook.co.kr/detail/S000001582823). The YDS column repeats the original without a French-grade conversion. Treat them as single-guide route identifiers, then check names and lines against a local topo.

### Beginner: up to 6a (YDS 5.10a and below)

Both walls have listed 5.10a routes. The lower wall gives shorter options, while the upper wall has a longer route at the same grade. Choose between those experiences before simply choosing the tallest wall.

| Route | Grade | YDS | Length / pitches | Sector | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| To Make Love / 사랑만들기 | 5.10a | 5.10a | 10 m | Lower wall | Single source: 2012 Climb |
| Trash / 쓰레기 | 5.10a | 5.10a | 12 m | Lower wall | Same guide |
| Existing C / 기존 C | 5.10a | 5.10a | 20 m | Upper wall | Same guide; names elsewhere need not identify this line |

### Intermediate: 6a+ to 6c+ (YDS 5.10b–5.11c)

Intermediate routes occur on both walls. Trying the rock on the lower wall before moving to longer upper-wall objectives is one option; crowding and conditions should determine whether the move makes sense.

| Route | Grade | YDS | Length / pitches | Sector | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stella / 스텔라 | 5.10c | 5.10c | 10 m | Lower wall | Single source: 2012 Climb |
| Open Space in the Woods / 숲속의빈터 | 5.11b | 5.11b | 15 m | Lower wall | Same guide |
| Hey Jude / 헤이쥬드 | 5.11b | 5.11b | 14 m | Upper wall | Same guide |

### Advanced: 7a to 7c+ (YDS 5.11d–5.13a)

Short lower-wall routes also provide harder objectives: a taller wall is not automatically a harder wall. The lower wall alone offers steps from 5.12 to 5.13 in the older guide.

| Route | Grade | YDS | Length / pitches | Sector | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Threshold of Autumn / 가을문턱 | 5.12a | 5.12a | 12 m | Lower wall | Single source: 2012 Climb |
| To You / 투유 | 5.13a | 5.13a | 10 m | Lower wall | Same guide |
| Festival 2 / 축제 2 | 5.12a | 5.12a | 18 m | Upper wall | Same guide; numbered Festival lines are distinct |

### Expert: 8a and up (YDS 5.13b and up)

The guide used here lists no named route in this band. If harder sport climbing is the main purpose of your trip, obtain the current local topo and establish enough objectives before allocating a full day to Daeryuk-bong.
