As you gain height on Insubong, Seoul gradually opens up behind the trees. This granite peak in Bukhansan National Park makes a city stay and a full day of multipitch climbing possible on the same trip. A subway connection to the foot of the mountain, however, does not make it an introductory outdoor lead venue.

This guide helps you decide whether Insubong suits your team, when to go, how to approach without a car, and how Insu A, Chouinard B and harder slab routes compare.

## What kind of crag

**인수봉** appears in English as Insubong or Insu-bong. It is the climbing peak, distinct from **Baegundae (백운대)**, the neighboring summit reached by a popular hiking trail. Both names matter when navigating: reach the Baegundae trailhead first, then follow the approach toward Insubong.

The granite offers cracks, friction slabs, chimneys and steeper face climbing. Many routes require removable protection alongside fixed bolts and belay stations. A few visible bolts do not make a route suitable for a rack of quickdraws alone.

The master index in the 2012 [*Climb: Rock Climbing in Korea*](https://product.kyobobook.co.kr/detail/S000001582823) lists **51 routes** for Insubong. [Mont Blanc Lines' 2025 East Face topo](https://www.montblanclines.com/products/%ec%9d%b8%ec%88%98%eb%b4%89-insubong-east-face) has **43 numbered lines**. One covers the broader crag in an older guide; the other selects lines on one face. The difference does not tell us how many routes have been added or removed.

The appeal is a full granite multipitch day while staying in Seoul. You still need to manage route finding, protection, team speed and the descent. **Being able to climb 5.8 is different from leading a team through a 5.8 multipitch route.** For a first visit, a partner or local guide familiar with your specific route and descent is more valuable than simply choosing the lowest number.

## When to go

[Rick Ruffin's account hosted by the Korean Alpine Club](http://www.cac.or.kr/eng/sub1/sub1_09.php) recommends spring and autumn, broadly mid-March through June and September through November. July and August are hot and wet; winter is cold. This is an undated historical account, useful for broad seasonal tradeoffs. Spring climbing still depends on that year's thaw restrictions.

The same account describes predominantly east-facing routes, with south- and west-facing options. **The East Face receives morning sun**: useful on a cold day, less comfortable in warm weather. Changing faces to escape the sun also changes the approach, route finding and potentially the descent. Decide that before setting out.

For an initial trip, aim for a dry spring or autumn day, then choose the date around access, temperature and wind. Winter adds icy approaches as well as cold hands: [KNPS specifically warns of ice and the need for traction on the Baegundae hiking route](https://main.knps.or.kr/front/portal/visit/visitCourseGuide.do?parkId=121500&vcursId=121500V008). A sunny climbing face does not remove that approach problem.

Allow more time on weekends. Both the 2012 guide and the 2025 topo publisher describe crowds, and delays on popular lines affect your descent as well as the climb. Choose a weekday when possible and agree on a turnaround time before leaving the ground.

## Getting there

### Public transport

The [official Seoul tourism page](https://english.visitseoul.net/seoul-hiking/Sweeping-views-of-Seoul-Baegundae%E2%80%93-Ui-Gugok-Trail/ENN042345), checked in October 2026, directs visitors to **Exit 2 of Bukhansan Ui (북한산우이), the terminus of the Ui–Sinseol Line**. It also suggests a taxi from the station to the Baegun Visitor Center. Show the driver **백운대탐방지원센터**, the Korean name of the Baegundae trailhead facility, rather than asking only for Bukhansan.

The tourism page's five-minute walk is to the Seoul Hiking Tourism Center, not the cliff. A taxi saves an uphill paved approach with climbing gear; the [KNPS Baegundae course page](https://main.knps.or.kr/front/portal/visit/visitCourseGuide.do?parkId=121500&vcursId=121500V008) lists roughly 2 km between the Ui area and Doseon Plaza.

KNPS gives **0.8 km and about 30 minutes from Baegundae Visitor Support Center to Harujae (하루재)**. Continue toward the Insu area, then use a route-specific topo for the final approach to your chosen face. The official page's full 1.9 km, 90-minute itinerary goes to the Baegundae hiking summit; it is not a universal approach time for Insubong climbs.

### Driving

Locate **백운대탐방지원센터 / 도선광장** on the Ui side before setting out. Bukhansan has multiple entrances: reaching the same national park from another direction can leave you far from your intended East Face approach.

### Parking

The entrance is near Doseonsa Temple, but temple-area parking should not be assumed to be an unrestricted all-day climbers' car park. The official course page checked in October 2026 does not establish current capacity or climber parking conditions. Confirm those with the manager before driving. KNPS also warns of crowds during major temple events. For a climbing-focused visit, subway plus taxi is a straightforward starting plan.

## Rules before you go

**Check notices for the named cliff and approach trail.** The [KNPS Bukhansan control page](https://www.knps.or.kr/front/portal/safe/acsCtrDtl.do?menuNo=8000340&parkId=121500) identifies specific affected areas. The partial restriction checked in October 2026 concerned a trail around Gucheon Valley, not a blanket closure of Insubong. Equally, a park remaining partly open does not prove your particular approach and cliff are open.

Recheck thaw notices before a spring trip. A [2026 notice reproduced by the Seoul Alpine Federation](https://saf.or.kr/forum_01/11188) explicitly included Insubong and allowed changes depending on weather. It was that year's arrangement, not a recurring calendar to copy into a future itinerary.

**Ask the Bukhansan office about the current declaration process.** KNPS has a [general rock-use application service](https://www.knps.or.kr/smartsafe/permission/list.do), but its generic procedure does not establish how Insubong is currently handled. A [2025 *Monthly Mountain* report](https://san.chosun.com/news/articleView.html?idxno=25147) quoted KNPS describing the Bukhansan system at that time as voluntary reporting. Confirm the current named-rock requirements and how visitors without Korean mobile verification can comply. Do not transplant another national park's permit rules.

Fixed hardware also needs a current assessment. The [2012 guide](https://product.kyobobook.co.kr/detail/S000001582823) warns about old bolts and runouts, while the [2025 topo](https://www.montblanclines.com/products/%ec%9d%b8%ec%88%98%eb%b4%89-insubong-east-face) records newer chained anchors. Neither guarantees every station you will encounter. Bring protection for the selected route and establish the ascent, rope configuration, descent and retreat options before leaving the ground. The route lengths below are not rappel-rope instructions.

For an emergency, call **119**; the [National Fire Agency's EMS information](https://www.nfa.go.kr/eng/ems/service/) explicitly includes foreigners. Save the Korean crag and route names and offline maps, and preserve phone battery. Ask your own insurer whether **trad climbing, multipitch climbing, search and rescue, medical treatment and repatriation** are covered. A general travel-insurance label is insufficient: the [BMC product summary](https://services.thebmc.co.uk/modules/insurance/r/downloads/BMC_IPID_2025_26_1225_Edition_1225.pdf), for example, specifies activity eligibility and exclusions. Your own policy controls your coverage.

## Routes by level

YDS and V-grade conversions follow the [Mountain Project international grade comparison chart](https://www.mountainproject.com/international-climbing-grades) and are approximate; "approx." marks grades the sources give without a/b/c (such as 4 or 5+). The original grade is the one that counts.

**This table is a mixed collection, not an East Face route list.** The beginner band includes everything the 2012 guide and [theCrag's Insubong page](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/south-korea/insubong) list, including lines that need aid (A0/A1) and lines that are not on the East Face. Above that band it keeps only routes the 2012 guide and the 2025 East Face topo both cover, because there is no second source to cross-check. A leading "2025 ✓/—" in Notes says whether the route appears on the 2025 East Face topo; "—" does not mean the route is missing, only that the topo covers the East Face alone.

**These sources already use YDS, which is retained rather than reverse-converted to French grades; the YDS column repeats the original grade without a conversion.** A multipitch grade describes climbing difficulty; A0, A1 and A2 denote aid and must not be dropped. Lengths and pitch counts mainly come from the 2012 guide and identify that version, rather than supplying current pitch or descent instructions.

### Beginner: up to 6a (YDS 5.10a and below)

For the easiest line, start with **Lonely Road**: theCrag calls it the easiest route on Insubong, 5.6 in six pitches. **Pigeon** is only a grade harder, but its third pitch is aid, so it is not a free route, and it is the main descent line. For classic moderate granite multipitch, compare **Insu A and Chouinard B** first. Both sources give 5.8. The old guide's Insu A is shorter; the longer Chouinard B puts more weight on team efficiency. "Beginner" here is a grade band, not a recommendation for a first outdoor lead.

### Expert: 8a and up (YDS 5.13b and up)

The guide tables and 2025 East Face topo used here do not provide a verified route in this band. Names from other crags or unverified entries are not used to fill it. If this is your main sport-climbing grade range, compare other areas in the series and decide whether to reserve a separate day for Insubong multipitch.

### Getting down

Insubong has more than one descent, and the wrong anchor costs you a rope length.

- **After summiting:** walk to the south side, follow the chains to the furthest right anchor and rappel **Pigeon (비둘기)**. theCrag records two 30 m rappels to the ground and warns that the wrong anchor leaves you short.
- **Lonely Road:** theCrag's description is to walk SE from the summit to find a rope, then west to a chain of roughly 10 m of bolts; the westernmost anchors reach the ground in 60 m.
- **Rappelling the route:** people do it on both lines, but you have to check the fixed gear yourself. The 2012 guide gives no descent configuration, and the lengths in this article cannot be used to plan rope length.

"Two 30 m rappels" means two rappels of 30 m each, not two 30 m ropes; one 60 m rope doubled covers a single 30 m rappel. The actual rope plan depends on the anchors you choose, team size and your retreat options.

## Where to look things up

- **Current management information:** the [KNPS Bukhansan control page](https://www.knps.or.kr/front/portal/safe/acsCtrDtl.do?menuNo=8000340&parkId=121500) and [rock-use service](https://www.knps.or.kr/smartsafe/permission/list.do) cover named restrictions and the application entry point respectively.
- **Transport and hiking approach:** [official Seoul tourism directions](https://english.visitseoul.net/seoul-hiking/Sweeping-views-of-Seoul-Baegundae%E2%80%93-Ui-Gugok-Trail/ENN042345) and the [KNPS Baegundae course](https://main.knps.or.kr/front/portal/visit/visitCourseGuide.do?parkId=121500&vcursId=121500V008). Distinguish the tourism center, trailhead, Baegundae summit and climbing start.
- **Guidebooks:** Grades and route counts here come mainly from online databases; the guidebook is the authority. This article also directly checks the following 2012 guide tables and preserves differences with the newer regional topo.
  - [*Climb: Rock Climbing in Korea*](https://product.kyobobook.co.kr/detail/S000001582823), Dong-il Ryou and Jonn Jeanneret, Outdoor Global (아웃도어글로벌), first edition 2012. English descriptions, with Korean place and route names; selected roped crags across Korea. Insubong occupies printed pages 230–237. It is not a source for present transport or access rules.
  - [*Insubong – East Face*](https://www.montblanclines.com/products/%ec%9d%b8%ec%88%98%eb%b4%89-insubong-east-face), produced and published by Mont Blanc Lines, initial version May 18, 2025. English description, Korean and romanized route names; a digital East Face topo, licensed for personal use, rather than a whole-crag guide.
