Jeokbyeok (적벽, also spelled Juk-byuk or Red Wall) stands beside Biseondae in Seoraksan, next to Janggunbong. The easiest mistake when reading the old guide is to notice 5.8 or 5.9 and overlook the A1 or A4 that follows.

This guide separates free and aid grades, helps you assess whether the wall suits your team, and explains the approach from Sokcho.

## What kind of crag

The [2012 Climb guide](https://product.kyobobook.co.kr/detail/S000001582823) lists six routes and describes a west-facing granite wall with steep cracks and overhangs. It is shorter than neighboring Janggunbong, but not consequently easier. [Mountain Project’s regional introduction](https://www.mountainproject.com/area/115392007/seoraksan-national-park-sokcho) likewise describes challenging overhanging climbing at Red Wall.

This is an objective for climbers already experienced in aid systems and steep-wall logistics. If the aim is moderate trad multipitch, compare specific lines at Janggunbong or Yuseondae rather than selecting Jeokbyeok by its lowest YDS number.

**Read free and aid grades separately.** A description of 5.8/A1 still includes aid climbing. Mura’s 5.9/A4 demands a different level of preparation and judgment; it should not be treated as another introductory 5.9.

## When to go

The [2012 guide](https://product.kyobobook.co.kr/detail/S000001582823) gives a west aspect, so afternoon sun matters for heat and water. Cool mornings can also be uncomfortable, and Sokcho’s city temperature is not the whole climbing forecast. A dry day in June or September–October is a reasonable first-visit candidate, subject to authorization, weather and wall conditions.

An overhang does not make the whole objective rainproof. Approaches, runoff, belays and retreat remain weather-dependent. Aid climbing may keep a team on the wall for a long time: leave a weather margin and consult [Seoraksan control information](https://www.knps.or.kr/front/portal/safe/acsCtrDtl.do?menuNo=8000340&parkId=120400).

## Getting there

### Public transport

Base yourself in Sokcho and take bus **7 or 7-1 to 설악산소공원 (Seoraksan Sogongwon)**. The [official Sokcho timetable](https://bis.sokcho.go.kr/schedule), consulted in October 2026, lists services by **Gangwon Passenger (강원여객)** and **Donghae Sangsa (동해상사)**. The [Korea Tourism Organization](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=204732) also gives these buses from the Sokcho Intercity Bus Terminal. Select the stop for your accommodation and direction in the city system; timetable endpoint times are not departures from your stop or return times from the park.

Walk from Sogongwon toward Biseondae, then follow the permitted approach for your selected line. The [2012 guide](https://product.kyobobook.co.kr/detail/S000001582823) describes the Jeokbyeok and Janggunbong Front Wall branches together before they separate. An old warning sign or faint trail is not current authorization to enter. Confirm **적벽** with the park and your local partner rather than reaching the neighboring wall and then searching for a start.

### Driving

Navigate to **설악산소공원**, the Seorak-dong entrance on the Sokcho side. A generic search for Seoraksan may send you toward a different valley. Driving gets you to the walking approach, not to the base of the cliff.

### Parking

Use the entrance-area car parks and follow the manager's current arrangements. Confirm access and collection times before leaving a car for a long multipitch day. Leave room in the schedule for walking back and reaching the car; a distant lower parking space can change the return plan.

## Rules before you go

Before booking a climbing day, tell the **Seoraksan office (+82-33-801-0900)** your date, **적벽 / Jeokbyeok**, exact route and team size. Use the [KNPS rock-use service](https://www.knps.or.kr/smartsafe/permission/list.do) to arrange the applicable process and confirm your authorization. The generic portal alone does not establish the current quota or procedure for every named wall; visitors without Korean mobile verification should clarify their application method with the office.

Check the [official control page](https://www.knps.or.kr/front/portal/safe/acsCtrDtl.do?menuNo=8000340&parkId=120400) for the approach as well as the rock. In October 2026 its extracted notice still carried an April reference date, so it should not be read as confirmation of today's conditions. A hiking trail being open does not authorize every climbing branch; annual fire-prevention dates are not a universal rock-climbing calendar.

Aid pitches can keep a team on the wall much longer. Establish protection, aid equipment, rope systems and exit options for each pitch before committing. Equipment suggestions in the [2012 guide](https://product.kyobobook.co.kr/detail/S000001582823) do not establish current fixed-hardware condition.

The old table’s Echo total differs from its pitch-length sum; an unnamed route also has inconsistent aid markings. The table below retains identifiable route information without turning conflicting totals into descent instructions.

Carry helmets and protection appropriate to the chosen route, and agree on retreat options before starting. Published route lengths are **not rappel-rope requirements**. In an emergency call **119**, as described by the [National Fire Agency](https://www.nfa.go.kr/eng/ems/service/), and provide the Korean rock and route names. Ask your insurer specifically about trad and multipitch climbing, rescue, medical treatment and repatriation. [BMC's policy summary](https://services.thebmc.co.uk/modules/insurance/r/downloads/BMC_IPID_2025_26_1225_Edition_1225.pdf) illustrates why activity exclusions and the full policy wording matter; use your own insurer's terms.

## Routes by level

YDS and V-grade conversions follow the [Mountain Project international grade comparison chart](https://www.mountainproject.com/international-climbing-grades) and are approximate; "approx." marks grades the sources give without a/b/c (such as 4 or 5+). The original grade is the one that counts.

The original sources use YDS. Values are retained without reverse-converting to French grades; aid suffixes are kept.

### Beginner: up to 6a (YDS 5.10a and below)

The free grade falls in this band, but both examples include aid. Only teams already familiar with the corresponding systems should consider them objectives.

| Route | Grade | YDS | Length / pitches | Sector | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Solo / 독주길 | 5.8/A1 | 5.8/A1 | 75 m / 3 pitches | West Face | Single 2012 guide source; pitches list A0, A1 and 5.8. |
| Mura / 무라길 | 5.9/A4 | 5.9/A4 | 100 m / 4 pitches | West Face | Single 2012 guide source; A4 is not introductory aid. |

### Intermediate: 6a+ to 6c+ (YDS 5.10b–5.11c)

Echo combines harder free climbing with A1 and A2. Plan aid as part of the route, not an improvised fallback when free moves become difficult.

| Route | Grade | YDS | Length / pitches | Sector | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Echo / 에코길 | 5.11c/A2 | 5.11c/A2 | 4 pitches; use current topo for length | West Face | Single 2012 source: total 98 m versus pitch sum 103 m. Not descent-planning data. |

### Advanced: 7a to 7c+ (YDS 5.11d–5.13a)

College of Education has a higher free grade in the old guide but still lists two A0 pitches. Do not delete the aid suffix and treat this as a complete free-climbing description.

| Route | Grade | YDS | Length / pitches | Sector | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| College of Education / 교대길 | 5.12b/A0 | 5.12b/A0 | 70 m / 3 pitches | West Face | Single 2012 guide source; pitches 2 and 3 list A0. |

### Expert: 8a and up (YDS 5.13b and up)

The sources used here do not establish a route at 5.13b or above. Difficulty at Jeokbyeok also lies in aid grades and steep-wall systems; there is no reason to invent a free-climbing example for this band.

## Where to look things up

- **Transport:** [Sokcho's official timetable](https://bis.sokcho.go.kr/schedule) for the two operators' 7/7-1 services; select your boarding stop separately.
- **Park management:** [KNPS control notices](https://www.knps.or.kr/front/portal/safe/acsCtrDtl.do?menuNo=8000340&parkId=120400) and [rock-use applications](https://www.knps.or.kr/smartsafe/permission/list.do), with direct office confirmation for the selected rock and route.
- **Online route records:** [Mountain Project's Seoraksan index](https://www.mountainproject.com/area/115392007/seoraksan-national-park-sokcho); useful for comparing current entries, but not an access authority.
- **Guidebooks:** Grades and route counts here come mainly from online databases; the guidebook is the authority. The tables distinguish the 2012 guide from other accounts; use a current route-specific topo.
  - [*Climb: Rock Climbing in Korea*](https://product.kyobobook.co.kr/detail/S000001582823), Dong-il Ryou and Jonn Jeanneret, Outdoor Global (아웃도어글로벌), first edition November 2012. English descriptions with Korean place and route names; selected roped crags across Korea. The Seoraksan section is on printed pages 196–205. It does not supply current access or hardware guarantees.
