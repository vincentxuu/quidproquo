Korean winter climbing is not confined to gyms. Munsusan combines sunny sport routes with an Ulsan city base, although a winter reputation does not make every cold day pleasant. This guide helps you select a sector, compare lengths and arrange the final uphill leg.

## What kind of crag

**문수산 / Munsusan** is near Ulsan. This article covers the climbing walls near Munsusa, rather than the summit hike. Both [*Climb* (2012)](https://product.kyobobook.co.kr/detail/S000001582823) and [Mountain Project](https://www.mountainproject.com/area/106798792/munsusan-ulsanbusan-area) describe south-facing gneiss, with upper and lower crags separated by a trail.

The book estimates about a hundred routes across nine walls; MP lists routes by upper and lower areas. These are different measures, not figures to add into a current total. There are short routes of roughly ten metres as well as Gijyeon lines exceeding thirty metres.

Book headings such as **Rabbit Boulder** and **Union Boulder** are sector names here: their YDS-graded routes are roped climbs more than ten metres long. Do not pack only pads because of the heading. Choose target sectors rather than carrying equipment up and down merely to visit everything.

## When to go

The [2020 MP area description](https://www.mountainproject.com/area/106798792/munsusan-ulsanbusan-area) identifies Munsusan as a particularly popular winter crag; its south aspect and prolonged sunshine agree with the book. I would investigate **calm sunny days from late autumn to early spring**, especially when seeking an alternative to colder mountain venues near Seoul.

A climber may be warm while a belayer remains in wind or tree shade. Bring adjustable layers and check wind and temperatures throughout the day. Warm spring and autumn days can be too sunny; [KMA’s](https://www.kma.go.kr/neng/biz/climate/climate-of-korea.do) hot, humid, rainy summer background further reduces the appeal of a south-facing wall. Any useful tree shade depends on the sector.

## Getting there

### Public transport

Reach Ulsan, then plan the connection toward **율리 / Yul-li** and the mountain entrance. The [city bus information page](https://www.ulsan.go.kr/u/traffic/contents.ulsan?mId=001003002000000000), consulted in October 2026, provides timetable and live-service links; the [Traffic Management Center instructions](https://www.its.ulsan.kr/noti/busInfo.do) describe stop, route and transfer searches. Search **문수산전망대** and **율리** for present options rather than copying old bus numbers.

MP’s 2020 approach account notes a substantial uphill leg between the bus and upper parking. For a first visit with rope bags, a taxi from the city or foothill to the observatory may be more practical. Arrange a return pickup rather than relying on finding a taxi at the top.

### Driving

The [official Ulsan webzine](https://webzine.ulsan.go.kr/contents/view.do?bbsId=BBSMSTR_000000000180&nttId=15457) gives the observatory entrance as **울산 울주군 청량읍 문수산길 514**. Navigate to **문수산 전망대 주차장**, not just the mountain name. The official description concerns a hiking entrance; the climbing sectors still require a topo.

MP describes the upper approach along the temple path, branching left before a railed stone staircase, and the lower approach from below the parking area. These are climber descriptions from 2020: compare current signs and a topo, respecting any restricted temple area.

### Parking

The [city’s 2025 article](https://webzine.ulsan.go.kr/contents/view.do?bbsId=BBSMSTR_000000000180&nttId=15457), consulted in October 2026, confirms parking at the observatory. Use designated spaces, keeping bends, temple access and emergency routes clear. Hikers share the entrance on weekends. Follow current signs for boundaries and charges; an observatory space is not permission to camp overnight.

## Rules before you go

The upper and lower walls are connected terrain. The 2012 book warns against dislodging rocks onto people below; watch your feet while searching for starts or moving equipment, and wear helmets at the base as well as on the wall. Keep the shared temple and hiking path clear, reduce noise, take rubbish out and avoid new shortcuts.

The book records past KOTRi replacement work, not today’s maintenance status on every line. Inspect bolts and anchors and choose another route if protection is questionable. Short and long sectors also need different rope planning: the book lists Existing B and Route for Memories at 34 metres, so a generic 60-metre recommendation cannot establish a direct lower-off.

Works, forest management or temple activities may change entrance arrangements. Confirm with local climbers and site managers before travelling, then follow current signs.

Emergency assistance: call **119**, the number described by the [National Fire Agency](https://www.nfa.go.kr/eng/ems/service/). Save the Korean crag name, your route and an offline location before setting out. Ask your insurer specifically about outdoor lead climbing, rescue, medical treatment and repatriation; policy names alone do not establish coverage.

## Routes by level

YDS and V-grade conversions follow the [Mountain Project international grade comparison chart](https://www.mountainproject.com/international-climbing-grades) and are approximate; "approx." marks grades the sources give without a/b/c (such as 4 or 5+). The original grade is the one that counts.

These are named historical records from *Climb* (2012), printed pages 36–37. Original YDS grades are retained rather than reverse-converted to French grades. Each row identifies the single book source. Lengths are not current descent instructions.

### Beginner: up to 6a (YDS 5.10a and below)

Short Rabbit routes and the longer Gijyeon example fall in the same grade band but need very different rope planning. A short initial route helps assess the rock.

### Expert: 8a and up (YDS 5.13b and up)

The adopted precisely graded selection contains no route here. Vision is recorded only as 5.13*, without a letter; it cannot be assigned to 5.13b or above.
