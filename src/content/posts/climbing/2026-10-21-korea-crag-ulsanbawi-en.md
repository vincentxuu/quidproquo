Ulsanbawi (울산바위) is Seoraksan’s granite wall of peaks. Tourists take stairs to an observation platform, climbers follow cracks up individual walls, and ridge parties traverse the chain of summits. The same place name can therefore describe very different days.

This guide separates wall routes from the long ridge, helps you compare objectives, and explains transport from Sokcho.

## What kind of crag

First decide which Ulsanbawi you mean. [Monthly Mountain’s 2025 feature](https://san.chosun.com/news/articleView.html?idxno=25056) distinguishes wall multipitch from ridge climbing: the walls emphasize cracks and chimneys, while the ridge involves repeated climbing, rappelling, detours and navigation. For a single climbing day, start by comparing a specific wall route rather than treating the complete ridge as a longer summit hike.

The [2012 guide index](https://product.kyobobook.co.kr/detail/S000001582823) lists 26 routes; the 2025 feature describes roughly 20-plus identifiable wall lines. These are different inventories at different dates. More significantly, restored routes and free-climbing variants may change the meaning of an old name, grade or pitch count.

Wide cracks and chimneys require full-body technique and effort. Indoor grades will not predict your speed. The leader needs competent removable protection skills, and followers need to manage exposure and long pitches.

## When to go

For a first visit, consider **a dry day in June or September–October**, then adjust around that year’s access and weather. This is a planning preference, not an opening guarantee. The [KNPS control page](https://www.knps.or.kr/front/portal/safe/acsCtrDtl.do?menuNo=8000340&parkId=120400) is the management reference; spring fire restrictions, residual snow and temporary weather controls can affect approaches.

The massif has multiple aspects, so a single south-facing label is inadequate. Check your actual line in the topo: plan for sun in warm weather and exposed belays in cold wind. The [2025 ridge feature](https://san.chosun.com/news/articleView.html?idxno=25056) treats the complete ridge as a multiday undertaking; autumn daylight must cover descents as well as climbing.

## Getting there

### Public transport

Base yourself in Sokcho and take bus **7 or 7-1 to 설악산소공원 (Seoraksan Sogongwon)**. The [official Sokcho timetable](https://bis.sokcho.go.kr/schedule), consulted in October 2026, lists services by **Gangwon Passenger (강원여객)** and **Donghae Sangsa (동해상사)**. The [Korea Tourism Organization](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=204732) also gives these buses from the Sokcho Intercity Bus Terminal. Select the stop for your accommodation and direction in the city system; timetable endpoint times are not departures from your stop or return times from the park.

From Sogongwon follow signs toward Sinheungsa, Heundeulbawi (흔들바위) and Ulsanbawi. The [official tourism itinerary](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=204732) of 7.6 km and four hours is a **round-trip summit hike**, not an approach time for every climb. Locate the specific wall or ridge start with your selected topo rather than following tourists to the top of the stairs and then searching for a route.

### Driving

Navigate to **설악산소공원**, the Seorak-dong entrance on the Sokcho side. A generic search for Seoraksan may send you toward a different valley. Driving gets you to the walking approach, not to the base of the cliff.

### Parking

Use the entrance-area car parks and follow the manager's current arrangements. Confirm access and collection times before leaving a car for a long multipitch day. Leave room in the schedule for walking back and reaching the car; a distant lower parking space can change the return plan.

## Rules before you go

Before booking a climbing day, tell the **Seoraksan office (+82-33-801-0900)** your date, **울산바위 / Ulsanbawi**, exact route and team size. Use the [KNPS rock-use service](https://www.knps.or.kr/smartsafe/permission/list.do) to arrange the applicable process and confirm your authorization. The generic portal alone does not establish the current quota or procedure for every named wall; visitors without Korean mobile verification should clarify their application method with the office.

Check the [official control page](https://www.knps.or.kr/front/portal/safe/acsCtrDtl.do?menuNo=8000340&parkId=120400) for the approach as well as the rock. In October 2026 its extracted notice still carried an April reference date, so it should not be read as confirmation of today's conditions. A hiking trail being open does not authorize every climbing branch; annual fire-prevention dates are not a universal rock-climbing calendar.

[Monthly Mountain’s 2025 feature](https://san.chosun.com/news/articleView.html?idxno=25056) explicitly describes Ulsanbawi climbing through the KNPS application service (**secondary source: climbing-media report; confirm current named-route procedures with the office**). A year-round hiking description is not climbing authorization.

The old guide warns of aged hardware. The [2025 history feature](https://san.chosun.com/news/articleView.html?idxno=25033) documents wall restoration in the 2010s and ridge maintenance in 2023–2024. Work on particular lines does not establish that every anchor on the massif has been replaced.

Carry helmets and protection appropriate to the chosen route, and agree on retreat options before starting. Published route lengths are **not rappel-rope requirements**. In an emergency call **119**, as described by the [National Fire Agency](https://www.nfa.go.kr/eng/ems/service/), and provide the Korean rock and route names. Ask your insurer specifically about trad and multipitch climbing, rescue, medical treatment and repatriation. [BMC's policy summary](https://services.thebmc.co.uk/modules/insurance/r/downloads/BMC_IPID_2025_26_1225_Edition_1225.pdf) illustrates why activity exclusions and the full policy wording matter; use your own insurer's terms.

## Routes by level

YDS and V-grade conversions follow the [Mountain Project international grade comparison chart](https://www.mountainproject.com/international-climbing-grades) and are approximate; "approx." marks grades the sources give without a/b/c (such as 4 or 5+). The original grade is the one that counts.

The original sources use YDS. Values are retained without reverse-converting to French grades; aid suffixes are kept.

### Beginner: up to 6a (YDS 5.10a and below)

For moderate multipitch, compare Sasun Crack and Munri-dae, but choose the actual topo version first. Pitch counts differ between the old guide and database entries.

| Route | Grade | YDS | Length / pitches | Sector | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sasun Crack / 사선크랙 | 5.10a (MP); 5.9 (2012) | 5.10a / 5.9 | MP: 5 pitches; 2012: 234 m / 7 pitches | Wall | 2025 feature: about 230 m / 7 pitches. Sources are listed separately; confirm the linkup and pitch division. |
| Munri-dae / 문리대길 | 5.10a (MP); 5.9/A0 (2012) | 5.10a / 5.9/A0 | MP: 8 pitches; 2012: 201 m / 7 pitches | Wall | Old A0 retained; confirm protection and linkup for the selected version. |

### Intermediate: 6a+ to 6c+ (YDS 5.10b–5.11c)

Anda-miro is a harder crack multipitch option listed by MP. The older Silver description includes aid, so its free grade is only part of the route.

| Route | Grade | YDS | Length / pitches | Sector | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Anda-miro / 안다미로 | 5.11a (hardest) | 5.11a | 6 pitches; length from topo | Wall | Grade from MP alone. |

### Advanced: 7a to 7c+ (YDS 5.11d–5.13a)

Venus warrants more preparation than its old 5.10c label suggests. The 2025 feature describes steep cracks, chimneys and large protection; MP assigns a harder free grade.

| Route | Grade | YDS | Length / pitches | Sector | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

| Yoban / 요반길 | 5.12a (MP); old table 5.9 | 5.12a / 5.9 | MP: 10 pitches; old table: 7 pitches, conflicting total | Wall | Old overall entry 225 m versus pitch sum 280 m. Accounts differ: confirm linkup, pitch division and free/aid style. |

### Expert: 8a and up (YDS 5.13b and up)

The sources used here do not establish a route at 5.13b or above. The full ridge also cannot be judged by its hardest move alone: repeated descents, navigation and endurance determine completion.

## Where to look things up

- **Transport:** [Sokcho's official timetable](https://bis.sokcho.go.kr/schedule) for the two operators' 7/7-1 services; select your boarding stop separately.
- **Park management:** [KNPS control notices](https://www.knps.or.kr/front/portal/safe/acsCtrDtl.do?menuNo=8000340&parkId=120400) and [rock-use applications](https://www.knps.or.kr/smartsafe/permission/list.do), with direct office confirmation for the selected rock and route.
- **Online route records:** [Mountain Project's Seoraksan index](https://www.mountainproject.com/area/115392007/seoraksan-national-park-sokcho); useful for comparing current entries, but not an access authority.
- **Guidebooks:** Grades and route counts here come mainly from online databases; the guidebook is the authority. The tables distinguish the 2012 guide from other accounts; use a current route-specific topo.
  - [*Climb: Rock Climbing in Korea*](https://product.kyobobook.co.kr/detail/S000001582823), Dong-il Ryou and Jonn Jeanneret, Outdoor Global (아웃도어글로벌), first edition November 2012. English descriptions with Korean place and route names; selected roped crags across Korea. The Seoraksan section is on printed pages 196–205. It does not supply current access or hardware guarantees.
  - [Monthly Mountain’s 2025 route feature](https://san.chosun.com/news/articleView.html?idxno=25056) compares walls and ridges; its [history and maintenance feature](https://san.chosun.com/news/articleView.html?idxno=25033) explains restoration. Korean-language editorial guides, not station-by-station descent topos.
