Korean climbing often brings Insubong to mind first: granite slabs and cracks above Seoul, with the city unfolding behind you as you gain height. But if your ideal trip means clipping bolts and working moves on short routes, Gochang’s limestone may be a better match. Bouldering opens up another map altogether.

This series covers natural climbing areas in South Korea: sport, trad, multipitch and bouldering. Each article focuses on a crag or a clearly defined area, starting with [Insubong](/posts/climbing/2026-10-21-korea-crag-insubong-en). This overview helps you choose equipment, a base and the sources to consult next.

## Choose the style before the base

These are representative destinations, not a national inventory. Jeju and Seoraksan contain distinct climbing areas; their names alone do not identify a crag.

| Destination | Region | Main attraction | Before choosing |
|---|---|---|---|

| [Seoninbong / 선인봉](/posts/climbing/2026-10-21-korea-crag-seoninbong-en) | Seoul, Dobongsan | Granite multipitch and fist cracks | Check named routes, rockfall notices and approach |

| [Janggunbong / 장군봉](/posts/climbing/2026-10-21-korea-crag-janggunbong-en) | Gangwon, Seoraksan | Granite long routes above the valley | Plan approach, team pace and retreat |
| [Jeokbyeok / 적벽](/posts/climbing/2026-10-21-korea-crag-jeokbyeok-en) | Gangwon, Seoraksan | Steep free and aid lines | Do not drop aid grades from the description |

| [Musucheon / 무수천](/posts/climbing/2026-10-21-korea-crag-musucheon-en) | Jeju | Basalt river-valley roped climbing | Water levels and heavy rain can change the day |

Rock and style descriptions draw on the relevant chapters of *Climb* and the linked area sources. Inclusion does not establish current permission to climb. Busan, Chungcheong and other bouldering areas offer further options through Korean guides and regional directories; one trip does not need to become a national tour.

## Narrow the choice by season

The [Korea Meteorological Administration](https://www.kma.go.kr/neng/biz/climate/climate-of-korea.do) describes a summer rainy season, Changma, large seasonal temperature differences and lower autumn humidity. That is the national backdrop. Aspect, altitude, wind and drying conditions determine which wall makes sense.

| Period | Planning starting point | What can change the decision |
|---|---|---|
| Spring and autumn | Compare Seoul granite, Wonju and Gochang | Spring thaw or fire restrictions need separate checks; autumn can also bring typhoon effects |
| Summer | Look for shaded sectors and flexible weather windows | Prolonged rain, humidity and flash flooding; a sheltered overhang does not shelter the approach or descent |
| Winter | Investigate low, sunny walls such as Munsusan | South-facing does not guarantee warmth; wind, shade and daily temperatures still matter |

Munsusan’s winter reputation comes from its [Mountain Project area description](https://www.mountainproject.com/area/106798792/munsusan-ulsanbusan-area), not a promise about every wall near Ulsan. Seonunsan also needs a sector-level decision: *Climb* describes southwest-facing Soksal, a shaded passage at Tugu and vertical climbing at Mun. Identify your intended sector before booking accommodation.

## Think about the last leg of transport

Research reaching the city and reaching the route separately. A bus to the foot of a mountain is not a bus to the first bolt. Include the return journey.

Seoul makes a base for individual Insubong days. For several Gochang sport areas, work out connections between accommodation and each trailhead before deciding whether to rent a car. On Jeju, include pads in the transport plan: vehicle space, collection arrangements and meeting points can matter more than airport transfers.

Save the Korean name, the actual entrance and the return meeting point. A map search for “선운산” or “북한산” identifies a large mountain area; parking, hiking entrances and climbing approaches are separate locations. Old guidebook bus numbers, fares and shortcuts through temple grounds are not current travel instructions.

## Reading the grades

Korean roped-climbing sources commonly use **YDS**, such as 5.9, 5.10a and 5.12b. Bouldering often uses **V grades**. This series retains original grades and uses the [Mountain Project international comparison chart](https://www.mountainproject.com/international-climbing-grades) when a conversion is useful. Conversions are approximate comparisons.

Three details matter beyond the number:

- **Style:** sport-climbing 5.10 experience does not establish competence on granite slabs or cracks of the same grade.
- **Protection and length:** a multipitch route’s hardest pitch says little about protection spacing, route-finding or descent complexity.
- **Aid notation:** the A0 in “5.10a A0” carries additional information. Dropping it changes what the grade describes.

For each objective, read the original name, original grade, free or aid style, pitch descriptions and descent together. Two topos can attach different finishes to the same route name.

## Where to look things up

Korea has established specialist magazines, local topo makers and smaller databases. Use each for the task it serves.

| Source | Useful for | How to use it |
|---|---|---|
| [월간산 / Monthly Mountain](https://san.chosun.com/news/articleView.html?idxno=10118) and [사람과산 / People and Mountains](https://www.sansan.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=9990) | Named route descriptions, development, restoration and climbing culture | Keep the publication date; restoration coverage is not a permanent hardware guarantee |
| [FLASH.climblog](https://flash.climblog.kr/routes?crag=간현암) and [Granite](https://granite.kr/c/anyang?tab=route) | Korean route names, ascents and boulder problems | Find the original name before watching beta; separately listed pitches are not necessarily separate complete routes |
| [The Topo](https://thetopo.com/countries/korea-republic-of) and [theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/south-korea) | Area directories, route information and topo authors | The Topo was formerly 27 Crags; these are two names for the same platform |
| [Korea on the Rocks / KOTR](https://www.koreaontherocks.com/home/) | English-language area maps and information contributed by foreign climbers | Follow older reports to current access and transport sources |
| [Korea National Park Service / KNPS](https://www.knps.or.kr/front/portal/safe/acsCtrList.do?menuNo=8000340) | Park restrictions and individual park announcements | Read the detailed scope; a closed trail does not mean a whole park is closed to climbing |

Naver Blog, Daum Cafe and Tistory also host pitch-by-pitch photographs. Seek named creators and original posts, then compare dates. Several blogs reproducing the same topo still constitute one underlying source.

- **Guidebooks:** Grades and route counts here come mainly from online databases; the guidebook is the authority. Read the edition alongside the information:
  - [*Climb: Rock Climbing in Korea*](https://www.ukclimbing.com/logbook/books/climb_rock_climbing_in_south_korea-1362), Dong-il Ryou and Jonn Jeanneret, Outdoor Global (아웃도어글로벌), first edition 2012. English descriptions with Korean place and route names, covering areas around the country. It is useful for geography and climbing styles; the introduction explicitly distinguishes it from an accurate route guide. Old transport and access instructions need updating.
  - [*Insubong – East Face*](https://www.montblanclines.com/products/%ec%9d%b8%ec%88%98%eb%b4%89-insubong-east-face), published by Mont Blanc Lines, first version 2025. English descriptions with Korean and romanized route names, covering Insubong’s east face. This is a regional topo, not a new edition of *Climb* or a complete descent guide.

## Shared etiquette and finding a partner

Check the managing authority before relying on an ascent report. The [KNPS rock-wall service](https://www.knps.or.kr/smartsafe/permission/list.do) describes an application process, but the applicable rules depend on the specific park, wall and current announcement. There is no basis for treating that page as a uniform national permit rule. Private land, temples, parking and camping also have their own managers; a route in an old book does not establish present access.

Use designated parking, keep roads and paths clear, take rubbish out and follow posted fire and camping rules. Do not cross a fence to reach an old start. If an entrance has changed, ask the manager or local climbers.

Start partner enquiries through KOTR’s English-language network, local gyms or climbing guides. KOTR’s homepage links to [iGuideKorea](https://www.iguidekorea.com/) for courses and customized climbing trips. If you need instruction, equipment or an introduction to multipitch systems, ask about those services explicitly. A contact link does not guarantee availability on your dates.

Before meeting anyone, specify dates, destination, the styles and grades you lead, whether you independently manage belays and rappels, your equipment and a rain alternative. Climb together indoors or on short routes with straightforward retreats before committing to a full mountain day.

## A first-trip decision

Suppose you have four days and normally sport climb 5.10. Do you want several short routes each day, or a granite multipitch objective?

For the first, compare individual routes at Gochang’s Halmae, the more moderate Seonunsan sectors and Ganhyeon. Establish enough warm-ups and objectives before solving transport. For the second, start with Insubong, then assemble a team with suitable trad and descent skills or book an appropriate guide. Your sport grade does not replace that preparation.

If bouldering is the goal, spend the planning time on local topos, pads and conditions. There is little reason to carry a roped-climbing rack between regions you do not intend to use it in. One base and a flexible day usually give a short trip more climbing time than three distant regions.

This edition selects 13 crags and named areas across Seoul, Gangwon, Jeolla, the southeast and Jeju; it is not a national route list. Individual articles cover approaches, rules, routes by level and information sources. Actual climbing still needs a topo appropriate to the chosen route, weather judgement and suitable skills.
