Pha Tam Kam is the valley that holds Green Climbers Home, with crags wrapped around the two camps. This guide covers only the walls within a 5–20 minute walk: Tree Lounge, Swiss Oldies, Elephant, Hilton, Schnecke, Schöffl Block, The Roof, Music Hall, plus Botanical Gardens and Bayerischer Wald. Hangover, Partymeile, Open All Hours and Burnout, on the other side of the valley, are in the [valley walls guide](/posts/climbing/2026-10-21-laos-crag-pha-tam-kam-valley).

This guide helps you settle three things: **which camp to book, which wall matches your grade, and which wall's shade saves your afternoon.** The transport, visa and insurance details are in the [Thakhek base guide](/posts/climbing/2026-10-21-laos-crag-thakhek-base).

## What kind of crag this is

The whole valley is karst limestone towers, with routes from 12 to 40 metres. The walls around camp have several distinct characters:

- **Slabs and vertical faces:** Tree Lounge, Swiss Oldies, Elephant, Hilton. Holds range from small edges and pockets to big tufas, and the grades suit teams climbing 5a to 6c.
- **Short, steep tufa walls:** Schnecke, Schöffl Block, Music Hall. The routes are not long, but the movement is continuous and you have to read the tufa.
- **A fully horizontal roof:** The Roof. It is the best-known wall here, with 43 routes from 6b+ to 8a+ and perma-draws throughout, so there is no stripping.

French grades are the local standard; YDS conversions are approximate, and the original grade is the one that counts.

## When to go

The Thakhek season runs from October to May, with December and January the driest and coolest. The walls face different directions, so shade is a planning tool in hot weather:

- **Morning:** Tree Lounge, Swiss Oldies and Schöffl Block have shade most of the day and are good early choices.
- **Afternoon:** Elephant comes into the shade after midday; The Roof is a cave and stays shaded most of the day, which makes it the comfortable afternoon option.
- **Late afternoon:** the routes beside the Roof and at Hilton catch the sun, so in summer wait until after 4 pm.

The Roof sheds light rain, but after heavy rain the rock above seeps, so do not treat the cave as all-weather. The camp closes for the monsoon from May to September, and crags can become inaccessible.

## Getting there

### Public transport

There is no public transport to Pha Tam Kam; you have to reach GCH first. The smoothest route from Taipei is Bangkok Don Mueang, Nakhon Phanom, then the international bus across the Third Friendship Bridge to **Thakhek**, followed by a 20-minute tuk-tuk to camp. Timetables, visas and insurance are in the [Thakhek base guide](/posts/climbing/2026-10-21-laos-crag-thakhek-base). A tuk-tuk can also be hired from Thakhek town straight to the camp gate.

### Driving

**You do not drive into the valley.** The crags are walked to from GCH, and the camp has parking for cars and motorbikes; park at camp and walk in.

What does matter is the road to camp: the bus from Vientiane takes 7 to 9 hours, and Route 13 mixes tarmac and dirt, turning muddy in the wet season.

### Parking

The camp has parking for cars and motorbikes. Staff can arrange tuk-tuks or rentals. Some room types include transfers.

## Rules before you go

**Bolts and anchors.** Routes here use **10 mm expansion bolts**. Anchors are two bolts linked by a sling, with a fixed screwgate or maillon on one of them as the lower-off. GCH makes two points:

1. **Do not clip gear to the sling joining the two bolts.** That sling is part of the anchor.
2. When cleaning the anchor, thread the rope through the lower-off ring, not through the sling.

Fixed bolts **cannot be inspected daily**, so check hangers and nuts yourself. Carry a wrench and deal with loose hardware.

**Two climbers have died at Elephant.** On 30 January 2025, one tied the rope to a gear loop on the harness and the loop failed during the lower. On 14 February 2026, a climber fell to the ground while cleaning an anchor; the investigation could not fully reconstruct the rope setup. Neither was a rock or bolt failure, and both happened while cleaning — run the sequence through with your partner before you go up.

**There is no ambulance.** Serious injuries usually cross to Nakhon Phanom in Thailand, and the border closes at 22:00. Buy insurance that covers climbing and medical evacuation, and carry a basic first-aid kit.

**There are only a few multipitches.** GCH says four in the area; ClimbGrades says three. Chinese New Year is four pitches, 130 metres, 6b (UKC also lists 6a+). Confirm the current rope length, anchors and descent with the camp before trying it.

The original grade is the one that counts.

### Expert: 8a and up (YDS 5.13b and up)

Very few named routes here are publicly verifiable. theCrag grades Big Smile 7c+ while UKC grades it 8a, so it sits in the advanced table; only one route is listed here with an explicit 8a in the UKC photo captions.

| Route | Grade | YDS | Length | Sector | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Relapse | 8a | 5.13b | — | Near The Roof | UKC photo caption |

Ask the camp which 8s are actually climbable and in what condition. The perma-draws on The Roof are convenient, but wear and tightness still need checking.
