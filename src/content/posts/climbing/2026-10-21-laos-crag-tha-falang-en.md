Tha Falang is the nearest independent crag to Green Climbers Home: less than 2 km downstream, about a 20–30 minute walk. Most visitors spend their time at Pha Tam Kam, so Tha Falang stays quiet and the routes feel more raw. The Crag Journal's 2023 author counted "over 30 routes"; theCrag listed 33 on 21 October 2026.

If you are in Thakhek for more than a week, want a different wall, want to escape the crowds, or want longer routes, it is worth a day. But this is a crag that has been bolted and is not maintained daily — not the kind of wall people walk past every morning at camp.

## What kind of crag this is

Tha Falang is a limestone wall facing the river valley. The routes are longer than the camp walls at Pha Tam Kam, with styles ranging from vertical faces and tufas to a large cave. The Crag Journal's description is that the rock is good, but some routes have plants growing on them and need cleaning; some sectors are dirty, with dust and cobwebs.

The sectors are:

| Sector | Style | Routes | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Disneyland | Beginner to intermediate, vertical | Several | Good warm-up |
| Zombieland | Next to Disneyland, short routes | Several | — |
| Wonderland | Long routes, tufa | Several | Needs endurance |
| KeLu Block | Separate block | Few | Short routes |
| Phantasialand | Big cave, long and overhanging | Several | The headline sector |
| Xe Bang Fie Cave | Cave | Few | Different approach |

theCrag does not publish a complete per-sector count, only the total of 33. "Several" is left as is rather than invented into precise numbers.

## When to go

Thakhek's dry season runs from October to May, and the same applies here. Shade information for Tha Falang is thin: The Crag Journal does not describe the aspect, and theCrag does not mark sun exposure. In practice:

- **Morning:** the river and cave areas are usually shadier, good for longer routes first.
- **Afternoon:** some vertical faces catch the sun; in hot weather, check whether your route is in shade.
- **Wet season:** river levels rise and the path can become impassable; rock seeps after rain, except in the cave.

With no live information, plan a first visit for the morning, carry enough water and sun protection, and leave by early afternoon.

## Getting there

### Public transport

There is no public transport to Tha Falang. The basic option is to walk downstream along the valley from GCH for about 20–30 minutes; the Crag Journal puts the distance at under 2 km. The path passes farmland and the river, and can flood or turn muddy in the wet season.

### Driving

You can ride a motorbike to within a few minutes of the crag and walk the rest; the camp can arrange a taxi or rental. Driving lets you carry more water and gear, at the cost of riding the same track back.

### Parking

There is no formal parking at the crag; leave the motorbike on a wide spot by the track. **Do not park where it blocks farm vehicles or house access.**

## Rules before you go

**No daily maintenance.** Tha Falang's routes are not checked every day like the walls at camp. Bolts, hangers and anchors can be loose or rusty; inspect them yourself, and carry a wrench and a sling.

**Routes may be overgrown.** The Crag Journal notes that some routes need cleaning, which means dust, cobwebs or loose flakes. Watch for rockfall while leading, and wear a helmet at the belay.

**No rescue resources.** The crag is 20–30 minutes from camp, so a carry-out is harder than at Pha Tam Kam. There is no ambulance, and serious injuries have to cross to Nakhon Phanom in Thailand.

**Do not go alone.** The quiet is the point, but it is also the risk. Go with at least one other person and tell camp your plan.

## Routes by level

YDS conversions follow the [Mountain Project international grade comparison chart](https://www.mountainproject.com/international-climbing-grades) and are approximate; "approx." marks grades the sources give without a/b/c (such as 4 or 5+). The original grade is the one that counts.

Public data for this area is thin: theCrag does not publish a per-route index for Tha Falang and only lists nearby route icons, so names, grades and lengths for the rest depend on the *Laos Rock Climbing Guidebook*. **Where the sources list nothing, nothing is forced into the tables.**

### Beginner: up to 6a (YDS 5.10a and below)

**Not listed by the sources.** theCrag publishes no per-route data for this band, and the guidebook does cover it but has not been checked page by page here. Asking the camp which wall the beginner lines are on is faster than guessing.

### Intermediate: 6a+ to 6c+ (YDS 5.10b–5.11c)

**Not listed by the sources.** The 2023 Crag Journal author notes the good rock, the overgrown routes and the large potential here, but lists no verifiable intermediate route names.

### Advanced: 7a to 7c+ (YDS 5.11d–5.13a)

This is the only band with named routes that can be verified publicly.

| Route | Grade | YDS | Length | Sector | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Caterpillar | 7a | 5.11d | — | Tha Falang | theCrag icon |
| March Hare | 7a+ | 5.12a | — | Tha Falang | theCrag icon |

Both appear among theCrag's nearby icons. They are long and continuous, and reward endurance and tufa-reading.

### Expert: 8a and up (YDS 5.13b and up)

**Not listed by the sources.** theCrag does not show Tha Falang 8s among its public icons; the area is mostly 5s to 7s. Do not assume the routes are easy just because the crag is quiet.
