Thakhek sits in Khammouane Province in central Laos, on the east bank of the Mekong, opposite Nakhon Phanom in Thailand. The town is about 12 km from the Pha Tam Kam crags. In the valley below them is Green Climbers Home (GCH): two camps, a restaurant, gear rental and courses. Most climbers do not stay in Thakhek and commute; they stay at the crag.

This guide covers arrival and risk: **how to get here from Taipei, how to handle the visa, whether to stay at Camp 1 or Camp 2, what it costs, and what to know about bolts and anchors** — plus the four accidents reported in 2025 and 2026. The routes and individual walls are in the [Pha Tam Kam camp walls](/posts/climbing/2026-10-21-laos-crag-pha-tam-kam-camp) and [valley walls](/posts/climbing/2026-10-21-laos-crag-pha-tam-kam-valley) guides.

## What kind of crag this is

GCH occupies the Pha Tam Kam valley, with the two camps about 500 metres apart. Camp 1, the original camp, opened in 2012 at the far end of the valley; the restaurant, bar and most room types are here. Camp 2 opened in 2015 near the valley entrance and the road, and has mobile signal but no Wi-Fi.

GCH says Camp 1 has 180 routes within a 200-metre radius and the nearest crag is 50 metres away. Camp 2 is about 200 metres from the morning crags and 250 metres from the famous Roof. That sounds like marketing until you see the valley: the route density really is high enough to spend several days on one wall or change sector every day.

The rock is karst limestone. Routes run from 12 to 40 metres, from friction slabs and vertical faces to fully horizontal roofs. GCH puts the national total at more than 600 routes, most of them in Thakhek. theCrag listed 636 routes in Thakhek on 21 October 2026: 530 sport routes at Pha Tam Kam and 33 at Tha Falang.

## When to go

- **October to May:** the climbing season, and the camp operates through it. GCH calls **November to February** peak season: daytime temperatures of 22–28°C, dry air, and nights that occasionally drop to around 10°C. December and January are driest and have the best friction, and the camp is also fullest.
- **Early February to the end of March:** the burning season. GCH says Thakhek is usually affected less than northern Thailand, but it varies by year.
- **Mid-April to mid-May:** the hottest stretch, when afternoon rock becomes too hot to hold.
- **May to September:** the monsoon, when the camp closes and crags can become inaccessible.

## Getting there

### Public transport

This is the smoothest route from Taiwan and the one GCH recommends.

1. Fly Taipei to Bangkok Don Mueang (DMK). There are no direct flights from Taipei to Laos; this is the international leg.
2. Fly Don Mueang to Nakhon Phanom (KOP), several times a day, about 1 hour 40 minutes.
3. Take a taxi from the airport to the bus station in town, about 20 minutes.
4. Take the international bus from Nakhon Phanom to Thakhek at **09:00, 11:30, 14:30 or 16:30**. The trip takes about two hours including the border. The return bus leaves at 09:30, 11:30, 14:30 and 16:30.
5. From Thakhek bus station to GCH, a tuk-tuk takes about 20 minutes.

If your flight lands at 16:25, the 16:30 bus is effectively impossible to catch. GCH suggests taking a taxi from the airport straight to the Thai border and trying to board the same bus there; if it has gone, take a tuk-tuk across the bridge. **Pedestrians are not allowed to walk across the Friendship Bridge.**

**Via Vientiane:** the bus takes 7 to 9 hours and costs 180,000–260,000 kip (GCH's 2026 figure; a Laotian Times report on Pi Mai extra services in April 2026 quoted 230,000–300,000 kip). GCH puts it bluntly: "If somebody tells you it only takes 5 hours, don't believe them." Lao roads look reasonable, but buses stop often and average speeds are lower than the map suggests.

**Via Pakse:** a VIP bus costs about 180,000 kip and a local bus about 80,000 kip; the journey takes around seven hours. This suits travellers coming up from the south.

### Driving

GCH runs an airport shuttle: US$80 per vehicle for one or two passengers, US$100 for three to eight. It is expensive for a solo traveller, but useful if you arrive late or do not want to deal with the border.

Lao driving differs from Taiwan: keep left, expect many motorbikes, and expect potholes on country roads. **Do not rent a car without an international licence** — a hired car and driver is safer.

### Parking

The camp has parking for cars and motorbikes. The crags in the Pha Tam Kam valley are walked to, so there is no second drive.

## Rules before you go

### Visas and the border

Taiwanese passports have two options:

- **Visa on arrival:** single entry, 30 days, US$40 according to [Taiwan's Bureau of Consular Affairs](https://www.boca.gov.tw/sp-foof-countrycp-03-117-2e720-02-1.html). You need a passport valid for at least six months and two photos. GCH says the fee is typically US$30–50 depending on nationality, and that the notes must be clean and undamaged.
- **eVisa:** US$50 from [laoevisa.gov.la](https://laoevisa.gov.la/). Taiwanese passport holders must supply an extra photo on arrival for a separate "green visa stamp booklet".

**The Third Friendship Bridge is the complication.** Laos' eVisa site does not list the bridge as an entry point, but the Lao immigration page for the crossing says visa on arrival and eVisa are available. GCH says most nationalities can get visa on arrival there, while explicitly stating that **Indian passports cannot use the Nakhon Phanom/Thakhek crossing**. In other words, the crossing does have nationality restrictions, so "most nationalities" is not a safe inference for Taiwanese passports.

I searched Backpackers, PTT, Dcard, Mobile01, blogs, YouTube, Reddit and Xiaohongshu and **found no official statement or recent first-hand report of a Taiwanese passport obtaining visa on arrival at the Third Friendship Bridge**. The Taiwanese reports I did find were from Vientiane airport, Luang Prabang airport, the First Friendship Bridge and the Fourth Friendship Bridge. In each case the visa and stamps went into a separate booklet, so check that nothing is stamped in the passport itself.

The conservative approach: **do not buy an eVisa for this crossing.** Carry more than US$40 in clean notes and three photos, and email GCH before you travel to ask about recent Taiwanese passports. For zero risk, fly to Vientiane or get a paper visa from a Lao embassy first. Entering Thailand overland on the return uses one of the two annual land-entry allowances, and both Thai entries require a TDAC.

### Bolts and anchors

Thakhek's routes use **10 mm expansion bolts**. Anchors consist of two bolts linked by a sling, with a fixed screwgate or maillon on one bolt as the lower-off. GCH makes two points:

1. **Do not clip gear to the sling joining the two bolts.** That sling is part of the anchor, not a place for quickdraws or a belay device.
2. When cleaning the anchor, thread the rope through the lower-off ring, not through the sling. For extended top-roping, build a separate anchor with two quickdraws or a locking carabiner.

GCH also states plainly that fixed bolts **cannot be inspected daily**, and that climbers must check hangers and nuts themselves. The December 2025 accident at Communist Wall happened when the hanger nut on the second bolt came loose and the hanger detached after repeated falls and swings. Carry a wrench and deal with loose hardware.

### Accident records and medical care

In July 2026, GCH published investigations and lessons from four accidents. These are not statistics; they are what happened at this crag in the past two years:

| Date | Sector | What happened | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30 Jan 2025 | Elephant | After cleaning the anchor, the climber tied the rope to a gear loop on the harness. The loop failed during the lower. | Fatal; the first climbing fatality in Laos |
| 5 Mar 2025 | Roof | The first quickdraw was back-clipped; the rope unclipped during a fall. | Ground fall, broken ankle, hospital in Nakhon Phanom |
| 27 Dec 2025 | Communist Wall | After repeated falls, the hanger nut on the second bolt loosened and the hanger detached. | Ground fall, no injuries |
| 14 Feb 2026 | Elephant | A climber fell to the ground while cleaning the anchor; the rope was too short, or the wrong end was threaded. The investigation could not establish the sequence with certainty. | Fatal |

The first investigation concluded "personal error": rock, bolts, anchor, rope, harness and belay device had not failed. The rope was attached to a gear loop, which is designed to carry equipment, not a person. In the second accident, the party removed the equipment before investigators could document it; police ultimately accepted the account that the climber had threaded the wrong end of the rope through the anchor.

Both fatalities happened while **cleaning an anchor**. Before you go up, walk through the sequence with your partner: which ring the rope goes through, where the knot is tied, who checks before the lower, and what commands you use. Do not project on your first day after a long flight; fatigue turns familiar routines into slips.

**Medical care:** there is **no ambulance**. In the 2025 incident the injured climber was taken by tuk-tuk and then a pickup truck to Thakhek Provincial Hospital; serious cases usually cross to Nakhon Phanom in Thailand. The border closes at 22:00, so a night injury may not reach Thailand until morning (this is an inference, not an official guarantee).

- Buy travel insurance that explicitly covers **climbing, search and rescue, and medical evacuation**. "Travel insurance" on its own is not enough. Many policies exclude lead climbing and exclude riding a motorbike without a licence.
- Carry a basic first-aid kit, personal medication and cash. The camp is far from pharmacies and hospitals.
- After the 2025 death, police ordered a climbing stop and the investigation was repeatedly delayed; more than 90% of guests left. **Be prepared for a temporary closure.** Do not stake the last days of a holiday on climbing here.

### Gear

GCH recommends a **60–80 m rope, 15 quickdraws, a helmet and a headlamp**. ClimbGrades' 2026 guide is more conservative: **70 m minimum, 80 m better, 14–18 quickdraws**, plus a clip stick. Some routes exceed 30 metres, so a single 60 m rope is not enough for every lower-off.

If you are not carrying gear, GCH rents it:

- Equipment package (70 m rope, two harnesses, 12 quickdraws, belay device, two pairs of shoes, two helmets and more): **US$35 per day, US$235 per week**.
- Individual items: 60 m rope US$7/day, 70 m US$7.50, 80 m US$8.30; six quickdraws US$2.50, 12 US$5; harness and chalk bag US$4.10; climbing shoes US$5.50; helmet US$1.50.

### Rooms, food and payment

Prices from the 2026 site (first figure low season, second main season, November to February):

| Room | Low season | Main season |
|---|---|---|
| Camp 1 / Camp 2 double, private bathroom | US$25 | US$30 |
| Camp 2 double, shared bathroom | US$20 | US$25 |
| Triple, private bathroom | US$30 | US$35 |
| Camp 1 six-bed dorm | US$11 | US$12 |
| Camp 2 four-bed dorm | US$11 | US$12 |
| One-person tent | US$6 | US$6.25 |
| Two-person tent | US$9 | US$10 |

Tents include a mat and bedding, and the shared bathrooms have hot water. Prices and room types change; the booking page is authoritative. **The 2026/27 season is capped at 70 guests** (down from about 120 across both camps in previous seasons). Camp 2's restaurant burned down two years ago and has not been rebuilt, so this season Camp 2 offers rooms, a self-service drinks area, a small bar and a limited food menu; the full restaurant is at Camp 1, about 500 metres away. The site does not publish exact 2026/27 opening and closing dates.

At the Camp 1 restaurant, fried rice is about 42,000–52,000 kip, curry 60,000–65,000 kip, and rice an extra 16,000 kip. At roughly 21,000 kip to the dollar, that is about US$2–3.50 a meal. The camp accepts kip, Thai baht, US dollars and euros in cash, but **foreign notes must be clean and undamaged**. Cards carry a 6.9% fee. Thai PromptPay and Lao OnePay QR payments work, and GCH recommends the Moreta Pay app.

**Signal:** Camp 1 has no mobile signal; walk about 200 metres to the yoga studio and you are back online. Camp 2 has signal but no Wi-Fi. Remote work is possible from Camp 2, but speeds vary through the day. GCH recommends Lao Telecom (LTC) and sells SIM cards at camp.

### Partners and rest days

GCH is the easiest place to find a partner. Ask around the restaurant at breakfast, lunch and dinner; popular walls (Swiss Oldies, Hangover, Partymeile and Tree Lounge in the morning; the Roof and Elephant in the afternoon) often have solo climbers. The [Climbing Lao](https://www.facebook.com/groups/4697101927048694/) Facebook group is useful before you arrive.

GCH runs courses from "Just Climb" to lead and technique, booked at least 24 hours ahead. 2026 prices: one person, half-day Just Climb or Top Rope US$55, full day US$96; one person, full-day lead course US$102; one person, half-day technique US$75. Sharing with more people brings the per-person price down.

Thakhek town has restaurants, a market, massage and travel agencies, plus the 425 km "Thakhek Loop" motorbike circuit, usually done over three or four days. Near camp there are caves, swimming spots, yoga, volleyball and slacklining; the cave behind Camp 1 goes through the mountain to the other side. You do not need to go into town to take a rest day.
