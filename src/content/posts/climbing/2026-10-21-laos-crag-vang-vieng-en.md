Vang Vieng sits about 150 km north of Vientiane and is Laos' second climbing area. theCrag listed 154 routes on 21 October 2026, spread across several karst towers outside town. Unlike Thakhek, there is no "camp plus walk to the crag" base here: the crags are strung along Route 13, and most people stay in town and ride out each day.

Vang Vieng's advantages are convenience, scenery and easy pairing with other outdoor activities. Its drawbacks are distance between crags, bolt warnings that are only partly current, and big differences in sun between the dry and wet seasons. Pha Tang needs a day of its own — see [that guide](/posts/climbing/2026-10-21-laos-crag-pha-tang).

This guide helps you settle: **which wall matches your grade, which bolts you cannot trust, and how many places fit in a day.**

## What kind of crag this is

Vang Vieng's walls are karst limestone, from friction slabs and vertical faces to overhangs and tufas. Routes are mostly 10 to 30 metres, from 4a to 8b.

The main sectors and what they are like:

- **Sleeping Wall:** the closest to town, 29 routes and 402 ascents. It sits by the small flying fox at Interzone resort and is **shaded all day long and stays cool**. It has a Steep Roof and a canyon; the canyon is narrow, and with several groups in it feels cramped.
- **Secret Canyon:** 17 routes and 67 ascents. You walk through Angel Cave into the canyon, and the **entrance costs 40,000 kip**. Lights have been added in the cave and a handrail at the wall base, because visitor numbers have grown so much that climbers end up feeling like exhibits.
- **Pha Daeng:** about 20 routes from 5b to 7a+, 10 to 25 metres, with good rock and views across to Vang Vieng. Some routes get sun all day; a few are permanently shaded.
- **Tham Nam Them:** 20 routes from 5c to 8b, about 7 km north of town. It was once listed as closed because of corroded bolts, and no current official notice was found for 2026.
- **Tuber's Paradise Wall:** just two routes, reached by a scrambly trail past Secret Canyon on the left, following the red marks.

| Sector | Style | Grades | From town | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sleeping Wall | Canyon, vertical, tufa | 4a–7c | Closest, 10–15 min by motorbike | Beginners, half-day trips |
| Steep Roof | Overhang, short routes | 7a–7c | Same cluster as Sleeping Wall | Intermediate climbers avoiding crowds |
| Secret Canyon | Short walls, cave entrance | 4a–7a | Near town | Trying a cave, limited time |
| Pha Daeng | Vertical, good rock | 5b–7a+ | 20–30 min by motorbike | People who want quality routes |
| Tham Nam Them | Vertical and overhang | 5c–8b | 7 km north of town | Advanced, check the bolts first |
| Tuber's Paradise Wall | Small wall | 2 routes | Via Secret Canyon | On the way past |

If you only have a day, pick **Pha Daeng**: better rock than Sleeping Wall, routes concentrated in one place, and four to six routes in a morning. If you want an easy half-day, **Sleeping Wall** is the most convenient, with the understanding that you may share the wall with tour groups.

## When to go

Vang Vieng's dry season runs from October to April, with November to February the most comfortable.

- **Nov–Feb:** 25–30°C by day, cool at night, good for full climbing days. Sleeping Wall and Secret Canyon are shaded all day, so the afternoon still works.
- **Mar–Apr:** above 35°C at midday and parts of Pha Daeng get full sun; dawn and dusk are better.
- **May–Sep:** afternoon storms and seepage; the Sleeping Wall canyon can hold water and the dirt roads turn muddy.

## Getting there

### Public transport

The China–Laos Railway takes about **52 minutes** from Vientiane to Vang Vieng and is the fastest option now. The station is outside town; a tuk-tuk into town takes roughly 10 to 15 minutes. Buses take three to four hours, with fares varying by service.

There is no direct train from Thakhek; you usually go back through Vientiane or take a long-distance bus of 7 to 10 hours. Most people slot Vang Vieng between Vientiane and Luang Prabang.

### Driving

There are no taxis in Vang Vieng; people use tuk-tuks and motorbikes. A motorbike rents for about **100,000–150,000 kip per day**, and shops usually hold your passport as deposit. Bring an international driving licence, since police do random checks. Roads to the crags mix tarmac and dirt and turn muddy in the wet season.

If you are not comfortable on the roads, take a tuk-tuk or use a climbing school's transfer.

### Parking

- **Sleeping Wall:** ride north of town, cross the metal bridge, then take the dirt road on the right marked for Interzone Resort; you can **park right next to the crag**. Google Maps does not show the dirt roads after the bridge, but theCrag's OSM layer does.
- **Secret Canyon:** park near the blue Angel Cave footbridge and walk 5–10 minutes through the cave into the canyon.
- **Pha Daeng:** there is space to leave a motorbike below the wall.

## Rules before you go

**Bolts.** theCrag flags **Snake Skin 7a** at Sleeping Wall with "Old and rusty bolts" and **White Jungle 6b+** with "Rusty bolts"; **Arete 7a** carries a fixed-gear warning. At Secret Canyon, **I Like It Tight 5b** and **The Light at the End of the Tunnel 4** say the chain needs to be substituted. These are not the results of a full 2026 inspection, but they are enough reason to check hangers, nuts and anchors yourself. Tham Nam Them was once closed because of bolt corrosion.

**Access has changed.** theCrag's February 2023 update records that the old trail from Sleeping Wall to Secret Canyon now has a **barbed wire fence across it**, so that route is closed; the Tuber's Paradise approach is also falling into disuse. The main entrance is the blue footbridge.

**Entry fee.** Walking through Angel Cave into Secret Canyon costs **40,000 kip** — under two dollars. theCrag also honestly records a way around the fee, but that is an evasion route and not recommended.

**Permits.** An old SummitPost page says Vang Vieng climbing required a permit from Wildside Eco Group, US$5 per group per day. That information dates from around 2013 and **no current notice was found for 2026**. Ask local climbing schools, your guesthouse or a guide before you go.

**Medical care.** Vang Vieng has a provincial hospital; serious cases usually transfer to Vientiane, about two hours by road. There is no mountain rescue team, so an evacuation depends on your partners and local people.

**Traffic.** Riding is the main risk. Wet-season roads are slippery, there is little night lighting, and riding after drinking is common here.

### Expert: 8a and up (YDS 5.13b and up)

**No named route is listable from the sources.** theCrag's grade table shows one 8a and one 8b at Tham Nam Them, but the publicly readable page does not name them, and no other Vang Vieng sector has an 8. Ask a local guide or climbing school about the current situation when you arrive: names, condition, and whether your rope is long enough.
