Southeast Asian limestone usually brings Thailand's Krabi or Vietnam's Cat Ba to mind. Laos rarely makes the same list, yet karst towers run the length of the country. [Green Climbers Home (GCH)](https://greenclimbershome.com/climbing) puts the national total at more than 600 routes, most of them in Thakhek and Vang Vieng. Routes run from 12 to 40 metres, grades from 4 to 8c, and the style ranges from friction slabs and vertical faces to roofs hung with stalactites.

This series covers one area per guide, eight in total. This overview helps you choose a base — Thakhek, Vang Vieng or Luang Prabang — and covers when to go, how to get there, and which risks to settle before you leave.

## What kind of crag this is

Laos climbing is concentrated in three places, with very different density:

- **Thakhek (Pha Tam Kam, Tha Falang):** theCrag listed 636 routes on 21 October 2026, the largest cluster in the country and the only one with a climbing camp. Routes run 12 to 40 metres, 5a to 8a+, with three or four multipitches.
- **Vang Vieng (Sleeping Wall, Secret Canyon, Pha Daeng, Pha Tang):** theCrag lists 154 routes, scattered outside town. The rock is the same limestone as Thakhek, but the routes are shorter, there are more 4a to 5c beginner lines, and shade matters more.
- **Luang Prabang (Pak Ou, Gecko Wall):** only 19 routes on theCrag, but the setting at the confluence of the Mekong and the Nam Ou is the best of the three.

The third source is the **guidebook**: *Laos Rock Climbing Guidebook*, 2023 edition, published by GCH, about US$12. Alongside Thakhek it covers Sala Hinboun, Vang Vieng, Pha Tang, Xaysomboun, Luang Prabang and Nong Khiaw.

## Picking a crag by region

A Laos climbing trip is not really a "stay in one city, change crag every day" trip. Thakhek's crags surround the camp and are reached on foot. Vang Vieng's crags are scattered outside town and are hard to reach without a motorbike. Luang Prabang has few routes and works better as part of a wider trip than as a climbing destination on its own.

| Base | Main crags | Style and grades | Best for | Downside |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Thakhek](/posts/climbing/2026-10-21-laos-crag-thakhek-base) | [Pha Tam Kam](/posts/climbing/2026-10-21-laos-crag-pha-tam-kam-camp), [Tha Falang](/posts/climbing/2026-10-21-laos-crag-tha-falang) | Limestone sport, 5a–8a+, 12–40 m, plus 3–4 multipitches | Teams that want to walk to the crag every day, from beginner to 8a | No direct flights; long overland transfer; limited medical care |
| [Vang Vieng](/posts/climbing/2026-10-21-laos-crag-vang-vieng) | Sleeping Wall, Secret Canyon, Pha Daeng | Karst limestone, 4a–7c, short to medium routes | People who want a town base, a motorbike and other activities | Crags are spread out; some bolt warnings are old |
| [Pha Tang](/posts/climbing/2026-10-21-laos-crag-pha-tang) | Na Pha Daeng wall | White limestone, 5b–8b, long routes and tufas | A extra day from Vang Vieng for quieter, longer climbing | A 20-plus km ride and an approach through farmland |
| [Luang Prabang](/posts/climbing/2026-10-21-laos-crag-luang-prabang) | Pak Ou / Eagle Wall, Gecko Wall | River limestone, 3–7c+, mostly short to medium | Combining climbing with a cultural trip and a boat ride | Only 19 routes in the whole area |

If you have seven to ten days, **Thakhek** is the most efficient choice: the camp sits below the crags, route density is high, and supplies and partners are easiest to find. If you want to combine climbing with Vang Vieng and Luang Prabang, base yourself in Vang Vieng for two nights and give Pha Tang a full day.

## When to go

The dry season runs roughly from October to May, and the climbing camp operates during that window. GCH calls **November to February** peak season: daytime temperatures of 22–28°C, dry air, and nights that occasionally drop to around 10°C. December and January are driest and have the best friction, and the camp is also fullest.

| Months | Conditions | Planning note |
|---|---|---|
| Oct–Nov | End of the wet season; some walls may still seep; fewer visitors | Good for avoiding crowds, but have a rain plan |
| Dec–Feb | Dry, cool, best friction; camp at its fullest | Book lodging and transport early; bring a warm layer |
| Mar | Getting hot; burning-season air quality can worsen | Climb early, choose shaded walls; sensitive travellers should avoid |
| Apr–May | Hottest months; camp quieter; Pi Mai and Rocket Festival in April | Climb at dawn and dusk, or choose cave walls with shade |

GCH puts the burning season at roughly **early February to the end of March**. Thakhek is usually affected less than northern Thailand, but it varies by year. Mid-April to mid-May is the hottest stretch, when afternoon rock becomes too hot to hold. The camp closes for the monsoon, and rain can also make crags inaccessible.

Luang Prabang and Vang Vieng start their dry season a little earlier (October to April), and the north has a noticeably worse burning-season air quality than the centre of the country.

## Getting there

There are no direct flights from Taipei to Laos. The usual route is Taipei to Bangkok Don Mueang, a domestic flight to **Nakhon Phanom (KOP)**, then across the Third Thai–Lao Friendship Bridge to **Thakhek** — the route GCH recommends. The alternative is to fly to **Vientiane** and continue north or south by bus or train.

- **Thakhek:** several daily flights from Don Mueang to Nakhon Phanom, about 1 hour 40 minutes. The international bus from Nakhon Phanom to Thakhek leaves at 09:00, 11:30, 14:30 and 16:30 and takes about two hours including the border. A tuk-tuk from Thakhek bus station to GCH takes about 20 minutes.
- **Vang Vieng:** about 52 minutes from Vientiane on the China–Laos Railway, or three to four hours by bus. The station is outside town, so allow for a transfer.
- **Luang Prabang:** about two hours from Vientiane by train, or another 50 minutes north from Vang Vieng. Pak Ou is about 30 km upstream from Luang Prabang and is reached by boat or motorbike.

Detailed schedules, fares and visa procedures are in the [Thakhek base guide](/posts/climbing/2026-10-21-laos-crag-thakhek-base). One rule applies everywhere: **do not put the flight and the overland border crossing at the end of the same day.** Bus times change, and visa-on-arrival processing is not guaranteed to fit a tight connection.

## Rules before you go

### Visas and entry

Taiwanese passports can use visa on arrival or an eVisa. Taiwan's Bureau of Consular Affairs lists visa on arrival as **single entry, 30 days, US$40**, with two photos and a passport valid for at least six months. The eVisa costs **US$50**, but Taiwanese passport holders must also supply a photo for a separate "green visa stamp booklet" on arrival.

The awkward part is the **Third Friendship Bridge at Thakhek**. Laos' eVisa site does not list the bridge among its entry points, while the Lao immigration page for that crossing says visa on arrival and eVisa are available. GCH says most nationalities can get visa on arrival there, but explicitly states that Indian passports cannot use the Nakhon Phanom/Thakhek crossing. **For Taiwanese passports, I could not find an official statement or a recent first-hand report of visa on arrival at this bridge.** If you do not want to gamble, fly to Vientiane or obtain a paper visa from a Lao embassy before travelling.

### Route data and bolts

Laos has no single official route database. In practice, climbers use three sources:

- **[theCrag Laos](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/laos):** the largest route count — 636 in Thakhek, 154 in Vang Vieng and 19 in Luang Prabang as of 21 October 2026. Logged-in users can generate a PDF.
- **The *Laos Rock Climbing Guidebook*:** published by GCH, 2023 edition, about US$12, sold on site. No digital edition was found.
- **Mountain Project:** fewer routes (about 473 in Thakhek), but useful for cross-checking individual grades and locations.

The counts differ because each database covers a different scope, not because one is wrong. **A route count is not a safety guarantee.** Thakhek's routes use 10 mm expansion bolts, and anchors are two bolts linked by a sling with a fixed lower-off on one of them. GCH explicitly warns climbers not to clip gear to the sling joining the two bolts, and notes that fixed bolts cannot be inspected daily — climbers must check hangers and nuts themselves. On theCrag flags rusty bolts on Snake Skin and White Jungle at Vang Vieng's Sleeping Wall, and two Secret Canyon routes need their chains replaced.

### Accidents

In January 2025 and February 2026, two fatal accidents occurred at the Elephant sector in Thakhek, both while climbers were cleaning anchors. In March 2025 a climber hit the ground at the Roof after a back-clipped draw unclipped; in December 2025 a hanger nut came loose at Communist Wall. The details and lessons are in the [base guide](/posts/climbing/2026-10-21-laos-crag-thakhek-base), because they affect whether you go, how you choose partners, and what insurance you buy. The lack of an ambulance is the single most important thing to sort out before any Laos climbing trip.

## Where to look things up

### Grade conversions

Laos uses French grades. Conversions follow the [Mountain Project international grade comparison chart](https://www.mountainproject.com/international-climbing-grades) and are approximate; "approx." marks grades the sources give without a/b/c (such as 4 or 5+). The original grade is the one that counts. Every article in this series has a YDS column in its "Routes by level" section.

### Route indexes

- [theCrag: Laos](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/laos), [Thakhek](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/laos/thakhek), [Vang Vieng](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/laos/vang-vieng), [Luang Prabang](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/laos/area/2851606167).
- [Mountain Project: Laos](https://www.mountainproject.com/area/106649458/laos): cross-checks individual routes.
- **Guidebook:** *Laos Rock Climbing Guidebook*, 2023 edition, about US$12, sold on site.

### Finding partners

The GCH restaurant in Thakhek is the easiest place to find a partner, and the [Climbing Lao](https://www.facebook.com/groups/4697101927048694/) Facebook group is useful before you arrive. Vang Vieng and Luang Prabang have no equivalent hub, so arrange partners in advance.

## How to read this series

- For Thakhek: start with [Thakhek and Green Climbers Home](/posts/climbing/2026-10-21-laos-crag-thakhek-base), then the [Pha Tam Kam camp walls](/posts/climbing/2026-10-21-laos-crag-pha-tam-kam-camp) and [valley walls](/posts/climbing/2026-10-21-laos-crag-pha-tam-kam-valley).
- For quiet long routes: [Tha Falang](/posts/climbing/2026-10-21-laos-crag-tha-falang) is a 20–30 minute walk from GCH, with few routes and even fewer climbers.
- For a Vang Vieng base: start with the [Vang Vieng town crags](/posts/climbing/2026-10-21-laos-crag-vang-vieng), then give [Pha Tang](/posts/climbing/2026-10-21-laos-crag-pha-tang) a full day.
- For Luang Prabang: [Pak Ou and Gecko Wall](/posts/climbing/2026-10-21-laos-crag-luang-prabang) need a boat or a motorbike and have few routes, so treat them as part of a wider itinerary.

## Limits of this series

- The author has not visited these crags. The content is assembled from GCH's site, theCrag, UKC, The Crag Journal, ClimbGrades and Mountain Project. Treat first-hand accounts according to their publication year.
- Prices, schedules, opening dates and bolt conditions change. Each guide gives the date it was checked; re-check the official pages before you travel.
- Nong Khiaw, Sala Hinboun and Xaysomboun in northern Laos have topo coverage in the GCH guidebook but very little online information: Nong Khiaw has no theCrag or Mountain Project entry, Sala Hinboun has only a 2023 second-hand note of "28 routes", and Xaysomboun has eight routes and is described as a military zone where climbing is effectively banned. They are not separate guides here, and old notes are not a good reason to go.
