Batu Caves is not one wall but a group of them, and each one charges differently, sits on different land, faces a different direction and carries bolts of a different age. Across these five: White Wall is free, Comic Wall is private land that is only tolerated, Gua Lepak charges tourists RM10, Red Rocks is soft to the point of being polished, and half of Nanyang is government land. This guide compares all five so you can pick the wall for the day, work out what you will pay, and see which approaches have reported rockfall, glass and bees.

**This article covers White Wall, Comic Wall, Red Rocks, Nanyang Wall and Volleyball Site (Gua Lepak) only.** [Nyamuk](/posts/climbing/2026-10-21-malaysia-crag-batu-caves-nyamuk-en) is a free natural wall with no permit, and [Damai](/posts/climbing/2026-10-21-malaysia-crag-batu-caves-damai-en) is a ticketed park crag; both have their own guides, and their rules do not transfer between walls. The series starts with the [overview](/posts/climbing/2026-10-21-malaysia-crags-overview-en). The parent area's subarea table also lists Kura Kura (16 routes), Roadshow (8) and Publicity (3); those pages were not opened, so they stay out of this article.

## What kind of crag

All five walls belong to [the Batu Caves karst complex](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/batu-caves): the parent page records 362 routes across 10 subareas, limestone around 400 million years old, cliffs roughly 150 m high, 13 km north of Kuala Lumpur in Gombak, Selangor (updated 2026-10-18). The same page warns that humidity accelerates corrosion and that many routes are maintained by local volunteers.

The differences between the walls matter more than the numbers:

| Wall | Routes | Grades | Height | Ascents | Aspect and sun | Fee | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| White Wall | 30 | 5c–7c | 25 m | 865 | East; morning sun, shade after 12.30 pm | Free (2023 blog) | theCrag 2026-10-19; full route list with lengths and bolt counts |
| Comic Wall | 12 | 5c–7a | 16 m | 203 | South; shaded all day | not listed | theCrag 2026-10-21; private land, only "tolerated" |
| Red Rocks | 15 | 5c–7b | 16 m | 56 | North; shade most of the day | not listed | theCrag 2026-10-20; 13 sport and 2 trad; some lines need a 60 m rope |
| Nanyang Wall | 47 | 5c–8b | 18 m | 979 | East; shade from around 10 am | not listed | theCrag 2026-10-14; all sport; the left side is government land |
| Volleyball Site / Gua Lepak | 45 | 5c–8b | 19 m | 961 | East; morning sun, better in the afternoon | RM10 tourists, RM5 locals | theCrag 2026-10-21; on the roof of the Gua Lepak food court |

A few things that are easy to misread:

Comic Wall's route count differs between sources — TheCrag lists 12 routes from 5c to 7a (2026-10-21), while [a 2023-12-28 round-up](https://moredaysoff.wordpress.com/2023/12/28/the-best-rock-climbing-in-malaysia-is-in-kuala-lumpur/) says "fifteen routes 5b to 7c". The gap may reflect rebolting and new routes, or simply different coverage.

Red Rocks is very soft and polished, and TheCrag compares it to Pra-nang; that is a different Malaysian crag and outside this series.

The routes at Volleyball Site were cut short: the Gua Lepak food court was built against the wall around 2023, and most original routes are now about half their former length.

The "area is OPEN" notice at Nanyang Wall is dated January 2013 and is no longer a current access statement.

## When to go

Aspect matters more than season for these five.

Comic Wall and Red Rocks are shaded all day (Comic south-facing, Red Rocks north-facing), which is exactly what you want at midday but means you plan your own water and shade. White Wall, Nanyang Wall and Volleyball Site all face east: morning sun, then shade after 12.30 pm at White Wall, after around 10 am at Nanyang, and the afternoon is better at Volleyball Site (theCrag wall pages, 2026-10-14 to 2026-10-21). TheCrag tags White Wall "Wet in rain" as well, which makes it a poor choice in a downpour.

November to March is the northeast monsoon season across peninsular Malaysia ([Malaysia's meteorological department](https://www.met.gov.my/en/pendidikan/fenomena-cuaca)), and Kuala Lumpur has no real dry season: rain tends to arrive in the afternoon, short but heavy ([Crag Journal 2023](https://thecragjournal.com/2023/01/01/climbing-in-malaysia-a-journey-through-south-east-asia/)). None of these five walls has a published closure rule, but the soft limestone at Red Rocks and the constant shade at Comic both suffer after rain, so keep a wet-weather alternative in mind — a managed park like Damai with toilets and lighting is the obvious fallback.

## Getting there

### Public transport

All five walls sit in the same Batu Caves area, so the simplest plan is KTM Komuter to Batu Caves station and then a Grab to the parking spot for each wall ([batucaves-guide](https://batucaves-guide.com/plan-your-visit/how-to-get-there) lists about 35 minutes from KL Sentral to Batu Caves at RM2.60 one way, checked October 2026). There is no bus service between the station and the walls, so the last leg is a ride-hail or a walk.

If you want two walls in one day, order the parking stops: Red Rocks and White Wall both sit on the Damai Extreme Park side, Nanyang is at the end of Jalan SM 3A, and Gua Lepak is on the food court roof. The gate to the roof is sometimes unattended, and with nobody at the gate there is nobody to wait for.

### Driving

Navigate to each wall's own parking point rather than to "Batu Caves" — the lanes in this area are easy to get wrong.

- **White Wall**: about a 5-minute walk from Damai Extreme Park (2023 blog).
- **Red Rocks**: approached from the same Damai Extreme Park side; park by the BMX park and walk about 5 minutes.
- **Nanyang Wall**: park at the end of Jalan SM 3A, cross a small bridge and walk about 50 m, then turn left between the gardens.
- **Comic Wall**: next to a small Indian temple; parking is not documented, so look at the ground on arrival.
- **Volleyball Site**: on the roof of the Gua Lepak food court, reached through the upper level.

### Parking

Parking is separate for each wall: White Wall and Red Rocks borrow the space around Damai, Nanyang uses the street end, and Comic Wall and Gua Lepak have no documented parking. Do not block driveways or the working space of the food court.

## Rules before you go

Comic Wall is the one to read first: it is private land, TheCrag lists the access status as "Tolerated" and says climbers should respect the situation, otherwise the owners "might start cutting off the bolts". It is the only wall here whose land is publicly stated to be private; the other four publish no land status at all. Nanyang's left side is government land with a caretaker on hand (chickens and street dogs on site), while the right side has tufa and the easier routes.

One wall states fees clearly: Volleyball Site charges RM10 for tourists and RM5 for locals. The claim that White Wall and Red Rocks carry no usage fee rests on a December 2023 blog post rather than an operator notice, and no announcement confirms it still holds in 2026.

Bolt condition differs wall by wall, and everything below is a community report rather than an operator notice:

- **Comic Wall**: a report created around mid-August 2026 says the anchors have been replaced but that "almost all the lines have quite rusty bolts on the way up" and should be earmarked for rebolting; a separate comment reports a loose top-anchor nut on Kampai. Many anchors were changed to stainless chain in 2020.
- **Red Rocks**: in around June 2026 someone reported glass shards at the trail start under a large tree, with a reported foot impalement near the fixed ropes; in late August 2026 someone reported a bee colony in the hole at the start of Yen's Route and Nacho Cheese, just below the fixed rope. A loose sixth bolt was reported on Return of the Spotted Owl – Left, and both Owl routes were rebolted on 14 May 2023. Most bolts are aged, some have been rebolted, and the newer routes sit far to the left.
- **White Wall**: Lockdown Madness has a logged loose hold, Kick a Cloud has a loose fifth bolt, The Swinging Gumby has loose rock and runout, and the fourth pitch of White Awake was reported as brittle.

White Wall is the only wall here with a full published route list including lengths and bolt counts; elsewhere, single-route lengths are mostly unpublished. Check [theCrag's Malaysia forum](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/forum) a week before you go, where the newest field reports appear first.

Emergency assistance: call **999**. The nearest hospital is Hospital Selayang, Lebuhraya Selayang–Kepong, 68100 Batu Caves, +603-6126 3333. Ask your insurer specifically about outdoor lead climbing, rescue, medical treatment and repatriation; policy names alone do not establish coverage.

## Routes by level

YDS and V-grade conversions follow the [Mountain Project international grade comparison chart](https://www.mountainproject.com/international-climbing-grades) and are approximate; "approx." marks grades the sources give without a/b/c (such as 4 or 5+). The original grade is the one that counts.

The Grade column keeps TheCrag's original French grades and the YDS column holds the conversion; the Sector column names the wall. Only White Wall has lengths and bolt counts published for every route, so elsewhere the length column reads "not published".

### Beginner: up to 6a (YDS 5.10a and below)

White Wall is the easiest of the five to plan properly, because Aimless has both a length and a bolt count. Comic Wall's Schizophrenic Fool is 7 m and 2 bolts, the shortest route in this selection. Nanyang and Gua Lepak each have a 6a in their iconic block.

| Route | Grade | YDS | Length / pitches | Sector | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Aimless | 5c | 5.9 | 20 m, 8 bolts | White Wall | theCrag 2026-10-19; shares an anchor with Rainbow Connection |
| Schizophrenic Fool | 5c | 5.9 | 7 m, 2 bolts | Comic Wall | theCrag 2026-10-21; the shortest route in this selection |
| Opium War | 6a | 5.10a | 22 m, 12 bolts | White Wall | theCrag 2026-10-19 |
| White Awake | 6a | 5.10a | 55 m, 4 pitches | White Wall | theCrag 2026-10-19; brittle rock reported on pitch 4 |
| Quit Smoking | 6a | 5.10a | not published | Nanyang Wall | theCrag 2026-10-14; iconic block |
| Coves | 6a | 5.10a | not published | Volleyball Site | theCrag 2026-10-21; iconic block |

### Intermediate: 6a+ to 6c+ (YDS 5.10b–5.11c)

The verifiable named lines sit mostly on White Wall. Kick a Cloud is two pitches and 33 m long, so it needs a rope you can actually pull.

### Advanced: 7a to 7c+ (YDS 5.11d–5.13a)

Three walls, one line each. Nacho Cheese is the route TheCrag marks as "newly rebolted circa 2026", and a bee colony has been reported below its fixed rope.

| Route | Grade | YDS | Length / pitches | Sector | Notes |

## Where to look things up

- [TheCrag's Batu Caves parent page](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/batu-caves): the entry point for all ten subareas, the humidity-and-corrosion warning and the note about volunteer-maintained bolts, and the place to compare fees and hardware wall by wall.
- [TheCrag's White Wall](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/batu-caves/area/13950919), [Comic Wall](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/batu-caves/area/13950991), [Red Rocks](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/batu-caves/area/13950895), [Nanyang Wall](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/batu-caves/area/13950943) and [Volleyball Site](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/batu-caves/area/13950847): each wall's full route list, sectors, aspect, fees and recent warnings; the White Wall page also lists the length and bolt count of every route.
- [TheCrag's Damai Wall](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/batu-caves/area/13950871): the ticket, helmet and hardware rules of the neighbouring ticketed park, useful for comparison rather than for mixing the rules.
- [More Days Off, 2023-12-28](https://moredaysoff.wordpress.com/2023/12/28/the-best-rock-climbing-in-malaysia-is-in-kuala-lumpur/): White Wall newly bolted and free, Nanyang in shade after about 10 am, Comic's "fifteen routes 5b to 7c", and Gua Lepak at RM10 per person with recent rebolting; compare its 2023 figures with TheCrag's 2026 version.
- [theCrag's Malaysia forum](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/forum): check it a week before you go, where the newest field reports appear first.
- **Guidebooks:** Grades and route counts here come mainly from online databases; the guidebook is the authority.
  - Malaysia has no current printed national guidebook. [TheCrag's Malaysian publications page](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/publications) states that no guidebook can be found.
  - *Climb Malaysia – The climbing guide* (2007) is out of print; it covered most Batu Caves walls of the time but not later development. [Mountain Project's Batu Caves page](https://www.mountainproject.com/area/106334639/batu-caves) describes the first edition as dated and short of routes, though enough to recognise popular lines, and [a theCrag discussion](https://www.thecrag.com/discussion/545814402/climb-malaysia-guide-book) records it as unavailable in three Kuala Lumpur climbing shops in 2014 and still unavailable in 2023.
  - [The UKC bibliography](https://www.ukclimbing.com/logbook/books/climbing_guide_to_malaysia-722) also lists *Climbing Guide To Malaysia* (ISBN 981-04-9073-8), with no author or publication year, covering Batu Caves, Bukit Takun and Nyamuk; no seller has been found for it either.
  - None of these five walls has a named bolting organisation: TheCrag credits individual developers route by route (Radzi, Danial Idraki, Laurent Lacabanne, Nasarizal, Patrick Andrey, Man Dyno, Akmal Noor, Aril), so tracking who put up a given line means reading the route entry itself.
