Bukit Keplu is a limestone hill in [Kodiang](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/area/12321894648), Kedah, with paddy fields on top, caves inside and 28 sport routes between 5c and 7b+. It is a morning crag: the heat arrives around 3pm, and in rain most of the routes stay dry. This guide helps you judge whether a half day fits, which of the two approaches to take, whether parking will block a farmer, and why the helmet is not optional.

## What kind of crag

[theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/area/12321894648) is unusually specific: limestone, located in Kodiang, Kedah, bolted by CAS, PCD, HACCK and PC, a limestone crag with caves and beautiful paddy field views. 28 sport routes from 5c to 7b+, split into two sectors — **Siputeh** with 9 routes and 21 ascents, **Si Mata Tiga** with 19 routes, 37 ascents and 55 m of wall. The page was last updated on 2026-10-18.

The wall shapes are a combination of **overhung, slab and vertical**, with afternoon sun. Two more lines on the page are worth more than most descriptions: *Dry in the rain*, and most routes are well protected when it rains so you barely get hit. For a limestone crag that is an unusual advantage — you can keep it as a wet-season backup.

The history is good too. The name Kok Plu comes from the Siamese word for "Betel Hill", and the hill holds a string of caves: Gua Kerbau, Gua Gong, Gua Ikan, Gua Gelap, Gua Pasir, Gua Pelamin and Gua Kelawar. Bat guano was collected here from the 1950s to the 1980s, and the old collecting poles are still visible in the walls.

It suits teams who want to get through two or three hours of 5c to 7b+ sport climbing and who can accept that only one route is published by name. Si Mata Tiga at 55 m is the wall that feels like a complete crag in its own right.

## When to go

This is a **morning crag**: the page says it starts to get hot around 3pm, depending on the weather. Combined with the dry-in-the-rain note, the strategy is not to avoid the wet season but to move your clock earlier.

Kedah's wet season follows the northeast monsoon from November to March ([MetMalaysia on weather phenomena](https://www.met.gov.my/en/pendidikan/fenomena-cuaca)). Late December through February is the most reliable overlap of cool mornings and dry rock. Mornings in the wet season are still worth the trip — you just start earlier.

Afternoon thunderstorms change limestone for real: wet rock loses friction, and rainwater loosens rock that was already marginal. "Most routes stay dry" means the rain is blocked by the roof, not that rockfall risk disappears — that is a separate point, below.

## Getting there

### Public transport

The page advises bringing your own transport in as many words: *It is recommended to have your own transport to the crag.* No bus or Grab plan is published for the crag. You can usually get a car around Kodiang, but nothing online supports the schedule or the reliability of the return trip. If you do not want to gamble on that, drive.

### Driving

Navigate to the [Bukit Keplu parking pin](https://maps.app.goo.gl/fASNxHbJ1tUVBxP6A) from the theCrag page, which marks the approach as under 5 minutes. At the junction there are two ways in, and this is the most useful paragraph in the whole guide:

- **Left: a sturdy bridge, then a 20-minute jungle hike to Si Mata Tiga.** Follow the jungle trail and make a steep left ascent through a clearing in the branches. Longer and rougher, but it reaches the 55 m wall with 19 routes.
- **Right: a temporary log bridge, then a 5-minute walk on a clear trail to Siputeh.** Easier on the legs, and the right choice for a few short lines or a session with a deadline.

A "temporary" bridge means exactly that: it is built from timber, so it can be slippery after rain and it may be under repair. Both approaches include mud. Do not wear new shoes.

### Parking

The access notes are clear: **climbing is allowed**; park on the side of an **empty plot of land before the paddy field**, and **do not block the road**; the tracks to the paddy fields are really narrow, so **leave room for local farmers**. In practice: park on the edge of the empty plot, stay off the field bunds, and move the car after unloading so farm machinery can pass.

Where to stay: several homestays are nearby, and the page names two — **Bayu Damai Homestay**, about 500 m from the crag, and **Sena Village Homestay**, about 2 km away. If you want to be on the wall in the first light, take the closer one.

## Rules before you go

**Climbing is allowed, and the community rockfall report deserves to be taken seriously.** The crag page itself carries no rockfall rule; the community record says there is a risk of fatal rockfall and that the belayer must wear a helmet. That is a climbing-community report rather than a published rule from a managing body, but it describes what can hit you, so act on it: **the belayer wears a helmet**, you wear one too, and you look up before committing to roof and cave lines.

**Inspect every fixed point.** In August 2026 someone reported a loose hanger on Habuk Mata at Bukit Keplu in the theCrag Malaysia forum. A forum report is a report, not a survey, but it is enough to know that the hardware here is your job: touch every hanger you intend to clip, and skip anything that moves. Read the forum again a week before you go — the same reminder applies to every crag in this series.

**Do not block the farm road.** The paddy fields are somebody's livelihood and the tracks are machinery width.

**Emergency: call 999**, the Malaysian emergency number. Neither the page nor the sources used here list the distance to the nearest hospital or ambulance. Ask your insurer specifically about outdoor lead climbing, rescue, medical treatment and repatriation.

## Routes by level

YDS and V-grade conversions follow the [Mountain Project international grade comparison chart](https://www.mountainproject.com/international-climbing-grades) and are approximate; "approx." marks grades the sources give without a/b/c (such as 4 or 5+). The original grade is the one that counts.

### Beginner: up to 6a (YDS 5.10a and below)

The sources list nothing. The page's range starts at 5c, but no route at 5c, 5c+ or 6a is published by name. If you want the easiest line, you read the wall yourself.

### Intermediate: 6a+ to 6c+ (YDS 5.10b–5.11c)

The sources list nothing. This is the thickest band within the 28 routes, and again only the range is published, with no route names.

### Advanced: 7a to 7c+ (YDS 5.11d–5.13a)

One named route is published on the page, and it is also the only iconic route listed. Komplikasi is 7b+, ★★★.

## Where to look things up

- [theCrag: Bukit Keplu](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/area/12321894648): sector and route index, ascent counts, approach and parking notes; page updated 2026-10-18.
- [Bukit Keplu parking pin](https://maps.app.goo.gl/fASNxHbJ1tUVBxP6A): the navigation link the theCrag page provides, with an approach marked under 5 minutes.
- [theCrag Malaysia forum](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/forum): recent hardware and access reports, including the August 2026 report of a loose hanger on Habuk Mata.
- [Mountain Project](https://www.mountainproject.com): the other crowdsourced database, with less Malaysian coverage than theCrag; useful to cross-check grades.
- **Guidebooks:** Grades and route counts here come mainly from online databases; the guidebook is the authority.
  - [*Climb Malaysia – The climbing guide*](https://climbingaway.fr/en/travel/climbing-guide-books/climb-malaysia-the-climbing-guide), published 2007, **out of print**; it covered most crags of the time and none of the later development. Climbing shops in Kuala Lumpur could not source a copy as early as 2014, and it was still unavailable in 2023 ([theCrag discussion](https://www.thecrag.com/discussion/545814402/climb-malaysia-guide-book)).
  - [*Climbing Guide To Malaysia*](https://www.ukclimbing.com/logbook/books/climbing_guide_to_malaysia-722), ISBN 981-04-9073-8, author and publication year not listed, covering [Batu Caves](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/batu-caves), [Bukit Takun](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/bukit-takun-crags) and [Nyamuk](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/batu-caves/area/13951039). A search on the ISBN returns only this bibliography entry, and whether it is the same book as the one above remains unestablished.
  - Malaysia has no current printed nationwide guidebook. [theCrag's Malaysian publications page](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/publications) states that no guidebooks exist. The practical move is to generate a PDF of your sectors on theCrag and keep it on your phone.
  - [*Rock Climbing in Thailand and Laos*](https://pizbube.ch/en/shop/rock-climbing-in-thailand-and-laos), 9th edition (2018) plus the 2025 update, has a chapter of sidetrips to Perlis, Malaysia. It is not a source for this crag, but it is the last printed line of information for the region.
