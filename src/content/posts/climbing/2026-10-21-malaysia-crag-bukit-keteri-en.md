Bukit Keteri, in [Perlis](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/area/422129439), is the easiest Malaysian crag to organise: the commuter rail station is a few hundred metres from the wall, 77 sport routes run from 4a to 8c+, and entry needs neither a permit nor a ticket. The same place concentrates the risk too — most of the bolts are the batch air-freighted from Europe in 2007, and neither their material nor their replacement history has been published; the South sector's Kasih Wall is restricted after an ancient mural was discovered.

This guide helps you size your grade list, work out which sector gets sun when, reach the crag without a car, and see which sector is currently closed to you. The other Perlis crag to weigh against it is [Gua Kelam](/posts/climbing/2026-10-21-malaysia-crag-gua-kelam); the season and transport comparison across the country is in the [series overview](/posts/climbing/2026-10-21-malaysia-crags-overview).

## What kind of crag

**Karst limestone hills, standing in the rice paddies.** The [Mountain Project page for Bukit Keteri](https://www.mountainproject.com/area/106862498/bukit-keteri) describes the place as "karst limestone towers in rice paddies". [The theCrag crag page](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/area/415519470) (updated 2026-10-17) gives the terrain as two adjacent freestanding outcrops, and the style as pure sport climbing with no trad listings.

**The two databases disagree about which hill is developed.** This changes which sector you stand in and which face gets sun, so both readings are set out here:

| Source | What it says |
|---|---|
| [theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/area/415519470) (updated 2026-10-17) | "two adjacent freestanding outcrops of which only the northern hill has been developed, and this only partly on its West and North face" — only the northern hill is developed, and only partly, on its west and north faces |
| [Mountain Project](https://www.mountainproject.com/area/106862498/bukit-keteri) (page content shared 2010-08-18) | "There are two mountains, with most of the climbing on the west face of the southern hill. The northern hill looks to have a lot of potential for multipitch routes, but as of now there has been no development." — most climbing is on the west face of the southern hill; the northern hill is undeveloped |

The two pages are years apart and neither cites the other. Practically, do not pick a sector or a sun angle from the hill name: look at the wall when you arrive, and ask the local Perlis Climbers.

**Scale.**

| Sector | Routes | Grades | Ascents | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bukit Keteri (total) | 77 | 4a–8c+ | 1,323 | theCrag 2026-10-17 |
| West | 56 | 4a–8c+ | 1,244 | theCrag 2026-10-19; four sub-sectors — Belly Button, Raja, Mata, Gulag |
| South (Kasih Wall) | 12 | 5b+–7b | 47 | theCrag 2026-10-21; access restriction |
| Robot Wall | 9 | 5c–8a+ | 33 | theCrag 2026-10-18 |

[Mountain Project](https://www.mountainproject.com/area/106862498/bukit-keteri) lists 50 sport routes on the hill. The gap between 77 and 50 is coverage and date, not the crag growing or shrinking; new lines keep arriving — the trip notes record King of England 7b in 2025-07 and the multipitch Terima Kasih 7a in 2025-09 (South sector).

**The 2007 bolting expedition.** [More Days Off's 2023-12-28 write-up](https://moredaysoff.wordpress.com/2023/12/28/the-best-rock-climbing-in-malaysia-is-in-kuala-lumpur/) records the 2007 Mammut Pro Team trip: David Lama, Reini Scherer, Cédric Lachat, Christina Schmid, Anna Stöhr, Katharina Saurwein and Juliane Wurm reached Perak and Perlis, set 91 routes between Bukit Keteri and Lost World of Tambun in Ipoh, and air-freighted 1,000 bolts over from Europe ([8a.nu's 2008 report](https://www.8a.nu/articles/teamtrip-mammut-in-the-name-of-his-majesty-the-sultan-of-perlis)). More Days Off puts Keteri itself at 50 routes from 5c to 8c and mentions a large tufa cave. theCrag records that the first climbers here were the Crown Prince of Perlis and his wife.

**Who it suits.** A 2023 climber recommended being able to climb 6b before coming here (trip notes). The Mountain Project comment notes that the pockets are enormous, "blur the line between a jug and a small cave" — an advantage for anyone who favours big holds, and a warning for technical lines that demand power.

## When to go

**January to April.** [The theCrag Perlis page](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/area/422129439) states outright that "the best time to climb here is between January and April". The reason is where the rain falls: [MetMalaysia](https://www.met.gov.my/en/pendidikan/fenomena-cuaca) puts the northeast monsoon between November and March, with the heavy rain on the peninsula's east coast, western Sarawak and eastern Sabah. Perlis sits on the west coast, outside that core. Perlis has no published monthly rainfall figures of its own, so January–April is community experience rather than weather statistics.

**Time of day.** A Mountain Project comment (Nate Ball, 2019-03-15, quoting Justin Polgar) suggests mornings on Raja, Belly Button, Mata and Gulag, and afternoons into evening on Robot Wall for wind and shade. The theCrag Perlis page and Mountain Project also record shade on the west faces before 2 pm.

**Monkeys.** The same comment says monkeys knock dirt and small rocks over the edges. Belayers wear a helmet, and packs and bottles stay out of the fall line below the wall.

## Getting there

### Public transport

[KTM Komuter](https://www.ktmb.com.my/assets/pdf/2026/Jadual-Komuter-Utara_Update%2026022026.pdf)'s northern line (Butterworth–Padang Besar) has a stop called **Bukit Ketri** ([name and location](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukit_Ketri_railway_station)), between Arau and Padang Besar. On the timetable in force from 2026-07-24 there are a dozen-plus trains a day: the first from Butterworth leaves at 05:20 and reaches Bukit Ketri at 06:59, the last arrives around 23:19 ([timetable and fares](https://www.train36.com/penang-padang-besar-komuter-train.html)). Fares and services change, so re-check the KTMB timetable before you travel.

**The station name has a catch.** [The theCrag crag page](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/area/415519470) calls the train station "Tasik Melati" and "a stone throw away" from the crag, with an approach distance of 376 m; the timetable has no Tasik Melati. The two are most likely old and new names for one station, but nothing published states that outright. Navigate to Bukit Ketri as the timetable names it, then ask locally.

The walk from the station is spelled out in the Mountain Project comment (2019-03-15), about 15 minutes: right out of the station, left down the road past the mosque, across to the parking lot, then the left side of the buildings to a grass and dirt road; left for Raja, Belly Button and Robot Wall, right for Gulag. A 2023 video also shows the crag being walked from the station ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PAExKArHBBU)).

**Coming from Kuala Lumpur.** ETS trains run KL Sentral to Arau in about 4.5 to 5.3 hours, six a day, roughly RM73 to RM108 ([timetable and fares](https://www.train36.com/ets-kl-sentral-to-arau.html)); you need a second vehicle from Arau. The nearest airport to the Perlis crags is Alor Setar (AOR) in Kedah, about 35 km from Kangar.

### Driving

[theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/area/415519470) gives the car route: KL → North-South Highway → Changlun → Kangar → Road 7 → Beseri T-junction → Mata Ayer.

The ride-hail drop-off can be the coordinate from the Mountain Project comment (2019-03-15), **6.5198200, 100.2603725**: a large roof structure and parking lot, sometimes a market, opposite Masjid Kampung Bukit Keteri on road R122.

### Parking

Entry is free — the Mountain Project comment (2019-03-15) records "No access fees". Cars park under the large roof structure; the same comment notes a market there every Tuesday evening, when parking gets harder.

## Rules before you go

**The South sector and Kasih Wall are restricted.** [The theCrag South page](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/area/5535864117) (updated 2026-10-21) states in its access notes: "Section has access restriction due to the discovery of ancient mural. Do contact local climbers for access permission." Because of the mural discovery this sector is restricted and you need permission from local climbers first. Haz Chu Rainchecked? and Terima Kasih both sit in it, so without permission you give up the whole South sector.

**Forest permits: not on the list, but the status is changing.** The Perlis forest-permit list (August 2026 version) names seven sites that need a permit and a forest guide: Gunung Perlis, Bukit Pelarit, Pintu Wang Gunung, Bukit Wang Mu, Bukit Jernih, Bukit Chabang and Bukit Manek. **Bukit Keteri is not among them** ([permit list](https://tahanoutdoor.com/blogs/camping/perlis-hiking-permits-list)), and the August 2026 [Bukit Keteri hikers guide](https://tahanoutdoor.com/blogs/camping/bukit-keteri-hikers-guide) lists no permit requirement either. The theCrag Perlis page and Mountain Project both treat the area as public land, with parking at the gate and no fee.

That is not the same as "do not ask". [A Perlis Geopark post from April 2025](https://www.facebook.com/perlisgeopark) says Bukit Keteri was recently gazetted a forest reserve and a geopark geological heritage site; no climbing regulation has been published either way, and permit lists get updated. **Ask Perlis Climbers on WhatsApp before you travel**: [Perlis Climbers](https://www.facebook.com/perlisclimbers), perlisclimb@gmail.com, +60 12-946 6569 (Amirul).

**Bolts. This is the part of the crag you have to take responsibility for yourself.**

- **Most are the 2007 batch.** A 2023 video says roughly nine tenths of the bolts are the 2007 air-freighted batch, that spacing is wide in places and the first bolt sits high, and that 20 draws are enough ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PAExKArHBBU)). The **material of that batch, and whether it has been replaced, is still not published anywhere.** The same batch at Tambun in Ipoh is recorded by theCrag as rusty and unmaintained; Bukit Keteri has a local group maintaining it, and the two crags cannot be used to infer each other.
- **It is maintained, and it needs help.** The 2023 video credits a local Perlis Climb Development with maintaining the crag; [More Days Off (2023-12-28)](https://moredaysoff.wordpress.com/2023/12/28/the-best-rock-climbing-in-malaysia-is-in-kuala-lumpur/) meanwhile says it "does need maintenance", and calls on climbers to help with cleaning and sling replacement instead of only consuming routes.
- **Community reports from 2026.** In the theCrag discussion threads (community reports, not maintenance records): in 2026-02 someone reported that a hanger on a Kasih Wall multipitch route was not put in by the local group; on 2026-06-19 the first two pitches of Haz Chu Rainchecked? were rehung with Singing Rock 316 stainless hangers, the third pitch the same, and the next day, 2026-06-20, a fresh "loose hanger" report appeared ([route page](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/route/12066386115), [thread](https://www.thecrag.com/discussion/13201723950/potentially-dangerous-boltsplates)); in 2026-10 the route Disturbing picked up both a "vegetation blocking bolts" and a "loose rock" report.

The practice is simple: **look at every fixed point you intend to use before you clip it**, and bring more draws than you think you need where spacing is wide.

**Rappel anchors.** A Mountain Project comment (Nate Ball, 2019-03-15, quoting Justin Polgar) reads: "All anchors have 1 or 2 stainless steel ovals, or carabiners. The 'Perlis Climbers' lower off of these, but they are not replaced often, so I would suggest rappelling off the anchor if you are comfortable with it." The anchors here are one or two stainless steel ovals or carabiners; Perlis Climbers lower from them and do not replace them often. If you are comfortable with it, rappel straight from the anchor rather than assuming a newer setup exists.

**Other.** The Mountain Project page also carries a note on dress customs in Perlis; dress as locals do near the villages.

**In an emergency call 999** ([Malaysian government](https://www.malaysia.gov.my/en/categories/safety-and-community/public-safety/mers-999-emergency-line)). Save the crag name and coordinates offline before you set out. Ask your insurer specifically about outdoor lead climbing, rescue, medical treatment and repatriation; policy names alone do not establish coverage.

## Routes by level

YDS and V-grade conversions follow the [Mountain Project international grade comparison chart](https://www.mountainproject.com/international-climbing-grades) and are approximate; "approx." marks grades the sources give without a/b/c (such as 4 or 5+). The original grade is the one that counts.

The grade column holds the original French values from theCrag, and the YDS column the conversion. Routes without a published length read "not published". West and Robot Wall sub-sectors come from the theCrag sector pages; South has a single sub-sector, Kasih Wall.

### Beginner: up to 6a (YDS 5.10a and below)

A small number of named low-grade lines are publicly listed. Permatang K3 is in the South sector, reached by taking the right side of the Gulag area to a green fixed line on the left ledge.

| Route | Grade | YDS | Length / pitches | Sector | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Permatang K3 | 5c+ | approx. 5.9+ | not published | South (Kasih Wall) | theCrag South page photo caption; page updated 2026-10-21 |
| Orang putih mai belah | 5c | 5.9 | 32 m | Robot Wall | theCrag Robot Wall topo; page updated 2026-10-18 |

### Intermediate: 6a+ to 6c+ (YDS 5.10b–5.11c)

Susu and Dust in the Wind are the West sector's iconic lines; View, Back Bone and Ayaq Timbui sit at the same band on Robot Wall, and Kari Hijau Australia is the South sector's option. Haz Chu Rainchecked? is the South sector multipitch, and its hangers drew two successive warnings in June 2026.

| Haz Chu Rainchecked? | hardest 6b+/6c | 5.10d | 70 m, 3 pitches (per-pitch grades not published) | South (Kasih Wall) | theCrag photo caption; the source writes 6b+/c, which converts to the 5.10d–5.11a/b range — the first value is used here; hangers replaced 2026-06-19, loose-hanger report 2026-06-20 |

### Advanced: 7a to 7c+ (YDS 5.11d–5.13a)

Belly Button on West and Robot Wall are where this band is dense. Zamboney and Scherer Blaerer are the lines theCrag marks as iconic at the harder end; Reini Robot and Shaved Beaver sit on Robot Wall.

### Expert: 8a and up (YDS 5.13b and up)

The 8a-and-up lines sit on Robot Wall and West. Langkasuka is West's iconic 8a; the three 8a-and-up lines on Robot Wall run 28 to 30 m.

- [Mountain Project: Bukit Keteri](https://www.mountainproject.com/area/106862498/bukit-keteri): the geology description, the differing accounts of the two hills, and the very practical 2019-03-15 comment covering anchors, monkeys, the walk from the station and the Grab drop-off.
- [More Days Off, 2023-12-28](https://moredaysoff.wordpress.com/2023/12/28/the-best-rock-climbing-in-malaysia-is-in-kuala-lumpur/): the 2007 Mammut expedition and sector route counts; its 2023 numbers need checking against theCrag's 2026 versions.
- [Perlis Climbers](https://www.facebook.com/perlisclimbers): the latest routes, bolt condition and access in Perlis. theCrag says a lot of Perlis information is not online and to contact them — perlisclimb@gmail.com, WhatsApp +60 12-946 6569 (Amirul).
- [KTMB's KTM Komuter timetable](https://www.ktmb.com.my/assets/pdf/2026/Jadual-Komuter-Utara_Update%2026022026.pdf): northern line services.
- **Guidebooks:** Grades and route counts here come mainly from online databases; the guidebook is the authority.
  - Malaysia has no current printed national guidebook. [The theCrag Malaysia guidebook page](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/publications) states that no guidebook can be found.
  - [*Climb Malaysia – The climbing guide*](https://www.climbingaway.fr/en/travel/climbing-guide-books/climb-malaysia-the-climbing-guide), published 2007 and out of print: a theCrag discussion records that by 2014 it could not be bought in three Kuala Lumpur climbing shops, and that in 2023 climbers were still searching for it and only borrowed a local copy at Damai.
  - [*Climbing Guide To Malaysia*](https://www.ukclimbing.com/logbook/books/climbing_guide_to_malaysia-722), ISBN 981-04-9073-8, author and year unlisted; the review notes that some fixed points on listed routes are gone or covered by vegetation. Searching the ISBN turns up no other record, so it cannot be confirmed whether this is the same book as the one above.
  - The current [*Rock Climbing in Thailand and Laos*](https://pizbube.ch/en/shop/rock-climbing-in-thailand-and-laos) (Piz Buch, 9th edition 2018 plus a 2025 update) has a chapter, "Sidetrips to Perlis, Malaysia", covering the Perlis crags.
