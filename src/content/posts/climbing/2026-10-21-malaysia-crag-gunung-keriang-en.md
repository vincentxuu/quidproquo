If you want to fit half a day of climbing into Kedah, Gunung Keriang is the easiest option: the wall sits inside a park, entry is free, and the approach is a walk from the car. It is also the thinnest one: a single named route is published online, and the page carries neither a rock type nor approach notes. This guide helps you judge whether it is worth a half day, what transport to arrange, and why you cannot pick routes from the online list alone.

## What kind of crag

The main source is [theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/area/3776038536)'s [Gunung Keriang Recreational Park](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/area/3776038536) page: 37 sport routes across two sectors. **Graveyard Section** has 17 routes on a 14 m wall with 5 grade steps and 107 ascents; **HACCK Section** has 20 routes on a 23 m wall with 2 grade steps and 127 ascents. The page was last updated on 2026-10-19.

On rock type the honest answer is short: **the page lists no geology**. Other crags in the same state are mostly limestone, but borrowing a neighbour's rock type is a different claim from what this page states. Look at the actual rock in front of you before choosing lines.

In style, the 37 routes concentrate in the middle bands. The published list has nothing at 6a or below, so there is no easy option to ease into. The split between sectors is blunt: Graveyard Section carries 5 grade steps, HACCK Section carries 2 grade steps across 20 routes.

Route names are the biggest limit. The page publishes exactly one named route, **Mansayno** (6c, ★★★). So "37 routes" is a number, not a route list you can carry to the wall.

It suits teams who can already read rock, want a half day of mid-grade practice, and are willing to bring their own topo. If your goal is a long menu of options, this is not the place.

In 2026 a Singapore gym ran a 2.5-day trip that linked Gunung Keriang with [Bukit Keteri](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/area/415519470) in [Glass City](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/area/422129439) and Lubuk Pedati in Baling ([Instagram post](https://www.instagram.com/p/DOOB2LMEucV)). That is the closest reference for how to fit the crag into an itinerary.

## When to go

The main rainy season on the peninsula is the northeast monsoon from November to March, with the heaviest rain on the east coast. Kedah sits on the west coast and is usually not the worst-affected side, but afternoon thunderstorms and short, intense downpours change both friction and rockfall risk on limestone ([MetMalaysia on weather phenomena](https://www.met.gov.my/en/pendidikan/fenomena-cuaca)).

The page records no aspect for this crag and publishes no season table. So "which side gets shade and when" and "does friction survive rain" are questions your partners answer from experience, not from this source. Do not read "37 routes" as a fixed quality of rock in fixed weather.

Practically: treat it as a morning or late-afternoon half day, push the timing earlier during the November-to-March wet season, and look at the rock before you open the first route.

## Getting there

### Public transport

No public transport option is published for this crag, and the page lists no approach notes. In practice you bring your own vehicle, driving in from [Kodiang](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/area/3776038536) or Jitra to the recreation park in Kedah.

theCrag's own Glass City page says that a lot of the information is not online, and Kedah has even less of it. Have a backup before you commit to a schedule: drive yourself, or confirm in advance that you can still get a car into the town you land in.

### Driving

Navigate to **Gunung Keriang Recreational Park** by its name on Google Maps, then follow the park signs to the wall. The page offers no approach description or parking marker, so how the route runs inside the park and where to leave the car are things you work out on the ground. Do not carry a neighbouring crag's approach over to this one.

### Parking

The crag itself is inside the park and free to climb on, as the theCrag summary states in plain terms. Whether the park charges at its gate, and where parking sits, is not listed on the page; follow the signs on site. The park paths are for visitors, so keep rope bags and gear out of the walking route.

Where to stay: the crag publishes no accommodation. Kodiang and Jitra are the towns you drive in through, so book the night before rather than after you reach the park.

## Rules before you go

**Free and no permit, but also the least information.** This is a public recreational park and the theCrag summary states free access for the public; there is no permit or fee entry.

**Check the hardware yourself.** The page lists no bolt condition and no maintenance record. Inspect every fixed point and hanger you plan to use, and skip anything that moves.

**Local contacts.** No local contact is published for this crag. The nearby group for the Glass City crags is [Perlis Climbers](https://www.facebook.com/perlisclimbers), perlisclimb@gmail.com, WhatsApp +60 12-946 6569. One sector here is named after HACCK, the Kedah climbing group credited as a bolting party on the neighbouring Bukit Keplu page.

**Emergency: call 999**, the Malaysian emergency number. The theCrag page and the sources used here give no distance to an ambulance or hospital in the Kedah interior. Ask your insurer specifically about outdoor lead climbing, rescue, medical treatment and repatriation; policy names alone do not establish coverage.

## Routes by level

YDS and V-grade conversions follow the [Mountain Project international grade comparison chart](https://www.mountainproject.com/international-climbing-grades) and are approximate; "approx." marks grades the sources give without a/b/c (such as 4 or 5+). The original grade is the one that counts.

### Beginner: up to 6a (YDS 5.10a and below)

The published list has nothing at 6a or below. Your first route here is one you read off the wall yourself; there is no published first line to follow.

### Intermediate: 6a+ to 6c+ (YDS 5.10b–5.11c)

This is where the 37 routes concentrate, and the only band with a published name. Mansayno is the single named route on the page (★★★), with no listed length or sector.

| Route | Grade | YDS | Length / pitches | Sector | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mansayno | 6c | 5.11a/b | — | — | theCrag named route ★★★; no length or sector listed (page updated 2026-10-19) |

### Advanced: 7a to 7c+ (YDS 5.11d–5.13a)

The sources list nothing. The 20 routes in HACCK Section span 2 grade steps and the 17 in Graveyard Section span 5, and no route in the 7s is published by name.

### Expert: 8a and up (YDS 5.13b and up)

The sources list nothing. This band is completely blank in the published data.

## Where to look things up

- [theCrag: Gunung Keriang](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/area/3776038536): route and sector index, ascent counts, wall heights; page updated 2026-10-19. With a login you can generate a custom PDF to take offline.
- [theCrag Malaysia forum](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/forum): recent reports on rockfall, loose bolts and access elsewhere in the country; read it again a week before you go.
- [Perlis Climbers](https://www.facebook.com/perlisclimbers): the local group for the nearby Glass City crags, perlisclimb@gmail.com, WhatsApp +60 12-946 6569.
- [Mountain Project](https://www.mountainproject.com): the other crowdsourced database, with less Malaysian coverage than theCrag; useful to cross-check grades.
- **Guidebooks:** Grades and route counts here come mainly from online databases; the guidebook is the authority.
  - [*Climb Malaysia – The climbing guide*](https://climbingaway.fr/en/travel/climbing-guide-books/climb-malaysia-the-climbing-guide), published 2007, **out of print**; it covered most crags of the time and none of the later development. Climbing shops in Kuala Lumpur could not source a copy as early as 2014, and it was still unavailable in 2023 ([theCrag discussion](https://www.thecrag.com/discussion/545814402/climb-malaysia-guide-book)).
  - [*Climbing Guide To Malaysia*](https://www.ukclimbing.com/logbook/books/climbing_guide_to_malaysia-722), ISBN 981-04-9073-8, author and publication year not listed, covering [Batu Caves](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/batu-caves), [Bukit Takun](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/bukit-takun-crags) and [Nyamuk](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/batu-caves/area/13951039). A search on the ISBN returns only this bibliography entry, and whether it is the same book as the one above remains unestablished.
  - Malaysia has no current printed nationwide guidebook. [theCrag's Malaysian publications page](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/publications) states that no guidebooks exist. The practical move is to generate a PDF of your sectors on theCrag and keep it on your phone.
  - [*Rock Climbing in Thailand and Laos*](https://pizbube.ch/en/shop/rock-climbing-in-thailand-and-laos), 9th edition (2018) plus the 2025 update, has a chapter of sidetrips to Perlis, Malaysia. It is not a source for this crag, but it is the last printed line of information for the region.
