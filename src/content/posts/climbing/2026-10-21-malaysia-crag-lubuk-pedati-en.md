Lubuk Pedati sits on the long Gunung Baling range in [Baling](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/area/7739183217), Kedah: 35 sport routes across four sectors named after animals, with 20 of them in the busiest sector. The price is the road. It needs your own vehicle, the last stretch is narrow mud, and the plants on both sides will scratch the car. This guide helps you judge whether the remoteness is worth a dedicated trip, how to get the final stretch right, and what the online record still leaves out.

## What kind of crag

The [theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/area/7739183217) page is short: *Lubuk Pedati is located in Baling, Kedah. Crag bolted is part of the long Gunung Baling mountain range.* Then 35 sport routes across four sectors, each named after an animal:

| Sector | Routes | Ascents | Wall | Grade steps |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hornet Section | 20 | 127 | 20 m | 9 |
| Otter Section | 5 | 98 | 19 m | not listed |
| Goat Section | 7 | 29 | 20 m | not listed |
| Bat Section | 3 | 24 | 11 m | not listed |

**Hornet Section is the body of the crag**: 20 routes across 9 grade steps, the widest spread on site. Otter's 5 routes are the most popular by ascent count (98), and Bat is the smallest sector with 3 routes on an 11 m wall. The page was last updated on 2026-10-19.

The page carries no description for this crag: rock type, wall shape and aspect are all blank. Do not carry a neighbouring crag's geology over to it. The 9 grade steps in Hornet mean there is a real ladder of difficulty on the wall, but what the rock is like you have to see for yourself.

Route names are better here than at most Malaysian crags: all five named routes are marked with three stars. It suits teams who can already read rock and are willing to spend half a day of driving for a remote wall. In 2026 a Singapore gym ran a 2.5-day trip that linked Lubuk Pedati with Gunung Keriang and [Bukit Keteri](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/area/415519470) in [Glass City](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/area/422129439) ([Instagram post](https://www.instagram.com/p/DOOB2LMEucV)), which is a ready-made itinerary reference.

## When to go

Baling is in the Kedah interior and takes the northeast monsoon rains from November to March directly. The page publishes no season table for this crag, so the seasonal judgement has to come from when the rain falls and how much of the day is still climbable: **climb in the morning, leave in the afternoon**. That is the general interior-crag answer, not a special condition of this one.

In the wet season the variable is the road, not the rock: the final stretch is mud, and whether you can drive in after rain has to be re-judged each time. Leave slack in the plan for getting the car out again.

## Getting there

### Public transport

The access note says it plainly: go by your own transport, because **public transport in this area is scarce**. There is no published service between Baling town and the crag, and no online basis for whether a Grab can be summoned out on the back roads. The practical answer is to drive.

### Driving

The approach on the page reads like directions written for a signpost, and it is worth following literally:

1. Search for **Gua Jepun**.
2. From Gua Jepun, go straight down the road and turn into the muddy road when you see a mechanic's shop called **Jin Seng Construction** on the left.
3. Turn left and keep going straight; a bridge appears on your left.
4. Ahead there is a **three-way split**: the left path goes onto the bridge, the right path leads into the houses, and **the small middle path is the one you want**.
5. Take the middle path all the way to the end; the **Lubuk Pedati river** runs along on your left.

The last warning is on the page too: **plant growth on both sides of the road may scratch your car**. The distance is short, but branches will mark the bodywork and slow you down. Decide how deep you are driving in before you start — this is a crag that only works if you have your own wheels.

### Parking

The page lists no parking spot. The track serves the houses along it, so leave room to pass and do not block the road.

Where to stay: the page says **any homestay nearby Lubuk Pedati should suffice**. Baling is a small town and accommodation is not the bottleneck; the vehicle is.

## Rules before you go

**Openly accessible, with no written rules at all.** The page lists no fee, no permit and no crag regulations. "Nothing written" is not the same as "nothing to watch": routes maintained by local volunteers and the habits of the surrounding village still apply, and you still have to judge them.

**Hardware information is blank in the same way.** There is no maintenance record and no bolt condition on the page. Inspect every fixed point, and skip any hanger that moves.

**Prepare the car.** Narrow mud plus plants that scratch: wash the car first, think about clearance under the body, and keep a cloth in the cabin. Save the driving route as an offline map before you set off — road names out here do not always match what a map shows.

**Emergency: call 999**, the Malaysian emergency number. Neither the page nor the sources used here give the distance to a hospital or ambulance in the Baling interior. Ask your insurer specifically about outdoor lead climbing, rescue, medical treatment and repatriation; policy names alone do not establish coverage.

## Routes by level

YDS and V-grade conversions follow the [Mountain Project international grade comparison chart](https://www.mountainproject.com/international-climbing-grades) and are approximate; "approx." marks grades the sources give without a/b/c (such as 4 or 5+). The original grade is the one that counts.

### Beginner: up to 6a (YDS 5.10a and below)

The sources list nothing. None of the five named routes is at 6a or below, and the page publishes no names below that.

### Intermediate: 6a+ to 6c+ (YDS 5.10b–5.11c)

Four named routes fall in this band, which is where the page publishes most of its names.

## Where to look things up

- [theCrag: Lubuk Pedati](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/area/7739183217): sector and route index, ascent counts, the full approach description; page updated 2026-10-19.
- [theCrag Malaysia forum](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/forum): recent hardware and access reports from other crags; read it again a week before you go.
- [Mountain Project](https://www.mountainproject.com): the other crowdsourced database, with less Malaysian coverage than theCrag; useful to cross-check grades.
- **Guidebooks:** Grades and route counts here come mainly from online databases; the guidebook is the authority.
  - [*Climb Malaysia – The climbing guide*](https://climbingaway.fr/en/travel/climbing-guide-books/climb-malaysia-the-climbing-guide), published 2007, **out of print**; it covered most crags of the time and none of the later development. Climbing shops in Kuala Lumpur could not source a copy as early as 2014, and it was still unavailable in 2023 ([theCrag discussion](https://www.thecrag.com/discussion/545814402/climb-malaysia-guide-book)).
  - [*Climbing Guide To Malaysia*](https://www.ukclimbing.com/logbook/books/climbing_guide_to_malaysia-722), ISBN 981-04-9073-8, author and publication year not listed, covering [Batu Caves](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/batu-caves), [Bukit Takun](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/bukit-takun-crags) and [Nyamuk](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/batu-caves/area/13951039). A search on the ISBN returns only this bibliography entry, and whether it is the same book as the one above remains unestablished.
  - Malaysia has no current printed nationwide guidebook. [theCrag's Malaysian publications page](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/publications) states that no guidebooks exist. The practical move is to generate a PDF of your sectors on theCrag and keep it on your phone.
  - [*Rock Climbing in Thailand and Laos*](https://pizbube.ch/en/shop/rock-climbing-in-thailand-and-laos), 9th edition (2018) plus the 2025 update, has a chapter of sidetrips to Perlis, Malaysia. It is not a source for this crag, but it is the last printed line of information for the region.
