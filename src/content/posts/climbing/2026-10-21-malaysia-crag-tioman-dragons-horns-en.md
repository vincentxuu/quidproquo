Most Malaysian crags are limestone walls a few dozen metres high where you can put up ten routes in an afternoon. Dragon's Horns is something else entirely: granite, whole routes of two or three hundred metres, no shortcuts, no shuttle, and nobody arranging the jungle approach for you.

This guide helps you decide three things: where the multipitch grades actually sit on the South Tower, which months give you the most reliable ferry, and how to get there without a car or a chartered island boat.

## What kind of crag

Dragon's Horns sits on the southern tip of Tioman (Pulau Tioman), directly above Kampung Mukut. [The theCrag area page](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/mukut) lists 14 routes and 54 logged ascents, with the sector table showing South Tower 12, North Tower 1 and Mystery Wall 1. The path list on the same page also carries a "Puncak Anak" entry with a 100 m trad route, but that entry sits outside the sector count table, so "14 routes" is the sector-table reading.

The rock is granite: spires and jungle big wall. Two main summits — the east face looks substantially taller than the south face, the page says "maybe 600 meters" — while the established routes on the south tower reach about 300 m, and the east peak takes sun from morning to noon. [A 2023 round-up](https://moredaysoff.wordpress.com/2023/12/28/the-best-rock-climbing-in-malaysia-is-in-kuala-lumpur/) calls the place "granite multi-pitches", and here the word is literal: each line takes two or three hundred metres of your own protection.

The style is trad and mixed multipitch. Freebird is a trad line; Naga is bolted but runout on the easier ground; Benjo Delight is mostly trad with pitons and one 3 m aid section; Waking Dream wants a full trad rack plus knifeblades, lost arrows and hooks. The crag page gives a French range of 5c+ to 7c+, but the easiest complete line is Freebird at 6a.

It suits teams who already manage multipitch, are willing to carry gear through hours of jungle, and accept that the ferry timetable sets the calendar. If you want a short line to work a move on, there is nothing here for you.

## When to go

The season here is not about sunlight on rock, it is about the ferry. The northeast monsoon dominates November through February.

- [The Mersing Harbour Centre 2026 schedule page](https://mersingharbourcentre.com/ferry-schedule.html) marks April to September as peak season and warns that sailings can be suspended, particularly during the December to February monsoon season.
- [The tiomanferry FAQ](https://tiomanferry.com/faq.html) puts the monsoon at November through February, says windy days can see the ferry prohibited from running for that day altogether by the Malaysia authorities, and notes that the ferry does not run from Tanjung Gemok during the monsoon.
- A [speedboat operator's closed-dates page](https://www.tiomanspeedboat.com/tioman-island-closed-dates) claims a mandatory closure from 1 November to 28 February, enforced by the Marine Department and applying to every ferry and speedboat operator. That is a claim on a commercial page rather than a Marine Department notice, so the exact dates stay contested.

Practical reading: treat March to October as the plannable window and November to February as unreliable rather than formally closed. All of this comes from operator pages, so confirm the actual schedule for your season with the ferry company instead of copying dates from old trip reports.

On sun: the east peak is lit from morning to noon while the established routes sit on the south tower, so leaving early buys you the shaded hours. The approach is hours of jungle bushwhacking (see below), which means the ferry crossing and a multipitch route do not belong on the same day.

## Getting there

### Public transport

You reach Tioman by getting to Mersing or Tanjung Gemok first and then taking a boat; the crossing takes 1.5 to 2 hours. [The Mersing Harbour Centre schedule page](https://mersingharbourcentre.com/ferry-schedule.html) asks you to arrive 60 minutes before departure.

The binding constraint is the jetty. [The tiomanferry Kampung Mukut page](https://tiomanferry.com/kampung_mukut.html) states that "the jetty at Mukut is not compatible with the public ferry out of Mersing or Tanjung Gemok", so the public ferry only reaches Genting, and from there you take a water taxi or the boat run by your accommodation to Kampung Mukut. Older beta that tells you to take the ferry to Mukut is wrong.

In the monsoon the public ferry can be prohibited for a whole day, and no ferry runs from Tanjung Gemok between November and February (see the [tiomanferry FAQ](https://tiomanferry.com/faq.html)). Schedules have to be confirmed with the operator; these pages publish no fixed timetable.

### Driving

Drive to the jetty terminal at Mersing or Tanjung Gemok, and treat everything after that as transfers: the water bus to Genting and the water taxi to Mukut both run on loose schedules. If you would rather not wait, a chartered boat is the alternative. The prices theCrag quotes are US$50 to 75 one way by speedboat from Berjaya, or RM50 to 70 return by speedboat to Mukut; neither figure carries a date, so ask again before you travel.

### Parking

The car stays on the mainland side, and island transport is the water taxi or your accommodation's boat. Neither the number of parking spaces nor the charges appear in this material, so treat parking and the ferry ticket as one risk: when the sailing moves, the car has to move with it.

**The last leg is on foot.** From Kampung Mukut, follow the shoreline to the stilt huts, take the established path uphill, cut left through dense brush to a ravine or riverbed, then skirt left along the wall base to the high point that marks the start of Waking Dream. That is hours of jungle bushwhacking with gear, food and water, with patchy mobile coverage and villagers as the only support. Budget it as a full day of exertion.

## Rules before you go

There is no published climbing fee or permit, and the theCrag area page lists no marine-park climbing permit; the money goes on boats.

**Bolts and protection.** Naga has bolted anchors and bolts, but the easier ground is runout and a small cam rack helps. Benjo Delight is mostly trad with pitons and one short aid section, and all but one anchor is bolted. Waking Dream needs a full trad rack plus knifeblades, lost arrows and hooks. No route on the crag carries a dated bolt-condition report. Malaysia's volunteer bolting organisation, [Malaysian Bolting Fund](https://www.camp5.com/score-sweet-rock-climbing-equipment-while-supporting-rock-climbing-in-malaysia), covers crags around Kuala Lumpur (more than 218 routes and pitches by the end of 2021) rather than Tioman. Inspect every piece of protection you intend to use.

**Local support.** The contacts theCrag names are Tam and Uncle Sam at Simukut Hiilview, and Kampung Mukut keeps a logbook with beta in it. Only the villagers know what the jungle approach is really doing, so make contact before you set out.

**Emergencies.** Call 999. Tioman has no published information on a hospital, clinic or rescue service, so the time cost of getting off the island after a serious injury is yours to work out. Ask your insurer separately about outdoor lead climbing, trad, multipitch, search and rescue, medical treatment and repatriation; the policy name alone does not establish coverage.

## Routes by level

YDS and V-grade conversions follow the [Mountain Project international grade comparison chart](https://www.mountainproject.com/international-climbing-grades) and are approximate; "approx." marks grades the sources give without a/b/c (such as 4 or 5+). The original grade is the one that counts.

The original grades here are French (theCrag's convention), neither UIAA nor YDS. The "Original grade" column keeps the source wording, the YDS column is the converted reference value, and the bands below follow the YDS.

### Beginner: up to 6a (YDS 5.10a and below)

Freebird is the easiest complete line. The lower bound the crag page gives, 5c+, does not map to a named route, so this band holds one route.

| Route | Original grade | YDS | Length / pitches | Wall | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Freebird | 6a, 5c, 5a, 5c, 6a, 6a, 5c | hardest 5.10a | 250 m, 7 pitches | South Tower | theCrag (2026-10-21): trad line, pitch grades as listed on the page; the topo sits off-site on verticalpirate-escalade.com |

### Intermediate: 6a+ to 6c+ (YDS 5.10b–5.11c)

Naga and Damai Sentosa represent this band: Naga is the bolted-but-runout mixed line, Damai Sentosa is a 2013 line opened with Arnaud Petit. Their hardest pitches are 6b+ and 6c+ respectively.

| Route | Original grade | YDS | Length / pitches | Wall | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Naga / Dragon | 6b+ | 5.10d | 270 m, 8 pitches (hardest pitch 6b+) | South Tower | theCrag (2026-10-21): bolted but runout on the easier ground; small cams (micro to BD #1/2) help |
| Damai Sentosa | 6c+ | 5.11c | 280 m, 7 pitches (hardest pitch 6c+) | South Tower | theCrag (2026-10-21): a 2013 line opened with Arnaud Petit |

### Advanced: 7a to 7c+ (YDS 5.11d–5.13a)

This is the body of the crag: Benjo Delight, Muka, Polish Princess, Batu Naga and Waking Dream. Waking Dream is the longest at 10 pitches and 300 m, listed by theCrag at 7c+ with aid; [a 2013 blog post](https://www.headlampsclusterfucksandotherverticaladventures.com/2013/09/the-dragons-horns-on-tioman-island-malaysia-part2-the-horns-and-a-brief-history-of-waking-dream/) records the same line as V 5.9 A2 and 400 m, so both the length and the grade need checking on the ground.

| Route | Original grade | YDS | Length / pitches | Wall | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Benjo Delight / f\*\*k Nestle | 7a A0 | 5.11d | 230 m, 8 pitches | South Tower | theCrag (2026-10-21): mostly trad with pitons and one 3 m aid section; first ascent April 2025 |
| Muka | 7a+ | 5.12a | 360 m (pitch count not listed) | South Tower | theCrag (2026-10-21): trad line, 2010 |
| Polish Princess | 7b+ | 5.12c | 270 m, 8 pitches | South Tower | theCrag (2026-10-21): trad line, 2010 |
| Batu Naga / Stone Dragon | 5.12a (theCrag records it as US 5.12a) | 5.12a | 300 m, 8 pitches | South Tower | theCrag (2026-10-21): trad line, Cedar Wright and Lucho Rivera, 2011 |
| Waking Dream | 7c+ (with A0 aid) | 5.13a | 300 m, 10 pitches | South Tower | theCrag (2026-10-21): needs a full trad rack plus knifeblades, lost arrows and hooks; the [2013-09-17 blog post](https://www.headlampsclusterfucksandotherverticaladventures.com/2013/09/the-dragons-horns-on-tioman-island-malaysia-part2-the-horns-and-a-brief-history-of-waking-dream/) calls it V 5.9 A2, 400 m |

### Expert: 8a and up (YDS 5.13b and up)

No named route with published details reaches this band; the crag page caps the range at 7c+. Going higher means opening your own line, and there is no published record of that here.

## Where to look things up

- [theCrag: Dragon Horns](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/mukut): the full route list, sector counts, ascent numbers and the approach description.
- [theCrag: South Tower](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/mukut/area/2922362889): grades, lengths and pitch counts for the 12 routes, the gear list and the descent notes; the Waking Dream gear list is here.

- [Tioman SpeedBoat: island closed dates](https://www.tiomanspeedboat.com/tioman-island-closed-dates): the claimed 1 November to 28 February closure, which is an operator's claim rather than a notice.
- [Headlamps: The Dragon's Horns on Tioman Island, Malaysia (Part 2)](https://www.headlampsclusterfucksandotherverticaladventures.com/2013/09/the-dragons-horns-on-tioman-island-malaysia-part2-the-horns-and-a-brief-history-of-waking-dream/) (2013-09-17): first-hand route history, a pillar-by-pillar list and the other set of numbers for Waking Dream.
- [moredaysoff: The best rock climbing in Malaysia is in Kuala Lumpur](https://moredaysoff.wordpress.com/2023/12/28/the-best-rock-climbing-in-malaysia-is-in-kuala-lumpur/) (2023-12-28): the second source describing the Dragon's Horns as granite multipitch.
- **Guidebooks:** Grades and route counts here come mainly from online databases; the guidebook is the authority.
  - [theCrag's Malaysia guidebook page](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/publications) states that no guidebook can be found for Malaysia, which makes the online database the primary source.
  - [*Climb Malaysia – The climbing guide*](https://climbingaway.fr/en/travel/climbing-guide-books/climb-malaysia-the-climbing-guide): published 2007 and out of print; it was unavailable in three Kuala Lumpur climbing shops in 2014, still unobtainable in 2023, and was only borrowed from a local at Damai. It covers most crags of its day and none of the development since.
  - [theCrag discussion thread (2014)](https://www.thecrag.com/discussion/545814402/climb-malaysia-guide-book): the record of hunting for a physical copy.
  - [*Climbing Guide To Malaysia*](https://www.ukclimbing.com/logbook/books/climbing_guide_to_malaysia-722): listed in the UKC bibliography (ISBN 981-04-9073-8) with no author or year, covering Batu Caves, Bukit Takun and Nyamuk; equally unobtainable.
  - [The archived climb.my pages](https://web.archive.org/web/2025/http://www.climb.my/routes/perlis/) belong to the *Climb Malaysia* website, which no longer loads, and the archive keeps only a title with no route content.
  - [Wira Adventure Consultant's book page](https://www.wiraconsultant.com/products-page/books/climb-malaysia-the-climbing-guide): still lists *Climb Malaysia* at MYR 40 with no stock information.
  - [The Crag Journal: Climbing in Malaysia](https://thecragjournal.com/2023/01/01/climbing-in-malaysia-a-journey-through-south-east-asia/) (2023-01-01): the state of Malaysian climbing and the difficulty of obtaining a guide.
  - The upshot: Malaysia currently has no obtainable printed national guidebook, so the route tables here follow theCrag, and new topos or new lines depend on online databases and the local community.
