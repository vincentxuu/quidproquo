Ulu Piah is private limestone on the Mount Hugh Low massif near [Tambun](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/area/10135722960) and [Ipoh](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/area/10135722960) in [Perak](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/area/10135722960): 27 routes with a grade spread from 5c to 8b, one of the few Malaysian crags with genuine 8s. It is not a walk-in crag. **You have to notify the landowner before you go**, the approach road is bad enough to want ground clearance, and community reports describe a fatal rockfall risk. This guide handles the three things that decide your day: who to call, how to drive in, and what to bring.

## What kind of crag

The [theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/area/10135722960) summary is complete: **Limestone**, located in **Tambun, Ipoh, Perak**, part of the Mount Hugh Low massif. The crag was discovered by **More Manjat** and bolted by the **Climbing Addict Society** crew (@climbingaddictsociety). The page also records *Morning sun*. Last updated 2026-10-19.

The 27 routes sit in four sectors, each a letter plus the Malay place name:

| Sector | Routes | Ascents | Wall | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A(njing) | 4 | 23 | 9 m | — |
| B(iawak) | 5 | 60 | — | — |
| C(icak) | 15 | 115 | 15 m | sport and bouldering |
| D(ubuk) | 3 | 45 | 12 m | — |

C(icak) is the main wall: 15 routes, 115 ascents, with bouldering problems as well as sport lines. What makes this crag unusual is the **grade spread** — the page lists grade steps from 5c all the way to 8b:

| Grade band | 5c | 5c+ | 6a | 6a+ | 6b | 6b+ | 6c | 6c+ | 7a | 7a+ | 7b | 7b+ | 7c | 7c+ | 8a | 8a+ | 8b |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Routes | 1 | 1 | 2 | 4 | 2 | 0 | 4 | 0 | 3 | 0 | 0 | 3 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 |

That distribution is useful in practice: 10 routes sit between 6a and 6c+, the easiest band to plan around, there are 8 routes at 7a and above, and 3 in the 8s. The catch is that **the page publishes only two route names** (Friend and Mencari Impian); the other routes have grades and no names, and the published distribution accounts for 25 routes. Route selection here runs on bands, not on names.

It suits teams with 6a and above under their belt who want a serious 7a or 8 project, and who are willing to make a phone call before the trip. Bouldering is possible too, in C(icak).

## When to go

The page records **morning sun**: the wall is sunlit in the morning, so the best slot is early and the afternoon is not when you want to be on the rock. Perak is inland, and the northeast monsoon rain from November to March is not the coastal kind, but **the wet season shortens the usable morning** ([MetMalaysia on weather phenomena](https://www.met.gov.my/en/pendidikan/fenomena-cuaca)).

This crag also has a variable the others do not: **morning sun and afternoon thunderstorms** turn the same wall into two different conditions. Limestone baked by the sun stays dry with reliable friction; after an afternoon storm both the rock and the starts can be wet. Climb early, pack up in the afternoon, and you never have to think about it.

## Getting there

### Public transport

The page is explicit: go by your own transport, because **public transport in this area is scarce**. There is no published service around Tambun, and no online basis for whether a Grab can be summoned onto that road. Driving is the only workable option.

### Driving

The approach is short, but the road is not good:

1. Search for **Bai Yun Si Thai Buddhist Temple**.
2. Drive about **50 m further** past the temple.
3. The crag is **on the left, beside the river**.

The page adds the key warning in the same paragraph: **a car with higher ground clearance is advisable, because the approach is rough and pothole-ridden**. That is not a formality; it describes the surface. A low-slung car should watch its underside on this stretch, and the last few dozen metres may not be passable at all.

### Parking

The page lists no parking spot and no fee. The road comes in only once, so leave room to pass and do not block it.

Where to stay: the page says **any homestay nearby Tambun should suffice**. The community record adds that **there are no shops near the crag**, so bring water, food and everything you might need to fix.

## Rules before you go

**Notify the landowner before you leave.** The crag page states this in the description: contact the landowner by phone or WhatsApp and tell him how many people are coming and what time. The contact published on the page is **Haji Hamid, +60 19-573 6787**. This is the owner's private arrangement, not a permit and not a fee, and turning up unannounced is a different thing entirely.

**The rockfall risk comes from community reports.** The community record describes a **risk of fatal rockfall and requires the belayer to wear a helmet**. The crag page carries no such rule, so treat it as a community report rather than a published regulation — but it describes what might land on you, so act on it: **the belayer wears a helmet**, you wear one too, and look up before committing to a roof or cave line.

**Bring a bolt extension.** The community record mentions bringing an extension for the bolts. Combined with the note that there are no shops nearby, that means water, food and the tools to deal with a loose hanger all come with you.

**Check the hardware yourself.** The page lists no maintenance record and no bolt condition. Inspect every hanger you plan to clip and skip anything that moves.

**Emergency: call 999**, the Malaysian emergency number. Neither the page nor the sources used here give the distance to a hospital or ambulance in the Tambun interior. Ask your insurer specifically about outdoor lead climbing, rescue, medical treatment and repatriation; policy names alone do not establish coverage.

## Routes by level

YDS and V-grade conversions follow the [Mountain Project international grade comparison chart](https://www.mountainproject.com/international-climbing-grades) and are approximate; "approx." marks grades the sources give without a/b/c (such as 4 or 5+). The original grade is the one that counts.

### Beginner: up to 6a (YDS 5.10a and below)

The sources list no named route. The published distribution has 1 route at 5c, 1 at 5c+ and 2 at 6a, four in total, but not one of them is named.

### Intermediate: 6a+ to 6c+ (YDS 5.10b–5.11c)

This is the thickest band in the published distribution, 10 routes (4 at 6a+, 2 at 6b, none at 6b+, 4 at 6c, none at 6c+). One of them is named.

| Route | Grade | YDS | Length / pitches | Sector | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Friend | 6a+ | 5.10b | — | — | theCrag named route ★★; no length or sector listed (page updated 2026-10-19) |

### Advanced: 7a to 7c+ (YDS 5.11d–5.13a)

The distribution holds 8 routes in this band (3 at 7a, none at 7a+, none at 7b, 3 at 7b+, 1 at 7c, 1 at 7c+), and one of them is named.

| Route | Grade | YDS | Length / pitches | Sector | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mencari Impian | 7a | 5.11d | — | — | theCrag named route ★★★; no length or sector listed (page updated 2026-10-19) |

### Expert: 8a and up (YDS 5.13b and up)

The sources list no named route. The distribution has 3 routes in this band (one each at 8a, 8a+ and 8b), which makes this one of the few Malaysian crags where the published data really does reach the 8s, but not a single route name is published.

## Where to look things up

- [theCrag: Ulu Piah](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/area/10135722960): sectors, grade distribution, ascent counts, the approach, and the landowner notification; page updated 2026-10-19.
- [theCrag Malaysia forum](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/forum): the latest hardware and access reports; read it again a week before you go.
- [Mountain Project](https://www.mountainproject.com): the other crowdsourced database, with less Malaysian coverage than theCrag; useful to cross-check grades.
- **Guidebooks:** Grades and route counts here come mainly from online databases; the guidebook is the authority.
  - [*Climb Malaysia – The climbing guide*](https://climbingaway.fr/en/travel/climbing-guide-books/climb-malaysia-the-climbing-guide), published 2007, **out of print**; it covered most crags of the time and none of the later development. Climbing shops in Kuala Lumpur could not source a copy as early as 2014, and it was still unavailable in 2023 ([theCrag discussion](https://www.thecrag.com/discussion/545814402/climb-malaysia-guide-book)).
  - [*Climbing Guide To Malaysia*](https://www.ukclimbing.com/logbook/books/climbing_guide_to_malaysia-722), ISBN 981-04-9073-8, author and publication year not listed, covering [Batu Caves](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/batu-caves), [Bukit Takun](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/bukit-takun-crags) and [Nyamuk](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/batu-caves/area/13951039). A search on the ISBN returns only this bibliography entry, and whether it is the same book as the one above remains unestablished.
  - Malaysia has no current printed nationwide guidebook. [theCrag's Malaysian publications page](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/publications) states that no guidebooks exist. The practical move is to generate a PDF of your sectors on theCrag and keep it on your phone.
  - [*Rock Climbing in Thailand and Laos*](https://pizbube.ch/en/shop/rock-climbing-in-thailand-and-laos), 9th edition (2018) plus the 2025 update, has a chapter of sidetrips to Perlis, Malaysia. It is not a source for this crag, but it is the last printed line of information for the region.
