> 🌏 [中文版](/posts/climbing/2026-10-21-malaysia-crags-overview)

Think of Malaysian climbing and the picture is limestone, caves, pockets and jungle. The country sits inside the limestone belt made famous by Krabi and Railay, yet it has crags of its own, spread from Perlis in the north down the peninsula, across the coast, and into Borneo. What they share is not a single big crag but scatter: mostly small, and each one has its own way in.

This series covers Malaysian natural crags — sport, trad, multipitch and bouldering. One crag or one clearly bounded set of walls per guide, starting with [Bukit Keteri](/posts/climbing/2026-10-21-malaysia-crag-bukit-keteri). This overview helps you decide what gear to bring, which state to base yourself in, and where not to go this year.

## Choose the style before the base

Below are the 13 crags this series covers, not a directory of every crag in Malaysia. The table puts options you might compare on one trip side by side. Every wall at Batu Caves counts as its own crag: prices and bolt condition do not transfer between them.

| Crag | State | Main style | Before you choose |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Bukit Keteri](/posts/climbing/2026-10-21-malaysia-crag-bukit-keteri) | Perlis | Limestone sport, 4a–8c+ | Walkable from the train station, but nine tenths of the bolts are a 2007 batch |
| [Gua Kelam](/posts/climbing/2026-10-21-malaysia-crag-gua-kelam) | Perlis | Cave sport, 36 routes | The park charges entry, and weekends get busy |
| [Pulau Tuba](/posts/climbing/2026-10-21-malaysia-crag-langkawi) | Langkawi | Sport on private land, 20 routes | Trespassing is prosecuted; you need permission by phone first |
| [Gunung Keriang](/posts/climbing/2026-10-21-malaysia-crag-gunung-keriang) | Kedah | Free sport, 37 routes | Inside a public recreation park, with very few named routes published |
| [Bukit Keplu](/posts/climbing/2026-10-21-malaysia-crag-bukit-keplu) | Kedah, Kodiang | Morning sport, 28 routes | Caves and rockfall; two approaches of five and twenty minutes |
| [Lubuk Pedati](/posts/climbing/2026-10-21-malaysia-crag-lubuk-pedati) | Kedah, Baling | Sport, 35 routes | A muddy final stretch and almost no public transport |

| [Fairy Caves](/posts/climbing/2026-10-21-malaysia-crag-fairy-caves) | Sarawak, Bau | Cave and sandstone sport, 88 routes | Wet rock in the rains, and some walls are closed |

## Narrow the choice by season

[Malaysia's meteorological service](https://www.met.gov.my/en/pendidikan/fenomena-cuaca) puts the northeast monsoon between November and March, with the heaviest rain on the peninsula's east coast, western Sarawak and eastern Sabah. The western corridor — Perlis, Kedah, Perak — sits outside the core of that belt, which is why it is easier to plan than the east of Thailand.

| Window | Where to start | What changes the plan |
|---|---|---|
| January–April | Perlis, Kedah, Perak, Sarawak | The best months in Perlis; [theCrag's Perlis page](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/area/422129439) says to avoid extreme heat, bugs and the wet season |
| May–October | Perlis, Batu Caves, Langkawi | Sun angle matters more than season; the north-facing Damai Wall gets all-day sun from mid-March to August |
| November–March | Batu Caves, Ipoh | Kuala Lumpur has no real dry season — about 280 mm over 16 rain days in December, and the rain usually arrives in the evening but comes hard |
| December–February | Tioman, Sarawak | Do not plan eastern Malaysia then: ferries are cut or suspended for stretches, and Sarawak rock gets wet in the rains |

At Batu Caves, near the equator, the season matters less than the latitude. Damai faces north yet, being equatorial, gets all-day sun from mid-March to August and all-day shade from August to mid-March; [White Wall](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/batu-caves/area/13950919) takes morning sun and shades after 12.30; [Comic Wall](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/batu-caves/area/13950991) faces south and is always in shade. At [Fairy Caves](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/area/754333059) in Sarawak, everything except the west-facing Zoo Wall looks south, so it is shaded all day from May to September; December to April is the heavy rain season and the rock gets wet, while Batman Wall and Stage Wall have a big roof and can be climbed year round.

Two seasonal closures are easy to miss. Selangor's forestry department closes recreational activity in forest reserves during the monsoon each year — 17 November 2025 to 31 January 2026 was one instance — and no notice was found saying whether the private Batu Caves walls fall inside that rule. Perlis's forest permit register in August 2026 still lists seven sites that need a permit; [Bukit Keteri is not on it](https://tahanoutdoor.com/blogs/camping/perlis-hiking-permits-list), although a geopark post that same year said the hill had only recently been gazetted a forest reserve.

## Think about the last leg of transport

Split "getting to the city" from "getting to the start of the climb". A train that reaches a town does not mean you can shoulder a rope and start climbing, and the way back counts too.

- **Kuala Lumpur to Batu Caves**: KTM Komuter from KL Sentral to the Batu Caves station, RM2.60 and about 35 minutes; Grab around RM25–40.
- **Kuala Lumpur to Perlis**: ETS from KL Sentral to Arau, roughly 4.5 to 5.3 hours, six trains a day, about RM73–108, then a Grab. The other option is the Komuter Utara from Butterworth to Padang Besar, which passes a "Bukit Ketri" station; the full fare is RM11.40 and the 24 July 2026 timetable lists a dozen-plus trains a day, with the first from Butterworth at 05:20 reaching Bukit Ketri at 06:59 and the last arriving around 23:19. The crag page calls the station "Tasik Melati", which is most likely the old name for the same stop, but no explicit statement was found.
- **The nearest airport to Perlis** is Alor Setar in Kedah, about 35 km from Kangar and 45 minutes by taxi; Penang also works, with a longer transfer.
- **Ipoh** is roughly 2.5 hours north of KL by train. Gunung Lang is about RM10 and 3 km from the old town by Grab; for the Kedah and Perak crags the official advice is to bring your own transport.
- **Pulau Tuba** means walking east from Kuah Ferry Station to a separate jetty, buying a ticket for RM5; boats run 09:00 to 19:00 at odd hours only.
- **Tioman** is a two-to-four-hour ferry from Mersing, then a speedboat or fishing boat to Kampung Mukut. The public ferry's jetty is not compatible with Mukut's, so you get off at Genting and transfer.
- **Sarawak** is a bus from Kuching to Bau, about 30 km, then 9 km more. Kuching is the nearest city with hospital care.

When you prepare a route, save the crag name, the entry coordinates and the pickup point at the same time. Navigating to "Batu Caves" gets you to the temple, not to any wall.

## Reading the grades

Malaysia mostly uses **French grades** — [theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia) tags every page FR — while older material (the 2007 guidebook, parts of [Mountain Project](https://www.mountainproject.com/area/106072512/malaysia)) uses YDS. This series keeps the original grade and converts with the [Mountain Project international chart](https://www.mountainproject.com/international-climbing-grades); the "approx." marks the grades a source gives without an a/b/c, such as 5+.

Two things really affect route choice:

- **Route counts and grades are community entries.** The same wall shows different numbers in different sources, because people add lines, rebolt and regrade. theCrag's own Batu Caves parent page warns that a lot of its information is out of date.
- **Bolt age changes how a grade feels.** Damai's expansion bolts are 20 to 30 years old and some anchors are worn slings; Comic Wall had a report of rusting hardware across the wall in August 2026; at Bukit Keteri nine tenths of the bolts are the 2007 batch air-freighted from Europe and their material is still not published. A 6c on soft rock with old bolts is harder than the number says.

When you pick an objective, put the original name, the original grade, the pitches and the descent together. Two topos with the same route name may end at different anchors, so a total grade alone is not a comparison.

## Where to look things up

Malaysia has no current printed national guidebook, which means the online databases are the primary source. The division of labour is more useful than hunting for one site that does everything.

| Entry point | Good for | How to use it |
|---|---|---|
| [theCrag Malaysia](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia) | National route counts, grade spread, sector sizes, access and fees | Each page carries Access / Fixed Gear / Flora and Fauna warnings with a creation date; log in to build a custom PDF for offline use |
| [Mountain Project Malaysia](https://www.mountainproject.com/area/106072512/malaysia) | Route descriptions, geology and history | Less complete than theCrag, so use it to cross-check |
| [Perlis Geopark](https://www.facebook.com/perlisgeopark) | Land gazettements and geological sites | Posts only; no formal regulation |
| [Jabatan Perhutanan Negeri Perlis](https://www.facebook.com/forestryperlis) | Forest reserve permits | The Perlis permit is RM5 per person, and a licensed forest guide (Malim Gunung Perhutanan) is mandatory above 600 m, one guide to seven hikers |
| [Gua Damai Extreme Park](https://guadamai.com/press/) | Park fees, facilities and equipment rules | The park publishes its own permits and prices |
| [Sarawak Forestry Corporation](https://sarawakforestry.com/parks-and-reserves/wind-cave-fairy-cave-nature-reserve/) | Access and fees for the Fairy Caves reserve | Reserve notices |
| [Perlis Climbers](https://www.facebook.com/perlisclimbers) | The latest lines and access in Perlis | perlisclimb@gmail.com, WhatsApp +60 12-946 6569; theCrag says much of the Perlis information is not online, so just ask them |
| [Malaysian Bolting Fund](https://www.malaysianboltingfund.com/) | Development and rebolting around Kuala Lumpur | More than 218 routes and pitches since April 2013, stainless steel or titanium only, roughly RM700–800 of hardware per route; donation boxes at Camp5 check-in desks |

- **Guidebooks:** Grades and route counts here come mainly from online databases; the guidebook is the authority.
  - [*Climb Malaysia – The climbing guide*](https://www.climbingaway.fr/en/travel/climbing-guide-books/climb-malaysia-the-climbing-guide), published 2007 and out of print. Mountain Project says the first edition is dated and missing newer lines but still enough to recognise the popular ones; by 2014 people in Kuala Lumpur could not buy it in any of the three climbing shops, and in 2023 a climber searched everywhere and only borrowed a local copy.
  - [*Climbing Guide To Malaysia*](https://www.ukclimbing.com/logbook/books/climbing_guide_to_malaysia-722), ISBN 981-04-9073-8, with no author or year listed; the review notes that some fixed points are gone or buried in vegetation, and a search by ISBN turns up no other record, so it may simply be the same book.
  - [*Rock Climbing in Thailand and Laos*](https://pizbube.ch/en/shop/rock-climbing-in-thailand-and-laos), 9th edition (2018) plus a 2025 update, includes a "Sidetrips to Perlis, Malaysia" chapter covering Perlis.

## Shared etiquette and finding a partner

Check the land manager first, then the climbing records. The [Perlis forest permit register](https://tahanoutdoor.com/blogs/camping/perlis-hiking-permits-list) listed seven sites needing a permit and a forest guide in August 2026, and Bukit Jernih is on it — Bukit Jernih is also a climbing crag. Bukit Keteri is not on the list, but the register changes, so look again before you go.

On site: park where you are told to, keep farm tracks and roads clear, and take your rubbish with you. A lot of the walls in Perlis, Langkawi and Sarawak sit on private land. theCrag is blunt about Comic Wall: nothing stops you climbing there, but the site is private and an unhappy owner could pull the bolts. The Pulau Tuba page is blunter still — get permission from Rozaiman at BlankFace Adventures, and trespassers will be prosecuted.

Three more things change the plan:

- **Fees are not standardised.** Damai charges RM10 for foreigners and RM5 for Malaysians, with toilets and showers extra, requires helmets for climber and belayer, and the park management asks for a 70 m rope and 16 quickdraws. The Gua Lepak rooftop charges RM10 for tourists and RM5 for locals. Nyamuk, White Wall, Nanyang, Comic and Red Rocks are free.
- **Ramadan changes the rhythm.** Muslim-run eateries close during the day, Ramadan bazaars appear in the evenings, and the run-up to Hari Raya brings a national rush with congested roads and sold-out train tickets. Perlis and Kedah are among the more conservative states, so plan some slack into finding food during the day.
- **Insurance and the emergency number.** The emergency number in Malaysia is [999](https://www.malaysia.gov.my/en/categories/safety-and-community/public-safety/mers-999-emergency-line) (NG MERS 999, free to call). Ask your insurer specifically about outdoor lead climbing, search and rescue, medical treatment and repatriation; a travel policy name is not coverage. Mobile coverage is unreliable on Tioman and in Sarawak, which is where this matters most.

To find a partner, start at the gyms and local organisations. [Camp5](https://www.camp5.com/) in Kuala Lumpur is where the Malaysian climbing community is densest, and [MIR Adventures](https://miradventures.com/guidedrockclimbingbatucavesmalaysia) runs a guided Batu Caves day at RM230 per person including equipment. In Perlis, contact Perlis Climbers directly — their page puts it plainly: if you have no partner, a Perlis Climber will definitely go climbing with you. In Sarawak, get in touch with the local Kuching climbers, or call the Fairy Caves gate contact to arrange access.

Whoever you find, write down the date, the crag, the style and grade you can lead, whether you can set up and lower off anchors, what gear each of you brings, and the wet-weather plan.

## A first-trip decision

Say you have five days, normally climb 6a, and want to try Malaysian outdoor rock.

First decide whether the trip is about "several short lines" or "one long limestone route". The first version means staying in Kangar or in a village near Bukit Keteri, making [Bukit Keteri](/posts/climbing/2026-10-21-malaysia-crag-bukit-keteri) West the main base and adding [Gua Kelam](/posts/climbing/2026-10-21-malaysia-crag-gua-kelam) on the side; or staying in Kuala Lumpur for two days at Batu Caves, a day at [Gunung Lang in Ipoh](/posts/climbing/2026-10-21-malaysia-crag-gunung-lang) and a session at [Ulu Piah](/posts/climbing/2026-10-21-malaysia-crag-ulu-piah). The second version means [Dragon's Horns](/posts/climbing/2026-10-21-malaysia-crag-tioman-dragons-horns), which asks for trad, multipitch and the fitness to carry a load through jungle — and should not be scheduled in the monsoon.

**Do not plan Bukit Takun right now.** The name attracts a lot of searches: a 250-metre monolith in Selangor with 150 sport pitches and real multipitch potential, which climbers have been turned away from repeatedly since late 2025. [theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/malaysia/bukit-takun-crags) marks it as not allowed and writes that as of January 2026 it is unclear how to get a permit, or even whether one is possible; [UKC](https://www.ukclimbing.com/logbook/crags/bukit_takun-18112) says the forestry department banned entry in 2026 with no known reopening. Selangor's permit list puts Bukit Takun under "permit required, guide not required", but the online application system has stayed shut: it briefly showed applications opening on 1 February 2026 and then nothing, and the most recent post, in September 2026, still says access is refused. Only one Japanese climber has entered with forestry permission. This series gives it no article of its own precisely because it is not somewhere to plan.

This edition covers 13 crags across Perlis, Kedah, Perak, Selangor, Pahang and Sarawak. It is not a route-by-route list of Malaysia. Each guide handles the approach, the rules, the routes by grade and the places to check; climbing still needs a topo that matches the route, a weather judgement and the skills to back it up.
