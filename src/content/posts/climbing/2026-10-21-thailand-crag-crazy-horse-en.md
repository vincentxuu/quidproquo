Crazy Horse sits in the Mae On valley 45 minutes from Chiang Mai city, and it is **the best developed crag in northern Thailand**. theCrag logged 289 routes on 21 October 2026, 8,408 ascents and 52 photos, graded 4a to 8b, across 22 sectors.

**But the most important part of this guide is not the routes — it is the access status.** Crazy Horse is the only crag in this series with plenty of real-world evidence of people climbing and **no official reopening notice anywhere**. **Before planning a 2027 trip, that is the thing to resolve.**

## What kind of crag this is

Limestone. theCrag's summary:

> **Crazy horse is the premier sport climbing area in northern Thailand.** All styles of climbing from technical slabs to steep tufa climbing in a beautiful setting.
> On offer are a mix of fingery, technical and powerful sport routes on slab, vertical and steep limestone faces.

**theCrag's summary and description contradict each other**: the summary says 289 routes, but the description says "'Crazy Horse Buttress' is located near Chiang Mai and is the best developed crag in northern Thailand, with **about 150 routes**". **That is the gap between what the database logs and how much rock actually exists, not an arithmetic error.** Both are given here so you know what the 289 covers.

**The route tables in this guide cover 16 sectors and 233 routes.** Six more (The Evening Terraces 3, The Furnace 11, Vision Wall 10, Naam Prik Wall 7, The Ant Hill 13, Brah Wall 12) **returned 403 from theCrag on 21 October 2026** while that area's index was being rebuilt. **This guide does not quote grades or lengths for those 56 routes** — they are on theCrag if you want them. **The Ant Hill is one of the sectors that carries a warning badge, so read it before you go.**

**Most of the sectors require just a few minutes walk on well maintained trails from the parking, but the biggest wall (Heart Wall) is 15 mins walk.** That is theCrag's own wording — and Heart Wall happens to be the sector with the most routes (36). On a first visit, budget that walk.

### Heart Wall is the main wall

**Most of Heart Wall's 36 routes have Thai names**, a hallmark of the local development wave around Francis Haden and friends. The crag's hardest routes also cluster here:

- **Jai Glah Reu Plao / Are You Courageous? (7a+)**: "Stunning climbing all the way to the anchors. It really doesn't get more enjoyable than this. Get on it!"
- **Jai Teung Jai / Heart to Heart (6c+, 35 m)**: ★★★.
- **Ruam Jai / Together (6a+, 36 m)**: "This is the best at the grade in the area. Simply stunning climbing all the way to the top."
- **Jai Yen Yen / Patient / The Proposal / Calm (6c+, 21 m)**: ★★★.
- **Jai Det / Fearless (7a+, 13 m)**: "Tough and short with a balancy committing finish. Little runout with a dodgy fall possibility. Pull hard young…"
- **Jai Kwang / Unknown / Open-Minded (6c/c+, 26 m)**: "Bouldery and powerful start, and a great thin slab section. Bit of a squeeze job…"

### The multipitch lives in two cave sectors

**Anxiety State Crisis Cave (24 routes, 31 m)** is a genuine cave crag:

- **In Deep Space (5c, 80 m, 4 pitches)**: "A four pitch climb that starts on the left face of the cave after walking in from the lower entrance."
- **The Darkness Beckons (6a+, 70 m, 5 pitches)**: from the In Deep Space P1 anchor, traverse left three bolts.
- **Anxiety State Crisis (7a, 42 m, 2 pitches)**: "takes a line up a featured pillar in the center of the cave."
- **Space Manoeuvers (from 6c, three pitches)**, **Spaceman (6c / 7b+, two pitches)**, **Sunseeker (6c+ / 6b+, two pitches)**.

**Reunion Buttress (29 routes, 18 m)**:

- **Reunion (6a+, 52 m, 3 pitches)**.
- **Walk the Plank (7a+, 15 m)**: ★★★; **accessed from halfway up Reunion Pitch 3, with the belay anchor inside the cave**.
- **Northern Exposure Ext (6b, 35 m)**: can be climbed as one 6b pitch.
- **Total Biscuits (6a+)**, **Sweet Harmony (5c)** and **Happiness Happening (6b)** all sit on the ledge in the cave.

## When to go

**The season question here is not just weather — it is air quality.**

| Period | Conditions | Planning note |
|---|---|---|
| Nov–Feb | Dry and cool | The best window. Chiang Mai manages 28–33°C by day and 15–16°C at night |
| Mar | Burning season peak | **Chiang Mai AQI passed 150 on 2026-03-04**; sensitive travellers should avoid it |
| Apr–May | Hot | Climb early and late |
| Jun–Oct | Monsoon | Afternoon thunderstorms disrupt climbing |

**The burning season usually runs January to April and is worst in March.** It overlaps Krabi's peak season almost exactly — and it *is* Chiang Mai's peak season. **If you want both, go in November or after April.**

## Getting there

### Public transport

**There is no scheduled bus.** CMRCA runs a shuttle, and it is the easiest option:

- **Leaves 08:30, returns 16:30.**
- **Minimum four people.**
- **495 THB return.**

theCrag's approach note: "Stop in at the CMRCA in Chiang Mai for more info and to pick up a copy of the guide (500 baht). **They also provide a shuttle running out to the cliff in the morning and back in the evening.**"

### By car

About 45 minutes from Chiang Mai city on Highway 107 toward Mae On (theCrag: "located in the municipality of Mae On, a 45 minute drive from Chiang Mai").

**But the car park is a dirt road.** theCrag is blunt about it: "Google maps has the location of the crazy horse car park. It's a dirt road, so you might find it hard to get taxis to drive up it. They can drop you, if necessary, on the main road, and **you can walk the 10-15 minutes uphill** to get to the main car park."

### Parking

The car park has maintained trails leading to the sectors. **Most are a few minutes away; Heart Wall is 15 minutes.**

## Rules to know before you go

### Access status: the biggest unknown

**The timeline laid out:**

| When | What happened | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 2018-07 | Closed over a criminal investigation between local authorities | CMRCA closure page |
| 2022-09 | Reopened | the text of the reopening petition |
| 2024-06 | **Prohibition signs appeared again**; the Thai climbing association petitioned the Chiang Mai governor to reopen | same, CMRCA 2024-08 |
| 2024-08 | The last post in CMRCA's related category is a joint call to reopen | CMRCA |
| 2026 | **No official reopening notice**, but multiple signs of people climbing | see below |

**What is the evidence of climbing in 2026?**

- CMRCA still sells the shuttle ([CMRCA transport page](https://www.thailandclimbing.com/climber-info/getting-to-crazy-horse)).
- UKC's crag page says "reopened according to CMRCA".
- Someone uploaded a short climbing video from here in January 2026.
- A guided trip was announced for January 2027.

**But there is no official reopening notice from any source.** This is the only closure in the whole series that does not even have a first-hand source.

**So the correct phrasing is: "there is plenty of evidence of people climbing, and no official reopening notice."** Not "it has reopened", and not "it is still closed".

**What to do before you go**: ask CMRCA directly. theCrag itself advises "stop in at the CMRCA in Chiang Mai for more info" — **and that office is the same body that published the closure notice and launched the reopening petition.** It is the single most important thing to confirm in this guide.

### Management may be moving to the local community

A separate local article says Crazy Horse **has been closed more than once and that management is shifting to the local community**, and advises checking first. **That is a second-hand report, so treat it as a prompt to ask, not as established fact.**

### Bolts and gear

Travel notes record: **a 70-metre rope is the most suitable (60 metres covers about nine tenths of the routes), 14 to 18 quickdraws. The bolts are very tight, roughly one every 1 to 1.5 metres on the easier routes.**

**theCrag carries no per-route bolt information for Crazy Horse.** That differs from Krabi — inland crags are classed as lower-corrosion-risk "benign" crags in the same material study ([Crag Chemistry 2024-09](https://cragchemistry.com/2024/09/23/thailand-crags-1/)) — but **no data does not mean no problem.**

### One note about monkeys

Heart Wall's **Peaun Jai Rai / My Friend is Mean (5a)** carries a line worth knowing: "Super fun, super easy and all the way to the top. **Sometimes you even get monkeys at the anchors trying to steal**…"

It is theCrag's only monkey warning, but it is real.

## Where to look things up

- **Online databases:** theCrag's [Crazy Horse page](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/thailand/chaing-mai) has all 289 routes, the spread across 22 sectors, the approach, the accommodation and the guidebook note. Each sector page has per-route grades, stars and lengths.
- **Guidebooks:** grades and route counts here come mainly from online databases; the guidebook is the authority. The guide that covers Crazy Horse is **[A Guide to Rock Climbing in Northern Thailand](https://www.thailandclimbing.com/climber-info)** (CMRCA Press, 3rd edition, 220 pages, 500 THB, more than 150 pitches across 18 northern sectors). **Buy it at the CMRCA office in Chiang Mai, and ask about access status while you are there — that is the single most important thing to do in this guide.**
- **Shuttle and local information:** [CMRCA's Crazy Horse transport page](https://www.thailandclimbing.com/climber-info/getting-to-crazy-horse) has the shuttle times, price and minimum group size. It is also the fastest way to get an answer on whether you can climb.
- **The actual rebolting list:** **not findable online.** That is normal for inland crags; the guidebook and the lists in the CMRCA shop are the real sources.
