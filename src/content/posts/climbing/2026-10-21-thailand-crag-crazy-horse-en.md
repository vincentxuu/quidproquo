From the car park the first thing you see is a block of limestone above the main area shaped like a horse's head, and that is where Crazy Horse gets its name. With dense bolting, maintained trails and most sectors a few minutes' walk away, it is easy to assume this is a mature crag you can visit any time. **The thing to check first is whether it is actually open.**

This guide helps you decide whether Crazy Horse suits your level, how to plan outside the November–February window, how to get there without a car, and who to ask about access before you go.

## What kind of crag

The Thai name is **หน้าผาม้าพยศ** ("the cliff of the unruly horse"), the name CMRCA uses on its Thai pages. In English it is usually Crazy Horse Buttress, and theCrag also lists the alias Mae On (the district name). The crag sits near the **Tham Muang On (ถ้ำเมืองออน)** cave temple, on the other side of the same hill. Set your navigation to the Crazy Horse car park marked on Google Maps (theCrag).

The rock is limestone. theCrag's summary calls it "the premier sport climbing area in northern Thailand", with everything from technical slabs and vertical faces to steep tufas. **It is almost entirely bolted sport climbing**, but the long cave lines involve abseils and loose rock, so they call for different kit from the single-pitch routes (see Rules before you go).

There are two counts. theCrag's summary gives **289 routes in 22 sectors** (read 21 October 2026), while the description on the same page says "about 150 routes"; that description has no date and looks like it was written early and never updated. climbgrades' 2026 Chiang Mai guide says "over 200". These counts come from different points in time and can't be converted into each other.

**The route tables here cover only 16 of those sectors, 233 routes.** The other 6 sectors (The Evening Terraces, The Furnace, Vision Wall, Naam Prik Wall, The Ant Hill and Brah Wall, 56 routes in all) returned 403 from theCrag when read on 21 October 2026, and this guide does not cite them. The Ant Hill carries a warning flag on theCrag, so open it yourself before you go.

| Sector | Routes | Character |

**The draw is the bolting.** climbgrades puts bolts on the easier routes about 1 to 1.5 m apart and says runouts are rare. The same guide is just as direct that most routes fall between 5 and 7a+, with few above 7b. **Leading 6a indoors and leading a 4-pitch cave route then abseiling back out are two different things.** On a first visit, do a few short routes at Little Monkey Cove or Tamarind Village to get used to the rock before deciding whether to go into the caves.

## When to go

Chiang Mai's high season runs November to February, dry and cool. chiangmai.net's climate data puts daytime highs at about 28–33°C and nights at about 15–16°C over that period.

**The thing most likely to change your plans here is not rain but burning-season air.** Northern Thailand's burning season runs roughly January to April and is worst in March; IQAir reported Chiang Mai's AQI above 150 on 4 March 2026, a level at which everyone should cut back on outdoor activity.

Insects have seasons too. climbgrades breaks Chiang Mai's seasons down further: smoke season February to April, bee season October to January, wasp season April to October. That comes from a single guide, but it matches the wasp and bee warnings in theCrag's route notes (see Rules before you go).

| When | Conditions | Advice |
|---|---|---|
| November–February | Dry, cool | The best window; watch air quality from late January |
| March–April | Peak burning season, hot | Check that day's AQI before leaving; sensitive groups should avoid it |
| May | Hot | Climb mornings and evenings only |
| June–October | Wet season | Climbable between showers; limestone seeps and gets slick after rain |

**Aspect and sun**: neither theCrag nor CMRCA gives aspects sector by sector. The best available is The Wandering Climber's 2019 write-up (secondary):

| Sector | Sun |
|---|---|
| Crazy Horse Area (main wall) | South-facing, in the sun from about 11:00 |
| The Aircon Wall | Shade at midday and a breeze from the cave; Vertical Life also names it as the hot-season (March–May) option |
| The Hanging Gardens, The Junkyard | Afternoon shade |
| Heart Wall | Shade all day, and some routes stay dry in the rain |

The cave sectors (Anxiety State Crisis Cave, Windy Cave) have natural roofs, and Vertical Life adds that The Furnace stays consistently shady. On a hot day: the main wall in the morning, Aircon Wall or the caves at midday, then Heart Wall or the Junkyard in the afternoon.

**Crowds**: weekend mornings in high season are busy. climbgrades suggests that climbers without partners turn up around 9:30 on a weekend and ask to join groups in the car park, the main area or Tamarind Village. On weekdays the crag may be empty, so if you go on your own, go as a complete team. Vertical Life warns that school groups often swarm Tamarind Village from November to February.

## Getting there

### Public transport

**There is no direct bus.** The practical option is CMRCA's red-truck (songthaew) shuttle. Per the [CMRCA transport page](https://www.thailandclimbing.com/climber-info/getting-to-crazy-horse), checked October 2026:

- It leaves **Progression Vertical Climbing Gym** in Chiang Mai at 08:30 and leaves the crag at 16:30.
- **It runs only with at least 4 passengers that day**; book at the shop or online by 19:30 the evening before.
- Drinking water is provided at the crag and lunch can be added; see the transport page for the fare.

In principle you can also ride a songthaew or minivan heading through Mae On and get off at the Crazy Horse turn, but climbgrades says you have to flag one down on the roadside for the return and it is not reliable. Grab usually won't go this far.

If you take a taxi, show the driver **หน้าผาม้าพยศ แม่ออน** (the Crazy Horse cliff in Mae On). theCrag warns that the last stretch is a dirt road and drivers may refuse it and drop you on the main road, **leaving a 10 to 15 minute uphill walk** to the main car park. Count that separately from the walk from the car park to the walls.

### Driving

theCrag puts the drive from Chiang Mai at about 45 minutes. Head east through San Kamphaeng on Highway 1006, then turn onto 1317 towards Tham Muang On (per a 2015 traave travel feature, secondary), and set your navigation to the Crazy Horse car park on Google Maps. Take the last dirt section slowly after rain.

Many people rent a motorbike, but the CMRCA transport page warns that police readily fine foreigners riding without a motorbike licence; also check whether your insurance still pays out if you ride unlicensed or without a helmet.

### Parking

The main car park is at the end of the dirt road, with toilets and a hut next to it (climbgrades). The official pages I checked give no capacity or fee. From the car park, **most sectors are a few minutes' walk and Heart Wall is 15 minutes** (theCrag). climbgrades notes that Heart Wall, Gatekeeper Buttress and The Rooftop involve steep walking, so wear hiking shoes.

## Rules before you go

**First: ask CMRCA directly whether you can climb before you go.** Access is the biggest unknown here. The timeline:

| When | Event | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 2018 | Access suspended because of a criminal investigation between the Mae On district office and the Ban Sahakorn 2 sub-district office; the governor asked that it stay closed, and CMRCA advised climbers to avoid other northern Thai crags as well | [CMRCA closure notice](https://www.thailandclimbing.com/crazy-horse-closure) (primary) |
| 2022-09 | Reopened | Reopening petition text |
| 2024-06 | Prohibition signs went up again; Thaiger reported the community was still waiting for local administrative approval to reopen | Reopening petition text; Thaiger (secondary) |
| 2024-08 | CMRCA shared the Thailand Climbing Development Association's petition asking for reopening | [CMRCA, August 2024](https://www.thailandclimbing.com/blog/help-re-open-crazy-horse-buttress) (primary) |
| 2025–2026 | People climbing as usual, but no official reopening notice | See below |

The 2018 CMRCA notice is blunt: climbing there during the investigation counted as **trespassing on government land**, and if someone had an accident that needed local ambulances or hospitals, **the village head would be held responsible**. The notice is still on CMRCA's site with no update date.

Signs in 2025–2026 that people are climbing:

- CMRCA's transport page still lists the shuttle, and its booking system still lists a half-day guided climb at Crazy Horse (checked October 2026).
- A Vertical Life feature in its Spring 2025 issue says there is a big "Do not climb" sign at the entrance, yet people climb every day, no one from law enforcement has asked anyone to leave, and tour groups run regular trips.
- climbgrades (updated August 2026) and Ada House (July 2026) both say climbing is currently permitted, but neither cites any notice.
- UKC's crag page says it has reopened "according to CMRCA".
- People kept uploading climbing videos from here to YouTube through 2024 and 2025, most recently Sunseeker, Trojan Horse and Reunion in December 2025 (see the Videos column in the route tables).

**So the accurate statement is: "plenty of people climb there, but there is no official reopening notice."** It is neither "reopened" nor "still closed". The quickest way to confirm is to ask in person when you book the shuttle at Progression Vertical the evening before: the organisation that posted the closure notice, shared the reopening petition and runs the shuttle is the same one.

**Management is changing hands.** The 2018 notice says that under Thai land law a private company cannot lease this land, so CMRCA decided to hand its 15 years of buildings and facilities at the crag to the Mae On community, which had applied to the district, provincial and national land agencies to manage the area (primary). Ada House's 2026 article also says stewardship is shifting to the community (secondary). If there is a community fee or sign-in on site, go by what's on site.

**Seasonal avoidance: nests.** theCrag's route warnings (read 21 October 2026): Mar Jark Jai and Deung Duut Jai on Heart Wall ask climbers to "avoid this route for 2 months" because of a nearby bird's nest, and Tang Jai has an active owl nest with two eggs behind the big flake; Sweet Harmony on Reunion Buttress has a wasp nest; and the only 8a+ has a one-line note, "Watch for the bees." None of these warnings is dated, so check on site. Vertical Life is blunter: in cooler weather giant Asian honey bees build sail-shaped nests at the top of climbs, and when the swarm shimmers in unison like a Mexican wave you should **lower off immediately**; people have been stung hundreds of times. In the rainy season, wasps nest in sheltered cracks.

**Fixed gear and kit.** Vertical Life reports that the crag is inspected every year and rebolted with stainless steel. theCrag doesn't record bolt material route by route, but **The Beehouse (6c+) in Crazy Horse Area is flagged "Many loose bolts"**. climbgrades recommends **a 70 m rope (60 m covers about 90% of routes, and a handful of extensions need 80 m), 14 to 18 quickdraws, and a helmet**. In Deep Space's note says "lots of loose rock so check everything before pulling on it and wear a helmet". The cave multipitches abseil back to the cave floor, so check your rope length and descent first. On the cave-roof traverses (Anxiety State Crisis, Sunseeker, Space Manoeuvres), a fall can leave you hanging in space, unable to get back on the rock: UKC's 2011 guide recommends carrying prusiks, and Vertical Life says they are for experienced pairs who know how to ascend a rope.

In an emergency, call **1669** (the Ministry of Public Health's free medical emergency line, which routes you to the nearest hospital); for English-language help, call the tourist police on **1155** (GOV.UK healthcare guidance; Thai Government Public Relations Department, November 2025). **All of these tables come from theCrag's route-by-route pages for 16 sectors (read 21 October 2026).** Each band picks routes with high star ratings or specific descriptions; it is not a complete list. The ★ in the notes are theCrag stars, and routes without stars have no rating on theCrag. For multipitch routes, the grade column gives the hardest pitch and the length column gives the total length and pitch count. theCrag also lists 2 boulder problems (V4, V5), but there is too little data and they are not included.

Routes with videos get an extra Videos column on the right of the table; click the thumbnail to open that route's videos. A video's grade and route name describe that clip only; choose routes from the table's sources.

### Beginner: up to 6a (YDS 5.10a and below)

The 4s and 5s are concentrated at **Little Monkey Cove, Tamarind Village and The Hanging Gardens**, and Heart Wall has a few 5s too. **"Beginner" is a grade band, not a recommendation for a first day outdoors**: In Deep Space and Sentinel are only 5s, but both are 4-pitch lines. For a first day, compare two routes in Tamarind Village: **4500** (5a, 20 m single pitch, three stars) to get used to the rock and to leading, and **Makham Wan** (5c+, two pitches, 45 m) to practise multipitch changeovers and abseiling for the first time; Vertical Life also calls Makham Wan a good introduction to multipitch.

### Intermediate: 6a+ to 6c+ (YDS 5.10b–5.11c)

**This is Crazy Horse's deepest band**, with about half of the 233 routes. Heart Wall's Thai "Jai" series and Gatekeeper Buttress's key-themed routes are almost all two- and three-star 6a+ to 6c+ lines; to fill a day at one grade, stay on one of those walls. In this band, compare **Ruam Jai** (6a+, 36 m, with a mid-height anchor at 20 m) and **The Gatekeeper** (6b, 25 m): both are three stars, but they differ in length and rope needs, and with a 60 m rope you can only take Ruam Jai to the mid-height anchor. The cave multipitches also fall in this band. They aren't hard but they take time, so plan them separately from the short routes.

### Expert: 8a and up (YDS 5.13b and up)

The 16 sectors read for this guide have only two 8s. theCrag's crag page gives a grade range up to 8b, so that line is presumably in one of the 6 unread sectors and isn't listed here. ### Classic routes: multipitch and single pitch

The grade tables above choose routes by difficulty. This section adds 13 lines that CMRCA and several climbing publications commonly treat as Crazy Horse classics. **Not all of them are named by two or more sources**, so they sit in their own section: the multipitches follow CMRCA's multi-pitch guiding page, and the single pitches follow CMRCA's 2015 classics video, UKC's 2011 guide and Vertical Life's 2025 feature. Grades and lengths come from theCrag throughout, and where a source writes them differently, the notes give both.

Classic does not mean easy. The multipitch group runs from In Deep Space in the 5s to Space Manoeuvres at 7b, and on the cave-roof traverses the hard part is whether you can rescue yourself after a fall, not the grade.

#### Multipitch classics

CMRCA's multi-pitch guiding page lists Trojan Horse, In Deep Space, A Horse With No Name and Into the Sun as beginner multipitches, and Anxiety State Crisis, Sunseeker and Headhunters as advanced. Pitch grades and lengths come from theCrag's pages for each pitch.

### Route logs online

Beyond the guidebook and theCrag, climbers' blogs and the climbing press keep a batch of firsthand route logs. They add approach details, timing and on-the-ground feel; grades and pitch counts still follow the tables above. Most were written between 2015 and 2019, so go by the current situation for facilities and access.

| Route | Log to read | What that log adds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| In Deep Space | theCrag's [route page](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/thailand/chaing-mai/route/1049642385) | Pitch-by-pitch lengths and bolt counts; on pitch 2, after traversing three bolts right, the line splits twice: straight up is the 6a, and you take a sharp right, even traversing down, to reach the next anchor; two abseil options |

## Where to look things up

- **Access and shuttle**: the [CMRCA transport page](https://www.thailandclimbing.com/climber-info/getting-to-crazy-horse) has shuttle times, the minimum group size and how to book. The [closure notice](https://www.thailandclimbing.com/crazy-horse-closure) and the [Crazy Horse Updates category](https://www.thailandclimbing.com/blog/help-re-open-crazy-horse-buttress) are where the official position lives, but for current status ask CMRCA in person or by email (+66 86 911 1470, info@thailandclimbing.com).
- **Online databases**: [theCrag's Crazy Horse page](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/thailand/chaing-mai) for the 22 sectors and route-by-route grades, stars, lengths and route warnings (nests, loose bolts); [UKC's crag page](https://www.ukclimbing.com/logbook/crags/crazy_horse_buttress-3036) for climbers' comments.
- **General guide**: [climbgrades' Chiang Mai climbing guide](https://climbgrades.com/blog/chiangmaiguide) (updated August 2026) covers seasons, gear, finding partners and transport, though its statement on access cites no source.
- **Emergency numbers**: [the Thai Government Public Relations Department's tourist assistance numbers](https://thailand.prd.go.th/en/content/category/detail/id/2078/iid/447104).
- **Guidebook**: Grades and route counts here come mainly from online databases; the guidebook is the authority. This guide did not check the guidebook's route tables directly.
  - [A Guide to Rock Climbing in Northern Thailand](https://www.thailandclimbing.com/climber-info), CMRCA Press, 3rd edition, English, 220 pages, covering 18 areas and more than 150 routes in northern Thailand. CMRCA's Thai page says the first edition came out in 2001; the 3rd edition's year wasn't found, but Southern Oscillations was using it in 2015 and The Wandering Climber says it was printed in 2016 with about 200 routes. Southern Oscillations warns that the book often lists fewer bolts than are on the wall. It is sold at the CMRCA shop in Chiang Mai; routes opened after 2018 aren't in it.
  - Digital: Vertical Life's 2025 feature says the most up-to-date guide is now digital, on the Crag Finder app; whether CMRCA's Climbing in Northern Thailand app (launched 2016) is still maintained is unconfirmed. I installed neither to check.
