Lampang is northern Thailand's old capital, north of Chiang Mai, and its airport platform is the most beautiful in the country — a leftover of the opium and teak trade. **Thirty minutes outside town, in a valley, is northern Thailand's newest crag**: developed by CMRCA (the Thai Climbing Development Association), with 25 routes logged on theCrag on 21 October 2026, graded 5b to 6c+.

But the most important part of this guide is not the routes. It is **how you get in**: **Lampang is on military land. You cannot simply drive up and climb.**

## What kind of crag this is

Limestone, tagged "morning sun" on theCrag. Three sectors, none of them large:

| Sector | Routes | Height | Character |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Veteran Wall | 11 | 21 m | The most routes, all developed in May–June 2019 by Ooan Kongsingh, Apichart Korkeaw and others |
| The Ranger wall | 10 | 25 m | The tallest wall, developed July 2019, mostly 6b to 6c |
| No Name | 4 | — | The least documented sector |

CMRCA's own description says "over 50 routes, both single- and some multi-pitch climbing" — **theCrag logs only 25**. That is a gap in logging scope, not a dispute about the number.

### Why only 25 routes

Three things stack up:

1. **The crag is new.** Nearly all 25 routes theCrag lists were developed between May and July 2019, with no obvious record of new routes since.
2. **Access is restricted.** You can only climb on days when a guide is present, so the number of usable days is small to begin with.
3. **The routes exist but are not tidied up.** Almost everything online is theCrag's page and CMRCA's own site.

theCrag's page says it outright: **"This crag does not have a description"** — someone filed a request asking the community to write one. **This is a crag still in the making**, and its information density is far below Krabi's.

### Loose rock and helmets

theCrag's summary is direct: **"There is still a lot of loose rock, so please wear helmet."** The sector pages repeat the "Loose" tag.

That is the shared feature of new northern Thai crags — Crazy Horse and Chiang Dao are mature limestone, while Lampang has barely been developed. **At Lampang a helmet is not optional.**

## When to go

Northern Thailand's seasons match Chiang Mai, and the dry season from November to February is the best window.

| Months | Conditions | Planning note |
|---|---|---|
| Nov–Feb | Dry and cool, roughly 19–30°C | The best window. Chiang Mai manages 28–33°C by day and 15–16°C at night — pack a warm layer |
| Mar | Burning season peak, poor air quality | Chiang Mai AQI passed 150 on 2026-03-04; sensitive travellers should avoid it |
| Apr–May | Hot | Climb early and late |
| Jun–Oct | Monsoon | Loose rock plus rain is a dangerous combination; not recommended |

**Lampang has no park or protected-area seasonal closure on record**, which is simpler than Chiang Dao — you are not chasing an annual park notice. But the burning-season air quality problem is exactly the same as in Chiang Mai and Chiang Dao.

## Getting there

### Public transport

Lampang town has a train station on the northern line, **but no public transport connects the town to the crag**. The last 30 minutes from town to the crag is by car, by charter, or on transport arranged through CMRCA.

### By car

About 30 minutes from Lampang town to the crag ([thailandclimbing.com's Lampang trip page](https://www.thailandclimbing.com/lampang-climbing-weekends)). Most people drive up from Chiang Mai — roughly two hours north on the highway, through the Mae Hong Son-style mountain stretches on the way.

**The practical problem with driving:** CMRCA's page publishes no open dates and no prices, which means **you have to contact them before you know which day works**, not the other way round.

### Parking

theCrag lists the approach as **5–10 minutes, gentle gradient**, so the car park is close to the rock. The last 5 to 10 minutes is on foot from where you park.

## Rules to know before you go

### You must register with CMRCA in advance

This is the most important section of the guide. theCrag marks Lampang's access as **Paid + Private**, and explains:

> Military property. You have to pay and get permission from CMRCA in advance in order to climb in this area.

CMRCA's own page is more specific: **all climbers must register with CMRCA before arriving at the Lampang site, and must be accompanied by a CMRCA staff member while at the crag.** Which means:

1. **You cannot drive in on your own and climb.** This is the single biggest difference between Lampang and every other crag in this series.
2. **There is a fee**, and the amount is not listed on the public page.
3. **The crag only opens on days CMRCA schedules**, and those days are tied to guided trips.
4. CMRCA's page also mentions attaching a **negative rapid-test certificate** — that section of the page has no visible date, so **ask whether it is still required**.

### A problem with the documents themselves

**CMRCA's page lists neither open dates nor prices**, and still carries the rapid-test requirement. Together those two gaps mean **you cannot plan a trip from the website alone.** The only reliable route is to contact CMRCA directly.

Separately, a 2023 Crag Journal trip report through Thailand recorded that on arrival in Lampang they were told **it was not currently possible to climb**. That was the situation in 2023, and CMRCA has since developed it to more than 50 routes — **but "could not climb in 2023" and "can climb in 2026 if you register first" are different claims**, and old trip notes are not the current answer.

### Bolts

theCrag carries no per-route bolt information for Lampang, **so there is no published bolt data for this crag**. Inland crags are classed as "benign" with lower corrosion risk in the same material study ([Crag Chemistry 2024-09](https://cragchemistry.com/2024/09/23/thailand-crags-1/)), but **that does not mean Lampang's bolts have been checked** — it is a brand-new crag. Check the hanger, the nut and the wear on the quickdraws yourself before you clip.

## Routes by level

YDS and V-grade conversions follow the [Mountain Project international grade comparison chart](https://www.mountainproject.com/international-climbing-grades) and are approximate; "approx." marks grades the sources give without a/b/c (such as 4 or 5+). The original grade is the one that counts.

**The table below comes entirely from theCrag's Veteran Wall and Ranger Wall pages, read 21 October 2026** — 21 of the 25 logged routes. The No Name sector's 4 routes have no per-route information.

### Beginner: up to 6a (YDS 5.10a and below)

Lampang's beginner routes cluster on the Veteran Wall, all in the 5s. **The Ranger Wall only goes down to 5c**, so the easiest lines are on the Veteran Wall.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| Mud Dogs | 5b | 5.8 | 18 m | Veteran Wall | Ooan Kongsingh, 2019-05 |
| White Walker | 5c | 5.9 | 22 m | Veteran Wall | Ooan Kongsingh, 2019-06 |
| Summer Strom | 5c | 5.9 | 17 m | Veteran Wall | Ooan Kongsingh, 2019-05 |
| Aventers | 5c | 5.9 | 21 m | Ranger Wall | Ooan Kongsingh, 2019-07 |

### Advanced: 7a to 7c+ (YDS 5.11d–5.13a)

**Lampang has no verifiable routes at 7a or above.** The 25 routes theCrag logs top out at 6c+, and the page's grade filter has no 7a band at all.

theCrag's Nearby Icons list does include **No name 1 (7a)** among the representative routes. **That is a discrepancy between two parts of the same database**: the sector's grade filter has no 7a, yet the icon list carries one route marked 7a. The likely explanation is that it belongs to a different sector (one of the four No Name routes) or that the data has not been synced. **This guide does not list it in any band** — when a band has no verifiable route, the only honest thing to do is say so.

If you want to climb 7a at Lampang, bring *A Guide to Rock Climbing in Northern Thailand* and ask on site. It is not findable online.

### Expert: 8a and up (YDS 5.13b and up)

**Not listed by any source.** The 25 routes theCrag logs top out at 6c+; there is nothing at 8a or above. That is unsurprising for a newly developed crag, but readers should know it: **the ceiling at northern Thailand's newest crag is currently 6c+.**

## Where to look things up

- **Online databases:** theCrag's [Lampang page](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/thailand/area/5727439374) has all 25 routes, the three sectors and the access rules. [The Veteran Wall](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/thailand/area/5727486399) and [The Ranger wall](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/thailand/area/5727446778) have per-route grades, lengths and setters. This is virtually the only online route database for Lampang.
- **First-hand access information:** [CMRCA's Lampang page](https://www.thailandclimbing.com/lampang-climbing-weekends) is the only reliable source for whether you can go, when, for how much, and whether a rapid test is still needed — but the page itself lists neither dates nor prices, so **contact them**. CMRCA also runs a [multipitch guided trip](https://www.thailandclimbing.com/courses/lampang-multi-pitch-guided-experience).
- **Guidebooks:** grades and route counts here come mainly from online databases; the guidebook is the authority. The guide that covers Lampang is **[A Guide to Rock Climbing in Northern Thailand](https://www.thailandclimbing.com/climber-info)** (CMRCA Press, 3rd edition, 220 pages, 500 THB, covering more than 150 pitches across 18 northern sectors). **I could not find its publication year or confirm whether a newer edition exists**, and its contents may lag behind development after 2019. The Pocket Guide and King Climbers are southern Thai books and **do not cover Lampang**.
