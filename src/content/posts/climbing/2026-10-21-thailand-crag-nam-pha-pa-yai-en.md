Nam Pha Pa Yai sits in the hills of Saraburi province about 130 km northeast of Bangkok, with the climbing camp at the foot of the cliff: tents, tree houses and an earthen bungalow, a restaurant and a farm. Not far from the capital, and rarely noticed by international climbers. theCrag logged 101 routes there on 21 October 2026, graded 4a to 8b.

**But the first thing this guide has to say is: the biggest wall at Nam Pha Pa Yai cannot be climbed right now.**

## What kind of crag this is

Four walls, across the river:

| Wall | Routes | Height | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| River Wall | 65 | 23 m | **Closed since January 2024 over access problems** |
| Bat Cave Wall | 17 | 22 m | Climbable |
| Peaceful Wall | 12 | 22 m | Climbable |
| School Wall | 7 | 10 m | Climbable |

theCrag's description of the camp reads: "Two cliffs in one location. One cliff is in the shade all day long, the other one is in the shade at 10 am. Top quality rock."

**The sun here works differently from the other crags in this series.** theCrag tags River Wall as "morning sun", and for Bat Cave Wall and Peaceful Wall it notes "in the shade at around 10 am but all day long for the easy routes under the trees" — in practice, **climb the shaded low-grade lines under the trees at midday rather than baking on the roofs from 11am to 3pm**.

### What the closure actually costs you

The closure covers **that one wall**, not the camp. The camp still operates and still takes bookings. But the damage is real:

- **The best part of those 65 routes is gone.** All ten of theCrag's iconic routes — Mineral Mystic, The Most ... of the World, You are a Legend, Sweet Lip, Jungle's Thai, Babel, Les Chemins de Katmandou, Monty Python's Flying Circus and the rest — are on River Wall. Not one of the 36 routes on the three open walls appears on that list.
- **The whole 8a and 8b band is behind the closure**: Les Chemins de Katmandou 8a, Monty Python's Flying Circus 8b, (R)Evolution Extension 8a, La Roca 8a, plus Forbidden Condom 7c+ and Babel 7c.
- theCrag's warning on the River Wall page reads **"Supposedly closed climbing access a year ago, no signs if it's changed yet"** — a warning filed two years ago. Travel notes also record another site marking it "closed indefinitely".

**So the current state is: the main wall is shut, nothing announces a reopening, and there is no evidence it is open again.** Check theCrag's River Wall page for a new warning before you leave.

## When to go

| Period | Conditions | Planning note |
|---|---|---|
| Nov–Feb | Dry season, most comfortable | The best window. Bangkok runs 30–33°C by day and around 23°C at night from December to February |
| Mar | Getting hot | Climb early and late |
| **Apr–Jun** | **The camp is closed** | Do not plan this stretch. The camp shuts and neither the restaurant nor the lodging is available |
| Jul–Oct | Monsoon | Walls stay wet and the roads outside the camp are hard in the rain |

**The camp closes from April to June** — confirm that before you book, not on arrival.

## Getting there

### Public transport

The official line is that you can arrive by bus, minibus, train or your own vehicle, **but there is no timetable and no schedule to look up in advance.** This is the thinnest transport information of any crag in this series.

**Two routes that actually work in practice:**

1. **Drive or charter** — roughly 2 hours from Bangkok (the camp's own FAQ page explains).
2. **Go with a group.** Climbing operators in Bangkok run fixed trips to the camp, and that is the realistic option if you have no car.

### By car

About 2 hours northeast out of Bangkok. The camp's own directions page (nppy.camp/faq) has the details.

### Parking and the river crossing

**You cross the river by zipline.** theCrag is explicit: the approach is "very fast approach on flat land, then a 100 meters zip line is installed to cross the river. The pulleys are available at the restaurant of the camp. It takes just a few minutes from the camp to the cliff."

Which means:

- **A 100-metre zipline is the only way across the river**, and the pulleys are collected at the restaurant.
- It is about 10 minutes on foot from the camp to the cliff.
- The zipline is a height exposure. If you are bringing kids or are uncomfortable with heights, check yourself on that first.

## Rules to know before you go

### Fees

theCrag marks the camp **Paid**: **100 THB for the zipline and parking, even if you are not staying at the camp.**

It is one of the few Thai crags where the price is written down in public.

### The camp itself

theCrag's description of the camp reads like a hostel attached to a farm:

> The camp offers cheap accommodation in tents or bungalow (tree house and earthen house). Thai and international food is served at the restaurant. It works a bit like the mountain refuge — no menu, but a daily proposal from the chef depending on what is given by the garden of the camp or the local market.

**This is a camp-based crag**, quite unlike Krabi where you go back to your hotel at the end of the day. You can sleep at the foot of the cliff and walk 10 minutes to the routes. That is worth a lot for multipitch or multi-day trips.

### Bolts

theCrag carries no per-route bolt information for Nam Pha Pa Yai, **so there is no published bolt data for this crag**. Central and southern inland crags are classed as "benign" with lower corrosion risk in the same material study ([Crag Chemistry 2024-09](https://cragchemistry.com/2024/09/23/thailand-crags-1/)). **But no data does not mean no problem** — check the gear yourself as always.

### The main wall warning

The access warning on the River Wall page has **not been lifted**. The page was last updated on 14 October 2026 and the warning is still there.

## Is the trip still worth it

**Judged on what you can actually climb now, no — not worth a special journey.**

The reasoning is straightforward: the camp still takes bookings, the 100 THB zipline and parking fee is clear, and the April–June closure is documented. All of that still holds. But **the closure takes 65 of 101 routes away, including every 8a and 8b route and every route theCrag names as iconic.** What remains — 36 routes across Bat Cave Wall, Peaceful Wall and School Wall — has no iconic route of its own and the least documentation.

**Three things would change that answer**, any one of them:

1. **The main wall reopens.** Nam Pha Pa Yai would then be one of the best hard limestone crags near Bangkok, with a density of 8a and 8b routes that is rare anywhere in Thailand.
2. **You are going to stay at the camp anyway.** Camp lodging plus a quiet environment is worth something on its own.
3. **You are going with a group.** A group trip handles transport and the river crossing, and you avoid solving the no-timetable problem.

**Before you go, do two things**: check theCrag's River Wall page for a new warning or a reopening notice, and ask the camp directly (nppy.camp) whether the main wall is open.
