The west side of Railay is the most famous multipitch climbing in Southeast Asia. **theCrag's own summary calls Lord of the Thais (7b, 5 pitches, 120 m) "one of the best in the world"**, and Circus Oz, Continental Drifters and Crystal Flame all sit on the same wall.

**But the thing to read first in this guide is not the routes — it is the descents.** The route notes repeat phrases like "needs two 60-metre ropes", "20 metres more rope to reach the ground" and "tie a knot in the end", and two routes are explicitly written up as **"this route is a mess, don't do it"**.

## What kind of crag this is

Limestone. The west side differs from the east in the sun: **the west walls are shaded in the afternoon** (theCrag's grouping of Tonsai's sectors says the west walls are shaded in the afternoon).

### The famous multipitch

**At Thaiwand Wall:**

- **Lord of the Thais (7b, 5 pitches, 120 m)**: theCrag says it "**will take you as far as you will get in this area**". The first pitch is shared with Circus Oz, up the right side of the big cave.
- **Circus Oz (7a, 4 pitches, 120 m)**: "**Popular and will get you as far of the deck as you will get in this area. Take 14 quickdraws and two 60m ropes. Be aware that the upper pitches are not rebolted with titanium!**" Descend via the anchors of Lord of the Thais, three rappels on two 60-metre ropes, **backclip to stay on the wall**.
- **Continental Drifters (7c+, 4 pitches, 99 m)**: "**Good but not as good as 'Lord of the Thais' and 'The King and I'. Take 14 quickdraws.**" **The first two pitches are not rebolted**; reach P3 via Monkey Love; **from the P3 anchor it is a 60-metre rappel to the ground, which needs two 60-metre ropes**.
- **Inaka (7b, 4 pitches, 120 m)**: theCrag's note is blunt — **"This route is a mess. Don't do it. There is a lot of loose rock and sharpies that can cut your rope. Also no rebolting of the upper pitches since 1996. Take 15 quickdraws and extra threads if you're really keen..."** The first pitch is now Hiroshi's Revenge.
- **The King and I (7b+)** shares anchors with Sanuked.

**The four multipitch routes at Candlestick Wall:**

- **Crystal Flame (6b+, 3 pitches, 70 m)**: theCrag calls it "**the classic line at 'Candlestick Wall'**. All titanium bolts." The third pitch is a chimney with only one sling.
- **The Candlestick (6c, 2 pitches, 70 m)**: **all titanium bolts.** Two details from theCrag are worth memorising: **"On descent, reclip the draws on P2 to avoid being stuck in free space. When abseiling P1, change over at the rap station — 70m rope does not reach the start."**
- **Thai Stick (6c, 2 pitches, 70 m)**: shares the start with The Candlestick then takes the line further right; **all titanium bolts**.
- **The Red Line (6c+, 2 pitches)**: the leftmost line on the red-coated rock; **all titanium bolts**.

**On the Phra Nang side**, the long routes include Giant Carnage and Sticky Monster, though theCrag's per-route detail there is thinner. **Up To You Extended (Hot Dragon) (7b+)** is the hardest Phra Nang line in Mountain Project's classics list.

**The west side is shaded in the afternoon**, which is a real advantage over the east side (where Muay Thai and Diamond Cave are shaded in the morning but packed with schools all day). **But multipitch costs time** — if you start in the afternoon you will be coming down in the dark.

**theCrag's tags for Thaiwand** are: always shady, wet in rain, windy, any tide. Candlestick carries the same four. **"Any tide" means the approaches to these two walls are tide-independent**, unlike Low Tide Wall on the east side.

## Getting there

### Public transport

**There is no road access.** Take a longtail boat from Ao Nang; only the Railay boats run, and they stop at Tonsai first. **Boats only run in daylight.**

### Approaches

**Thaiwand Wall (30–45 minutes, steep hike)** has the most detailed approach description in Railay:

> Walk all the way to the end of Railay beach. In the corner behind the Rayavadee resort, find a trail into the forest, which leads you to 'Wee's Present Wall' after about 50m. Turn right and follow the wall to the end. Follow the trail straight ahead and climb down a small boulder. **Keep following the steep trail for about 2 minutes until you see a fallen tree on the left.** From here, multiple paths end up at the crag. **The easiest way is passing below the tree, then continuing right for about 1 minute until you end up on the leftmost routes of 'Thaiwand Wall'.** After the tree, there is another trail turning left and uphill, which leads to the 'Candlestick Wall'.

**Candlestick Wall (45–60 minutes, climb in)** is harder:

> **Located left above 'Thaiwand Wall'. Challenging approach involving some easy though exposed climbing on fixed ropes.** Walk all the way to the end of Railay beach… **Just before you reach the left side of 'Thaiwand Wall', turn left and follow the trail uphill. Find a fixed rope and climb up about 30m at easy level to the belay anchor.**

**That is one of the approaches to take seriously**: 30 metres of fixed rope, and theCrag specifically calls it "easy though exposed". The descent note for the whole wall reads: **"Rap off. Backclip when lowering to make it back to the starting belay station."**

**The Phra Nang side is the easiest to reach** — straight behind the beach.

## Rules to know before you go

### Descent is the real risk here

**You have to work out the descent configuration before every multipitch route.** From the route notes:

- **Continental Drifters**: from the P3 anchor it is a 60-metre rappel to the ground, **which needs two 60-metre ropes**.
- **Circus Oz**: **three rappels on two 60-metre ropes**, **backclip to stay on the wall**; "on the last bit of the last pitch take care, there is some sharp rock and you're falling into a dihedral".
- **The Candlestick**: **a 70-metre rope does not reach the start**, so you change over at the rappel station; "reclip the draws on P2".
- **Candlestick Wall as a whole**: theCrag tags it "Rap off" and **"Backclip when lowering to make it back to the starting belay station."**
- **The long Phra Nang routes** carry similar rope-length notes.

**This is something a whole multipitch team works out together before leaving, not something you discover on the wall.**

### Bolt condition: different route by route

**Thaiwand Wall (29 routes)**:

- **Primal Scream (6a+)**: "Appears to be a mix of titanium bolts and stainless bolts at the top."
- **Spicy Speck Dreams (7c)**: "**Not rebolted with titanium. All stainless steel glue-in bolts.**"
- **Live and Let Thai (7a)**: "**Not rebolted with titanium.** 6 stainless steel glue-in bolts, 3 slings."
- **Taming the East (7a+)**: "**All stainless steel glue-in bolts.**"
- **Inaka (7b)**: "**This route is a mess. Don't do it.**"
- **Continental Drifters (7c+)**: **the first two pitches are not rebolted**.
- **Circus Oz (7a)**: **the upper pitches are not rebolted with titanium**.
- **Twenty Kilos of Steel (4)**: the access route into the big cave linking Thaiwand Wall and Escher World; 1 titanium bolt + 4 slings.
- **Hiroshi's Revenge (6b+)**: a mix of titanium bolts and slings.

**Candlestick Wall (5 routes)**: **all four multipitch routes are all titanium** (The Red Line, Crystal Flame, The Candlestick, Thai Stick). **This is the best-bolted sector in Railay** — because it has only five routes, all systematically developed around 1999–2000.

**The Phra Nang side**: theCrag's Phra-Nang Beach tags are slab, vertical, always shady, wet in rain. Bolt condition sits in the per-route descriptions.

**Three sectors are effectively closed wholesale:**

- **Universe Wall (5 routes)**: Slow-Pulsed Boy 7a+, Apocolysco 7b, Universe City 7b, Crucial Taunt 7b+ and The Shining Path 8a **all say "old rusty expansion bolts, do not climb unless rebolted"**. This is the cleanest whole-sector closure in Railay.
- **Happy Island (13 routes)**: **10 are un-climbable**. Don't Worry, Be Happy 6a ("not rebolted with titanium, 6 old slings, 1 stainless steel glue-in bolt"), Happy Thailand 6b+, Jungle Boogie 6b+, Waiting for Donkey Kong 6c, Razorback 6c+ (second pitch expansion bolts), Pee Mai 6c+, TV Route 7a, Coral Soul 7a+, Galette S'Abstenir 7a+, Land of Smiles 8a (**only the first pitch was rebolted with titanium**) and Violent Brain Fever 8a+ are all flagged. Only King Fisher 7a+ carries no warning. **This sector is effectively shut.**
- **The Defile (13 routes)**: Crystal Prison 7c and Allo, la Terre? 7b say "old rusty expansion bolts, do not climb"; Jungle Fever 5 is an "old route, not listed in the guidebooks" and is marked Closed; Khao Pat Gai 7a+ was rebolted with titanium but has **bad bolt placement and an unprotected crux**.

**Wee's Present Wall (20 routes)** is the way in to Thaiwand, and its condition is polarised: Hello Christine 6a+, Low Season 6c, The Marlin 6c+, Tres Equis 7b+ and Tarte Au Poil 7c+ are all titanium, but Klong Mot Gan 5b, John's White Shirt 6b, On the Edge 6c and Som Tam 7b all say "old slings and expansion bolts, do not climb unless rebolted", and the two 6as Roi-Et and I Don't Know are flagged as sharp rock on all slings.

### The approach itself is a risk

- **The fixed rope to Candlestick Wall** is "easy though exposed".
- **Twenty Kilos of Steel**, the approach into the big cave, has 1 titanium bolt + 4 slings.
- **theCrag tags Thaiwand's approach "Steep hike" and "30-45 min".**

| Humming Song | 6b | 5.10c | 15 m | Escher World | ★★; up the corner to the anchor right of the tree |

### Expert: 8a and up (YDS 5.13b and up)

**Railay West has exactly one route above 8a, and you cannot climb it.**

| Route | Grade | YDS | Length / pitches | Sector | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Inaka | 7b | 5.12b | 120 m, 4 pitches | Thaiwand Wall | ★; **theCrag writes "this route is a mess. Don't do it"**; no rebolting of the upper pitches since 1996 |
| Gandalf | 8a+ | 5.13c | 28 m | One-Two-Three | ★; **old rusty expansion bolts, do not climb** (covered in the [east side guide](/posts/climbing/2026-10-21-thailand-crag-railay-east)) |
| Mekong Haze | 8a | 5.13b | 9 m | Phra-Nang Beach | ★★; just right of Don't Buy Toys; a bit of a squeeze but looks great; all titanium bolts |
| Hot Dragon | 8b | 5.13d | 25 m | Phra-Nang Beach | ★; **Closed**; the original line was recycled into Half Dragon, **the old bolts have been chopped** |
| Fever Dream | 8a+ | 5.13c | 14 m | Escher World | ★; shares the start and anchors with The Beauty of Danger; **old rusty expansion bolts, do not climb** |
| The Beauty of Danger | 7c+ | 5.13a | 14 m | Escher World | ★★★; **old rusty expansion bolts, do not climb** |

**The west side's real ceiling is 7c+ (Continental Drifters).** The 8a+ space is taken by two routes that cannot be climbed: **Inaka because theCrag calls it a mess, Gandalf because of old expansion bolts.**

## Where to look things up

- **Online databases:** theCrag's [Thaiwand Wall](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/thailand/krabi/area/15687721) has 29 routes with per-route multipitch lengths, rope requirements, bolt condition and descent notes; [Candlestick Wall](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/thailand/krabi/area/15687793) has five routes with full pitch breakdowns; [Phra-Nang Beach](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/thailand/krabi/area/15687765) has the Phra Nang routes. **The "Descent notes" fields on those three pages are the heart of this guide.**
- **Guidebooks:** grades and route counts here come mainly from online databases; the guidebook is the authority. **For pitch counts, anchor positions and descent configuration, paper is better than online.** Two cover Railay: **[The Pocket Guide – Thailand](https://justclimbthailand.com/krabi-climbing-guidebook)** (10th edition, 2026-01, 238 pages, 1,000 THB, 800-plus routes) and **[King Climbers](https://railay.com/railay/climbing/climbing_book.shtml)** (10th edition, 2025, 85 areas and more than 1,000 routes).
- **The actual rebolting list:** **not findable online.** It is in the guidebooks and on printed lists in the King Climbers shop in Railay.
- **Multipitch guiding:** [justclimbthailand's course page](https://justclimbthailand.com/climbing-course-thailand/) lists guided multipitch sessions such as Crystal Flame (5,500 THB). **Walking a guide up one of these lines is the cheapest way to learn the descents.**
