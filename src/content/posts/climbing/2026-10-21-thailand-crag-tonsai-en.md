Tonsai is the island on the east side of the Phra-Nang peninsula; Railay is on the west, separated by a narrow channel. Tonsai is one of the most famous climbing spots in Southeast Asia: theCrag logged **448 routes** on 21 October 2026, with 25,344 ascents and 168 photos. **Greed (8c+) is flagged on theCrag as "currently the hardest ascent in Thailand".**

The core of this guide is not how famous the place is — it is that **bolt condition here is the least consistent in Thailand**: one route all titanium, three metres away one with rusty expansion bolts, and next to that one a description that simply says "Do not climb". The theCrag Ton Sai page also carries a warning: **"This area is actively being rebolted at time of writing (2023) and much of the info on this website is outdated."**

## What kind of crag this is

Limestone. theCrag's description: "Ton Sai has hundreds of routes to offer, from 5c to 8c. Expect three-dimensional limestone with huge jugs, tiny pockets, slopers, stalactites, tufas, caves."

The island's walls fall into three groups, and theCrag divides them by sun: **most walls on the beach side are in shade until noon, the west walls are shaded in the afternoon, and the east group is not specially marked.**

**Tonsai has 24 sectors, but the two biggest are the main wall (64 routes) and the bouldering field (71).** You do not need to research all of them on a first trip — the beach-side Ton Sai Wall & Roof, Dum's Kitchen and Fire Wall alone give you 120 routes.

### Multipitch is the signature here

Tonsai has the highest density of multipitch routes in Southeast Asia:

- **Humanity (6b+, 110 m, 7 pitches)**: theCrag says it "could be 5, 6 or 7 pitches, depending how you see the climb", with 45 bolts — the longest single line in the area. **But theCrag warns on it: "Dangerous and never climbed"** — dangerous and nobody has climbed it, because pitch 7 is a traverse on old slings.
- **Monkey See, Monkey Do (6b+, 85 m, 4 pitches)**: "Could be the best multi pitch at the grade on the peninsular. Great climbing, especially on the upper pitches."
- **Pavel, Wish You Were Here (7b, 95 m, 4 pitches)**: the first pitch is Schwer und Steil, all titanium.
- **Year of the Tiger (8a+, 100 m, 3 pitches)**: all titanium, "a classic multi pitch".
- **Peyote and Musang (both 7a, 120 m, 5 pitches)**: the two long multipitches on Cat Wall. **Rappelling Musang is difficult — theCrag says take 15 quickdraws.**
- **Eviction (8a, 52 m, 2 pitches)**: all titanium.
- **The Monitor (7a, 88 m, 4 pitches)**: the first pitch reaches a big cave with multiple windows, and **every multipitch on Sleeping Indian Wall starts from there.**

## When to go

| Months | Conditions | Planning note |
|---|---|---|
| Nov | End of the wet season | Fewer people, but walls may still be wet |
| Dec–Feb | Dry and cool, best friction | **Peak season.** theCrag is explicit that from November to March the easier routes "are usually pretty busy, especially during high season, and super polished" |
| Mar | Getting hot | Still the tail of peak season |
| Apr–May | Hot, start of the wet season | Cheaper rooms but wet rock |
| Jun–Oct | Monsoon | Walls stay wet. The Fire Wall page is specific: "When the wind comes from the seaside (from southeast), the wall gets wet when raining. When there is no wind, or the wind comes from northwest the wall stays mostly dry." |

**Peak-season crowding is a structural feature of this crag.** theCrag's overall Ton Sai description says: "The easier routes are usually pretty busy, especially during high season (November to March), and super polished, but still worth jumping on." Travel notes add that **peak season runs from mid-December to the start of March, and January and February have sold out.**

**The Fire Wall's rain note is worth remembering.** Whether that wall stays dry depends on wind direction: a southeast sea breeze plus rain means wet, no wind or a northwest wind means mostly dry. **It is the only sector where theCrag says anything about climbing in the rain.**

## Getting there

### Public transport

**There is no road access to Tonsai.** theCrag's access note reads: "No road access. From Ao Nang hire a longtail boat to take you around to Ton Sai. Boats only operate during the day."

Concretely: take the roughly 1-hour shuttle from Krabi airport to Ao Nang, then a longtail boat. **Boats heading to Railay also stop at Tonsai.**

### By car or motorbike

Most people do not stay on the island — they take a boat over from Ao Nang each day. Motorbikes can be rented in Ao Nang, but **you can only use one if you get the boat across**.

### Parking

**There is no car park on Tonsai.** Vehicles stay on the Ao Nang side.

### Lodging

theCrag puts it plainly: **"There are many different accommodations in Ton Sai and Railay, from cheap bamboo huts (no air cons, not very clean, not too safe and plenty of mosquitoes) to luxury resorts with air con and swimming pools. Camping is not allowed."**

Current situation, checked October 2026:

- **Open**: Tonsai Bay Resort, Basecamp, Garden View, Dream Valley, Green Valley, Chill Out, Pasook, Jungle Hut, Countryside.
- **Permanently closed**: Andaman, Banyan Tree, Thiew Khao, Saithong.
- **Basecamp runs 700 to 950 THB per night** (maximum 2 people), direct booking only (info@basecamptonsai.com), **with a 100 EUR deposit and a five-night minimum**.
- **There is no ATM on Tonsai** — bring cash from Railay.
- Peak season runs from mid-December to the start of March.

## Rules to know before you go

### Bolts: the big trap here

**There is no public official list of what has been replaced.** The Thaitanium Project that drove the rebolting has taken its site down, the old domain is now a gambling spam page, and the crowdfunding page says only that "around two thirds of the routes have been rebolted". Actual rebolting is done by local climbing shops, and the lists live in the two guidebooks and on printed lists in the shops.

theCrag's per-route descriptions are full of "all titanium bolts", **but those are observations reported by climbers, not an official list.** This series always says "theCrag records this as all titanium" for that reason.

**What actually matters are the routes flagged as un-climbable.** theCrag marks several on Tonsai, and these are current status, not stale information:

- **Beauty and the Beast (6c, Ton Sai Wall & Roof)**: marked **Closed** — "The route has been closed and the crux bolts removed".
- **Aunt Mary Johanna & Two Girls (6b+, Dum's Kitchen)**: **Closed** — "Old bolts and slings, overgrown. Cannot be climbed anymore."
- **Hot Springs (8c, Dum's Kitchen)**: theCrag gives it 0 stars — "Broken hold, needs repairs and rebolting. Protected by expansion bolts."
- **Polish-Czech (6b, Fire Wall)**: "2 stainless steel glue-ins, 7 slings, which might need replacement."
- **I No Speak England (6b, Ton Sai Wall & Roof)**: "5 stainless steel glue-in bolts, 6 slings. Stick-clip the 2nd bolt."
- **Whipper Snapper (6b+, Sleeping Indian Wall)**: "Old rusty expansion bolts. Do not climb!"
- **Shaft of Light (6c+, Sleeping Indian Wall)**: "Old rusty expansion bolts. Do not climb!"
- **Little Fish P2 (6b+, The Nest)**, **Trophy Hunter**, **Swingtown**, **Wild Thing**: old rusty expansion bolts or stainless glue-ins.
- **Flame Thrower (7b+, Fire Wall)**: "Old rusty expansion bolts. Cannot be climbed anymore."
- **Burning down the House (7a, Fire Wall)**: "Not rebolted. Old stainless steel glue-ins. Do not climb!"
- **The Ninth Life (7a+, Sleeping Indian Wall)**: marked **Closed** — "Due to fire in the past, the rock is very unstable here and stones pop off and fall down every other day."
- **Bird's Nest Drop (7c, Ton Sai Wall & Roof)**: "Old stainless steel glue-in bolts. Do not climb!"
- **Easy Approach (5, Fire Wall)**: "old slings".
- **Lost Cave, Drunky, Masala Ton Sai, Visa Run, Caroline's Last Day P2, Whipper Snapper, Frog's Hair, Project**: all marked un-climbable.
- **Generator Wall (all 6 routes)**: thetopo says "not yet rebolted, strongly advise against climbing". theCrag's per-route notes are more specific: **5 of the 6 say "old slings, be prepared to replace"** (The Ancient Lighthouse 6b+, The Bolt from Hell 6c+, Truls 8b+, The Way of the Emperor 7b, Pearl Jam 7b); only Rolling Thunder (7c+) is recorded as all titanium.
- **Shadow Wall (12 routes) and Iguana Park (6 routes)**: theCrag marks both whole sectors Closed. **All twelve Shadow Wall routes carry "old slings, do not climb"** (Little View Point 4, Panique a Bord 4, Mai Technology 5, Fistrouille Poupouillie 4, La Mano Negra 4, Bouts D'Nouilie 6a, Domingo 6b, Moulin D'Brainant 5, Fakir II the Return 5, Bloody Route 5, Mafiosito Style 5, Pico Pico 5). Iguana Park's six are likewise marked Closed throughout.
- **Resort Cave (6 routes)**: Sysiphus (8a) and Sissyfuss (8b) are the two hardest routes on the island, recorded as titanium bolts and slings sharing the same start and anchors; **the other four (Copy Wrong! 6a, Brösel 6b, Copyright! 6b, Hin Yong Lun) all say "old rusty expansion bolts and slings, do not climb"**.
- **Eagle Wall's Mai Rhu Sheu! (6a)**: "loose rock, old rusty expansion bolts, do not climb". Totem Pole (6a+) in the same sector is closed — "climbed the big column in front of the cave. Bolts have been chopped, because it was not possible to re…"
- **Big Wave Pillar's Short Wave (6a+)** is closed: "old route left of 'Big Wave' P2. Old expansion bolts seem to have been removed…"
- **Two Monkey World multipitch routes carry liana warnings**: Chunky Monkey (6b) — "lianas blocking access to belay ledge" — and Curious George (6b) — "covered with lianas". Holy Wall (7c, the second pitch of Monkey Bizness) says "old expansion bolts, do not climb".

**Three more things worth knowing:**

1. **The Pinnacle, the main wall of the 123 Wall, has been fully rebolted** — but the three old routes on the left, the four extensions above Kratoy, Gandalf and the third pitch of Massage Secrets have not.
2. **Judge the slings yourself.** Many routes rely on slings as well as bolts, and theCrag says "All slings. Be prepared to replace." in several places.
3. **The material research says titanium genuinely works** ([Crag Chemistry 2025-09](https://cragchemistry.com/2025/09/02/thailand-crags-2/): titanium bolts have been in local use for over 20 years with very few recorded failures, and the UIAA's ten-year testing found 304 stainless "in bad shape", 316 with surface corrosion and titanium "like new"). **But that does not mean this wall has been rebolted.**

### Other people's routes are your risk too

Several descriptions warn about shared anchors and fixed ropes:

- **Snake Skin, Cobra Head and Snake Whisky on Cobra Wall** all say to use the fixed rope for the belayer.
- **Snakeskin (6c, Sleeping Indian Wall)**: "Access by climbing the first pitch of 'The Monitor'."
- **Snake, Ganja & Rastaman (6b+, Sleeping Indian Wall)**: "Re-established after it had been recycled into 'The Monitor'."
- **Hin Rong Hai (6c, Ton Sai Wall & Roof)**: "Do not skip the crux by going right towards the anchor — only 6b then and risk of breaking the fragile tufas."

### Wildlife and rock

- **Baby Snake (6c, Cobra Wall)**: "Hornets Nest. Hard start through the overhang. **Careful, potentially dangerous lead fall!**"
- **By Way of Deception (7a, Dum's Kitchen)** and **Spanish Wreck (7c+, Ton Sai Wall & Roof)**: both flagged for wasps and hornets.
- **For You & For Me (6b, Ton Sai Wall & Roof)**: "anchors not equipped with rings. Do not lower through."
- **Road to Damascus (7b+, Cat Wall)**: "lowering from the anchor requires an extra 20m to reach the ground".
- **Schlingal Moritz (6a, Dum's Kitchen)**: "The rope sheath at the top anchor was damaged".

## Where to look things up

- **Online databases:** theCrag's [Ton Sai page](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/thailand/krabi/area/3097160649) has all 448 routes, the spread across 24 sectors, the approach and the accommodation note. Each sector page carries per-route grades, stars, lengths, bolt material and "do not climb" flags. **Every figure in this guide comes from there.**
- **Guidebooks:** grades and route counts here come mainly from online databases; the guidebook is the authority. Two cover Tonsai: **[The Pocket Guide – Thailand](https://justclimbthailand.com/krabi-climbing-guidebook)** (Sirichai Pongsopon, 10th edition, fully updated January 2026, 238 pages, 1,000 THB) and **[King Climbers – Rock Climbing Thailand Guide](https://railay.com/railay/climbing/climbing_book.shtml)** (Somporn Suebhait, 10th edition, 2025, ISBN 979-8306073606). **theCrag rates The Pocket Guide as the most accurate of the four southern Thai guides**, and its author is a local climber who rebolts routes himself, so its bolt markings are the likeliest to be current. **[Rock Climbing in Thailand and Laos](https://www.cordee.co.uk/rock-climbing-in-thailand-and-laos-cca542.html)** (9th edition 2018 plus a 2025 update) is older overall but does clearly mark each route's bolt condition.
- **The actual rebolting list:** **not findable online.** It is in the two guidebooks and on printed lists in the shops — the King Climbers shop in Railay and Watts bar in Tonsai. **Asking in the shop beats guessing from a website.**
- **Rental and guiding:** [justclimbthailand's rental page](https://justclimbthailand.com/climbing-equipment-sale-and-rental/) lists full-day rental (rope 500 THB, 12 quickdraws 400 THB), and the [course page](https://justclimbthailand.com/climbing-course-thailand/) has private guiding at 6,000 THB per day, multipitch guiding for Humanity 6,000 and Crystal Flame 5,500 THB.
