Hữu Lũng is a valley, not a wall. The cliffs are scattered through the orchards and rice fields within five kilometers of Yên Thịnh village, and each one has its own landowner, its own access fee, its own sun. This guide covers the nine sectors of the main valley: Head, Chúc Sức Khỏe, Squirrel, L'Ile Noire, La Conche, Dragon, Drone, Dead End, K Wall. The east side of the same valley and the newer walls are in [the next article](/posts/climbing/2026-10-21-vietnam-crag-huu-lung-west-en).

This guide helps you decide three things: which homestay to stay in, which wall to start on the first day, and how much cash and how many quickdraws to bring.

## What kind of crag

**Hữu Lũng / Yên Thịnh Valley** sits about 100 km northeast of Hanoi, a limestone karst valley. theCrag's crag description reads: "A remote karst valley at the foot of the northeastern range, 100 km from Hanoi, in rural countryside and traditional settlements."

- **Rock and style:** vertical faces, roofs, slabs and tufa are all there. The crag description says that "quality sport routes are spread over 17 walls within a 5 km radius, with representative grades running from a solid 6b to 7c", and it describes "a variety of limestone climbing techniques, including tufa, huecos and slopers, on vertical and steep walls".
- **Scale:** the whole of Hữu Lũng is 267 routes across 21 sectors on theCrag (October 2026). The nine sectors in this guide add up to 147 routes. The paper guidebook covering the same routes lists only 122 of them, and Vertical-Life lists 231 — three numbers, three registration scopes, not three different truths.
- **Bolts:** the whole area uses 316 stainless steel. The same source says the grades are "roughly accurate — this is generally not a place where grades are softened for holiday climbers", so if you expect a 6b, it is a 6b.
- **Who it suits:** people who want to walk out the door every day and climb two or three short routes, and who would rather not deal with ferries and boats. A typical full trip is 4 to 6 nights, one wall a day.

**What is unusual here is that the routes are in an orchard.** Most of the cliffs sit inside private orchards, and the landowners charge an access fee; the fruit trees are custard apple, so watch where you step and do not crush them. theCrag puts it plainly: "Most landowners will probably charge a fee, usually 20,000 to 25,000 VND. Bring exact change."

## When to go

Hữu Lũng has no weather station of its own, so use Hanoi's numbers as a reference (the same northeastern lowland, 100 km away).

| Month | Average high / low | Rainfall | Rain days | Sunshine hours | How to plan |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| December | 21.3 / 13.8 °C | 19.5 mm | 5.1 days | 124 hours | The driest, sunniest window, and the best friction |
| January | 20.2 / 14.1 °C | 19.8 mm | 10.3 days | 74 hours | Just as little rain, but twice as many rainy days |
| February | 21.7 / 16.4 °C | 20.2 mm | 12.4 days | 45 hours | Not much rain, but the rock stays wet; noticeably less sun |
| March | 25.9 / 20.5 °C | 35.8 mm | 15.9 days | 47 hours | It starts to heat up, and this is the month with the most rainy days |

**The real seasonal variable is sun, not rain.** Rainfall from December to February is low enough to ignore, but sunshine drops from 124 hours to 45, and under the roofs it stays cool and shaded all day, which makes for cold hands while you are belaying. If you want sun on a cold day, look at the aspect of the wall: Dragon Wall faces northwest, so it is in shade all morning until after midday and only gets sun in the afternoon; Drone Wall is "shaded in the afternoon, with shade starting on the right-hand side after 11 am", which complements Dragon Wall across the road; L'Ile Noire is tagged "sun in the morning", and it sits in the rice fields in the middle of the village, the most direct place to get sun on a cold day.

**A rainy day gives you fewer choices, not more.** Only two places in the whole of Hữu Lũng are explicitly tagged "dry in the rain": Dragon Wall (the roof) and Infinity Cave (the big cave). The other sectors — Head Wall, La Conche, L'Ile Noire and Squirrel Wall among them — carry no rain tag at all, which is the same as having no data, so do not treat them as rain-sheltered. What you should really avoid is the approach: for Dead End and The Arch, the dirt road and the clay path are more dangerous after rain than the rock itself.

**December to February is the quietest period**, which is good news if you want to climb without an audience; the cost is short days and cold evenings.

## Getting there

### Public transport

The cheapest and easiest option is the shuttle that VietClimb arranges with the homestay:

- **Every day at 09:00 and 16:00**, departing from **490 Minh Khai** in Hanoi, about 3 hours, dropping you at the door of the homestay, 250,000 VND one way.
- **The driver does not speak English**, so use Zalo **0084 813071992** to confirm the meeting point and the time. Departures change, so check with your accommodation or the driver before you leave.
- The other option is a service leaving **Gia Lam bus station at midday**, 160,000 VND, also about 3 hours, but it only reaches the market, which is still 2.5 km from the homestay (driver phone 0965060090).

theCrag's own bus route goes like this: get to Gia Lam station, take a bus to Lạng Sơn, get off at Yên Vuong and take a taxi into Yên Thịnh village — "this requires the ability to communicate in Vietnamese, because nobody speaks English". This is the cheapest way in, and it has the least room for error.

### Driving

- **Private car with driver:** a four-seater for 1,500,000 VND, about 2 hours, booked directly through VietClimb. Both homestays ([Mao Homestay](https://vietclimb.vn/homestays/mao-homestay), [Duc Bungalow](https://vietclimb.vn/homestays/duc-bungalow)) can help arrange it.
- **Motorbike:** about 2.5 hours if you rent one in Hanoi, which is theCrag's "standard mode of transport to the crag". The same passage spells out the practical detail: "it is easy to arrange on arrival at the homestay, usually assuming one bike for two people plus luggage; you may not get an automatic, so be ready to ride a manual". **There are no gas stations nearby**; local shops sell bottled petrol.
- **There is no scheduled direct bus between Hữu Lũng and Cat Ba Island**, and a car with driver is 2,580,000 VND.

### Parking

Parking is the least consistent thing about this place, and every wall does it differently. Here is what the theCrag sector pages say:

| Sector | Where to park | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Head Wall | Park at **Hữu Lũng Mountain Climbing Hostel** (21.604243,106.351115), walk 150 m north along the main road, go through the orchard gate, 5 minutes on foot | No parking fee (the hostel's owner is the owner of Head Wall), and guests are exempt from the access fee |
| Dragon Wall | Park under the trees near the wooden shelter on the west side of the cliff (21.613292,106.322973); the main approach goes from the corner at (21.614471,106.323475) over a low wall | Access fee 25,000 VND per person per day, collected by the landowner at the crag |
| Drone Wall | Drive past the building on the right, turn left through the gate and follow the narrow path, 30 seconds on foot | Access fee 20,000 VND per person, bring exact change |
| Dead End | Same directions as Dragon Wall: at the fork before the bridge turn left and follow the dirt road for about 2 km, through the iron gate to the second wooden gate; you can park at the wooden gate (21.608376,106.312565) or right in front of the cliff | Access fee 20,000 VND per person per day, collected by the farmer |
| La Conche | Park by the roadside (21.623785,106.347870), 200 m on foot | The landowner charges the fee |
| L'Ile Noire | 3 km from Mao Homestay; from Làng village west to Diễn village, a few hundred meters past the market on the left | Access fee 15,000 VND per day |
| Squirrel Wall, K Wall | The source lists no access or parking information | The landowner charges the fee |

Dead End has one practical note: if you park at the wooden gate, the farmer may move your car in front of the cliff, so **leave the steering lock off**, or he will ask you for the key.

## Rules before you go

**The access fee is cash, and it has to be exact.** That is the most practical rule at Hữu Lũng. The landowners mostly collect cash on the spot, the nearest ATM in the valley is a 40-minute drive, and "the road there is busy and riding a bike on it is risky". Passe-Muraille is free, and the rates for the other sectors are whatever the landowner charges on the day.

**Do not damage the fruit trees.** The cliffs are in an orchard, and the custard apple trees were planted by the landowner. theCrag warns specifically: "take care of the crops, especially the custard apple trees".

**The owner of L'Ile Noire is a family.** The sector page paints a vivid picture: you may meet a young woman landowner who is very talkative and "may be a bit unstable mentally"; she and her family are the owners; and once you have paid the access fee, nothing like "your climbing shoes getting thrown down the hillside" will happen.

**Check the bolts yourself.** The fixed-point warnings in the main valley are concentrated in a few places: on Head Wall, Sắc! is tagged "some bolts look rusty"; Squirrel Wall is tagged "corrosion of the anchor rings"; L'Ile Noire is tagged "the 7th bolt has a loose nut" and "the first bolt has a loose nut"; Dead End is tagged "the 4th bolt is loose"; Dragon Wall is tagged "missing fixed point"; Drone Wall is tagged "the bolt on the left anchor is loose". These are climber reports, with no dates and no evidence of a full re-bolt. **Bring a wrench and look at the hangers and nuts before you get on the wall.**

**The further the wall, the slower the rescue.** Head Wall is at the edge of the village; Dead End is 2 km down a dirt road. There is no ambulance in the valley, and a serious injury means a trip all the way to a hospital in Lạng Sơn.

**Emergency numbers**: police 113, fire 114, ambulance 115; the national search and rescue center is 020 7008 5000 (24 hours, but in Vietnamese only). Before you leave, save the crag name, the Vietnamese place names and an offline map location. Check the insurance line by line for outdoor lead climbing, search and rescue, medical treatment and evacuation.

**Wet stalactites break.** Mountain Project's warning is simple: do not climb when it is wet.

### The only multipitch

**Chúc Sức Khỏe** is the only multipitch route in the whole of Hữu Lũng: 7a+, 96 m, 5 pitches, with 3 stars on theCrag. The approach is written up as "entered via Head Wall", so in practice you walk over from Head Wall. It is the route where a 60 m rope is not enough — do the rope-length math yourself, down to the descent from the last pitch, instead of bringing a short rope because the maximum is 96 m.
