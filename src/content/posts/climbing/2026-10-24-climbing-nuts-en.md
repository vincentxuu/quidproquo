> 🌏 [中文版](/posts/climbing/2026-10-24-climbing-nuts)

A nut (Black Diamond calls it a stopper) is a wedge of metal on a steel cable that you slot into a narrowing in a crack. It has no moving parts and it's light and cheap: [UKClimbing's video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Tadx1NCOScU) points out that a full set of 11 costs about the same as one cam. That's why many climbers start their first trad rack with a set of nuts.

This post answers one question: which set to buy. For how nuts hold, the three conditions of a good placement and how to use a nut tool to remove one, see [Placing Trad Protection](/posts/climbing/2026-10-17-trad-protection-nuts-cams-en); we won't repeat that here. By the end you should be able to decide whether your first set is curved or offset and which sizes to get, and pick a set and a nut tool from the tables below.

## Four criteria

### 1. Shape: curved, offset, straight taper

[OutdoorGearLab's nut roundup](https://www.outdoorgearlab.com/topics/climbing/best-climbing-nut) (Ian Nicholson, updated 2024) sorts today's nuts into three kinds:

- **Curved**: one face concave, the other convex. In a crack it makes three points of contact, so it sits more securely and is easy to remove. BD Stoppers, DMM Wallnuts and Wild Country Rocks are all curved, and it's still the most common shape.
- **Offset**: thicker on one side in both directions, made for flaring cracks and pin scars. OGL says it's becoming the most popular shape because small cams have improved: people place cams in parallel cracks and save nuts for spots cams won't fit. The downsides are weaker performance in parallel cracks and harder removal.
- **Straight taper**: OGL says these are almost obsolete. They're the easiest to judge and to remove, but less secure in flaring or parallel cracks and on coarse, crystalline rock.

Buy curved first. UKClimbing lists a set of nuts among the first things to buy and leaves offsets for once you're climbing a wider variety of cracks. [Black Diamond's first-trad-rack guide](https://blackdiamondequipment.com/blogs/stories/how-to-build-your-first-trad-rack) also says a first rack should definitely include a set of Stoppers.

Some people go the other way: Brant Hysell of The Gravity Lab buys DMM offsets straight away for granite in the US and has barely used regular nuts (video in "Test and experience videos" below). That's granite experience; for Long Dong, see criterion 4.

**Something you can do tonight**: pick up a nut and look at it from the side, then turn it 90 degrees and look from the front. If it tapers in one direction only, with one face concave and one convex, it's curved. If it's thicker on one side in both directions, it's an offset.

### 2. Which sizes first

Advice splits into two camps, depending on whether you'll lean on nuts or on cams.

- **Cams first, nuts fill the mid sizes**: the gear list for [Adventure Taiwan's single-pitch trad course](https://www.adventuretaiwan.com/product/single-pitch-trad-climbing/) is BD Stoppers #5 to #11. The guide from [Valhalla Mountain Guides](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=68S7R_3lfPI) says the big nuts in a standard BD set are heavy and rarely used, so he carries only #5–#11.
- **Nuts first**: UKClimbing says to buy a set of 1–11 first. Almost every route in the UK needs at least one set, and longer routes need two.

Long Dong sits in between. [Mountain Project's Long Dong page](https://www.mountainproject.com/area/106594934/long-dong-dragon-cave) says most routes need a single rack of cams to 3 inches, and anyone wanting to climb everything there should add a set of nuts, including offsets and micros.

Size numbers don't carry across brands, and sometimes not even within one brand. In UKClimbing's video, DMM Alloy Offsets #7–11 correspond to Wallnuts #4–8. Compare the millimetre ranges in the manufacturer's tables instead.

**Something you can do tonight**: copy down the millimetre range of every size in the set you're considering, and next to it write the range of your smallest cam. Mark where they overlap and where there's a gap.

### 3. Small sizes: read the strength table before you buy

The smaller the nut, the thinner the cable, and strength drops fast. The standard only requires 2 kN; the real number is in the manufacturer's table and stamped on the nut (see [Placing Trad Protection](/posts/climbing/2026-10-17-trad-protection-nuts-cams-en) on reading the standard).

- **Within one set**: [BD Stoppers](https://blackdiamondequipment.com/products/stoppers) #1–2 are rated only 2 kN, and BD says they're for direct aid only, not fall arrest; it takes #6 and up to reach 10 kN.
- **Same shape, different brands**: among #7–11 offsets, DMM rates its nuts at 12 kN, BD at 10 kN, and [Trango Offset Nuts](https://trango.com/products/offset-nuts) at only 7 kN.
- **When sources disagree**: two retailers list different strengths for the smallest CAMP Pro Nut, and we couldn't find a manufacturer page to check. In that case, go by the number stamped on the nut.

**Something you can do tonight**: write the kN of every size in the set you're considering on one line, and circle anything below 10 kN. Those are the pieces where you have to be especially picky about placement.

### 4. At Long Dong: crystal-studded, irregular cracks

Long Dong's rock is described two ways: Taiwanese sources say 四稜 sandstone, Mountain Project says quartz-rich metaconglomerate, and both call it coarse and high-friction (see [How to Climb Cracks](/posts/climbing/2026-10-18-crack-climbing-technique-en)).

The most specific Taiwanese account of what this means for nuts is TOCC's [How to build your first trad rack](https://www.tocc-climbing.org/post/%E5%A6%82%E4%BD%95%E6%89%93%E9%80%A0%E7%AC%AC%E4%B8%80%E5%A5%97%E5%82%B3%E6%94%80%E8%A3%9D%E5%82%99) (in Chinese; Chen Zhen-yu, 2024):

- BD, DMM and Wild Country are the three most common brands in Taiwan.
- BD Stoppers are the cheapest, but Long Dong's cracks are irregular and covered in crystals. The Stopper's flat faces give a bit too little contact and friction, and the rope often tugs them out of place as the leader climbs, sometimes all the way out.
- DMM Wallnuts and Alloy Offsets have grooves that can find a matching feature or crystal in Long Dong's cracks. TOCC calls these two "arguably the best-performing products on the market".

That's the experience of one Taiwanese article. OGL's US roundup also says Stoppers tend to shift when the rope pulls on them, which points the same way.

**Something you can do tonight**: read the TOCC article. It also covers hexes and Tricams at Long Dong, which this post leaves out.

## Nut tool: hard, rustproof, with an end you can hit

You need one to clean gear as a second, and Adventure Taiwan's course list includes one. Brant Hysell says a pair of climbers only needs one, carried by the second; besides prying out nuts, it can hook a cam's trigger or work a stuck lobe loose ([video from 16:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6A_TPLt-uBQ&t=988s)).

TOCC's criteria are practical: pick a thick, hard stainless-steel tool that won't rust, ideally with a flat end you can strike with your palm. We found no nut tool roundup from 2024 onward, so for the two tools below we list only what the manufacturers say:

### Before buying second-hand: the Wild Country Superlight Rock recall

In 2023 Wild Country recalled the **Superlight Rock** (its lightweight single-cable nut, model 40-RSL; set 40-RSLSET). [The US CPSC recall notice](https://cpsc.gov/Recalls/2023/Salewa-USA-Recalls-Wild-Country-Superlight-Rocks-Cable-Wire-Chocks-Used-for-Climbing-Due-to-Fall-Hazard) gives the reason: in seaside conditions the cable can corrode, weaken and break. Long Dong is right on the sea.

[The manufacturer's safety notice](https://theuiaa.org/documents/safety/WC2023_EUROPEAN%20Safety%20Notice_EN.pdf) recalls every Superlight Rock produced before the end of 2022 (in the US, units sold from January 2017 to February 2023). The corrosion happens inside the swage, where you can't see it. The regular Rock and the Superlight Offset are not part of the recall.

The product name and batch code are printed on the swage. Fixed new production has batch codes starting with 06 or higher and a UKCA logo. If a friend or a second-hand group is selling Superlight Rocks, check the batch code on the swage against that notice, hosted by the UIAA.

## Which to pick, by situation

### Taking a course or seconding at Long Dong: build your first set

- A BD Stopper #5–11 set plus a nut tool. The sizes match Adventure Taiwan's course list.
- If you know you'll climb at Long Dong a lot, go straight to DMM Wallnuts; TOCC says they hold better there. TOCC's starter rack for a limited budget is also 7 cams (BD Z4 #.2, C4 #.3–#2) plus a set of Wallnuts #1–11, for 18 placements.
- When buying in Taiwan, read the product name carefully. Shops sometimes label cams as 岩楔 (nuts) too; BD Taiwan's site, for example, lists the C4 as "CAMALOT C4 .4 岩楔".

### You own a curved set and are starting on Long Dong's irregular cracks

- Add a set of DMM Alloy Offsets or BD Offset Stoppers #7–11.
- Follow UKClimbing: on days you carry offsets, take the matching mid sizes out of your curved set so you aren't carrying three of one size.
- Already on BD Stoppers? BD Offsets fit in. If strength and bite on coarse rock matter more, choose DMM (12 kN versus 10 kN, and OGL also prefers DMM).

### You want the easiest to remove

- CAMP Pro Nut: OGL calls it the easiest nut to remove.
- It comes in few sizes, so you'll probably add another set later. Check the strength stamp on the small sizes before using them.

### Heading to the UK or another nut-heavy area

- One set of 1–11 is the baseline; bring two for long routes. James Pearson takes up to three on UK sea cliffs.
- Before you go, ask locals or check an online guide whether the area is cam terrain or nut terrain.

## Inspection and retirement

[Black Diamond's older Stopper instructions](https://weighmyrack.com/sites/default/files/legacy_files/product_resources/Black%20Diamond%20Hex%20Technical%20Info.pdf) (archived by WeighMyRack) are specific about checking nuts:

- **Inspect before and after every use.** Retire a nut immediately if the cable has abraded or frayed strands, the head is cracked, or any part is corroded or deformed.
- **Don't keep bending the cable at sharp angles.** If a nut won't come out, loosen it with a nut tool instead of wrenching the cable; repeated bending weakens the cable until it breaks.
- **Wash and dry after salt water or sea air.** Long Dong's air is salty, and [Wild Lily Rocks' care guide](https://www.wildlily.rocks/post/%E6%B7%BA%E8%AB%87%E5%82%B3%E6%94%80%E8%A3%9D%E5%82%99%E4%BF%9D%E9%A4%8A) (in Chinese) also says to wipe gear with a damp cloth every time you get home, and every two or three months to deep-clean metal gear in warm water and dry it before storing. The Superlight Rock recall was caused by exactly this: cable corrosion in seaside conditions.
- **Second-hand nuts**: BD strongly discourages using gear of unknown history, because you have to know what it's been through to trust it.

For full inspection and retirement rules, see the last post in this series, [Inspecting, Caring For and Retiring Climbing Gear](/posts/climbing/2026-10-19-climbing-gear-inspection-retirement-en).

## Practice: four scenarios

Answers are below each question. Think first, then look.

**1. You can afford one piece of trad gear, and your partner already has a set of cams from .4 to #3.**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Buy a set of nuts. A set costs about as much as one cam but gives you many placements, and your partner's cams plus your nuts make exactly the course gear list. If you'll climb at Long Dong, consider DMM Wallnuts first.

</details>

**2. A shop assistant recommends a set of offsets as your first nuts, saying "everyone uses these now".**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

OGL does say offsets are getting more popular, but they do worse in parallel cracks and are hard for the second to remove. Buy a curved set first, and add offsets once you have it, replacing the mid sizes.

</details>

**3. You see a cheap second-hand set of Wild Country Superlight Rocks 1–6 in a climbing group.**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Check the batch code on the swage first. Every Superlight Rock produced before the end of 2022 was recalled in 2023, because seaside conditions can corrode the cable inside the swage and weaken it, with nothing visible from outside. Unless the batch code is new production starting with 06 or higher, don't buy them and don't use them.

</details>

**4. You want a few nuts smaller than #4 and find one whose #1 is rated just 2 kN.**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

2 kN is the standard's minimum. Some manufacturers say outright that 2 kN micros are for aid only, BD Stopper #1–2 for example, so read the instructions. Small nuts that are rated for protection still hold far less than mid sizes and only belong in excellent placements (see [Placing Trad Protection](/posts/climbing/2026-10-17-trad-protection-nuts-cams-en) for how to judge one). Small sizes vary a lot between brands; the smallest DMM Peenut, for example, is rated 4 kN. Read the manufacturer's table before choosing.

</details>
