Arigasayama is a rhyolite sport destination in Gunma known for steep walls, roofs and endurance, with east/west approaches to choose before the day. This guide helps you decide whether it suits your team, when to visit and what to confirm about transport and the approach.

## What kind of crag

The rock is **rhyolite** ([crag description](https://www.climbing-net.com/iwaba_detail/%E6%9C%89%E7%AC%A0%E5%B1%B1/)); the readable [ROCKTOPO inventory](https://www.rocktopo.net/areas/81/routes) has 209 graded entries. Its scope differs from a complete guidebook.

## When to go

The publisher lists April–November and [ROCKTOPO](https://www.rocktopo.net/areas/81) March–November. Summer condensation can defeat a rain-sheltered roof: choose sectors around sun, humidity and recent rain.

## Getting there

### Public transport

[Nakanojo Town](https://www.town.nakanojo.gunma.jp/soshiki/4/1147.html) links the JR Agatsuma Line and [Kan-etsu buses](https://kan-etsu.net/publics/index/25/) toward Shima/Sawatari. Reach Nakanojo Station, then coordinate the Sawatari timetable with a pickup; the hot-spring bus does not reach every climbing sector.

### Driving

Choose east or west sectors before navigating, then check the current topo and barriers. Earlier reports mention a west-entrance closure; they do not establish today’s status. Confirm with a local climbing source before choosing the road.

### Parking

The publisher warns against parking in front of the east-side barrier. Use designated spaces and keep forestry access clear; a full parking area does not justify passing a gate.

## Rules before you go

Approach, condensation and rockfall differ by sector; a fixed-rope passage is not a resting platform. Stay out from beneath suspect blocks and do not conduct improvised cleaning.

A 2024 report describes a suspect large block on no siesta;2025 visitors report wet sectors and leeches. These are dated observations, not a current inspection. Check local maintenance/access information and leave unfinished or unpublished projects alone.

For a serious injury requiring medical help, call **119**. The [FDMA guide](https://www.fdma.go.jp/publication/portal/items/portal001_pamphiet_english.pdf) asks callers to give the location and landmarks. Save the local crag name, target sector and road access offline; ask your insurer whether the climbing style, search and rescue, treatment and repatriation are covered.
