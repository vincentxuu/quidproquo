For a forest-bouldering trip in Japan, Ena Kasagiyama deserves a full climbing day. Its areas are spread across a mountain: choose your objective before choosing parking, rather than carrying pads around looking for a familiar photograph. This guide helps you plan the season, level and access.

## What kind of crag

**恵那・笠置山** is in Kasagi, Ena City, Gifu—not Kyoto’s Kasagi. [CLIMBING-net](https://www.climbing-net.com/iwaba_detail/%E7%AC%A0%E7%BD%AE%E5%B1%B1/) identifies hard tuff. Bouldering is the focus, while [ROCKTOPO](https://www.rocktopo.net/areas/128) also records face routes and cracks; a pad day and a rope day need separate plans.

The upper, Sato and Inomachi areas have different topo coverage but remain sectors of this destination. Start where you can manage the downclimb and landing. A pad under the start does not cover every possible fall direction.

## When to go

The [official Gifu tourism page](https://www.kankou-gifu.jp/spot/detail_8406.html) recommends **March–May and October–December**. Woodland elevation moderates summer heat but brings biting insects. Winter use depends on snow, not merely the temperature forecast for town.

For consecutive climbing days, consider wet rock, slippery landings and mountain-road conditions together. Check the manager’s current notices for traffic or events; a past one-day closure is not an annual rule.

## Getting there

### Public transport

As checked in October 2026, the [manager’s access page](https://www.kasagiclimbing.com/access) provides entrance and road maps, not a public shuttle to each sector. If arriving by rail at Ena, arrange pickup or a rental car in advance, including the return. State the sector and number of pads when arranging transport.

### Driving

The [Gifu tourism authority](https://www.kankou-gifu.jp/spot/detail_8406.html) directs drivers from Ena IC on the Chuo Expressway along Route 68 toward Shirakawa. Navigate first to the **Kasagiyama entrance registration point**, then follow the designated road after registering rather than improvising a closer forestry-road entrance.

### Parking

The [association’s rules](https://www.kasagiclimbing.com/rule) permit parking only in designated places and specifically prohibit parking near the paraglider takeoff. Use the official map for your sector, keep passing and turning space clear, and do not occupy parking bays with tents; prefer established campsites.

## Rules before you go

The [managing association’s rules](https://www.kasagiclimbing.com/rule), checked in October 2026, require an entrance contribution and entry-log registration before using the designated road in both directions. Construction notices and on-site instructions supersede old topos; stay out of unpublished areas.

Do not deliberately damage rock or alter established problems. Pads and shoes are available through the [association’s reservation channel](https://www.kasagiclimbing.com/post/topo-place). Confirm reservations and collection before deciding what to carry; an advertised rental service does not guarantee same-day availability.

For a serious injury requiring medical help, call **119**. The [FDMA guide](https://www.fdma.go.jp/publication/portal/items/portal001_pamphiet_english.pdf) asks callers to give the location and landmarks. Save the local crag name, target sector and road access offline; ask your insurer whether the climbing style, search and rescue, treatment and repatriation are covered.

## Routes by level

YDS and V-grade conversions follow the [Mountain Project international grade comparison chart](https://www.mountainproject.com/international-climbing-grades) and are approximate; "approx." marks grades the sources give without a/b/c (such as 4 or 5+). The original grade is the one that counts.

The full graded [ROCKTOPO table](https://www.rocktopo.net/areas/128/routes) contributes 306 entries, supplemented by the linked public topo/MP rows. Original YDS or Japanese grades are retained; unlisted lengths/pitches remain unspecified. Obtain the current topo for descent and protection. Names shared by different sectors/grades remain separate. A listed route is not a current-access recommendation.

Japanese grades define the boulder groups; verified thetopo Font grades appear separately where available. Rope routes use their corresponding YDS bands within each group, with their own table.

## Where to look things up

- [Kasagiyama association](https://www.kasagiclimbing.com/): current management, traffic and event notices.
- [ROCKTOPO](https://www.rocktopo.net/areas/128): boulder names, Japanese grades and sectors, not access permission.
- [Official topo and rental page](https://www.kasagiclimbing.com/post/topo-place): local sales and reservation points.

**Guidebooks:** Grades and route counts here come mainly from online databases; the guidebook is the authority.

- [*Kasagiyama Climbing Area Guide 2022*](https://www.kasagiclimbing.com/post/topo 2022): Japanese local topo announced by the association/KCC, revising upper areas and adding Inomachi West. The announcement does not enumerate separate author/publisher credits; none are guessed here. Other sectors have separate volumes—check the sales page. No book-interior topo was accessed.
