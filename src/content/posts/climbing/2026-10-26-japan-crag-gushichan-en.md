A visit to Gushichan requires choosing both a climbing sector and a workable local approach. This guide helps you assess the style, season and access checks needed for a first visit.

## What kind of crag

Ryukyu limestone boulders offer roofs, overhangs and mantles. Not every landing is sand: inspect rock and uneven ground before arranging pads. Grades use Japanese kyu/dan. A smaller roped inventory also exists here.

## When to go

[CLIMBING-net](https://www.climbing-net.com/iwaba_detail/%E5%85%B7%E5%BF%97%E9%A0%AD%E3%83%9C%E3%83%AB%E3%83%80%E3%83%BC/) lists Year-round; seek shade and ventilation in summer. This is established crag information, not a day-specific forecast or current access permission. Check recent rain, coastal wind and waves where relevant, and current local notices.

## Getting there

### Public transport

[Yaese tourism](https://yaese-kankou.com/about/) lists Ryukyu Bus 50 from Naha Bus Terminal to Gushikami. From the airport, the monorail reaches Asahibashi. This gets you to the settlement; plan the final leg to the boulders separately.

### Driving

Approach Yaese via Routes 331 or 507, then identify the Gushichan coastal entrance. A navigation line to the shore does not authorize driving onto the beach.

### Parking

Confirm current parking with local climbers or the town. No current dedicated climbing-parking notice was obtained, so this guide does not direct vehicles onto the shore.

## Rules before you go

CLIMBING-net explicitly dates its Gushichan information to before 2002. It records sacred rocks that must not be climbed, private parcels, and a restriction on shore driving. These are older secondary rules: avoid those activities and confirm current boundaries locally. The page does not establish blanket access in 2026. Use a legitimate campsite unless local camping permission has been confirmed.

For a medical emergency, call **119** and give your position and landmarks, following [FDMA guidance](https://www.fdma.go.jp/publication/portal/items/portal001_pamphiet_english.pdf). Save the Japanese cliff name and an offline map before leaving. Ask your insurer specifically about outdoor bouldering, mountain rescue, treatment and repatriation.
