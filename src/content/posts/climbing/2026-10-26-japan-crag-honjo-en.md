A visit to Honjo requires choosing both a climbing sector and a workable local approach. This guide helps you assess the style, season and access checks needed for a first visit.

## What kind of crag

Honjo groups limestone sectors along the Banjo River, including Yuhodo, Gyodo, Kirikabu and Inoue Tower. Roofs and overhangs make it a destination, while walkers and swimmers share the valley.

## When to go

[CLIMBING-net](https://www.climbing-net.com/iwaba_detail/%E6%9C%AC%E5%8C%A0/) lists Year-round; choose sector exposure. This is established crag information, not a day-specific forecast or current access permission. Check recent rain, coastal wind and waves where relevant, and current local notices.

## Getting there

### Public transport

No current bus connecting the climbing sectors was verified. Arrange a car or confirmed pickup after reaching the Saiki area by rail; campground access does not imply a service to every wall.

### Driving

The official [Onagara campground page](https://www.visit-oita.jp/spots/detail/5911) describes the valley approach via Routes 10 and 35. Each climbing sector still requires its own legitimate entrance.

### Parking

Campground parking information applies to that facility. Confirm the intended sector with the local contact, keeping bends, visitor paths and private entrances clear.

## Rules before you go

Confirm the sector and shared visitor route before laying out equipment. CLIMBING-net records a restriction around the fragile lower rock near the Koshinto monument at Inoue Tower. This is secondary information without a newly obtained management notice; do not expand climbing there. Follow current campground booking and use rules for any river-side stay.

For a medical emergency, call **119** and give your position and landmarks, following [FDMA guidance](https://www.fdma.go.jp/publication/portal/items/portal001_pamphiet_english.pdf). Save the Japanese cliff name and an offline map before leaving. Ask your insurer specifically about lead climbing, mountain rescue, treatment and repatriation.
