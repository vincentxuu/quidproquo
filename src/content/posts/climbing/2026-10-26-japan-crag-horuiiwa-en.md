A visit to Horuiiwa requires choosing both a climbing sector and a workable local approach. This guide helps you assess the style, season and access checks needed for a first visit.

## What kind of crag

This granite crag on Rokko appears under both 保塁岩 and 堡塁岩. Cracks and faces coexist. Visible bolts do not establish that a line requires only quickdraws; check the topo for protection.

## When to go

[CLIMBING-net](https://www.climbing-net.com/iwaba_detail/%E4%BF%9D%E5%A1%81%E5%B2%A9/) lists Year-round; check mountain weather. This is established crag information, not a day-specific forecast or current access permission. Check recent rain, coastal wind and waves where relevant, and current local notices.

## Getting there

### Public transport

Check the [Rokko Cable operator](https://www.rokkosan.com/cable/) for current service between the lower and upper stations, then locate the climbing approach. Historical construction notices may remain on the site; use applicable dates and the live service status.

### Driving

Check official Rokko transport and parking maps before driving. Do not use a road shoulder or private facility as an assumed destination.

### Parking

No current dedicated cliff-parking notice was verified. Use official public parking and confirm the walking route. CLIMBING-net identifies private-facility boundaries; do not cross them as a shortcut.

## Rules before you go

Identify the approach and private-facility boundaries first. Older CLIMBING-net notes are useful cautions rather than land authorization. Keep equipment compact when others are below and avoid dislodging rock. Check crack protection and descent line by line.

For a medical emergency, call **119** and give your position and landmarks, following [FDMA guidance](https://www.fdma.go.jp/publication/portal/items/portal001_pamphiet_english.pdf). Save the Japanese cliff name and an offline map before leaving. Ask your insurer specifically about lead climbing, mountain rescue, treatment and repatriation.

Blank lengths are not evidence for rope choice; identify the line, anchor and descent in a current topo before climbing.
