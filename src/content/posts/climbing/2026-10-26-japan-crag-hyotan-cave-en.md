Hyotan Cave in Iwaizumi is known for steep limestone, while Modern and Wakaba sectors also offer easier lines. A mixed-level team should identify its sectors rather than assuming every climb is a roof.

## What kind of crag

**ひょうたんケイブ** is in Iwate. CLIMBING-net identifies a roped climbing destination on limestone. [RockTopo’s public list](https://www.rocktopo.net/areas/64/routes) has 71 records; the base tables retain 67 graded entries after excluding ungraded projects and explicitly restricted or destroyed entries. This is database coverage, not a count of routes certified open.

## When to go

CLIMBING-net lists March–December and explicitly notes frequent seepage. Shelter overhead does not guarantee dry holds. Check road and tyre conditions separately when crossing the Hayasaka highlands. [CLIMBING-net](https://www.climbing-net.com/iwaba_detail/%E3%81%B2%E3%82%87%E3%81%86%E3%81%9F%E3%82%93%E3%82%B1%E3%82%A4%E3%83%96/)

## Getting there

### Public transport

JR Bus Tohoku operates the Hayasaka Kogen route from Morioka toward Iwaizumi and Ryusendo. Check a town stop and arrange the last transfer to the cliff; Ryusendo’s tourist stop is not the Hyotan climbing entrance. [Official transport source](https://www.jrbustohoku.co.jp/route/15/105/)

### Driving

Navigate with Iwaizumi and the Japanese crag name. CLIMBING-net’s older approach describes a tunnel and roughly 20 minutes on foot; follow current road and trail conditions.

### Parking

The official transport page confirms Iwaizumi bus service, not cliff parking permission. Old guides and the database mention a chain-fitting pull-off; that is not authorization to occupy a road facility for climbing. Confirm parking locally or arrange a drop-off during road work. [Primary source](https://www.jrbustohoku.co.jp/route/15/105/)

## Rules before you go

CLIMBING-net warns about old bolts. Assess seepage, the approach and hardware separately. A current original-manager parking agreement was not available in the public sources consulted; an old guide cannot grant permission. [CLIMBING-net](https://www.climbing-net.com/iwaba_detail/%E3%81%B2%E3%82%87%E3%81%86%E3%81%9F%E3%82%93%E3%82%B1%E3%82%A4%E3%83%96/)

For an ambulance or fire emergency call **119**. Give the location, a recognisable landmark, the injury and a callback number; the [Fire and Disaster Management Agency](https://www.fdma.go.jp/publication/portal/items/portal001_pamphiet_english.pdf) explains the call. Save the Japanese sector and route names offline. Ask your insurer specifically about sport climbing, trad and multi-pitch climbing, search and rescue, medical treatment and repatriation before travelling.
