Ishigakiyama in Aibetsu combines columnar rock, cracks and face climbing. Include Aibetsu in searches to avoid namesakes elsewhere. Crack objectives require a protection plan beyond an ordinary sport rack.

## What kind of crag

**石垣山** is in Hokkaido. CLIMBING-net identifies a roped climbing destination on andesite. [RockTopo’s public list](https://www.rocktopo.net/areas/49/routes) has 71 records; the base tables retain 71 graded entries after excluding ungraded projects and explicitly restricted or destroyed entries. This is database coverage, not a count of routes certified open.

## When to go

CLIMBING-net lists late May–early October. Forested rock can remain damp after rain. On a first visit, leave time to identify the crack, protection and descent before choosing a larger objective. [CLIMBING-net](https://www.climbing-net.com/iwaba_detail/%E7%9F%B3%E5%9E%A3%E5%B1%B1/)

## Getting there

### Public transport

Dohoku Bus lists Aibetsu services. Arrange the final transfer after reaching Aibetsu by rail or bus; the operator’s route index is not a climbing approach guide. [Official transport source](https://www.dohokubus.com/rosen_bus.html)

### Driving

Navigate to Ishigakiyama in Aibetsu, then use a climbing topo for the particular cliff. The summit trail and the starts at Gyoten-iwa or Soai-iwa are different objectives.

### Parking

Aibetsu’s official page presents the columnar rocks and Ainu cultural landscape and mentions climbing. It does not publish a cliff parking capacity or authorize camping. Use signed parking and respect the cultural site. [Primary source](https://www.town.aibetsu.hokkaido.jp/03/01/03/665)

## Rules before you go

CLIMBING-net lists both removable-protection and bolted climbing. Route names alone do not specify the rack; check the topo and current hardware for the intended line. [CLIMBING-net](https://www.climbing-net.com/iwaba_detail/%E7%9F%B3%E5%9E%A3%E5%B1%B1/)

For an ambulance or fire emergency call **119**. Give the location, a recognisable landmark, the injury and a callback number; the [Fire and Disaster Management Agency](https://www.fdma.go.jp/publication/portal/items/portal001_pamphiet_english.pdf) explains the call. Save the Japanese sector and route names offline. Ask your insurer specifically about sport climbing, trad and multi-pitch climbing, search and rescue, medical treatment and repatriation before travelling.

RockTopo provides the complete public graded list used here; separately readable local additions and cross-source grade differences are included in the same tables. Publisher image topos and paid guide interiors could not be fully read in the available tools, so use the linked local topo for line identification.

YDS and V-grade conversions follow the [Mountain Project international grade comparison chart](https://www.mountainproject.com/international-climbing-grades) and are approximate; "approx." marks grades the sources give without a/b/c (such as 4 or 5+). The original grade is the one that counts.

The source already uses YDS; it is retained in both grade columns.

Each row cites the public route list. Most entries lack length and pitch information; “not listed” cannot determine your rope length, rack or descent. Native names are retained to match a topo on site.
