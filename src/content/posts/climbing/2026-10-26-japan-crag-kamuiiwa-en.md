Kamuiiwa is a roped cliff beside the Ishikari River, separate from the Kamuikotan boulders. Its history includes major hard routes, but today’s itinerary must account for the east-face collapse.

## What kind of crag

**神居岩** is in Hokkaido. CLIMBING-net identifies a roped climbing destination on limestone. [RockTopo’s public list](https://www.rocktopo.net/areas/54/routes) has 56 records; the base tables retain 31 graded entries after excluding ungraded projects and explicitly restricted or destroyed entries. This is database coverage, not a count of routes certified open.

## When to go

CLIMBING-net lists April–November and describes river views from the south face. Reaching the base does not establish that every face is dry or stable; assess rockfall and approach conditions after rain. [CLIMBING-net](https://www.climbing-net.com/iwaba_detail/%E7%A5%9E%E5%B1%85%E5%B2%A9/)

## Getting there

### Public transport

The geopark lists buses from the Asahikawa and Fukagawa directions to Kamuikotan. Check the operator’s timetable. This reaches the visitor area; the roped cliff has a further approach. [Official transport source](https://daisetsuzan-kamuymintar-geopark.jp/geosite/kamui-kotan/)

### Driving

Use Route 12 and Kamuikotan as the navigation point. The geopark describes a one-way visitor-road arrangement; follow the signed entry and exit.

### Parking

The official one-way arrangement concerns the visitor road; it does not authorize parking on every forest road or clearing. Use designated spaces and keep access clear. [Primary source](https://daisetsuzan-kamuymintar-geopark.jp/geosite/kamui-kotan/)

## Rules before you go

**Secondary: CLIMBING-net’s November 2023 report** describes a major east-face collapse and lost routes; RockTopo also flags destroyed lines. Remaining graded east-face entries below are identification records, not clearance to climb. Confirm the whole east-face situation locally before approaching it. [CLIMBING-net](https://www.climbing-net.com/iwaba_detail/%E7%A5%9E%E5%B1%85%E5%B2%A9/) [2023 collapse report](https://www.climbing-net.com/news/kammui_20231126/)

For an ambulance or fire emergency call **119**. Give the location, a recognisable landmark, the injury and a callback number; the [Fire and Disaster Management Agency](https://www.fdma.go.jp/publication/portal/items/portal001_pamphiet_english.pdf) explains the call. Save the Japanese sector and route names offline. Ask your insurer specifically about sport climbing, trad and multi-pitch climbing, search and rescue, medical treatment and repatriation before travelling.

## Routes by level

RockTopo provides the complete public graded list used here; separately readable local additions and cross-source grade differences are included in the same tables. Publisher image topos and paid guide interiors could not be fully read in the available tools, so use the linked local topo for line identification.

YDS and V-grade conversions follow the [Mountain Project international grade comparison chart](https://www.mountainproject.com/international-climbing-grades) and are approximate; "approx." marks grades the sources give without a/b/c (such as 4 or 5+). The original grade is the one that counts.

The source already uses YDS; it is retained in both grade columns.

Each row cites the public route list. Most entries lack length and pitch information; “not listed” cannot determine your rope length, rack or descent. Native names are retained to match a topo on site.

## Where to look things up

- **Guidebook:** Grades and route counts here come mainly from online databases; the guidebook is the authority. Kitayama Makoto’s [フリークライミング 日本100岩場1 北海道・東北 増補改訂版](https://www.yamakei.co.jp/products/2815120446.html), Yama-kei, 2016 electronic edition, is a Japanese-language regional baseline. The publisher page was consulted; the interior topo was not available for this guide. Its publication date does not establish current access.
