Kasama’s granite boulders are scattered through the wooded slopes of Sashiro-yama. Finding the right rock, inspecting landings and identifying restricted boulders matter as much as the grade.

## What kind of crag

**笠間ボルダー** is in Ibaraki. CLIMBING-net identifies a bouldering destination on granite. [RockTopo’s public list](https://www.rocktopo.net/areas/78/routes) has 159 records; the base tables retain 158 graded entries after excluding ungraded projects and explicitly restricted or destroyed entries. This is database coverage, not a count of routes certified open.

## When to go

CLIMBING-net lists year-round climbing, with summer heat and mosquitoes. Wait for dry rock and shoes after rain and inspect landings hidden by leaves rather than copying pad positions from photographs. [CLIMBING-net](https://www.climbing-net.com/iwaba_detail/%E7%AC%A0%E9%96%93%E3%83%9C%E3%83%AB%E3%83%80%E3%83%BC/)

## Getting there

### Public transport

Kasama City gives about 25 minutes on foot from Kasama Station on the JR Mito Line to Sashiro Sanroku Park, or three minutes from the loop-bus Nichido Museum stop. These are times to the foothill park, not to the individual boulders. [Official transport source](https://www.city.kasama.lg.jp/page/page000145.html)

### Driving

Navigate to Sashiro Sanroku Park and approach the named boulder using a climbing topo. Do not follow navigation onto closed forest roads.

### Parking

Kasama City lists 59 parking spaces at the foothill park. JFA separately records prohibited entry around Daikoku-iwa from 2017. This concerns the named boulder, not a blanket Kasama closure; Daikoku-iwa is excluded below. [Primary source](https://www.city.kasama.lg.jp/page/page000145.html) [JFA](https://freeclimb.jp/activities/deal-with/issue-list/)

## Rules before you go

CLIMBING-net’s historical account describes earthquake damage to some boulders and problems. A surviving database name does not prove the line still exists. Entries explicitly marked unclimbable are excluded; inspect other landings on arrival. [CLIMBING-net](https://www.climbing-net.com/iwaba_detail/%E7%AC%A0%E9%96%93%E3%83%9C%E3%83%AB%E3%83%80%E3%83%BC/)

For an ambulance or fire emergency call **119**. Give the location, a recognisable landmark, the injury and a callback number; the [Fire and Disaster Management Agency](https://www.fdma.go.jp/publication/portal/items/portal001_pamphiet_english.pdf) explains the call. Save the Japanese sector and route names offline. Ask your insurer specifically about bouldering, search and rescue, medical treatment and repatriation before travelling.

## Routes by level

RockTopo provides the complete public graded list used here; separately readable local additions and cross-source grade differences are included in the same tables. Publisher image topos and paid guide interiors could not be fully read in the available tools, so use the linked local topo for line identification.

YDS and V-grade conversions follow the [Mountain Project international grade comparison chart](https://www.mountainproject.com/international-climbing-grades) and are approximate; "approx." marks grades the sources give without a/b/c (such as 4 or 5+). The original grade is the one that counts.

The source uses Japanese kyu/dan grades. The four groups below retain that system: cross-checked Font/V values are shown only where a separately cited source supplies them. “—” means unavailable, not an estimated conversion.

Each row cites the public route list. Most entries lack length and pitch information; “not listed” cannot determine your rope length, rack or descent. Native names are retained to match a topo on site.
