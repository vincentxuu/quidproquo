Kogashiyama concentrates short roped climbs around Fudo Falls. Homes, shrines and hiking paths share the area, making parking and unobstructed access part of the climbing plan.

## What kind of crag

**古賀志山** is in Tochigi. CLIMBING-net identifies a roped climbing destination on chert. [RockTopo’s public list](https://www.rocktopo.net/areas/80/routes) has 101 records; the base tables retain 100 graded entries after excluding ungraded projects and explicitly restricted or destroyed entries. This is database coverage, not a count of routes certified open.

## When to go

CLIMBING-net lists year-round climbing, but choose faces according to sun, wind and moisture. Short routes still require checking wet rock, loose material and the approach. [CLIMBING-net](https://www.climbing-net.com/iwaba_detail/%E5%8F%A4%E8%B3%80%E5%BF%97%E5%B1%B1/)

## Getting there

### Public transport

Use Utsunomiya as a transport base and arrange the final taxi or transfer to Kogashi. The city parking document does not establish a bus connection to the climbing entrance. [Official transport source](https://www.city.utsunomiya.lg.jp/_res/projects/default_project/_page_/001/041/173/01gaiyou.pdf)

### Driving

Navigate to Kogashiyama South Trail Parking, then use a topo for the Fudo Falls cliffs. Distinguish the municipal lot from old informal parking near the shrine.

### Parking

Utsunomiya’s document lists South Trail Parking at Kogashi-machi 1839-1, with 30 spaces and free 24-hour access. JFA separately records restrictions on old parking and approaches from 2018: the shrine front is for loading only, not parking. The municipal lot does not reopen those old spaces. [Primary source](https://www.city.utsunomiya.lg.jp/_res/projects/default_project/_page_/001/041/173/01gaiyou.pdf) [JFA](https://freeclimb.jp/activities/deal-with/issue-list/)

## Rules before you go

**Secondary: local rules reproduced by CLIMBING-net** prohibit entering Mesu Falls, cutting trees, leaving equipment at the shrine and using fires, and ask climbers to wear helmets. Choose among sectors with a confirmed permissible approach. [CLIMBING-net](https://www.climbing-net.com/iwaba_detail/%E5%8F%A4%E8%B3%80%E5%BF%97%E5%B1%B1/)

For an ambulance or fire emergency call **119**. Give the location, a recognisable landmark, the injury and a callback number; the [Fire and Disaster Management Agency](https://www.fdma.go.jp/publication/portal/items/portal001_pamphiet_english.pdf) explains the call. Save the Japanese sector and route names offline. Ask your insurer specifically about sport climbing, trad and multi-pitch climbing, search and rescue, medical treatment and repatriation before travelling.

## Routes by level

RockTopo provides the complete public graded list used here; separately readable local additions and cross-source grade differences are included in the same tables. The six older publisher grade-table images were read and compared here; cliff-line drawings and paid guide interiors were not read, so use a local topo for identification.

YDS and V-grade conversions follow the [Mountain Project international grade comparison chart](https://www.mountainproject.com/international-climbing-grades) and are approximate; "approx." marks grades the sources give without a/b/c (such as 4 or 5+). The original grade is the one that counts.

The source already uses YDS; it is retained in both grade columns.

Each row cites the public route list. Most entries lack length and pitch information; “not listed” cannot determine your rope length, rack or descent. Native names are retained to match a topo on site.

## Where to look things up

- **Guidebook:** Grades and route counts here come mainly from online databases; the guidebook is the authority. Kitayama Makoto’s [フリークライミング日本100岩場2 関東 増補改訂新版 御前岩ルート収録](https://www.yamakei.co.jp/products/2819180880.html), Yama-kei, 2019, is a Japanese-language regional baseline.
