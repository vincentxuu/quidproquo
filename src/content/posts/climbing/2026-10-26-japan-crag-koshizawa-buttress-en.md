Koshizawa Buttress is an Okutama multipitch cliff where modest technical grades coexist with exposed approaches, older protection and sharp rock edges. This guide helps you decide whether it suits your team, when to visit and what to confirm about transport and the approach.

## What kind of crag

The rock is **chert** ([crag description](https://www.kojitusanso.jp/tozan-report/detail/?fm=11084)); the readable [ROCKTOPO inventory](https://www.rocktopo.net/areas/102/routes) has 3 graded entries. Its scope differs from a complete guidebook.

## When to go

Choose a clear spring or autumn day for an initial visit; a [November 2021 club report](https://npo-horizonte.org/report/2021-11-7-koizawa/) describes autumn multipitch practice. Summer humidity and winter daylight/ice change the plan. After rain assess crossings, fixed-rope passages and descent, not only the cliff surface.

## Getting there

### Public transport

Take the JR Ome Line to Hatonosu; check the [JR East timetable](https://timetables.jreast.co.jp/timetable/list1237.html) for the return. Continue only by the approach confirmed with current local management; do not enter camp property or take obsolete shortcuts from old reports.

### Driving

Drive along Ome-kaido to Hatonosu and continue from authorized public parking on foot. The forestry road, camp property and mountain trails have different ownership and damage histories; road access to one point does not clear an approach to the cliff.

### Parking

Check current Hatonosu parking conditions rather than old free-parking or camp-fee reports. Keep homes and forestry entrances clear, and obtain current permission for any approach through camp property.

## Rules before you go

A [2019 publisher notice](https://www.climbing-net.com/news/koizawa_191017/) requested self-restraint after typhoon damage. A [2021 club report](https://npo-horizonte.org/report/2021-11-7-koizawa/) used the Garden Camp approach; that visit does not establish 2026 permission. **Confirm the entrance and crossings with local management or a qualified guide, and cancel if unresolved.**

Sharp edges threaten ropes and some protection is old. Bring trad-protection, route-finding, multipitch belaying and retreat competence; hardest grades of 5.7–5.9 do not certify low risk, particularly with shared descents.

For a serious injury requiring medical help, call **119**. The [FDMA guide](https://www.fdma.go.jp/publication/portal/items/portal001_pamphiet_english.pdf) asks callers to give the location and landmarks. Save the local crag name, target sector and road access offline; ask your insurer whether the climbing style, search and rescue, treatment and repatriation are covered.
