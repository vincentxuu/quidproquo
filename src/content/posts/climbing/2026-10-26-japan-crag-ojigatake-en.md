A visit to Ojigatake requires choosing both a climbing sector and a workable local approach. This guide helps you assess the style, season and access checks needed for a first visit.

## What kind of crag

Granite boulders are scattered across exposed hillsides and viewpoints. Plan this as a bouldering visit, considering friction, feet and mantles alongside landing protection. Old coastal roped records may describe altered terrain.

## When to go

[CLIMBING-net](https://www.climbing-net.com/iwaba_detail/%E7%8E%8B%E5%AD%90%E3%82%AC%E5%B2%B3/) lists Autumn through spring. This is established crag information, not a day-specific forecast or current access permission. Check recent rain, coastal wind and waves where relevant, and current local notices.

## Getting there

### Public transport

No current bus directly to the summit boulders was verified. From Uno or Kojima, check the actual trailhead service and arrange the uphill and return legs; a foothill stop is not arrival at the boulders.

### Driving

Use [Tamano’s page](https://www.city.tamano.lg.jp/soshiki/16/7688.html) to identify summit roads and parking. Its park-centre walking time is not the approach time to every boulder.

### Parking

The city lists public park parking areas. Carry pads from a proper space, keep roads clear, and share viewpoint paths with other visitors.

## Rules before you go

Ojigatake is in Setonaikai National Park, and the city notes permission requirements for structures and work on trees. Do not modify the landscape for a line. Its [bouldering-map page](https://www.city.tamano.lg.jp/soshiki/16/2213.html) asks visitors to clean rock and remove rubbish. Historical short boar-control closures do not establish a current blanket ban; check fresh notices.

For a medical emergency, call **119** and give your position and landmarks, following [FDMA guidance](https://www.fdma.go.jp/publication/portal/items/portal001_pamphiet_english.pdf). Save the Japanese cliff name and an offline map before leaving. Ask your insurer specifically about outdoor bouldering, mountain rescue, treatment and repatriation.
