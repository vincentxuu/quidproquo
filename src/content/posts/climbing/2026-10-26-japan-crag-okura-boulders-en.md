Okura’s boulders line the river near Sendai, including Shiru-iwa, Table Rock and Rindo-iwa. Water level changes both landings and available problems; inspect the way back as well as the rock.

## What kind of crag

**大倉ボルダー** is in Miyagi. CLIMBING-net identifies a bouldering destination on river boulders; rock type unspecified. [RockTopo’s public list](https://www.rocktopo.net/areas/67/routes) has 43 records; the base tables retain 43 graded entries after excluding ungraded projects and explicitly restricted or destroyed entries. This is database coverage, not a count of routes certified open.

## When to go

CLIMBING-net lists April–December, especially spring and autumn. Spring water can submerge problems, summer brings condensation and winter snow. City weather alone does not establish safe river conditions. [CLIMBING-net](https://www.climbing-net.com/iwaba_detail/%E5%A4%A7%E5%80%89%E3%83%9C%E3%83%AB%E3%83%80%E3%83%BC/)

## Getting there

### Public transport

Sendai’s transport bureau lists Jogi services at west-exit stop 14. The river approach follows after Jogi; CLIMBING-net’s older estimate is about 15 minutes, subject to trail and water conditions. [Official transport source](https://www.kotsu.city.sendai.jp/shibus/noriba/sendai/)

### Driving

Follow Route 48 toward Jogi Nyorai, then use a climbing approach map for the river. Do not drive through residential access simply to get closer to a boulder.

### Parking

**Secondary: local guidance reproduced by CLIMBING-net** asks climbers not to park at the Jogi treatment plant, not to climb at night and to stay quiet near homes. Confirm prolonged use of temple parking on site. The original manager’s current parking agreement was not available; an older guide cannot grant permission. [CLIMBING-net](https://www.climbing-net.com/iwaba_detail/%E5%A4%A7%E5%80%89%E3%83%9C%E3%83%AB%E3%83%80%E3%83%BC/)

## Rules before you go

The same guide warns of damaged approach-road sections and some fragile rock, and discourages resin-containing liquid chalk. Change objectives if the approach is damaged, water rises or the landing is compromised. [CLIMBING-net](https://www.climbing-net.com/iwaba_detail/%E5%A4%A7%E5%80%89%E3%83%9C%E3%83%AB%E3%83%80%E3%83%BC/)

For an ambulance or fire emergency call **119**. Give the location, a recognisable landmark, the injury and a callback number; the [Fire and Disaster Management Agency](https://www.fdma.go.jp/publication/portal/items/portal001_pamphiet_english.pdf) explains the call. Save the Japanese sector and route names offline. Ask your insurer specifically about bouldering, search and rescue, medical treatment and repatriation before travelling.

## Routes by level

RockTopo provides the complete public graded list used here; separately readable local additions and cross-source grade differences are included in the same tables. Publisher image topos and paid guide interiors could not be fully read in the available tools, so use the linked local topo for line identification.

YDS and V-grade conversions follow the [Mountain Project international grade comparison chart](https://www.mountainproject.com/international-climbing-grades) and are approximate; "approx." marks grades the sources give without a/b/c (such as 4 or 5+). The original grade is the one that counts.

The source uses Japanese kyu/dan grades. The four groups below retain that system: cross-checked Font/V values are shown only where a separately cited source supplies them. “—” means unavailable, not an estimated conversion.

Each row cites the public route list. Most entries lack length and pitch information; “not listed” cannot determine your rope length, rack or descent. Native names are retained to match a topo on site.
