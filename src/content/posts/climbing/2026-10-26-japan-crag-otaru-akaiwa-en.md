Akaiwa overlooks the sea outside Otaru, with face climbs, cracks and multi-pitch terrain. This is Otaru Akaiwa, not Akaiwa Seigankyo near Shimukappu; search with Otaru attached.

## What kind of crag

**赤岩** is in Hokkaido. CLIMBING-net identifies a roped climbing destination on andesitic agglomerate. [RockTopo’s public list](https://www.rocktopo.net/areas/55/routes) has 103 records; the base tables retain 87 graded entries after excluding ungraded projects and explicitly restricted or destroyed entries. This is database coverage, not a count of routes certified open.

## When to go

CLIMBING-net lists April–November. Coastal wind, wet rock and the approach can change the day’s choices. On a first visit, leave daylight for finding the sector and checking the descent. [CLIMBING-net](https://www.climbing-net.com/iwaba_detail/%E8%B5%A4%E5%B2%A9/)

## Getting there

### Public transport

Chuo Bus lists services at Akaiwa 2-chome, including routes 11, 12 and 42. Check a suitable connection from Otaru Station and the return service. The pass and individual cliffs still require a walking approach. [Official transport source](https://transfer.navitime.biz/chuo-bus/pc/diagram/BusCourseSearch?busstopId=00300011)

### Driving

Navigate to Otaru Akaiwa Pass, then plan the approach to the actual sector. Coastal descents and multi-pitch routes require their own equipment plan.

### Parking

The Do-o District Workers’ Mountaineering Federation published parking restrictions for toilet works on 19 August–31 October 2025. That dated notice is historical; read the same federation page for current forestry and parking information and follow on-site barriers. [Primary source](https://hcwaf.jwaf.club/sangaku11.html)

## Rules before you go

CLIMBING-net warns about old bolts. Coastal hardware needs careful inspection; a listed grade says nothing about rebolting or the rope length needed to descend. [CLIMBING-net](https://www.climbing-net.com/iwaba_detail/%E8%B5%A4%E5%B2%A9/)

For an ambulance or fire emergency call **119**. Give the location, a recognisable landmark, the injury and a callback number; the [Fire and Disaster Management Agency](https://www.fdma.go.jp/publication/portal/items/portal001_pamphiet_english.pdf) explains the call. Save the Japanese sector and route names offline. Ask your insurer specifically about sport climbing, trad and multi-pitch climbing, search and rescue, medical treatment and repatriation before travelling.

## Routes by level

RockTopo provides the complete public graded list used here; separately readable local additions and cross-source grade differences are included in the same tables. Publisher image topos and paid guide interiors could not be fully read in the available tools, so use the linked local topo for line identification.

YDS and V-grade conversions follow the [Mountain Project international grade comparison chart](https://www.mountainproject.com/international-climbing-grades) and are approximate; "approx." marks grades the sources give without a/b/c (such as 4 or 5+). The original grade is the one that counts.

The source already uses YDS; it is retained in both grade columns.

Each row cites the public route list. Most entries lack length and pitch information; “not listed” cannot determine your rope length, rack or descent. Native names are retained to match a topo on site.

## Where to look things up

- **Guidebook:** Grades and route counts here come mainly from online databases; the guidebook is the authority. Kitayama Makoto’s [フリークライミング 日本100岩場1 北海道・東北 増補改訂版](https://www.yamakei.co.jp/products/2815120446.html), Yama-kei, 2016 electronic edition, is a Japanese-language regional baseline. The publisher page was consulted; the interior topo was not available for this guide. Its publication date does not establish current access.

- [CLIMBING-net](https://www.climbing-net.com/iwaba_detail/%E8%B5%A4%E5%B2%A9/): editorial regional context and older approach information.
