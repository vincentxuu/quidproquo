A visit to Shimane Peninsula requires choosing both a climbing sector and a workable local approach. This guide helps you assess the style, season and access checks needed for a first visit.

## What kind of crag

This guide groups peninsula sectors such as Shichirui, Tayui, Kataku, Onose and Ukadomari. They are dispersed small cliffs, not one continuous wall. The local association describes an emphasis on 5.10–5.11, with varied rock and exposure between coasts.

## When to go

[CLIMBING-net](https://www.climbing-net.com/iwaba_detail/%E5%B3%B6%E6%A0%B9%E5%8D%8A%E5%B3%B6/) lists April–November (older information). This is established crag information, not a day-specific forecast or current access permission. Check recent rain, coastal wind and waves where relevant, and current local notices.

## Getting there

### Public transport

Choose the sector first, then consult [Ichibata Bus](https://bus.ichibata.co.jp/rosen/) for routes around Matsue. No verified single bus service links all the cliffs; plan the coastal final leg and return individually.

### Driving

Navigate to the specific sector and legitimate entrance, rather than simply “Shimane Peninsula.” Keep narrow-road passing spaces and fishing access clear.

### Parking

The [local association](https://sfa.jpn.org/eria1.html) specifically flags parking, toilets and waste as potential local problems. A database listing does not authorize parking at a harbour or in front of homes; confirm before arriving.

## Rules before you go

Each coastal entrance has its own boundaries. Check current information with the Shimane Free Climbing Association and avoid private paths or harbour facilities without permission. Swell and wind can change approaches; retain the option to abandon a sector.

For a medical emergency, call **119** and give your position and landmarks, following [FDMA guidance](https://www.fdma.go.jp/publication/portal/items/portal001_pamphiet_english.pdf). Save the Japanese cliff name and an offline map before leaving. Ask your insurer specifically about lead climbing, mountain rescue, treatment and repatriation.

Routes and original YDS grades below come from [ROCKTOPO](https://www.rocktopo.net/areas/151/routes), unless a note names another source. Each database-only row is a single-source candidate. Blank lengths are not evidence for rope choice; identify the line, anchor and descent in a current topo before climbing.

Video labels follow uploader metadata, and may use different grades or versions. A linked clip does not certify access or protection. Clips with unverified embedding are external links.
