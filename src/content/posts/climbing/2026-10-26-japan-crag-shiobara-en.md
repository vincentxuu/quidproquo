Shiobara’s Hoki River boulders are known for caves, steep climbing and very hard problems, alongside easier starts. Tuff holds and river landings require inspection; famous ascent videos are not live water-level reports.

## What kind of crag

**塩原ボルダー** is in Tochigi. CLIMBING-net identifies a bouldering destination on tuff (Oya stone). [RockTopo’s public list](https://www.rocktopo.net/areas/79/routes) has 94 records; the base tables retain 87 graded entries after excluding ungraded projects and explicitly restricted or destroyed entries. This is database coverage, not a count of routes certified open.

## When to go

CLIMBING-net describes year-round possibilities. Snow, floods and dampness still restrict individual problems. Avoid forcing attempts on wet tuff or treating extra pads as a solution to a flooded landing. [CLIMBING-net](https://www.climbing-net.com/iwaba_detail/%E5%A1%A9%E5%8E%9F%E3%83%9C%E3%83%AB%E3%83%80%E3%83%BC/)

## Getting there

### Public transport

JR Bus Kanto’s local timetable connects Nasushiobara and Nishinasuno stations with Shiobara Onsen. Check the appropriate Fukuwata-area stop and the final walk to Nodachi-iwa. [Official transport source](https://www.jrbuskanto.co.jp/jwp/bus_etc/timetable)

### Driving

Use Fukuwatari Municipal Parking as the main parking plan and the named boulders separately. Roads and river access are narrow; keep traffic moving while unloading.

### Parking

Use **Fukuwatari Municipal Parking** as the main plan. The Shiobara tourism association and ryokan union state that there is no temporary parking lot for Nodachi-iwa and direct climbers to the municipal lot, about ten minutes away on foot. Gear may be unloaded at the Fukuwatari prefectural lot before moving the car; this does not establish permission for a long climbing stay there. The narrow road lacks pavement in places. Do not improvise roadside parking; remove rubbish and cigarette ends and use no fires. [Official parking guidance](https://www.siobara.or.jp/information/20220606/69/)

## Rules before you go

**Secondary: rules reproduced by CLIMBING-net** also prohibit gas stoves and metal brushes. Nodachi-iwa is an official tourism destination; keep visitor paths open instead of blocking them with pads. [CLIMBING-net](https://www.climbing-net.com/iwaba_detail/%E5%A1%A9%E5%8E%9F%E3%83%9C%E3%83%AB%E3%83%80%E3%83%BC/)

For an ambulance or fire emergency call **119**. Give the location, a recognisable landmark, the injury and a callback number; the [Fire and Disaster Management Agency](https://www.fdma.go.jp/publication/portal/items/portal001_pamphiet_english.pdf) explains the call. Save the Japanese sector and route names offline. Ask your insurer specifically about bouldering, search and rescue, medical treatment and repatriation before travelling.

## Routes by level

RockTopo provides the complete public graded list used here; separately readable local additions and cross-source grade differences are included in the same tables. Publisher image topos and paid guide interiors could not be fully read in the available tools, so use the linked local topo for line identification.

YDS and V-grade conversions follow the [Mountain Project international grade comparison chart](https://www.mountainproject.com/international-climbing-grades) and are approximate; "approx." marks grades the sources give without a/b/c (such as 4 or 5+). The original grade is the one that counts.

The source uses Japanese kyu/dan grades. The four groups below retain that system: cross-checked Font/V values are shown only where a separately cited source supplies them. “—” means unavailable, not an estimated conversion.

Each row cites the public route list. Most entries lack length and pitch information; “not listed” cannot determine your rope length, rack or descent. Native names are retained to match a topo on site.
