The climbing destination is the cliffs around Sennindo and Kushiraku-iwa, not Risshakuji’s temple staircase. Vertical and steeper lines coexist; identifying the right sector is more useful than navigating to the place name alone.

## What kind of crag

**山寺** is in Yamagata. CLIMBING-net identifies a roped climbing destination on andesite. [RockTopo’s public list](https://www.rocktopo.net/areas/71/routes) has 63 records; the base tables retain 63 graded entries after excluding ungraded projects and explicitly restricted or destroyed entries. This is database coverage, not a count of routes certified open.

## When to go

CLIMBING-net lists April–October and notes summer heat. Plan timing around the selected face and inspect both rock and trail after rain. [CLIMBING-net](https://www.climbing-net.com/iwaba_detail/%E5%B1%B1%E5%AF%BA/)

## Getting there

### Public transport

Yamagata’s official tourism page confirms Yamadera Station on the JR Senzan Line. Temple walking times do not describe the cliff approach; use a climbing topo beyond the station. [Official transport source](https://yamagatakanko.com/attractions/detail_10700.html)

### Driving

After reaching Yamadera, identify the climbing access road and entrance rather than following navigation to the temple gate.

### Parking

JFA’s 2025 mowing notice closed the access road and former campsite on the mornings of 22 June and 27 July, even without a barrier. Those were dated 2025 restrictions; check current notices and work sites rather than treating them as annual closure dates. [Primary source](https://freeclimb.jp/access/10249/)

## Rules before you go

Keep the working road and residents’ access clear. Check protection and descent for the named line; a database length is not a complete belay or descent plan. [CLIMBING-net](https://www.climbing-net.com/iwaba_detail/%E5%B1%B1%E5%AF%BA/)

For an ambulance or fire emergency call **119**. Give the location, a recognisable landmark, the injury and a callback number; the [Fire and Disaster Management Agency](https://www.fdma.go.jp/publication/portal/items/portal001_pamphiet_english.pdf) explains the call. Save the Japanese sector and route names offline. Ask your insurer specifically about sport climbing, trad and multi-pitch climbing, search and rescue, medical treatment and repatriation before travelling.

## Routes by level

RockTopo provides the complete public graded list used here; separately readable local additions and cross-source grade differences are included in the same tables. Publisher image topos and paid guide interiors could not be fully read in the available tools, so use the linked local topo for line identification.

YDS and V-grade conversions follow the [Mountain Project international grade comparison chart](https://www.mountainproject.com/international-climbing-grades) and are approximate; "approx." marks grades the sources give without a/b/c (such as 4 or 5+). The original grade is the one that counts.

The source already uses YDS; it is retained in both grade columns.

Each row cites the public route list. Most entries lack length and pitch information; “not listed” cannot determine your rope length, rack or descent. Native names are retained to match a topo on site.

## Where to look things up

- **Guidebook:** Grades and route counts here come mainly from online databases; the guidebook is the authority. Kitayama Makoto’s [フリークライミング 日本100岩場1 北海道・東北 増補改訂版](https://www.yamakei.co.jp/products/2815120446.html), Yama-kei, 2016 electronic edition, is a Japanese-language regional baseline. The publisher page was consulted; the interior topo was not available for this guide. Its publication date does not establish current access.
- [RockTopo route list](https://www.rocktopo.net/areas/71/routes): original names, grades and sector grouping; crowd-sourced information needs a current local check.
- [CLIMBING-net](https://www.climbing-net.com/iwaba_detail/%E5%B1%B1%E5%AF%BA/): editorial regional context and older approach information.
