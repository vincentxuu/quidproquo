Yoshitsune Iwa near Lake Toya offers a compact collection of tuff routes. Access history matters: a 2025 voluntary restraint was followed by an explicit release, so the dates of notices are essential.

## What kind of crag

**義経岩** is in Hokkaido. CLIMBING-net identifies a roped climbing destination on tuff. [RockTopo’s public list](https://www.rocktopo.net/areas/56/routes) has 27 records; the base tables retain 27 graded entries after excluding ungraded projects and explicitly restricted or destroyed entries. This is database coverage, not a count of routes certified open.

## When to go

CLIMBING-net gives March–December; spring and autumn are practical starting points. This published season is not a guarantee of ice-free approaches in colder months. [CLIMBING-net](https://www.climbing-net.com/iwaba_detail/%E7%BE%A9%E7%B5%8C%E5%B2%A9/)

## Getting there

### Public transport

Use Donan Bus’s suburban-route and stop timetables to check Iwaya Kannon-mae and connections through Toya Station or Toyako Onsen. Confirm the stop-to-cliff approach and the return service. [Official transport source](https://www.donanbus.co.jp/kougai/)

### Driving

Navigate using Yoshitsune Iwa and Iwaya Kannon. Consolidate climbers and equipment before arrival to reduce vehicles near homes.

### Parking

On 18 November 2025, the Do-o District Workers’ Mountaineering Federation announced the end of voluntary climbing restraint after consultation with the town and Toyako Climbing Club. Its requests include carpooling, no shouting, smoking only inside cars and no fires. Follow these conditions and check for newer notices. [Primary source](https://hcwaf.jwaf.club/sangaku11.html)

## Rules before you go

The release does not certify individual bolts or anchors. Confirm the route, rope and descent, and keep belaying and equipment sorting clear of residents’ access. [CLIMBING-net](https://www.climbing-net.com/iwaba_detail/%E7%BE%A9%E7%B5%8C%E5%B2%A9/)

For an ambulance or fire emergency call **119**. Give the location, a recognisable landmark, the injury and a callback number; the [Fire and Disaster Management Agency](https://www.fdma.go.jp/publication/portal/items/portal001_pamphiet_english.pdf) explains the call. Save the Japanese sector and route names offline. Ask your insurer specifically about sport climbing, trad and multi-pitch climbing, search and rescue, medical treatment and repatriation before travelling.

RockTopo provides the complete public graded list used here; separately readable local additions and cross-source grade differences are included in the same tables. Publisher image topos and paid guide interiors could not be fully read in the available tools, so use the linked local topo for line identification.

YDS and V-grade conversions follow the [Mountain Project international grade comparison chart](https://www.mountainproject.com/international-climbing-grades) and are approximate; "approx." marks grades the sources give without a/b/c (such as 4 or 5+). The original grade is the one that counts.

The source already uses YDS; it is retained in both grade columns.

Each row cites the public route list. Most entries lack length and pitch information; “not listed” cannot determine your rope length, rack or descent. Native names are retained to match a topo on site.
