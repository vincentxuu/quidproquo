## What This Company Does

Atira builds an AI orchestration layer for industrial sales engineering — automating the process between an incoming customer request for quotation (RFQ) and the quote going out, work that normally takes sales engineers, technical specialists, legal, and commercial teams weeks to months to complete across departments.

Its core product connects to a customer's existing CRM, ERP, and CPQ systems, processes RFQ specifications that are often unstructured, multilingual, and accompanied by drawings and compliance standards, and automatically identifies requirements while generating configuration proposals, technical documentation, and pricing options. Atira estimates this kind of industrial sales engineering work accounts for more than $128B in annual labor spend globally, most of it still handled manually — a workflow traditional enterprise software has never truly touched.

Founded in November 2024, Atira has signed more than a dozen clients in under a year, ranging from mid-market manufacturers like Robel, Theegarten-Pactec, and Fette Compacting to global enterprises with more than €1B in revenue: ABB E-Mobility has deployed the platform globally, Chiron Group is expanding from Germany into the US, a large plant engineering group runs Atira on projects worth hundreds of millions of euros, and Rema Tip Top uses it across sales teams in Australia, Brazil, and Chile. Every one of these customers was closed with zero commercial headcount, through customer referrals alone.

## What This Funding Signals

### Implications for the Agent Ecosystem

An Accel partner put the thesis plainly in the firm's investment blog: "Soon, AI agents will operate on both sides of every complex B2B transaction, and the systems that coordinate them will be as fundamental to industry as ERP became a generation ago." This round signals VCs starting to treat the agent-to-agent coordination layer between companies — not just agent applications inside a single enterprise — as the next infrastructure opportunity, specifically targeting manufacturing's long-tail, high-complexity workflows that software has never truly reached. The new capital will grow Atira's go-to-market team, accelerate product development, support international expansion, and extend into adjacent commercial workflows: pricing intelligence, aftersales, and supplier coordination.

### What Investors Are Betting On

Accel partners Harry Nelis and Alex Reinert's logic: factories have been automated for fifty years, but the conversation between a customer and a supplier — the sales engineering process — has barely been touched by software, because the input is unstructured and the answer isn't known in advance. This continues a thread from Accel's earlier bet on Celonis (process mining): break into the "dark process" inside an enterprise that nobody has automated, then turn it into a data and coordination layer. Notably, this round's angel investor list includes Celonis co-founder and co-CEO Bastian Nominacher and Whirlpool CEO Marc Bitzer — the latter a manufacturing veteran putting his own money behind the bet, not just a financial investor.

### Numbers Worth Watching

- Customers report up to 80% shorter quote-to-bid cycles; most manufacturers otherwise decline 20-40% of inbound RFQs simply because they lack engineering bandwidth — Atira claims the same team size can now take on more orders
- More than a dozen clients signed in under a year, including global enterprises with over €1B in revenue (ABB E-Mobility, Chiron Group) — a customer density rarely seen at this stage for a seed-stage startup
- $17.5M raised total ($15M Seed plus a previously undisclosed $2.5M Pre-Seed), larger than most seed-stage vertical agent startups in industrial niches, reflecting investor premium for this specific niche

## Today's Takeaway

Most agent startups are competing for high-visibility categories like general-purpose assistants or coding agents, but Atira picked industrial sales engineering — a "dark process" nobody wanted to touch. Customers like ABB and Chiron, each with revenue north of a billion euros, sought the company out with zero commercial headcount on Atira's side, a reminder that the real pain points often hide in the most boring, least-covered corners rather than the loudest categories.
