## What the company does

Archy builds practice management software for dental offices — replacing the patchwork of outdated charting software and bolted-on AI tools with a single platform that has AI agents natively built in.

The core product bundles the essentials a dental practice needs — scheduling, charting, imaging, patient communications — with a suite of AI agents running on the same system of record: Archy Revenue files claims, collects insurance payments, and chases outstanding collections; Archy Verify checks insurance coverage and patient eligibility; Archy Connect handles patient communications and scheduling automatically; Archy Scribe turns completed appointments straight into SOAP notes; Archy Insight turns practice data into operating insights. The differentiator is that these agents aren't a layer bolted onto legacy software — they're native to the system of record, which is what lets them actually complete tasks rather than just suggest what to do.

The platform now serves more than 1,000 dental practices across 45 US states, processing over $300M in payments annually, with a team of about 130. Founder Jonathan Rat started the company after helping his dentist wife manage the technology at her own practice — a starting point that anchored Archy in the long-neglected long tail of single-location and mid-size dental practices.

## What this round signals

### What it means for the agent ecosystem

Dentistry has long been SaaS's long tail — fragmented, single-location, thin IT budgets, and software so dated that "the server in the closet" is an industry cliché. Archy's approach is to first displace the legacy practice management system and take over the system of record, then build agents natively on top of it. That points to a different path for vertical AI agents: instead of stacking a chat assistant on top of existing SaaS, merge the system of record and the system of action so agents can directly complete claims filing, reconciliation, and collections — work that previously required hiring a person.

### What investors are betting on

JMI Equity is a growth-equity firm focused on software, and it typically leads when a company already has a proven business model and is ready to scale, not when it's still validating an early concept. Existing investors TCV, Bessemer Venture Partners, and CRV chose to double down rather than get diluted out, signaling confidence in Archy's shift from "dental practice software" to "AI agents that do the administrative work." The logic mirrors other rounds this same stretch, like Clay and GenHealth.ai: VCs are now paying a premium for agents that actually finish a task end to end, not just for a smarter interface.

### Numbers worth watching

- Processing $300M+ in annual payment volume across 1,000+ practices signals this isn't an early product-market-fit story but a case with real transaction volume behind it
- $97M raised to date against that scale implies capital efficiency well above the typical "burn cash to grab market share" consumer-AI playbook, more in line with vertical SaaS capital discipline
- A 130-person team supporting 1,000+ practice customers is a far higher customer-to-headcount ratio than traditional enterprise software companies manage, suggesting agent automation is genuinely compressing the marginal cost of service

## Today's takeaway

It's easy to judge vertical AI agent startups by the surface-level label of "added an agent feature." Archy's story is a reminder that an agent needs to first own the system of record — full access to the data and workflow — before it can actually finish a task, rather than being bolted onto the outside of an existing system where it can only suggest, not act. That's why Archy chose to do the unglamorous work of replacing the legacy practice management system first, and only then layer agents on top — not the other way around.
