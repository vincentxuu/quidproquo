## What the company does

Hang Ten Systems delivers enterprise software using AI agents instead of the army of consultants a traditional systems integrator would deploy — handling software development, enterprise system migrations, finance and analytics, and HR work for large companies.

The core isn't a foundation model — it's an internal framework called Hobie that packages agentic code-generation capability into a reusable "skills library": capability built for one engagement can, in theory, be applied directly to the next similar one instead of being rebuilt from scratch. That positions Hang Ten as an "AI-era consulting firm" — taking on enterprise advisory and software-delivery contracts while feeding what it learns from each engagement back into the skills library, so the next client's delivery gets faster.

Founder Vishal Sikka is a former Infosys CEO and former SAP executive who, after leaving Infosys, founded enterprise AI company VianAI in 2019, raising a $50M seed and later a $140M round led by SoftBank Vision Fund 2. This time, with co-founder Sanjay Rajagopalan, Hang Ten had already signed several multi-million-dollar advisory and software-development contracts just four months after founding — which is why its two seed rounds closed only five weeks apart.

## What this round signals

### What it means for the agent ecosystem

The new capital is earmarked for three things: expanding delivery capacity for engagements already underway, growing the engineering and consulting teams, and continuing to build out the platform, infrastructure, and agentic skills library. Notably, Hang Ten is entering an already-crowded field — model companies like OpenAI and Anthropic are pushing into enterprise services themselves, and traditional consulting firms are folding generative AI into their existing offerings. Hang Ten's bet is that there's still room in the middle ground: rebuilding systems-integration outsourcing around agents can deliver faster and cheaper than either selling raw model APIs or selling traditional consulting headcount.

### What investors are betting on

Lead investor Xora, Temasek's early-stage platform, framed the logic through partner Phil Inagaki: "Enterprise technology has long been one of the largest cost lines in essential industries — slow to change, and slower still to translate AI into real economic value." The follow-on list includes Intel CEO Lip-Bu Tan, Micron CEO Sanjay Mehrotra, and Yahoo co-founder Jerry Yang, all joining as individual angels rather than through funds — a signal that, to some extent, vouches for the credibility Sikka built up at Infosys, SAP, and VianAI, rather than being purely a bet on this round's financial numbers.

### Numbers worth watching

- The two seed rounds closed just five weeks apart, totaling $85M — an unusually fast fundraising pace for a company four months old
- With only 20-25 employees, the company has already signed several multi-million-dollar contracts, pointing to a high-price, low-headcount consulting-delivery model rather than the traditional systems-integrator approach of staffing up with engineers
- Sikka's previous company, VianAI, took from a 2019 seed of $50M to a 2021 SoftBank-led round of $140M; by comparison, Hang Ten covered a similar amount of early-stage fundraising in four months — roughly an order of magnitude faster

## Today's takeaway

The usual assumption about "AI consulting firms" is that they're traditional consultancies with an AI-tooling shell bolted on. Hang Ten's skills-library design is a reminder that if a company can genuinely turn the agentic capability built up on each client engagement into a reusable asset — instead of restaffing a new team of engineers from scratch every time — the scaling curve of the entire consulting industry could get rewritten. That's also likely why chief executives at Intel and Micron were willing to place personal angel bets rather than wait for a longer revenue track record before stepping in.
