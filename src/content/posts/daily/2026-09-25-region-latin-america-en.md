## Region: Latin America

This week brought concrete moves across three layers of Latin America's AI landscape at once — corporate, policy, and cross-regional cooperation. This is the first time this series covers Latin America, and the timing follows three clear "firsts" landing in the same week: a Silicon Valley unicorn setting up its first local office, Chile's Congress passing its first reading of an anti-deepfake bill, and the EU launching its first transatlantic AI supercomputing network.

## Key Developments This Week

### AI Coding Startup Cognition (Devin) Opens Its First Latin American Office in São Paulo

Cognition, the AI coding startup valued at $48 billion, announced on September 22 that it is opening its first Latin American office in São Paulo, Brazil, after already landing more than ten major Brazilian enterprise clients remotely — including Nubank, Itaú, and Santander, some of the region's largest banks and consumer brands. Cognition's official blog frames the move as a long-term bet on Latin America's software engineering future, not a one-off market test. ([Cognition blog](https://cognition.com/blog/devin-comes-to-sao-paulo) · [Valor International](https://valorinternational.globo.com/business/news/2026/09/18/cognition-bets-on-brazil-as-ai-expansion-accelerates.ghtml))

### Chile's Congress Passes Anti-Deepfake Bill on First Reading; Brazil and Paraguay Advance Similar AI Legislation

Chile's Chamber of Deputies passed, on first reading, a bill regulating AI-generated content and penalizing deepfakes by a vote of 128 in favor, 2 against, and 5 abstentions in mid-September. The bill sets fines of up to 716 million Chilean pesos and extends digital-identity rights to protect a person's likeness even after death — but the regulator overseeing enforcement isn't expected to be operational until December 2026, and a separate proposal would push the law's effective date back a full year, from December 1, 2026 to December 1, 2027. Around the same time, Brazil and Paraguay are advancing their own deepfake and personal-data-related AI legislation, while Ecuador and Mexico are also adjusting their digital rules. ([CiberLATAM](https://ciberlatam.com/noticias/chile-aprueba-ley-antideepfakes-4aea667a) · [Tierra Marillano](https://tierramarillano.cl/2026/09/18/72-horas-para-borrar-una-mentira-asi-operaria-la-ley-antideepfakes-de-chile/) · [PyMEnoticias](https://www.pymenoticias.com/post/ley-antideepfakes-en-chile-el-proyecto-avanza-pero-la-agencia-que-debe-vigilarla-podr%C3%ADa-llegar-un))

### EU Launches EU-LAC Supercomputing Network, Linking 7 Latin American Countries for AI Cooperation

The European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking (EuroHPC JU) formally launched the EU-LAC AI Supercomputing Network on September 22 at the CARLA conference in Córdoba, Argentina, as part of the EU's Global Gateway strategy. The two-year initiative spans seven Latin American countries — Argentina, Brazil, Chile, Colombia, Costa Rica, Mexico, and Uruguay — covering both high-performance computing and AI infrastructure cooperation, and aligns with national programs in Spain and Brazil. ([EuroHPC JU announcement](https://www.eurohpc-ju.europa.eu/europe-and-latin-america-strengthen-digital-ties-high-performance-computing-and-ai-2026-09-23_en) · [HPCwire](https://www.hpcwire.com/off-the-wire/eurohpc-builds-hpc-and-ai-ties-with-latin-america-and-the-caribbean/))

## Deep Dive

I believe these three stories are best read together through the "Political" and "Technological" dimensions of the PESTEL framework, since Latin America saw concrete movement on both dimensions this week — pointing in somewhat different directions.

**Political dimension**: Chile, Brazil, and Paraguay advancing AI/deepfake legislation simultaneously shows major Latin American economies converging on regulatory timing. But Chile's case also reveals a gap between "bill passed" and "regulator actually operational" — the regulator won't exist until December, and might even be delayed by another year. That's an important signal for any AI company eyeing the Latin American market: the rules are taking shape fast, but enforcement timing and rigor remain highly uncertain, so the cost-benefit of getting ahead on compliance needs re-evaluating.

**Technological dimension**: Cognition's choice of São Paulo and the seven-country list covered by the EU-LAC supercomputing network overlap heavily — Brazil, Argentina, Chile, Colombia, and Mexico all appear in both. This shows that whether it's a Silicon Valley unicorn's commercial expansion or the EU's cross-border infrastructure investment, both are targeting the same cluster of Latin American economies that already have meaningful digital-economy scale, rather than spreading evenly across the whole region. This resembles the "Singapore magnet" dynamic seen in Southeast Asia, except in Latin America the magnet pulls toward a handful of large economies rather than a single city-state.

## Takeaways for Taiwanese Entrepreneurs

- **If you're building AI compliance or content-governance tools**: Chile's gap — bill passed, but the regulator not operational for six months to a year — is a window worth positioning for. Getting ahead with compliance consulting or content-moderation tools before the regulator starts enforcing gives a first-mover edge over waiting until the rules are fully in force. The same pattern is worth watching in Taiwan's own AI-related legislative timeline
- **If you're building enterprise AI adoption services targeting Latin American banks or financial firms**: Cognition's approach — landing flagship clients like Nubank, Itaú, and Santander first, then setting up a local office — shows that the market-entry playbook in Latin America is "prove yourself with marquee clients first, localize later," not the reverse. Taiwanese teams building enterprise AI services could test the waters by serving Latin America's flagship clients remotely first, validating demand before deciding whether to formally enter the market
- **If you're building cross-border data or compute infrastructure**: The seven countries covered by the EU-LAC supercomputing network are essentially the current shortlist of where Latin America's AI infrastructure investment is concentrated. Taiwanese supply-chain companies already serving global compute infrastructure customers, like TSMC and Quanta, can treat this list as a reference for where Latin American compute demand is likely to grow next

## Key Insight

I used to think Latin America's AI story mostly revolved around a single narrative — "Brazil is the region's largest market, and others follow." After reading these three stories this week, I realized the more accurate picture is that Silicon Valley capital (Cognition), EU infrastructure investment (EU-LAC), and local legislation (Chile/Brazil/Paraguay) all advanced a step in nearly the same week — and the countries targeted by commercial and infrastructure investment overlap heavily. That means Latin America's "magnet effect" isn't just about capital flows — it's also happening on the regulatory and infrastructure-investment front. For Taiwanese entrepreneurs, understanding which handful of countries form Latin America's core cluster may be more useful than trying to understand the entire region at once.
