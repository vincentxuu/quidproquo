## What the Company Does

OneByZero (OBZ) builds enterprise AI deployment and governance infrastructure — it focuses on taking generative AI stuck at the pilot stage inside large enterprises and turning it into governed "AI Coworkers" that actually run in production systems.

Its core product is the NEO platform plus a set of pre-built AI Coworkers — for example Jenny, which handles social media customer response, an ETL Crew that coordinates data migration, and Tara Reyes, which screens recruiting pipelines. Each role maps to a specific enterprise function, and OBZ's "forward-deployed engineers" customize, ship, and continuously tune them inside each customer's environment. NEO's core pitch is its governance layer: enterprises define what AI is allowed to do, where human judgment must stay in the loop, and every agent action leaves an audit trail — which is what lets OBZ get approved to operate inside highly regulated industries like finance and telecom, instead of staying stuck at proof-of-concept.

Over the past three years, OBZ has deployed inside financial services, telecom, and retail enterprises. The company says some customers have automated more than 90% of customer-facing interactions and cut certain data migration delivery times by 50%, with revenue growing more than 100% year-over-year for three straight years. It currently operates across nine markets — Australia, India, Indonesia, Malaysia, the Philippines, Singapore, Thailand, the US, and Vietnam — and is one of 22 global partners in AWS's GenAI Innovation Centre.

## What This Round Signals

### What It Means for the Agent Ecosystem

OBZ represents the validation of a "consulting plus agent platform" hybrid model in Southeast Asia's enterprise AI market. Rather than selling a self-serve agent builder, OBZ's approach of embedding agents into a customer's existing systems through its own engineering team looks more like a traditional systems integrator — except what it delivers is agents, not customized software. That reflects where demand actually sits right now: enterprises don't want more tools, they want someone to put agents into production and be accountable for the outcome.

### What Investors Are Betting On

Jungle Ventures managing partner Yash Sankrityayan's comment centers on "three years of real-world execution" — the thesis isn't the technology itself, but OBZ's track record of deploying AI inside large, complex organizations and showing verifiable results, in contrast to most agent startups still at the seed stage telling their story through demos. This also fits Jungle Ventures' long-standing focus on Southeast Asian SMEs and regional category leaders, rather than chasing Silicon Valley-style high-burn growth.

### Numbers Worth Watching

- The company says revenue has grown more than 100% year-over-year for three consecutive years — a high growth rate for a service-intensive category like enterprise AI deployment
- $20M is the company's first external raise in three years of operation, meaning it was entirely self-funded by revenue until now — a "profitable first, raise later" path that's uncommon among agent startups
- Post-raise plans to enter Japan and expand into healthcare and the public sector signal a shift from "private enterprise adoption" toward "highly regulated industries" — a path similar to the one Valon is taking in mortgage servicing

## Watchlist Status

OneByZero is not currently on the watchlist. Recommend adding it to section D10, alongside Moveworks, Glean, ServiceNow, and UiPath — tracking whether this "forward-deployed consulting team plus governed agent platform" hybrid can hold its margins at scale, or gets pulled toward the lower-margin structure of pure consulting.

## Today's Takeaway

Most enterprise agent startups pitch "our platform lets you deploy agents yourself." OneByZero's pitch runs the other way: "we put engineers inside your systems, and we own accountability for the agent that goes live." This round suggests at least one class of buyer — highly regulated, large, resource-constrained enterprises — is more willing to pay for "someone who sees it through" than for "a better tool."
