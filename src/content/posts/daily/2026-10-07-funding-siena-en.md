## What the Company Does

Siena builds conversational agents for consumer brands — it started as an empathic AI agent handling support tickets, and now wants to become the "Agent of Record" shared across every customer interaction a brand has.

The core logic: brands already run on systems of record — an ERP records orders, Shopify records the storefront, another system records loyalty points — but none of them records the relationship itself: what the customer said, why she returned something, what she asked in a DM last Tuesday. Siena Intelligence pulls conversations, orders, subscriptions, reviews, and loyalty data together into shared customer context, and Siena Memory carries that context forward so a customer moving from support to shopping to social to a phone call never has to re-introduce herself. The company has extended this logic from its original support agent into a Shopping Agent (product recommendations and checkout help), a Social Agent (comment and DM moderation and engagement), and a Quality Agent (evaluating agent work).

Current customers include hundreds of consumer brands such as SPANX, FIGS, HexClad, and Comfrt, with hundreds of millions of customer interactions automated to date. At Eskiin, the Social Agent lifted weekly comment engagement from roughly 1,600 to 2,400; at SPANX, Siena surfaced a shipping-complaint pattern tied to a carrier issue in one specific region.

## What This Round Signals

### What It Means for the Agent Ecosystem

Siena's narrative reflects consolidation in the customer-service agent category toward a "unified customer-context layer." Support, social, and marketing used to each run their own tools, each holding a slice of customer data. Siena's bet is that since it's already handling customer interactions anyway, it should build every channel's agent on one shared memory rather than let brands stitch tools together and build their own middleware. This echoes a broader pattern across the enterprise agent market: single-function agents are consolidating into platform-style "agent operating systems."

### What Investors Are Betting On

York IE general partner Tom Holahan's comment — that Siena "can become as essential to running a brand as its commerce platform" — points to a thesis that isn't about any single feature's conversion rate. It's a bet that Siena can lock in a position as core infrastructure, on the same tier as Shopify, as the brand's "customer relationship system." The risk in this kind of bet is that Siena has to win four fronts at once — support, social, shopping, and voice — against incumbents in each, rather than build a moat through one feature.

### Numbers Worth Watching

- The Social Agent lifted weekly comment engagement at Eskiin from 1,600 to 2,400 (+50%) and cut weekend reply times from 48 hours to under a minute — a concrete number for an agent directly replacing human shift coverage
- The company doesn't charge per seat, instead pricing on outcomes and context usage — meaning a customer's bill doesn't spike just from adding headcount as agent usage scales, a pricing shift increasingly common in the customer-service agent category
- Getting approval from Meta and TikTok to connect social conversations to customer context took six months, showing platforms still gate "handing customer conversations to a third-party agent" — a hidden cost for any other player trying to replicate the Social Agent model

## Watchlist Status

Siena is not currently on the watchlist. Recommend adding it to section D1 (Customer Service/CX) — tracking point: whether Siena's expansion from a single support agent into a cross-channel "Agent of Record" platform builds a real moat around shared customer memory, or ultimately gets picked apart by point solutions in each channel.

## Today's Takeaway

Most customer-service agent startups pitch "we do customer service better." Siena's pitch runs the other way: "customer service is just our first channel into owning customer understanding." This round suggests investors are willing to pay for the bigger prize of "who holds the shared memory of the customer relationship," not just for the efficiency of automating one function.
