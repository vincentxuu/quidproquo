## What the company does

Gallatin AI builds decision-support software for military logistics — digitizing U.S. military resupply data that today mostly lives on clipboards and whiteboards into something commanders can see and plan against in real time.

Its flagship product, Navigator, connects data and workflows across fuel, medical, and munitions supply chains so commanders get one unified view of readiness instead of checking separate systems one by one. CEO Woody Glier has described the status quo bluntly: "A lot of the logistics data that is generated today is very manual — it's very messy, literally like clipboard and pen and paper."

Navigator is already deployed with multiple U.S. Army and Air Force units, and the company landed a contract with the Pentagon's Defense Innovation Unit this summer. Founded in 2024 and out of stealth since early 2025, Gallatin has grown its total funding to $70M in under two years.

## What this round signals

### What it means for the agent ecosystem

Defense tech's capital has mostly chased hardware over the past few years — drones, autonomous vehicles. Gallatin's raise signals that AI for logistics data and decision workflows is now being treated as an equally strategic capability: the Pentagon itself has named "contested logistics" a critical technology area, alongside biomanufacturing and hypersonic weapons.

### What investors are betting on

8VC is a familiar name in defense tech, with a track record behind companies built in a similar mold (Palantir, Anduril). The thesis: even a battlefield with thousands of autonomous vehicles still needs someone — or some system — deciding what gets loaded, who pulls it off the shelf, and who gets it onto the truck or ship. Vehicles may be getting more autonomous, but the supply decision chain behind them hasn't caught up yet.

### Numbers worth watching

- Founded in 2024, Gallatin has reached $70M in total funding in under two years — a sign defense tech investors are more patient with software-layer logistics tools than typical B2B SaaS timelines
- Navigator is already validated across multiple branches (Army and Air Force), a rarer feat for a defense logistics startup
- The DIU contract this summer means the product has cleared the first screening gate of U.S. military innovation procurement, setting up a path to larger purchases

## Today's takeaway

I used to think defense tech's capital story was all hardware — drones, autonomous vehicles, missile defense. Gallatin is a reminder that the more autonomous the vehicles get, the more it matters who's deciding what gets resupplied and when — a software decision layer that's been overlooked, and is now filling a strategic gap the Pentagon itself has flagged.
