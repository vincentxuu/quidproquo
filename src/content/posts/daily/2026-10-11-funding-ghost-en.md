## What the Company Does

Ghost builds a personal computer made specifically to run AI agents — instead of running your agent in the cloud, it sells a piece of hardware you own and keep at home, running the agent around the clock.

Its flagship product, Core, is a screenless $3,499 desktop box with an Nvidia RTX Pro 4000 SFF Blackwell GPU built in, preloaded with three models — Qwen-3.8-Next, Qwen-3.8-27B, and Gemma-4-31B — and controlled through a phone app or voice. Core centralizes a person's desktop files, apps, and smart-home data onto the device so an agent can continuously understand the user's life and act on their behalf; every model weight, memory, and piece of personal data stays on the device itself, and the company says it keeps running even if Ghost the company shuts down.

Core opened for preorder on October 5, and the first batch sold out within hours. Founder Zain Javaid is 19 and previously worked as a quant; the rest of the founding team is also in their twenties.

## What This Round Signals

### What It Means for the Agent Ecosystem

Most agents today run on cloud servers controlled by large tech companies. Ghost is betting that once users start handing agents their personal data, credentials, and smart-home controls, "who owns this device and where the data lives" becomes as important a selling point as what the agent can actually do.

### What Investors Are Betting On

a16z's bet here is a "local compute plus privacy" story — as agents get granted more autonomy over money, credentials, and device control, keeping compute on hardware the user actually owns may hold up better long-term than staying dependent on cloud providers, a logic similar to their past bets on consumer hardware and decentralized compute.

### Numbers Worth Watching

- An $11M seed round is modest for a hardware company, but the first preorder batch sold out within hours — evidence that at least some early adopters will pay for an agent they actually own
- The $3,499 price already bundles in the GPU cost, with no separate graphics card purchase needed — a pricing bet against high-end desktops that can run local models but weren't built specifically for agents
- A founding team averaging around 21 years old is a rare case of a Gen Z team directly taking on the hardware and AI infrastructure layer

## Watchlist Status

Ghost isn't on the watchlist yet. Recommend adding it under section B8 (sandboxes / execution environments), tracking focus: consumer-grade agent-specific hardware, emphasizing on-device execution and a privacy pitch that doesn't depend on the cloud.

## Today's Takeaway

I assumed "agent hardware" would just be a side accessory that cloud providers sell. Ghost's first batch selling out in hours suggests that handing compute, memory, and data ownership back to the user could be an underrated path alongside cloud-based agents.
