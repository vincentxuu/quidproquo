## What the Company Does

Catalyst builds agents for retail trading: a user states an investing intent in plain language, and the agent turns it into an executable trading strategy, firing only after the user approves.

Users set risk parameters to constrain what the agent can do; the agent handles instrument selection, cost optimization, and execution timing. Co-founder Dylan Iskandar framed the goal as making sure "a farmer shouldn't need a hedge fund to be able to hedge" — bringing complexity that used to be reserved for professional desks to everyday retail investors.

The company was founded in 2025 by 25-year-old Justin Zheng and 21-year-old Dylan Iskandar. It has already run a pilot with power traders that the company says generated hundreds of millions of dollars in trading volume within a few weeks, and is now letting people off the waitlist as it opens up more broadly.

## What This Round Signals

### What It Means for the Agent Ecosystem

Catalyst is one of the largest early-stage bets so far in the "agent as investment advisor and execution layer" category. Sequoia partner George Robson described the agent as "a suit of armor as you navigate the financial world" — a sign that wealth management is shifting from giving advice toward agent-driven "intent to execution."

### What Investors Are Betting On

Sequoia recently led a $60M Series B for robotics-data company Mecka, underscoring its continued conviction in agents that make complex systems operable. The presence of trading-infrastructure players like Coinbase Ventures and Jump Trading among the co-investors suggests they see Catalyst as a potential distribution or execution partner, not just a financial bet.

### Numbers Worth Watching

- The pilot generated "hundreds of millions" in trading volume within a few weeks (exact figure undisclosed), validating retail appetite for agent-executed trades
- A company only a few months old landed a $30M seed led by Sequoia — one of the largest seed rounds in fintech agents this year
- Two trading-infrastructure firms (Coinbase, Jump Trading) joined the round, a notably higher level of strategic industry participation than a typical seed round

## Today's Takeaway

Most outlets call this a seed round, but at least one source called it Series A — a reminder that round labels at the earliest stage are getting blurry, since seed amounts are now approaching traditional Series A sizes. The dollar amount and the investor roster are more reliable signals than the label on the round.
