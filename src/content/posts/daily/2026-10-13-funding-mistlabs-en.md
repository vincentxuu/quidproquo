## What the Company Does

Mistlabs builds "ambient intelligence." Its product, Ambi, is designed to stay present across a user's devices and think ahead on its own, rather than an assistant that waits to be asked.

Ambi continuously builds context about the user across iPhone, Apple Watch, and Mac, so it can finish analysis or draft work before the user even asks. In the coming months, the company plans to roll out "agent-to-agent communication": when two users' Ambi agents are both present in the same meeting, each can negotiate on behalf of its own user's priorities to help find common ground.

Ambi just came out of beta, launching first on Apple devices, with no revenue figures disclosed yet. Next, the company plans to build its own wearable hardware, and this round will largely fund that hardware development.

## What This Round Signals

### What It Means for the Agent Ecosystem

Ambi moves "agent-to-agent communication" out of back-end multi-agent frameworks and into a consumer product. If agents representing different users can really negotiate on their behalf, it would be the first concrete consumer-facing instance of a multi-agent system — not just internal enterprise workflow automation.

### What Investors Are Betting On

Huaqin Technology, a hardware contract manufacturer, co-leading the round signals this is also about securing supply chain and manufacturing capacity for Mistlabs' future wearable, not a purely financial bet. Luminous Ventures is betting that "ambient intelligence" will be the next consumer AI product category after chatbots.

### Numbers Worth Watching

- A $26M Pre-A — between seed and Series A in size — shows investors staking a claim on the hardware race before the product has proven revenue at scale
- The company is building both software (the Ambi app) and hardware (its own wearable) at once, which puts more pressure on capital efficiency than a pure software company would face
- This is the only AI round today co-led directly by a hardware contract manufacturer, suggesting supply-chain players are getting into consumer AI wearables as early as the Pre-A stage

## Today's Takeaway

When a contract manufacturer shows up directly on the investor list, it usually isn't a bet on the company's software — it's a claim staked on future hardware orders. That's a different logic from a pure VC check, and worth watching for in the consumer AI hardware space.
