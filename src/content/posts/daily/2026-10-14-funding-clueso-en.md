## What the Company Does

Clueso automates enterprise video production. Instead of a human editing team, a set of AI agents operates the software directly, records the workflow on screen, then writes the script, generates the voiceover, and edits the footage into a brand-consistent tutorial or marketing video.

The product targets the high-volume, repetitive video demand inside companies — product tutorials, customer onboarding, training, and internal documentation — rather than consumer-facing text-to-video generation. Clueso's agents first learn a company's existing products, workflows, and brand style, then keep videos updated automatically as the underlying software or documentation changes, instead of producing one-off static files. The platform also supports MCP, so AI assistants like Claude and ChatGPT can create, edit, and manage videos directly through Clueso.

Founded in 2023 by three IIT Madras alumni — Akash Anand, Prajwal Prakash, and Neel Balar — Clueso grew from roughly 330 customers a year ago to more than 1,000 today, including Atlassian, Zendesk, Gainsight, Seismic, and Ironclad. The company says customers report productivity gains of more than 5x and an 80% reduction in video production costs.

## What This Round Signals

### What it means for the agent ecosystem

This round confirms that "agents operating existing software interfaces to generate content" is spreading beyond browser automation and coding into a more vertical, brand-sensitive domain: video production. Clueso plans to use the funds to deepen its agentic layer, expand editing capabilities, and tie video creation more tightly to other internal knowledge sources — which suggests the next competitive axis isn't "how good does the video look," but whether the agent can keep reading a company's constantly changing products and documentation.

### What investors are betting on

Lead investor Dharana Capital is an India-focused growth fund whose portfolio includes Urban Company, MyGate, Vyapar, and Petpooja, and which closed a $250M second India growth fund earlier this year. Backing an early-stage AI startup with a dual US-India HQ is unusual for this fund, suggesting it's extending its thesis from India consumer and SaaS bets into cross-border AI agent companies. Just as notable: more than 80% of the new capital came from existing investors (Y Combinator, f7 Ventures) doubling down — that kind of "existing backers writing the next check" signal typically says more about confidence in the growth curve than a first-time financial investor entering cold.

### Numbers worth watching

- Customer count grew roughly 3x in a year (from ~330 to 1,000+), while total funding grew from about $2M to $12M — growth is clearly outpacing capital burn
- More than 80% of the Series A came from existing investors rather than a new lead setting the price
- Customers self-report 5x+ productivity gains and an 80% drop in production costs — if those numbers hold up, they point to a real shift in enterprise content-production cost structure, not just marketing copy

## Takeaway

I'd assumed capital in "AI video generation" was concentrated on consumer-facing text-to-video tools (Runway, Pika, Luma and similar). Clueso's case shows there's a narrower but stickier demand on the enterprise side — agents that keep tutorial videos in sync with a constantly changing product UI, rather than producing one-off assets. That "self-updating as the system evolves" content model may matter more for customer retention than raw generation quality.
