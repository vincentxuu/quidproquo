## What the Company Does

Fragment builds money-movement infrastructure for agents: when an AI agent handles billing and accounting, Fragment uses double-entry bookkeeping to check that every dollar's source and destination balance before any money actually moves.

Developers plug Fragment's tools into their software. CEO Thomas Neckel's analogy: Claude Code and Cursor are building blocks that let agents automate coding, and Fragment is the set of financial building blocks that lets agents automate commerce. The point isn't making agents better at paying — it's checking that the books balance before they pay, to cut the cost of accounting errors.

The company has roughly 50 to 60 paying customers, including corporate card startup Ramp, financial software company BILL and digital marketplace Whop, which use Fragment as a shared transaction record and spend less staff time checking that numbers match across financial systems. It was co-founded by CEO Thomas Neckel and CTO Varun Mohan, a former Robinhood engineer.

## What This Round Signals

### What it means for the agent ecosystem

Agentic commerce infrastructure is being filled in layer by layer: Visa partnered with OpenAI in June to let agents shop within user-set limits, Mastercard launched a service for agents to pay each other, and Stripe and Tempo released a standard in March for agents to pay for tools and data. Those answer "how does an agent pay." Fragment answers "are the books right before it pays." The more cases of agents deleting databases or escaping test environments, the more companies need a verifiable check before money moves.

### What the investors are betting on

FirstMark managing partner Adam Nelson describes the goal as becoming the standard toolkit anyone uses to give their agents financial capabilities. The seed round was led by Stripe, which suggests payment companies themselves see the need for this layer. Note that this round closed in mid-2025 and is only being announced now, riding the current wave of attention on agentic commerce.

### Numbers to watch

- Roughly 50–60 paying customers, including Ramp, BILL and Whop
- $11M seed plus a $12M Series A, nearly the same size, which reads as continued validation rather than scaling
- More than a year between closing and announcing; the next round depends on whether customers and transaction volume keep pace with agentic commerce

## Watchlist Status

Fragment is not yet on the watchlist. Suggest adding to section D6 (financial services): it is the reconciliation and ledger layer for agent payments, complementary to the agent-payment protocols from Visa, Mastercard and Stripe.

## Today's Takeaway

I assumed the problem with agent payments was mostly authorization and limits. Fragment's approach is a reminder that even with the right authorization, books that don't balance are still a loss. The more money an agent can move, the more "reconcile first" looks like a basic control on par with permissions.
