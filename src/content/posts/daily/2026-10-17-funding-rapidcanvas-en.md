## What the company does

RapidCanvas calls itself a "managed AI execution" company — it doesn't just sell a platform, it bundles the platform with field AI engineers and commits to delivering custom AI solutions at SaaS-level economics.

Three products make up the stack: the Enterprise Context Engine captures a business's processes — requirements, tribal knowledge, data schemas, decision history — as an asset the enterprise owns outright; the Visual Development Canvas lets AI engineers build custom applications fast, drawing on a skills library that gives each new project roughly a 60% head start from already-benchmarked code; and the Operational Harness governs and monitors solutions once they're live in production. The bet behind this "Hybrid Approach" is that enterprises don't want another tool they have to figure out themselves — they want someone who delivers a result and stays accountable for it.

The target customer sits between $100M and $10B in annual revenue — companies with AI budgets and C-suite mandates but no proven path to outcomes, caught between pricey, slow-moving services firms and DIY platforms that assume in-house AI talent they don't have. More than 75 enterprise customers across manufacturing, financial services, retail, business services, and infrastructure have made 1.4 million AI-driven decisions on RapidCanvas-built applications so far in 2026, and over 40% of customers now run multiple AI use cases in production simultaneously. Annual recurring revenue has grown 8x over the past six quarters.

## What this round signals

### What it means for the agent ecosystem

The new capital goes to three places: scaling go-to-market and the network of private-equity and advisory partners who resell managed AI execution directly into their portfolio companies and clients; growing the field AI engineering team across the US, Brazil, and India; and deepening all three products, especially the governance layer in the Operational Harness. That allocation says something about where enterprise AI competition is actually happening — it's shifted from model capability to delivery capability. CEO Rahul Pangam put it plainly: "Vibe coding has made it easy to start building with AI. Getting AI into production reliably and safely still remains the biggest gap." That's exactly the gap a product like the Operational Harness exists to close, and it's why RapidCanvas positions itself as "human-led, agent-executed" rather than a pure automation platform.

### What investors are betting on

Vertex Ventures SEA & India has historically focused on early-stage bets in Southeast Asia and India (Grab, FirstCry, and Nium are its best-known wins), so leading a Series B for a US-headquartered enterprise company is unusual for the fund — it suggests Vertex wants to carry its "specialized AI applications for vertical use cases" thesis, already tested on India's Spyne (an AI platform for auto dealerships), into the US enterprise market. Hitachi Ventures investment director Radhika Ananth's comments spell out a sharper thesis of her own, which the firm calls "AInomics": enterprise AI will ultimately be judged by the economics of delivering outcomes at scale, and RapidCanvas is turning labor-intensive custom delivery into something repeatable and scalable — a shift from services economics to software economics. That also explains why a Japanese industrial-and-infrastructure-focused strategic investor is co-investing in an AI platform company.

### Numbers worth watching

- 8x ARR growth over six quarters is the most concrete growth signal in this round. Enterprise AI companies usually lead with customer-count growth; RapidCanvas chose to disclose ARR multiples instead, which says more about whether customers keep paying than about how many contracts got signed.
- Over 40% of customers now run multiple AI use cases in production — a harder-to-achieve expansion signal than new-customer growth, and a rare one in enterprise AI. It means that once the first use case proves out, customers hand more of their processes to the same agent vendor instead of re-evaluating competitors each time.
- This $29M Series B lands roughly two years after the $16M Series A in December 2024 — about 1.8x the prior round's size — but RapidCanvas chose not to disclose a valuation, breaking from the pattern of enterprise AI companies loudly touting valuation multiples. That suggests the company would rather anchor its narrative to delivered results than to how the market is pricing it.

## Watchlist status

RapidCanvas isn't on the watchlist yet. Recommend adding it to section D10 (Enterprise AI Platforms, currently Moveworks and Glean), tracking: the "AI platform plus field AI engineers" hybrid delivery model (distinct from D10's current self-serve, pure-software incumbents), 8x ARR growth, and the 40%-plus multi-use-case expansion rate.

## Takeaway

The most interesting thing about this round isn't the amount — it's that RapidCanvas deliberately withheld its valuation. While peers lean on valuation multiples to tell their story, a company that makes "8x ARR growth" and "40% of customers running multiple use cases" its only public numbers is betting that the ability to deliver results will convince the next round of investors more than how the market prices it today.
