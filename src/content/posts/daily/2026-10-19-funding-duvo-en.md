## What this company does

Duvo builds what it calls an "Enterprise Execution OS" — not another chat-first agent, but a layer where employees and AI agents run a company's day-to-day processes together, replacing the gray zone where staff manually shuttle data between ERPs, supplier portals, spreadsheets, and inboxes.

The core product, Duvo Clarity, starts by figuring out how a process actually runs in practice — through AI-led interviews, screen recordings, and existing documentation — including the exceptions that live only in employees' heads and never made it into any SOP. Agents then map that process to automatable steps, deciding what can run unattended and what needs a human approval. Execution happens through existing APIs where they exist, or directly through the same screens people already use where they don't — letting a company go live with its first process in weeks, without a major systems-integration project. Duvo positions itself as model-agnostic: the underlying AI can be swapped without rebuilding the workflow on top of it, and companies with stricter security or sovereignty needs can run open-weight models on their own infrastructure. The platform holds SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001, and ISO 42001 certifications.

Customers include UK supermarket chain Morrisons, health retailer Holland & Barrett, apparel brand Boden, European beauty e-commerce leader Notino, and marketplace Allegro. Duvo says it has unlocked more than $100M in value for clients — at Notino, a roughly $45M/year supplier-bonus reconciliation process went from 70 days to 15, cutting specialist hours per cycle from about 120 to under 10. Founder and CEO Tomáš Čupr is also founder and Group CEO of European online grocer Rohlik Group (valued at roughly $2B); Duvo grew directly out of the automation problems he hit while running Rohlik.

## What this round signals

### What it means for the agent ecosystem

The headline here isn't "a smarter agent" — it's "an agent that survives." Duvo's announcement directly cites Gartner's forecast: spending on business orchestration and automation technology will exceed $21B by 2029, yet more than 40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled by the end of 2027 due to rising costs, unclear business value, and inadequate risk controls. Duvo's bet is to position itself as the layer where every process shares the same connections, the same approval rules, and the same record of results — rather than each department running its own isolated agent pilot. That's a wager that the integration layer, not the agent itself, is the scarcer resource. The $32M is earmarked mainly for US expansion, starting with retail and CPG, led by former Kmart president and Accenture managing director Spencer Insolia, with former ALDI SÜD Holding CEO Christoph Schwaiger joining as senior advisor.

### What the investor is betting on

Balderton Capital's past bets include Darktrace (security AI), Revolut, and GoCardless, and in the enterprise-AI layer specifically, Writer — which happens to sit in this site's own watchlist under A2 (AI model/platform companies). Lead partner Rob Moffat's framing is that "getting AI agents to work reliably across the enterprise remains incredibly difficult," and what he's underwriting is Čupr's operating track record — three companies founded, including Rohlik — rather than a purely technical résumé. That lines up with Duvo's own thesis: the bottleneck isn't model capability, it's whether anyone actually understands the undocumented exceptions buried inside enterprise workflows. Duvo has also struck an alliance with Deloitte Central Europe, letting the consultancy use Duvo Clarity to scope automation opportunities for its clients — a go-to-market pattern that echoes how UiPath distributed through systems integrators early on.

### Numbers worth tracking

- $48M total raised is more than 3x the prior round ($15M Seed, December 2025) in just 10 months — a sign of how fast investors are leaning into the "agent execution layer" narrative
- Notino's supplier-bonus reconciliation: 70 days → 15 days, 120 hours → under 10 hours per cycle — the most concrete, verifiable productivity number in this round's announcement
- $100M+ in claimed customer value against ~70 employees works out to more than $1.4M in client-side value per employee — a rough quantification of Čupr's "raise every employee's GDP" framing

## Today's takeaway

Most agent-funding stories still measure success in "how many people this agent replaces." Duvo's founder says that's the wrong unit — he uses "GDP per employee" instead: not fewer people doing the same work, but the same people doing work they never had time for before (checking inventory risk shifts from "eight products a day per market" to "every signal, around the clock"). That framing matters for two reasons: it's how Duvo sidesteps the internal political resistance that "replacing employees" triggers, and it maps directly onto why Gartner expects 40% of agentic AI projects to get canceled — most of them don't fail because the model wasn't strong enough, they fail because nobody could tell the person signing the check what the agent actually produced.
