## What this company does

Pigment builds an AI-driven enterprise planning and performance management platform, aiming to replace the Excel spreadsheets and PowerPoint decks still running most finance departments. It connects finance, sales, and workforce planning data into a single platform, then lets AI agents build models, analyze data, and execute planning work directly on top of it.

The company was founded in Paris in 2019 by Eléonore Crespo, a former Google financial analyst and later an investor at Index Ventures, and Romain Niccoli, co-founder and former CTO of ad-tech company Criteo. Its technical core is a proprietary, patent-pending compute engine called Graphite, which lets thousands of agents and people work on the same governed model at once, scaling to hundreds of billions of data points while keeping results traceable. The company says 85% of new metrics on the platform are now created by an agent — a figure that comes from Pigment itself, not an independent audit.

Customers include Danone, DirecTV, Siemens, Snowflake, ServiceNow, and Unilever, alongside Anthropic, ElevenLabs, and Uber, all of which run their own financial planning on Pigment. L'Oréal recently chose Pigment to replace its salary planning tool for more than 95,000 employees — the largest deployment disclosed so far. ARR passed $100M this year and doubled year-over-year; co-founder Crespo told Bloomberg she expects it to double again next year, putting it near $200M.

## What this round signals

### What it means for the agent ecosystem

Pigment's pitch is agent-driven disruption of a market that's anything but new: enterprise performance management (EPM), long dominated by SAP, Oracle, IBM, Anaplan, and Microsoft. Straits Research puts the EPM market at $7.51B in 2026, growing 10.4% a year — not exactly a hot, emerging category. Anaplan, one of the category's leading vendors, was taken private by Thoma Bravo for $10.7B back in 2022, a deal typically read as a sign of a maturing, slowing market. Pigment is effectively reopening the growth story in that same mature market by making "agents that execute the plan" the new axis of competition. Smaller AI-native rivals are emerging too — Fintastic ($12M seed) and Plinto (€2.9M pre-seed, building an agent that catches invoice errors) — suggesting agents are reshaping this vertical at both the platform and point-solution level simultaneously.

### What the investors are betting on

Lead investor Scaleup Europe Fund is an EU-backed vehicle targeting €5 billion, selected to be managed by EQT in May 2026. Pigment is its third major investment, following satellite company ICEYE (a $1B round co-led in August) and French AI lab Mistral (a €3B Series D co-led in September) — a pattern that suggests European public capital is now treating "globally competitive software companies born in Europe" as a strategic priority, not just a return target. EQT managing director Shannee Braun framed it this way: "In most companies, the richest picture of how a business actually runs sits in its plans... Pigment has built that foundation with a single platform connecting finance, sales and workforce planning, and put AI agents to work on it, helping teams model, analyze and act in hours rather than months." NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang had already announced, in Paris on October 13, that NVIDIA would invest in Pigment (without disclosing the amount) — a bet that the "agent execution" layer will need dedicated infrastructure support, much like GPU training does today.

### Numbers worth tracking

- A $2.3B valuation at roughly 23x current ARR is notably lower than legal AI startup Legora, which raised the same week at roughly 44x ARR ($200M ARR, $8.8B valuation) — suggesting the market still prices "planning agents" at a meaningful discount to "legal agents," with the EPM market's perceived ceiling capping the multiple
- A 2025 Gartner survey found 59% of finance leaders were using AI in their finance function, barely up from 58% the year before — adoption has essentially stalled. A separate March 2026 survey found only 20% of finance AI investment is aimed at "better decisions," versus 45% still aimed at "productivity" — Pigment's agent pitch is explicitly targeting that smaller 20% segment
- The gap between "85% of new metrics created by an agent" and "59% of finance leaders say they use AI at all" hints that Pigment's own customer base has embedded agents into core workflows far more deeply than the industry average — though again, that 85% figure is self-reported and unaudited

## Today's takeaway

Enterprise planning software used to compete on "how fast and how accurate is the report." Pigment's bet changes the axis entirely: whoever lets an agent turn a plan straight into action, without a human re-checking the numbers, wins. What's notable is that this bet carries a lower valuation premium than legal AI does — on the very same day, Legora (legal AI) priced at 44x ARR and Pigment (planning AI) priced at 23x ARR, a gap that shows the market still disagrees sharply about which professional judgment gets automated by agents first. Right now, legal is priced to get there faster than enterprise planning.
