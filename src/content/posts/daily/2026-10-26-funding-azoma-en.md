## What the Company Does

Azoma builds an "Agentic Commerce Optimization" (ACO) platform — in short, it helps brands get their products noticed, understood, and recommended by AI shopping agents. Think of it as an SEO firm, except the thing it optimizes for isn't Google search rankings. It's LLM applications like ChatGPT and Gemini, retailer-built assistants like Alexa for Shopping, Walmart Sparky, and Instacart Clementine, and personal AI agents like Meta Muse, ChatGPT Dots, and Instinct.

Its platform combines measurement, optimization, and execution in one place, and holds two granted patents covering AI brand monitoring and AI product content generation. Its Digital Twin technology simulates different types of AI shopper personas, letting a brand see exactly how a given type of buyer is shown its products rather than relying on a single generic visibility score. The platform also covers emerging agentic commerce protocols, including the Universal Commerce Protocol and the Agentic Commerce Protocol, extending its optimization work to the surfaces where transactions actually get completed.

Current customers include global consumer brands with large household-goods portfolios — L'Oréal, Unilever, Mars, Beiersdorf, and Reckitt — and Azoma has grown revenue almost tenfold over the past year. It also co-authored, with the Digital Shelf Institute, an industry framework now deployed by 40% of the 50 largest CPG brands: the "5 C's of Agentic Commerce" — Completeness, Context, Citations, Correctness, and Customer Acquisition.

## What This Round Signals

### What it means for the agent ecosystem

The new capital will expand Azoma's platform beyond the LLM applications and retailer assistants it already covers, into emerging personal AI agents like ChatGPT Dots and Meta Muse, while deepening its optimization and attribution work built on dunnhumby data. This points to a new infrastructure layer taking shape: as consumers stop comparison-shopping on Google themselves and instead let an AI agent make the call, brands will need to redirect part of their SEO and e-commerce-content budget toward being understood and recommended by agents — a layer with entirely different rules (citation sourcing, persona simulation, protocol compatibility) than traditional SEO.

### What investors are betting on

Puma Growth Partners Investment Director Henri Songeur put it this way: Azoma has "built a defensible measurement solution in a category it helped create, with enterprise customers already in place." In other words, the bet isn't on whether this market will exist — it's on whether Azoma locks in the measurement standard first. Having its "5 C's" framework, co-authored with the Digital Shelf Institute, already adopted by 40% of the top 50 CPG brands follows the same playbook SEO tooling vendors once used — defining the metric, then selling the tools that meet it — except this time the "algorithm" isn't one search engine, but a sprawling, fast-moving set of LLMs and agent systems.

### Numbers worth watching

- Azoma's analysis of 38 million citations found that recommendation logic varies sharply across AI shopping engines: editorial content accounts for 51.9% of what Google Gemini cites, but only 7.6% on Google AI Overviews; affiliate content drives 39.6% of Alexa for Shopping's citations, versus under 2% on every other engine measured. Optimizing for one engine does not guarantee results on another — brands need a content strategy tailored to each AI agent
- McKinsey projects agentic commerce could reach $5 trillion globally by 2030, including up to $1 trillion in US retail revenue
- Scale figures cited by the founders: 350 million people used Alexa for Shopping last year; Walmart customers using its Sparky AI shopping assistant spend 40% more than those who don't; Instinct, a personal AI assistant that launched in August with zero marketing budget, already accounts for $1 billion in transactions and is growing 10% day over day

## Watchlist Status

Azoma isn't on the watchlist yet. Recommend adding it to section D8 (Retail / E-commerce), tracking: adoption of the "5 C's" Agentic Commerce Optimization measurement framework, how well its Digital Twin simulation technology holds up under validation, and its coverage progress on emerging protocols like the Agentic Commerce Protocol and Universal Commerce Protocol.

## Takeaway

Most AI-agent funding we've covered goes into the agents themselves (frameworks, sandboxes, memory) or the infrastructure agents need (compute, tools). Azoma is a reminder of something easy to overlook: once AI agents start mediating consumer purchase decisions at scale, an entire intermediary layer for "being seen by agents" immediately appears — the same way the SEO industry emerged once search engines went mainstream. This layer may turn out to be even more complex, because it isn't optimizing for one ranking algorithm, but for Gemini, Alexa, Instinct, and others simultaneously — each with its own rules, and all still changing fast.
