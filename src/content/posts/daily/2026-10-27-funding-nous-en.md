## What the company does

Nous outsources the entire job of managing household bills and admin to an AI agent. The London startup was founded in 2021 by Greg Marsh, who previously built the boutique-stay marketplace onefinestay and sold it to AccorHotels for $200M in 2016.

Its core product is an AI agent connected to a user's bills, insurance policies, and contracts that doesn't just suggest a cheaper deal — it actually switches providers for energy, broadband, mobile, and car insurance, cancels subscriptions the user doesn't need, and flags contracts about to renew at a much higher price. It also helps manage a household's calendar and books local tradespeople. The company layers an LLM on top of a rules engine: everyday tasks go through the LLM, but anything financial or sensitive is routed to rule-based logic. More importantly, Nous is registered with the UK Financial Conduct Authority (FCA), which lets its agent make regulated financial decisions — switching insurance, switching a provider — on a user's behalf, rather than stopping at the "here's a suggestion" layer most assistants stay in.

Scale so far: close to 250,000 UK households use it, managing roughly £685 million in household spending a year (about £2,765 per household), with an average saving of £734 per household.

## What this round signals

### What it means for the agent ecosystem

The UK FCA's Mills Review found that 20% of UK adults are open to letting AI make financial decisions for them, with trust, control, and access cited as the deciding factors. What makes this round notable is that Nous is one of the few consumer-facing cases that has actually gotten regulatory sign-off to let an agent sign contracts and switch providers on a user's behalf — not just draft a cancellation email. The new capital will fund expansion into more spending categories (a wider range of insurance and financial services) and into international markets.

### What investors are betting on

Heartcore Capital's Max Niederhofer has known Marsh for 20 years, and the thesis echoes his earlier bet on onefinestay (which still sits on his personal investment list). Mosaic Ventures, the original seed investor, coming back to add more money is the standard signal that an existing shareholder has conviction in the current valuation. The angel roster is also telling — GoCardless/Monzo co-founder Tom Blomfield, Faculty.ai's Marc Warner, and Funding Circle's James Meekings are all in — which points to money that understands fintech regulation, not capital chasing the agent hype cycle.

### Numbers worth watching

- User count went from zero to nearly 250,000 UK households, with Nous managing an average of £2,765 in spending per household per year
- Average savings of £734 per household is a directly verifiable ROI number, easier for regulators and consumers to check than most vague "AI agent saves you time" claims
- Total raised is just $26M including this round — against other agent-space rounds routinely clearing $100M+ in the same period, Nous is running a slow, actuarial, regulated-vertical playbook rather than valuation-by-narrative

## Today's takeaway

Most consumer AI agent narratives are stuck at the trust threshold of "will the user let an agent act on their behalf." Nous's entry point wasn't a technical breakthrough — it was regulatory compliance. By registering with the FCA first, so every financial action the agent takes sits inside a supervised framework, it made consumers more willing to actually hand over sensitive decisions like bills and insurance. That's the reverse of the usual order most agent companies follow — ship the product, backfill compliance later — but it looks like exactly what let a consumer agent touching sensitive financial data scale to 250,000 users.
