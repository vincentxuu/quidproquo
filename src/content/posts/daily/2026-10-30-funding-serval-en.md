## What the company does

Serval is an AI agent platform for automating enterprise service management (ITSM) — its core product, Catalyst, lets employees in IT, HR, finance, security, and legal departments describe workflows in natural language, and the agent generates and deploys the automation directly, with no engineering background required.

Its differentiation is replacing the traditional drag-and-drop no-code workflow builder with AI code generation. The company says most customers go live within 30 days, and cites one automotive manufacturer that migrated its entire IT service management, configuration management database (CMDB), and hardware/software asset management stack from ServiceNow to Serval in 45 days. Co-founders Jake Stauch and Alex McLeod previously led product and engineering at Verkada, where they repeatedly heard IT leaders complain about the same automation friction.

Customers include Fox Corporation, WPP, Live Nation, Ramp, Notion, and Lucid Motors, and the company recently added two Fortune 20 customers. Annual recurring revenue has doubled every quarter since the Series B (absolute figure undisclosed). This round also launches ProServal, a professional-services unit that embeds "automation engineers" directly inside customers to redesign their workflows — Serval plans to hire more than 100 of them over the next year, roughly half fresh graduates who complete a four-week training program before starting.

## What this round signals

### What it means for the agent ecosystem

Serval shows agentic AI spreading from "a tool for engineers" to "part of the daily work of non-technical employees." The launch of ProServal is the notable part — it isn't just selling software licenses, it's staffing people inside customers to redesign workflows directly. That hybrid "software + labor" model suggests agent companies are realizing that getting enterprise customers to actually adopt agents in core business processes takes more than a tool; it takes people to carry the last mile of implementation.

### What investors are betting on

Sequoia partner Anas Biad put it bluntly: Serval "is not AI for ITSM — it is ITSM built from AI." Sequoia has co-led all three of Serval's rounds, including this one, and the fact that existing backers like Elad Gil, General Catalyst, and Redpoint all came back suggests conviction in the growth rate hasn't wavered. Sequoia's own history backing ServiceNow — the incumbent Serval is trying to displace — gives it confidence the enterprise-workflow-automation market is big enough to support both the old guard and the new challenger at once.

## Today's takeaway

Most agent companies pitch "replace labor with tooling," but Serval's ProServal flips that — it sends people in first to redesign a customer's workflows into something an agent can actually run, and leaves the tooling behind. That's a reminder that the bottleneck in enterprise agent adoption is often not the technology but "who actually redesigns the process" — a step that still leans heavily on human judgment, and that software companies can't skip; they end up building their own services team instead.
