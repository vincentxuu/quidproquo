## What the company does

Wabi is a "personal AI agent" built by Replika founder Eugenia Kuyda — not a one-person, one-chat-window assistant, but an agent placed inside threads shared by multiple people, so couples, families, and friend groups all talk to the same agent together. On request, that agent builds small mini-apps on the fly.

The product originally positioned itself as "build an app from a prompt"; version 2.0 hands the agent itself the decision of what to build — once connected to email, Wabi decides on its own which threads and mini-apps to spin up. Examples given include a running restaurant list a couple checks off together, a weekly meal plan that turns into a grocery list, a trivia game for a birthday, and a tracker for a book club; it also handles chores like family schedules, appointments, and trip planning. Personal data shared is scoped to a single thread, and everyone in that thread can see when someone's information is being requested; Kuyda says Wabi is SOC 2 compliant. It currently runs on iOS, Android, and Mac, with Windows coming soon, and the product is free for now with no business model chosen yet.

Neither the official release nor press coverage discloses a user count. Kuyda's previous company, Replika, reached roughly 40 million users; Wabi is still at an early stage of validating this product shape.

## What this round signals

### What it means for the agent ecosystem

Wabi represents the "personal agent" category shifting from serving a single user to serving a group of people sharing a life together. TechCrunch placed this round alongside Instinct (which raised another $1B at a $10B valuation in September) and Tolan (which raised $30M), showing that beyond OpenAI's dots, Meta's Muse, and xAI's Grok Bot, independent startups are still pouring capital into claiming position in the "consumer personal agent" space — and Wabi's chosen angle, shared rather than individual, is a bet on differentiating in a spot the giants haven't yet staked out.

### What investors are betting on

IVP partner Alex Lim's framing is that Kuyda "has a unique sense for where AI is headed, with a track record proving she can execute" — this is largely a bet on the founder herself rather than the current product, given that Replika already proved Kuyda could scale an AI-companion product to roughly 40 million users. a16z, which backed the pre-seed, is continuing its long-standing bet on consumer AI companionship and personal assistants. Notably, this round's angel list includes Shopify founder Tobi Lütke and Salesforce founder Marc Benioff — enterprise-software backgrounds rather than purely consumer-internet figures — which may hint that some investors are watching whether Wabi's "shared collaboration plus agent-generated apps" mechanism could eventually migrate into enterprise use cases.

### Numbers worth watching

- Total disclosed funding is only $75M (pre-seed $20M plus this round's $55M) against a $1.25B valuation, for a company founded less than a year ago (the pre-seed only surfaced in November 2025) — an extremely fast valuation climb, yet the company deliberately leaves the round name and whether the valuation is pre- or post-money unstated, a lower level of transparency than most comparable funding news
- Neither the official release nor TechCrunch's coverage discloses a user number — compared to Kuyda's previous company Replika, which reached roughly 40 million users, Wabi can't yet point to even a basic usage metric, suggesting the product is still in a validation phase
- The product is currently free with no business model chosen, meaning much of this $55M is likely to be spent proving out the shared-agent product shape itself, rather than scaling an existing business model

## Today's takeaway

Most funding news presents "amount" and "valuation" as precise figures, but Wabi deliberately left gaps here — no round name, no clarity on pre- vs post-money — with only the founder herself calling it a Series A on X. That's a reminder that how complete an official release is can itself be a signal worth tracking: the vaguer the disclosure, the more it usually means some part of the story — usually the valuation basis or the business model — isn't yet ready for close scrutiny.
