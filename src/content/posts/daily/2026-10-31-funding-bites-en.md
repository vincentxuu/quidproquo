## What the company does

Bites builds AI-generated training content for "deskless" workers. It automatically turns any internal document, SOP, or rough idea into a short training video matched to the company's brand voice, uniforms, products, and locations, delivered through channels employees already use — no need to log into a traditional learning-management system (LMS).

Its core focus targets a group enterprise training has long overlooked: roughly 2.8 billion people, about 80% of the global workforce, who don't work at a desk — a population the login-based LMS model was never built for. Bites also includes an "Ask-AI" chat feature so frontline workers can ask natural-language questions and get answers directly relevant to their job. Customers include Unilever, Shake Shack, Coca-Cola, and Electra.

Founder Eran Heffetz's motivation came from personal experience: he previously ran several restaurants and bars in Tel Aviv — including Vicky Cristina, Little Italy, and Bread Story — and had to retrain staff constantly as employees came and went, convincing him traditional training methods simply don't fit frontline, in-person work. That personal pain point became the starting point for Bites.

## What this round signals

### What it means for the agent ecosystem

Strictly speaking, Bites isn't an agent that acts autonomously or makes decisions interacting with external systems — it's closer to a generative-AI-plus-microlearning content pipeline. That's a reminder that not every dollar in this wave of Series A-and-up funding is buying an "autonomous agent." A meaningful share is going toward using generative AI to redo content enterprises used to pay humans to produce — training videos, SOPs, knowledge bases. These companies have a lower technical bar, but the market is large and fragmented: 2.8 billion deskless workers represent a long-tail market enterprise software has largely ignored for decades.

### What investors are betting on

PeakSpan Capital focuses on later-stage growth investments in AI and software, so a PeakSpan-led Series A is unusual — it suggests PeakSpan judged Bites had already cleared product-market fit and chose to stake its claim as a later-stage investor rather than waiting for Series B or C. Existing backers Vertex Ventures and Horizon Capital both returned in full, signaling that conviction in the growth rate hasn't wavered just because the lead investor changed.

### Numbers worth watching

- Total funding of just $55M (with only $15M raised prior to this round) is a relatively modest amount of early capital — suggesting the company had already validated clear customer demand before this round
- A lean team of 30 people (split between a Tel Aviv development center and New York headquarters) supports global-brand customers like Unilever and Coca-Cola
- The target market — 2.8 billion deskless workers, about 80% of the global workforce — is one of the few cases in this funding wave where the narrative quantifies market size down to the population level

## Today's takeaway

The "deskless worker" population — 2.8 billion people — has been nearly invisible to enterprise training systems for decades, because the entire LMS model assumes employees sit in front of a computer. Bites started from the clumsiest but most authentic pain point imaginable: its founder couldn't find a decent training tool while running his own restaurants. That's yet another reminder that a vertical AI company's starting point usually isn't "we have a cool model" — it's "I personally lived with this problem for years."
