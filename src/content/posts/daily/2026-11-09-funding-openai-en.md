## What the company does

OpenAI builds the GPT model family and ChatGPT. Over the past two years its focus has expanded well beyond a chatbot into developer tools and enterprise agent products, including Codex (code generation), the Agents SDK (a framework for developers to build agents), and ChatGPT Agent (autonomous task execution for consumers).

OpenAI's previous round, in March 2026, raised $122B at an $852B valuation — the largest private tech raise at the time. This new round was originally pitched at $30B and a $1.4T valuation, but after the US midterm elections soured sentiment on data-center buildouts and Nasdaq tech stocks sold off, both the raise size and the valuation came down: the first tranche landed at just $11B, valuing the company at $1.25T.

OpenAI had signaled an IPO this year but has since delayed those plans, with expectations now pointing to 2027. In the same stretch, rival Anthropic's expected IPO valuation has also been marked down, from $2T to as low as $1.1T — a sign that private AI valuations are being repriced industry-wide, not just at OpenAI.

## What this round signals

### What it means for the agent ecosystem

This is the clearest signal yet: even the best-funded, most mature-product AI lab can't hold its original valuation target in a public-market pullback. $11B is only the first tranche — whether the full round actually reaches $20B, and whether the final valuation gets talked down further, will be a key gauge of AI valuations over the coming weeks, and a direct reference point for every other lab that's raising or heading toward an IPO, Anthropic included.

### What the investors are betting on

SoftBank and MGX, the two participants Reuters named, are regulars in this year's run of mega AI private rounds. SoftBank is already deeply tied to OpenAI, having led an earlier $40B round, and MGX is Abu Dhabi's sovereign tech-and-AI investment arm, with prior stakes in xAI and OpenAI. Neither is optimizing for a single deal's return; both are using sovereign-scale time horizons to lock in a position in whichever AI lab ends up winning. That they're still writing checks at a valuation growth rate (46.7%) well below the jump in the prior round suggests even a "discounted" OpenAI is still treated as a core holding that has to be in the portfolio.

### Numbers worth watching

- The valuation rose 46.7% ($852B to $1.25T) but still falls $150B short of the original $1.4T target — a sign that even OpenAI can't command the price it wants in the current market
- The $11B first tranche covers barely a third of the original $30B ceiling; even if the full round eventually reaches the expected $20B, that's still a third below target
- In the same week, Anthropic's expected IPO valuation was marked down from $2T to as low as $1.1T — a 45% drop, steeper than OpenAI's own markdown, suggesting the market is repricing the "second-place" AI lab even more harshly than the leader

## Watchlist status

OpenAI is already tracked in watchlist section A1 (Big Tech), with tracking focus on Codex, the Agents SDK, and ChatGPT Agent. We'd add "private valuations repricing alongside the public-market pullback" as a tracking focus — a leading indicator for the funding health of the whole AI industry.

## Today's takeaway

For most of the past year, the AI-lab funding narrative has been "ask for more, close it faster." This round flips that: even OpenAI landed barely a third over its ask, at a valuation $150B below its target. That's a sign the market's mood has shifted — by late 2026, it's no longer "valuations only go up," but "even the category leader gets a haircut from public-market panic." The terms other AI labs manage to raise on over the coming weeks will be a more reliable thermometer than this round alone.
