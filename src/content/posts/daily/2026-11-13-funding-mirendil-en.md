## What the company does

Mirendil is building AI agents that automate frontier AI research itself — the pitch is that a single researcher paired with a fleet of agents can do what used to take an entire team.

Over the past three weeks the company has shared its first public results, all pointing the same direction: it pretrained Mirendil-Base, a competitive coding model, from scratch in 3.5 months for under $10M in compute, then post-trained it into Fern, which holds up to 10 million tokens of context — enough for an agent to work continuously for days. Lor, now in private beta, lets hundreds of agents write code and run experiments in parallel for days at a time; Mirendil itself runs its own research on Lor every day.

Co-founders Behnam Neyshabur and Harsh Mehta met at Google in 2019, joined Anthropic together in late 2024, and left in December 2025 — their argument being that the capability to do frontier AI research shouldn't be confined to a handful of large labs. The company remains pre-product, and investors were already discussing a $5B valuation just three months in.

## What this round signals

### What it means for the agent ecosystem

This round signals that agents aren't just being shipped as products — they're being used to automate the AI research process itself. Mirendil's pitch isn't another chatbot or coding assistant; it's "replace a research team's headcount with agents," compressing what used to take a full lab down to a handful of people plus agents.

### What the investors are betting on

Kleiner Perkins and Clearlake Capital already led the seed round (June 2026, $200M at $1B) and doubled down for this Series A — a sign they're not betting on a single deal's return but on the broader thesis that "democratizing frontier AI R&D" will keep attracting capital. Less than a year after the founders left their jobs, reported valuation talk has already risen at least 5x, showing top-tier VCs are willing to pay for pedigree and early signal alone, without waiting for product or revenue proof.

### Numbers worth watching

- Only about five months separate the seed round from the Series A (June to November 2026) — far faster than the typical AI-startup fundraising cycle
- Total disclosed funding is $1.2B, yet the company still has no public product roadmap or revenue figures — this is purely a "trust the team" valuation model for now
- Bloomberg's September report put the negotiating target at $5B; if the final number lands near that, the valuation rose 5x in five months

## Watchlist status

Mirendil is not yet tracked in the watchlist. We'd recommend adding it to section A2 (AI models/platforms) — it's a leading example of commercializing "using AI to do AI research" with agents, and it's worth tracking whether its models (Mirendil-Base, Fern) and agent platform (Lor) eventually open up to outside customers.

## Today's takeaway

A company less than a year old, with no shipped product and no revenue, just raised $1B — a sign we're in a stage where pedigree and narrative are worth more than product. But the twist is that investors are betting agents themselves will make that "small team, huge ambition" story real: the money that used to require proven product-market fit now only needs early signal that "agents can actually replace a research team."
