## What the company does

Rox builds AI agents for enterprise sales — the goal is to replace how companies use traditional CRM software like Salesforce, letting agents proactively research accounts, prioritize prospects, and prepare outreach rather than just storing data.

The product is built on top of each customer's own data warehouse and plugs directly into existing systems, automating pipeline generation, deal execution, and account expansion. The company says its first-year ARR grew more than 10x, with net revenue retention above 700%; some enterprise customers reported ROI of roughly 25x within 90 days, including one German engineering firm at 24.3x. Customers include Ramp, New Relic, Trafigura, MongoDB, Groq, and America's Auto Auction. GitLab CIO Manu Narayan has said publicly that Rox's agents help the company's revenue team research and find prospects while fitting into its existing governance standards.

Co-founder and CEO Ishan Mukherjee previously founded Pixie Labs, acquired by New Relic for roughly $150M in 2020, and went on to serve as New Relic's chief growth officer. Other co-founders include Shriram Sridharan, a founding member of AWS's Aurora database team; former Google employee Diogo Ribeiro; and Stanford AI Lab professor Christopher Ré. Mukherjee himself previously led the product team for Apple's Siri Knowledge Graph.

## What this round signals

### What it means for the agent ecosystem

Rox is a signal that AI agents are now directly challenging CRM, one of enterprise software's most entrenched categories — not by bolting a chatbot onto Salesforce, but by arguing that CRM's entire "system of record" model is outdated, and that enterprise data should live in a company's own data warehouse with agents as the new operating layer. This fits the broader market anxiety dubbed the "SaaSpocalypse" this year, as investors bet that AI-native companies can route around the data lock-in that incumbent SaaS vendors have relied on.

### What the investors are betting on

General Catalyst partner Marc Bhargava put it bluntly: "There's a long graveyard of companies that said they'd take on ServiceNow or Salesforce." His reasoning for why this time is different: "the old systems were never built for agents, and boards are finally willing to start fresh." Sequoia partner Konstantine Buhler similarly stressed that Rox delivers "measurable results for Global 2000 sales teams and CROs in under a quarter." Neither firm's logic rests on long-term vision — both are betting that the early revenue numbers they're already seeing can scale and repeat.

### Numbers worth watching

- First-year ARR growth of more than 10x and net revenue retention above 700% are unusual figures for a Series B stage, and are the core justification for a $1.3B valuation against a $100M raise — a 13x multiple
- Some customer ROI figures reach 25x within 90 days, or even 24.3x in one case, but these are the company's own self-reported pilot numbers, not yet independently verified; Fortune's reporting explicitly cautions that "pipeline figures should not be treated as closed revenue"
- Grand View Research estimates the global enterprise agentic AI market was worth $2.6B in 2024, potentially reaching $24.5B by 2030 at a 46.2% CAGR — Rox's narrative rests on that projected market size, not the market as it stands today

## Watchlist status

Rox is not yet tracked in the watchlist. We'd recommend adding it to section D2 (Sales/GTM) — it's one of the largest and highest-valued raises so far in the sales-agent category, and explicitly names Salesforce as the incumbent to displace, making it worth tracking whether its pilot-stage pipeline numbers convert into durable retention and expansion revenue.

## Today's takeaway

What's most notable about Rox isn't its Salesforce-challenger narrative — a new company makes that pitch every year. It's that General Catalyst's stated reasoning this time isn't "strong team" or "big market," but "the old system's data lock-in advantage is disappearing." That suggests VCs are actually betting on the deeper structural shift of enterprises moving their data out of Salesforce — agents are riding that wave, not the reason it exists.
