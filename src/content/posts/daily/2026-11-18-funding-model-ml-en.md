## What the company does

Model ML builds personal AI agents for bankers, consultants and investors — its pitch is that a typical chatbot only answers questions, while its agent actually gets the work done.

The product runs inside a client's existing systems and data, understanding a user's clients, live deals and working preferences, and becomes more personalized the more it's used. For example, when news breaks about a company a banker follows, the agent assesses relevance, analyzes potential implications, and prepares a report in the firm's preferred format — the goal is to finish the analysis before the professional even asks, rather than just firing off a notification. The platform is model-agnostic, benchmarking different models on speed, cost and accuracy and routing tasks accordingly; a growing share of work already runs on its own fine-tuned open-source models, which the company claims can run at 2% of frontier-model cost and 5x the speed while matching or beating accuracy. On security, the platform supports deployment inside a customer's own infrastructure, customer data is never used to train models, and it holds ISO/IEC 27001:2022 and SOC 2 Type II certifications.

Customers span financial and professional-services firms across London, New York, Paris, Madrid, Tokyo, Singapore and Dubai, with ARR up 12x and daily active users up 26x year over year in Q3 2026. In the financial AI agent space, Model ML competes with New York-based Rogo ($160M Series D led by Kleiner Perkins, April 2026) and Hebbia ($130M Series B led by a16z, July 2024), differentiating itself by building a "personal agent" rather than a tool for a single research task.

## What this funding signals

### What it means for the agent ecosystem

This is another data point for vertical financial AI agents moving from "assisting with research" to "proactively finishing the work." The capital mainly goes toward expanding engineering in London and New York, accelerating product development and supporting broader deployments — suggesting the next phase of competition in this vertical shifts from "how accurate is the model's answer" to "does the agent dare finish the task before anyone asks," which demands deeper enterprise-system integration and a much higher trust bar.

### What investors are betting on

General Atlantic's global head of financial services, Paul Stamas, frames it as financial institutions moving quickly from AI experimentation to everyday use, and the bet is on how deeply customers already trust Model ML with increasingly complex work. QED Investors' Nigel Morris is more direct: 18 months after emerging from stealth, the product is already embedded deep inside some of the world's largest institutions, and he's betting on a team that "builds faster than any I've seen" rather than on any single feature's lead.
