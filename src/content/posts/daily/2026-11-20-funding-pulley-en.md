## What the company does

Pulley builds AI automation for construction permitting — pairing AI agents with licensed permitting experts to compress the approval process for building, opening a store, or constructing a factory from months or years down to days.

Its core product is an "AI-native permitting service" that combines deep regulatory knowledge with proprietary regulatory intelligence, covering private plan-review programs across all 50 states and more than 2,000 cities and counties (letting Pulley approve building plans directly in place of local jurisdictions). The platform pairs 75 licensed experts with AI agents: the agents handle regulatory matching, approval-strategy drafting, and first-pass plan review, while the experts make final calls and coordinate with jurisdictions. Customers typically go through just one round of jurisdictional feedback, and more than 40% of projects are approved with no feedback at all.

Current customers include Amazon, Starbucks, JLL, Equinix and Meta, spanning large retail, restaurant, banking, manufacturing and hyperscaler clients. Pulley says it has helped save 11,750 weeks of project time across $21 billion worth of commercial construction projects, with annual recurring revenue up 5x year over year.

## What this funding signals

### What it means for the agent ecosystem

This round's significance is that Pulley is applying AI agents to a domain almost nobody had touched with AI before — physical-world regulatory approvals — and not by replacing people, but by having AI agents and licensed experts split the work. The funding will expand the permitting service and launch new products to bring AI into even earlier decisions: where to build, what to build, and how to get it approved.

### What investors are betting on

Jump Capital partner Sach Chitnis's logic is that Pulley has turned "long-fragmented, labor-dependent work" into a scalable technology product, with an intelligence layer that gets smarter as more projects flow through it — consistent with the "data compounding" thesis many agent-infrastructure investors look for. CRV and Susa Ventures, which backed the earlier seed round, are doubling down rather than being replaced by later-stage mega-funds — suggesting early investors in this kind of deep-vertical-expertise-plus-AI company tend to keep adding rather than stepping aside.

### Numbers worth watching

- Review comments per project fell 65% year over year — one of the few agent vertical-application cases with clear quantitative evidence that the model gets smarter with use.
- ARR is up 5x year over year, but the company hasn't disclosed the actual amount or a valuation — less transparent than other rounds announced the same day.
- Announcing the $23M Series A and $41M Series B "together" suggests Pulley may have closed a smaller bridge round earlier and only recently added the larger tranche; the company hasn't explained the actual timeline.

## Today's takeaway

Pulley isn't positioning itself as an agent that replaces human labor — it's emphasizing that AI agents work alongside licensed experts. That's a reminder that in heavily regulated, liability-sensitive physical-world domains, the agent applications that actually ship today may not be full automation but a division of labor: AI does the heavy first-pass screening, humans make the final call. That kind of moat may run deeper, since it requires both AI capability and a genuine network of domain experts.
