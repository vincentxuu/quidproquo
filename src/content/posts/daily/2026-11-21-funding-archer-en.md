## What the company does

Archer is an AI hiring agent built by hackajob, a long-running technical recruiting platform. Its goal is to represent both sides of hiring — candidates and employers — instead of the "AI tools that only help one side" pattern that dominates the market.

The core mechanic: candidates tell Archer what they want in their next role, and Archer returns a filtered shortlist of jobs; recruiters tell Archer what "good" looks like, and Archer pushes qualified candidates straight into their ATS (applicant tracking system). The company says every hiring decision still stays with the recruiter — Archer handles sourcing and initial qualification, freeing up recruiters' time for the parts only people can do: building relationships and closing hires. After a decade focused on technical hiring, Archer is now expanding hackajob into non-technical roles like enterprise sales, front-office finance, and even data-center construction staffing.

Current customers include Google, Sierra, and Barclays. The company says ARR has grown 5x over the past year, while cost per hire has dropped 67% compared with traditional staffing agencies.

## What this funding signals

### What it means for the agent ecosystem

AI tools in recruiting have long been one-sided: candidates mass-apply with AI, recruiters mass-message with AI, and the result is a flood of messages on both sides with falling match quality. This round signals that the next competitive axis isn't "whose screening algorithm is more accurate" but "who can design an agent role that both sides actually trust" — a pattern worth watching for any two-sided marketplace (freelance work, business development, consultant matching) building agents.

### What investors are betting on

Volition Capital is a growth-equity fund that typically favors companies with a clear revenue growth curve over early-stage, unproven concepts. Leading Archer's round rather than an earlier seed round suggests Volition is betting on hackajob's decade of enterprise relationships and ATS integrations as the moat that lets Archer expand quickly into non-technical hiring — not simply betting on a novel recruiting-AI concept.

### Numbers worth watching

- Over 700,000 candidates signed up to work with Archer in the past 12 months — more than hackajob's cumulative signups over its previous five years combined, suggesting the growth curve accelerated only after the agent product launched, not from organic platform growth.
- Archer now makes 10,000 introductions a week, roughly half of them candidates a customer has never seen before, converting to interviews at 5x the customer's baseline rate — the only publicly disclosed, verifiable measure of match quality rather than raw volume.
- ARR up 5x year over year against a 67% drop in cost per hire: together, the narrative is "more matches for less money," not "higher revenue in exchange for a pricier AI service."

## Watchlist status

Archer (hackajob) is not yet on the watchlist. Recommend adding it under section D3 (HR / recruiting), alongside Mercor, Eightfold AI, Phenom, HireVue, and Findem, tracking its two-sided agent design and progress expanding into non-technical roles.

## Today's takeaway

Most recruiting AI startups choose to serve only one side — screening and recommending candidates to employers. Archer's bet runs the other way: serve both sides, and have its agent "negotiate" with the employer's agent on the candidate's behalf. The underlying assumption is that when both sides are already operating AI at scale, the only way to restore match quality isn't to make one side stronger — it's to have the two agents talk to each other directly. That's a different story from most "agents replace labor" narratives; what Archer is really selling is two agents checking each other.
