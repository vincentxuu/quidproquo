## What the company does

Sierra builds a platform that lets enterprises design and deploy their own customer-service AI agents. It was founded in early 2024 by former Salesforce co-CEO Bret Taylor and former Google executive Clay Bavor.

The product lets companies configure agents that handle phone and messaging support, hooking into existing enterprise systems to complete tasks on a customer's behalf — refunds, rebookings, claims. Sierra runs a "constellation" of models from OpenAI, Anthropic, and Meta that cross-check each other's outputs to cut down on hallucinations, and lets each customer tune the agent's "personality" to match its brand. Pricing is outcome-based — Sierra charges when a problem gets resolved or a claim gets processed, not per seat or per token — and Zendesk has since moved toward the same model.

Current customers include close to half of the Fortune 50: BNY, HSBC, Singtel, Wells Fargo, JPMorganChase, Pearson, Next, and Cox Automotive, along with SoFi, Ramp, and Brex. Insurer Guardian handed Sierra a million dental-claim calls a year, and its agents can now take credit-card payments directly through Stripe. ARR crossed $400M in October, just two and a half years after the company launched.

## What this funding signals

### What it means for the agent ecosystem

The new money goes toward building out the technology stack with more layers of service capability, and toward hiring a sales team fast enough to close enterprise contracts ahead of rivals. More notably, Sierra is working with Meta, Stripe, and Walmart on protocol definitions for how agents talk to each other. That means the competition in customer-service agents is no longer just about whose model is more accurate — it's about who locks in the standard for how agents talk to enterprise systems first.

### What investors are betting on

The lead investors, Greenoaks and Lightspeed, plus returning backers Benchmark, Sequoia, GV, and Tiger Global, amount to a who's-who of early AI investors doubling down on the same company across four-plus rounds. Greenoaks has led every round since October 2024 — a pattern of concentrating bets on a winner rather than spreading money across the sector. The $30B valuation landed in the week right after Anthropic's IPO, and co-founder Bret Taylor — who also chairs OpenAI's board — pointed to that IPO in a blog post as evidence that AI isn't a bubble. In that sense, this round is also doing some narrative work for the industry as a whole.

### Numbers worth watching

- The valuation doubled in six months: $15.8B in May 2026 to $30B in November 2026 — faster than the prior stretch, which took from September 2025 to May 2026 to go from $10B to $15.8B (a 58% gain).
- $400M ARR against a $30B valuation works out to roughly a 75x revenue multiple — well above peers in the same category like ElevenLabs ($22B) and Decagon ($4.5B), suggesting the market is pricing Sierra as the category's clear leader rather than one of many contenders.
- Total funding has gone from a $110M seed round in February 2024 to $3.34B over two and a half years, with every round marking the valuation up — none down.

## Watchlist status

Sierra AI is already tracked in watchlist section D1 (Customer Service / CX), where the original tracking notes were "Bret Taylor's CX platform" and "$10B valuation." Those should be updated to: $30B valuation, $400M ARR, and progress on the agent communication protocol it's building with Meta, Stripe, and Walmart — the latter may be worth watching more closely than the valuation number itself.

## Today's takeaway

Most customer-service agent startups are still fighting over accuracy and customization. Sierra is already fighting the next battle: defining the protocol agents use to talk to each other and to enterprise systems. Lock in the protocol first, and every agent that follows it has to pass through your territory — a much deeper moat than selling a single chatbot.
