## What the company does

AidenAI is an "AI Builder Company" that helps enterprises put AI into actual production, founded in 2023 by Srinivas Kamadi, a former senior vice president at Infosys.

Its core product, the AI Builder Platform, sits above the AI model layer: it connects to a company's existing systems and AI models, providing trusted knowledge, agent-building blocks, governance, and impact tracking, so enterprises can build, run, and govern their own AI agents without ripping out legacy systems. A second product, AiDAP, focuses on modernizing legacy technology so it can interface with AI. Unlike most companies that just sell a platform, AidenAI pairs it with an embedded engineering team — "frontier AI engineers" who design, build, and deploy solutions alongside clients, closer to Palantir's forward-deployed-engineer model than a typical subscription SaaS product.

The company currently serves more than 30 enterprise clients, concentrated in insurance, financial services, and healthcare, across six countries, with a workforce of more than 500. The new funding will scale the AidenAI Builder Academy, based in Princeton and Hyderabad, with a goal of growing the frontier-AI-engineer headcount past 1,000 by the end of 2027.

## What this funding signals

### What it means for the agent ecosystem

AidenAI's bet differs from most enterprise AI companies that sell a pure platform — it makes engineering services part of the product itself, competing on headcount scale rather than a purely software moat. That tracks with an emerging sub-category: the real bottleneck in enterprise AI isn't model capability, it's the engineering work of fitting agents into regulatory, data, and legacy-system constraints — the same category watchlist section E (FDE / AI Deployment Services) tracks for Scale AI, Cognizant, and Accenture, except AidenAI is a much smaller, more enterprise-AI-focused entrant in that model.

### What investors are betting on

A91 Partners, founded in 2018, manages about $1.5B and invests mainly in Indian consumer, technology, financial services, and healthcare companies. Partner Kaushik Anand put the thesis plainly: "While enterprise AI adoption is still in its early stages, AidenAI has spent the last three years helping over 30 large enterprises turn the promise of AI into live, measurable business outcomes." That's a bet not on a technical breakthrough but on a team that has already proven it can deliver AI to customers — for a company that bootstrapped on customer revenue for three years before raising its first institutional round, that's trading three years of delivery track record for its first outside capital.

### Numbers worth watching

- The engineering team is set to grow from ~500 today to more than 1,000 by the end of 2027 — more than doubling in 13 months, with headcount growth tied directly to customer growth rather than hiring ahead of demand.
- $32M against more than 30 enterprise clients works out to roughly $1M in capital per customer — a cost structure closer to a consulting firm than a typical SaaS company, suggesting most of the money goes toward engineering capacity rather than acquisition or marketing spend.
- The company was founded in 2023 and only closed its first institutional round three years later, by which point it had already built a 500-person team and 30+ enterprise clients — the reverse of the "raise first, chase users later" sequence most agent startups follow.

## Watchlist status

AidenAI is not yet tracked in the watchlist. Recommend adding it to section E (FDE / AI Deployment Services), with tracking notes: a hybrid platform-plus-engineering-team model for enterprise AI deployment, $32M Series A led by A91 Partners, targeting more than 1,000 engineers by the end of 2027.

## Today's takeaway

Most agent startups compete on model capability or platform features. AidenAI competes on whether anyone actually shows up to wire the agent into a customer's decade-old system — an unglamorous but very real gap: enterprises can afford the best models but can't find enough hands to integrate them. AidenAI turned that gap itself into the product, and backed it with three years of delivery proof — a harder thing to copy than a slide with an agent architecture diagram on it.
