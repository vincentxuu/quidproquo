Open any streaming app and the home page shows American, Korean, Japanese and Chinese dramas side by side, each billed as the show everyone is talking about. This series helps with two things. First, how dramas from each country differ in how much time they ask of you, whether they keep getting renewed, and how far their content goes. Second, a watchlist for each country, with the sources that recommend each show and legal streaming links in Taiwan.

This post is the entry point. By the end you will know which country's dramas suit you best as a starting point, and you will be able to read the recommendation tags in every watchlist that follows.

## Which countries' dramas people in Taiwan watch

The Market Intelligence & Consulting Institute (MIC) at Taiwan's Institute for Information Industry asked streaming viewers in its 2025 [Taiwan viewing behaviour survey](https://www.nownews.com/news/6674579) (in Chinese) which dramas they had watched:

| Type | Share who had watched |
|---|---|
| Korean dramas | 75% |
| Taiwanese dramas | 73% |
| Chinese dramas | 70% |
| Japanese dramas | 70% |
| American dramas | 68% |
| Taiwanese local-language serials | 49% |

The top five are only 7 points apart. The table shows that people in Taiwan watch dramas from every one of these countries; it does not show that one country's dramas are better. So this series does not rank countries. It sorts them by the kind of viewing experience you want.

## How dramas from each country differ

### Episodes per season and renewals

This is what decides how much time you are signing up for.

| Country | Typical length | Renewals | What you get |
|---|---|---|---|
| US | About 9 episodes per streaming season | Popular shows run for many seasons | Years with the same characters, but waits between seasons |
| South Korea | Down from 16 to 12, sometimes 8–10 | Usually ends after one season | Done in a weekend or two, with an ending |
| Japan | 10–12 episodes per season, about an hour each | Usually ends after one season | One season and out, a new batch every three months |
| China | No more than 40 episodes in principle | Sequels are capped too | Several weeks per show |
| UK | Often 6 episodes per series | Long-running shows exist, with short series | Like watching one long film |
| Latin America (telenovela) | One complete story, usually within a year | Planned ending | Long, but it always ends |

Some background, so you know when this table stops being accurate:

- **American seasons are shrinking.** Parrot Analytics found the average US streaming season fell from 10.7 episodes in 2018 to 9.3 in 2024 ([as reported by TVREV](https://www.tvrev.com/news/the-great-contraction-why-tv-seasons-are-shrinking-and-what-it-means-for-the-industry)). Broadcast seasons are shrinking too, so the old twenty-plus-episode season is now the exception.
- **The 16-episode K-drama is fading.** [The Korea Times](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/entertainment/shows-dramas/20250222/korean-dramas-shift-to-shorter-seasons-as-16-episode-format-fades) traces K-dramas getting steadily shorter: 16 episodes was the norm for years, and many recent shows have 12; Netflix's The Trauma Code has just 8.
- **Japanese dramas follow a quarterly schedule.** Commercial broadcasters start their dramas in January, April, July and October, in three-month blocks called *cours* ([nippon.com](https://www.nippon.com/ja/articles/-/6831), in Japanese). NHK's morning drama series and its historical *taiga* dramas are the exceptions, with far more episodes: the 1983 morning drama *Oshin* ran for a full year.
- **Chinese dramas have an episode cap.** A 2023 notice from the National Radio and Television Administration says TV and web dramas should "in principle not exceed 40 episodes" and may not be split into parts to get around the rule; if a franchise's sequels exceed the total cap, each must wait 12 months after the previous one ([tvoao](https://www.tvoao.com/a/215685.aspx), in Chinese).
- **British short series have exceptions.** The British shows that sell well abroad are mostly six-episode series, but long-running medical and police dramas can top 30 episodes a series ([Chief](https://www.chief.tv/post/why-are-uk-television-series-shorter-than-american-seasons)).
- **Telenovelas end.** Unlike American daytime soaps that run for decades, a telenovela is planned to wrap within about a year, with 30–45 minute episodes ([Wikipedia: Telenovela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telenovela)).

Taiwanese drama lengths are covered in the Taiwan post.

### How far the content goes

Since 13 June 2017, Taiwan's TV regulations have split programmes into five ratings: General, Protected, Parental Guidance 12, Parental Guidance 15, and Restricted. Restricted programmes can only air on adult channels ([Television content rating system in Taiwan](https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-tw/%E4%B8%AD%E8%8F%AF%E6%B0%91%E5%9C%8B%E9%9B%BB%E8%A6%96%E7%AF%80%E7%9B%AE%E5%88%86%E7%B4%9A%E5%88%B6%E5%BA%A6), in Chinese).

For choosing a show, that means an American or Korean drama on a regular TV channel may be cut or pushed to late night. For the original version, you usually need a streaming service. Before you press play, check the age rating on the service's title page.

Every show in this series comes with a one-line content note that separates violence, sex, drugs and gore, because "18+" doesn't tell you which of those you would rather avoid.

### Where to watch in Taiwan

The same MIC survey asked which streaming services viewers had used. The top five:

| Service | Share who had used it |
|---|---|
| Netflix | 65% |
| Disney+ | 36% |
| iQIYI | 35% |
| LINE TV | 29% |
| Hami Video | 16% |

75% of respondents paid for a subscription, most of them keeping one main service and adding a second now and then.

Licences move around: a show on Netflix today may be on another service next month. To find a show:

1. Search the title on [JustWatch Taiwan](https://www.justwatch.com/tw) to see which services carry it.
2. Open the service's own title page to confirm it plays in Taiwan, and whether it is included in a subscription, sold per episode, or free with ads.
3. If the two disagree, trust the service's title page.

Every show in this series links to the service's title page, and every post is stamped with the date the links were checked. No pirate sites, no affiliate links.

## How the watchlists are chosen

Follow a single list and you inherit its taste: awards favour what juries like, ratings favour broad appeal, fan communities favour die-hard fans. So each country's watchlist puts seven kinds of recommendation side by side:

| Category | Whose view | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| A Awards | Industry juries | Emmys, Baeksang Arts Awards, Golden Bell Awards, International Emmys, Series Mania |
| B Critics polls | Consensus among critics | BBC Culture's 100 greatest TV series of the 21st century, The Guardian's year-end list |
| C Audience ratings | Ordinary viewers | IMDb, Rotten Tomatoes audience score, Douban, Filmarks, MyDramaList |
| D Viewing data | How many people actually watch | Nielsen, Netflix global Top 10, national TV ratings |
| E Buzz metrics | How much people talk about it | Parrot Analytics demand rankings, Good Data's buzz rankings in Korea |
| F Fan communities | Dedicated fans | PTT drama boards, Dcard, Reddit |
| G Taiwanese viewers | What people in Taiwan watch | MIC viewing surveys, Taiwanese streaming charts |

Take category B. [BBC Culture's 21st-century TV poll](https://www.bbc.com/culture/article/20211014-the-greatest-tv-series-of-the-21st-century-who-voted) polled 206 critics, academics and industry figures from 43 countries in 2021, which makes it a better read on international consensus than any one outlet's year-end list.

A show makes a watchlist only if it clears all three bars:

1. **At least two categories recommend it.** For example, it won a Baeksang award and scores highly on MyDramaList.
2. **It can be watched legally in Taiwan.**
3. **Time:** shows that aired from 2016 on go in the "last ten years" group; older shows need a place on a critics poll or a major award to go in the "classics" group.

There is no cap on the number of shows. Anything that clears the bars goes in, so some countries will take several posts. Each show carries tags naming the category and the source, such as `A Baeksang Best Drama`, `C MyDramaList` or `F PTT Korean drama board poll`.

More tags means more kinds of viewers recommend it. When sources disagree (lots of buzz, lukewarm reviews), the entry says so and leaves the call to you. Shows that clear only one or two bars go in a watch-later list at the end of each post.

## Limits of this guide

- **Watchlists only include shows you can watch in Taiwan.** Some hits at home without a Taiwanese licence only appear on the watch-later list.
- **The Taiwanese viewer data comes from one survey.** MIC asked which countries' dramas people had watched, not how many hours, so it shows which countries have an audience, not which is most popular.
- **Streaming data is mostly published by the platforms themselves.** Netflix's charts only count Netflix shows; for other services, national ratings and review sites fill the gap.
- **Licences change.** Every post's streaming links carry a check date; after a while, trust the service's title page.
