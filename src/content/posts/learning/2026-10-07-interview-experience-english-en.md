The "interview" scenario in my English speaking practice area only had two sentences. This post adds ten, covering the four things interviews ask about most.

One thing first: "five years" and "three seconds to one second" are examples that show the pattern. They are not my résumé. When you practice, replace them with your real years, company, and numbers, or the sentences will not help in an actual interview.

## What I was trying to say

I was talking to an interviewer, formally but not stiffly. I was doing four things:

- Introducing myself: how many years, what kind of company I work for now
- Describing a project: what I owned, what the result was, what was hardest
- Describing a failure or a disagreement: what I misjudged, how it was resolved, what I changed
- Asking the interviewer: how the team splits the work, what the role's early goals are

## What you can say in this situation

Every sentence below is adapted from a dictionary pattern. None of them is a dictionary example.

### 1. Introducing yourself

| What I want to say | What works in this situation |
| --- | --- |
| 我有五年後端開發的經驗。 | I have five years of experience in backend development. |
| 我目前在一家做 AI agent 平台的公司工作。 | I currently work for a company that builds an AI agent platform. |

**experience**: Oxford lists three patterns, each with an example ([experience](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/us/definition/english/experience_1)):

- *experience in* + a field: *He gained extensive experience in the field of artificial intelligence*
- *experience as* + a role: *I have over ten years' experience as a teacher*
- *experience of* + a thing: *Do you have any previous experience of this type of work?*

So a field takes *in backend development* and a job title takes *as a backend engineer*. For the number of years, the dictionary has both *ten years' experience* and *years of experience*.

**work for**: Oxford's entry for work has *She works for an engineering company* ([work](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/us/definition/english/work_1)). *Currently* means "at the present time", as in *All the options are currently available* ([currently](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/us/definition/english/currently)). To describe what the company does, I use *build*, and the dictionary example is *We build computer systems for large companies* ([build](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/us/definition/english/build_1)).

### 2. Describing a project

| What I want to say | What works in this situation |
| --- | --- |
| 我負責設計並實作了這個功能。 | I was responsible for designing and building this feature. |
| 我們把回應時間從三秒降到一秒以內。 | We reduced the response time from three seconds to under one second. |
| 最難的部分是在不停機的情況下搬移資料。 | The hardest part was migrating the data without downtime. |

**responsible for + -ing**: Oxford's example is *Mike is responsible for designing the entire project* ([responsible](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/us/definition/english/responsible)). *Design* and *build* go together, as in *He designed and built his own house* ([design](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/us/definition/english/design_2)).

I first wanted to say *implement*. Oxford marks it as formal and defines it as making something that has been decided start to happen, with *implement changes/decisions/policies* ([implement](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/us/definition/english/implement_1)). There is no example with a software feature, so the main sentence uses *build*, which has a direct dictionary example.

**reduce … from A to B**: Oxford's example is *The number of employees was reduced from 40 to 25* ([reduce](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/us/definition/english/reduce)). To give only the size of the drop, use *by*: *Costs have been reduced by 20%*. *Under* means "less than", as in *an annual income of under £20 000* ([under](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/us/definition/english/under_1)).

**The hardest part was + -ing**: Oxford's entry for part has *The worst part was having to wait three hours in the rain* and *I gave up once I got to the hard part* ([part](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/us/definition/english/part_1)). In computing, *migrate* means moving programs or hardware from one computer system to another ([migrate](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/us/definition/english/migrate)). The dictionary's objects are programs or hardware, so using it with data is my own extension. *Downtime* is the time during which a machine, especially a computer, is not working ([downtime](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/us/definition/english/downtime)).

### 3. Describing a failure or a disagreement

| What I want to say | What works in this situation |
| --- | --- |
| 我當時低估了這件事的複雜度。 | I underestimated how complex it was. |
| 我跟同事意見不同，後來我們用資料來決定。 | I disagreed with a colleague, so we used data to make the decision. |
| 從那次之後，我會先寫測試再改程式。 | Since then, I've always written tests before I change the code. |

**underestimate how…**: Oxford lists *underestimate what, how, etc.*, with *We underestimated how long it would take* ([underestimate](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/us/definition/english/underestimate_1)).

**disagree with + person**: Oxford's example is *I must respectfully disagree with my colleague*. The topic of the disagreement takes *on*: *Victoria and I obviously disagree on this issue* ([disagree](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/us/definition/english/disagree)). The phrase is *make a decision*, as in *Who made the decision to go ahead with the project?* ([decision](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/us/definition/english/decision)).

**Since then + present perfect**: Oxford says since is used with the present perfect or past perfect for a period from a past time until now, as in *That was years ago. I've changed jobs since then* ([since](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/us/definition/english/since_1)). So this sentence uses the present perfect, *I've always written*.

### 4. Asking the interviewer

| What I want to say | What works in this situation |
| --- | --- |
| 團隊平常是怎麼分工的？ | How does the team divide up the work? |
| 這個職位前三個月最重要的目標是什麼？ | What are the most important goals for this role in the first three months? |

**divide (up) the work**: Oxford's examples are *We divided the work between us* and *Profits were divided up among the staff* ([divide](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/us/definition/english/divide_1)).

**role, goal**: Oxford defines role as the function or position that somebody has in an organization ([role](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/us/definition/english/role)), and goal as something that you hope to achieve, noting that goals are often long-term ([goal](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/us/definition/english/goal)).

This group has support at the situation level. The job site The Muse collects questions to ask an interviewer, including *What are the most important things you'd like to see someone accomplish in the first 30, 60, and 90 days on the job?* and *Can you tell me about the team I'll be working with?* ([51 Interview Questions You Should Be Asking](https://www.themuse.com/advice/51-interview-questions-you-should-be-asking)). The Muse is a commercial job site, not a language textbook. It shows that people do recommend asking this kind of question in interviews. My two sentences are shorter adaptations.

## Can I also say it this way?

| English | Who you say it to and when | Still the same meaning? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I have five years of experience in backend development. | Naming a field | Main version |
| I have five years of experience as a backend engineer. | Naming a job title | Yes, the focus moves from field to role |
| I currently work for a company that builds an AI agent platform. | Describing your current job | Main version |
| I currently work at a company that builds an AI agent platform. | Same situation | Yes. Oxford says at is used to say where somebody works, as in *He's been at the bank longer than anyone else* ([at](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/us/definition/english/at)) |
| I was responsible for designing and building this feature. | Stressing that it was your responsibility | Main version |
| I designed and built this feature. | Saying directly that you did it | Yes, and shorter |
| We reduced the response time from three seconds to under one second. | You have a before and an after number | Main version |
| We reduced the response time by two seconds. | You only give the size of the drop | Yes, but the information differs: no before and after numbers |
| I disagreed with a colleague. | The focus is on who | Main version |
| A colleague and I disagreed on the approach. | The focus is on what | Yes, with the topic added |
| How does the team divide up the work? | Asking how work is split | Main version |
| Can you tell me about the team I'll be working with? | You want to know more about the team | Different: broader than just how work is split; quoted from The Muse |

## Say it again with different content

Read the Chinese, say it yourself, then check the reference. Replace the numbers and details with your own.

1. 我有三年前端工程師的經驗。
   - *I have three years of experience as a frontend engineer.*
2. 我負責設計這個 API。
   - *I was responsible for designing this API.*
3. 我們把錯誤率降了百分之二十。
   - *We reduced the error rate by 20%.*
4. 最難的部分是找出原因。
   - *The hardest part was finding the cause.*
5. 我低估了這會花多久時間。
   - *I underestimated how long it would take.*
6. 從那次之後，我都會先確認需求。
   - *Since then, I've always confirmed the requirements first.*

## A short dialogue

This is a practice example I wrote. It is not a real interview, and the content is not real experience.

> A: Could you tell me about a project you worked on?
>
> B: Sure. I was responsible for designing and building a search feature. We reduced the response time from three seconds to under one second.
>
> A: What was the hardest part?
>
> B: The hardest part was migrating the data without downtime. I underestimated how complex it was.
>
> A: What did you change after that?
>
> B: Since then, I've always written tests before I change the code. May I ask a question? How does the team divide up the work?

## Overall

Of the ten, *experience in / as*, *responsible for + -ing*, and *since then* with the present perfect are worth practicing first, because every interview uses them. Almost all the sources are learner's dictionaries, which support meaning and sentence patterns. Only the questions for the interviewer have a situation-level example from a job site. There was no native-speaker testing and no frequency comparison, so this post only says "works in this situation". The years, company, and numbers in the sentences are examples. Replace them with your own.
