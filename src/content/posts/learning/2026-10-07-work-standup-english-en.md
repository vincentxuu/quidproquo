> 🌏 [中文版](/posts/learning/2026-10-07-work-standup-english)

The "work" scenario in my English speaking practice area only had two sentences. This post adds ten, covering four things a software engineer keeps doing in stand-ups and day-to-day collaboration. These sentences were not adapted from one real conversation. I listed what I wanted to say in Chinese first, then looked for dictionary support one sentence at a time.

## What I was trying to say

I was talking to a colleague or a manager, in a stand-up, over chat, or at someone's desk. I was doing four things:

- Reporting progress: what I finished yesterday, what I am still waiting for, when it will be done
- Saying where I am stuck: which step, or that I cannot reproduce a problem
- Asking for help: a look at a PR, or an explanation of how something works
- Disagreeing and estimating: doubts about an approach, how many days, what I need to confirm first

Atlassian's introduction to stand-ups lists three questions that match the first two: *What did I work on yesterday? What am I working on today? What issues are blocking me?* ([Stand-ups for agile teams](https://www.atlassian.com/agile/scrum/standups)). That source describes what a stand-up covers, not how to phrase it in English.

## What you can say in this situation

Every sentence below is adapted from a dictionary pattern. None of them is a dictionary example.

### 1. Reporting progress

| What I want to say | What works in this situation |
| --- | --- |
| 我昨天把登入流程修好了。 | I fixed the login flow yesterday. |
| 我還在等後端的 API。 | I'm still waiting for the backend API. |
| 這個今天下班前可以完成。 | I can finish this by the end of the day. |

**fix**: Oxford defines it as repairing or correcting something, with the examples *I've fixed the problem* and *find and fix the bug* ([fix](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/us/definition/english/fix_1)). The sentence has *yesterday*, so it uses the past simple *fixed*.

**still waiting for**: Oxford's entry for wait has *I'm still waiting for the results of my blood test* ([wait](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/us/definition/english/wait_1)). Swap in *the backend API* and you have the second sentence.

**by**: Oxford defines it as "not later than the time mentioned", with *Can you finish the work by five o'clock?* and *I'll have it done by tomorrow* ([by](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/us/definition/english/by_1)). For "before I leave work" I use *by the end of the day*. The point is that the work is done before that moment.

### 2. Saying where you are stuck

| What I want to say | What works in this situation |
| --- | --- |
| 我卡在權限設定這一步。 | I'm stuck on the permission settings. |
| 我在本機重現不出這個問題。 | I can't reproduce the issue locally. |

**stuck on**: Oxford lists *stuck (on something)*, meaning unable to answer or understand something, with *I got stuck on the first question* ([stuck](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/us/definition/english/stuck_1)).

**reproduce**: Oxford's entry for reproduce only covers the "copy" senses, not software problems. Support for this use comes from Mozilla's bug reporting guide, which says *steps to reproduce a bug* and *If you can't reproduce the problem* ([Bug Writing Guidelines](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/page.cgi?id=bug-writing.html)). *Locally* means on your own machine, and a GitHub documentation page is titled *Checking out pull requests locally* ([GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/pull-requests/collaborating-with-pull-requests/reviewing-changes-in-pull-requests/checking-out-pull-requests-locally)). These are technical documents. They support the terms, not how common the sentence is in speech.

### 3. Asking for help

| What I want to say | What works in this situation |
| --- | --- |
| 你有空可以幫我看一下這個 PR 嗎？ | Could you take a look at this PR when you have a moment? |
| 你可以跟我說明一下這段是怎麼運作的嗎？ | Could you walk me through how this part works? |

**Could you**: Oxford says it is used to politely ask somebody to do something for you, with *Could you babysit for us on Friday?* ([could](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/us/definition/english/could)).

**take a look at**: Oxford's noun entry for look has *Take a look at these figures!* ([look](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/us/definition/english/look_2)).

**when you have a moment**: Oxford's entry for moment has a request that is almost the same: *Could you look through this report when you have a spare moment?* ([moment](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/us/definition/english/moment)). Of the ten sentences, this one is the closest to a dictionary example.

**walk someone through**: Cambridge defines it as slowly and carefully explaining something to someone or showing someone how to do something, with *He'll walk you through the procedure* ([walk someone through something](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/english/walk-through)). I only read an excerpt of this entry, not the whole page.

### 4. Disagreeing and estimating

| What I want to say | What works in this situation |
| --- | --- |
| 我不太確定這個做法，我們可以先試小一點的範圍嗎？ | I'm not sure about this approach. Could we try it on a smaller scale first? |
| 這個大概需要兩到三天。 | This will probably take two to three days. |
| 我需要先確認需求，再給你時間估計。 | I need to confirm the requirements before I give you an estimate. |

**not sure about**: Oxford lists *sure about something*, with *Are you sure about that?* and *I'm not so sure about that one* ([sure](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/us/definition/english/sure_1)). Saying you are unsure first and then suggesting something is softer than telling the other person they are wrong. That is my judgment about tone. The dictionary only supports the pattern.

**Could we… on a smaller scale**: Oxford's entry for could has *Could we stop by next week?*, and scale has *on a small scale* ([scale](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/us/definition/english/scale_1)).

**take + time**: Oxford says take is used for the amount of time you need to do something and must come with an expression of time, as in *That should only take you ten minutes* ([take](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/us/definition/english/take_1)). *Probably* means something is likely, as in *It'll probably be OK* ([probably](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/us/definition/english/probably)).

**an estimate**: Oxford's noun example is *I can give you a rough estimate of the amount of wood you will need* ([estimate](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/us/definition/english/estimate_2)). As a conjunction, *before* takes a clause, as in *Do it before you forget* ([before](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/us/definition/english/before_2)). *Requirements* is usually plural, as in *a software solution to meet your requirements* ([requirement](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/us/definition/english/requirement)).

## Can I also say it this way?

| English | Who you say it to and when | Still the same meaning? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I fixed the login flow yesterday. | Reporting what you finished yesterday | Main version |
| I've fixed the login flow. | No time mentioned; the point is that it is fixed now | Yes, but you cannot add yesterday |
| I'm stuck on the permission settings. | Saying where you are stuck | Main version |
| The permission settings are blocking me. | Answering "what is blocking me" in a stand-up | Yes, with the blocker as the subject; adapted from Atlassian's *What issues are blocking me?* |
| I can't reproduce the issue locally. | You cannot reproduce it at all on your machine | Main version |
| I can only reproduce the issue occasionally. | You can sometimes reproduce it | Different: the problem is there but unstable |
| This will probably take two to three days. | Giving an approximate duration | Main version |
| I can give you a rough estimate now. | Requirements are not confirmed, but you offer a rough number | Different: you are offering a number first |

## Say it again with different content

Read the Chinese, say it yourself, then check the reference.

1. 我上週把付款流程修好了。
   - *I fixed the payment flow last week.*
2. 我還在等測試結果。
   - *I'm still waiting for the test results.*
3. 這個明天之前可以完成。
   - *I can finish this by tomorrow.*
4. 我卡在部署這一步。
   - *I'm stuck on the deployment step.*
5. 你有空可以幫我看一下這份文件嗎？
   - *Could you take a look at this doc when you have a moment?*
6. 這個大概需要一週左右。
   - *This will probably take about a week.*

## A short dialogue

This is a practice example I wrote, not a real conversation.

> A: Any updates?
>
> B: I fixed the login flow yesterday. Today I'm working on the permissions, but I'm stuck on the permission settings.
>
> A: What's blocking you?
>
> B: I can't reproduce the issue locally. Could you walk me through how this part works?
>
> A: Sure, after this meeting. How long will the rest take?
>
> B: It will probably take two to three days. I need to confirm the requirements before I give you an estimate.

## Overall

Of the ten, *by* for deadlines, *stuck on*, and *take* + time are worth practicing first, because they come up every day and a word-for-word translation from Chinese tends to go wrong. Almost all the sources are learner's dictionaries, which support meaning and sentence patterns. Only the Atlassian page describes what a stand-up covers. There was no native-speaker testing and no frequency comparison, so this post only says "works in this situation".
