> 🌏 [中文版](/posts/learning/2026-10-08-interview-english-from-teaching-materials)

My two earlier interview posts ([general patterns](/en/posts/learning/2026-10-07-interview-experience-english-en) and [my own stories](/en/posts/learning/2026-10-07-interview-agent-stories-english-en)) both started from the Chinese, and I then checked a dictionary for the pattern. For the situations themselves, the only thing I had read was one commercial job-search site. This time I changed the method: I read interview teaching materials and guidance first to see what they cover, then compared that with what I was missing.

## What I was trying to say

I read the body text of eleven sources this time. They fall into a few groups:

- Language teaching materials: three episodes of BBC Job Applications, two episodes of BBC Office English, one British Council article and four episodes of You're Hired, and the interview episode of Business English from Australia's ABC
- Government careers guidance: the common questions page and the STAR page from the UK's National Careers Service
- Universities: the STAR page from MIT's career advising office
- Official employer guidance: Amazon's interview process and Leadership Principles, and Google's interview preparation page
- Community resources for software interviews: four pages of the Tech Interview Handbook
- Management publications: one HBR article on questions to ask an interviewer. The original is behind a paywall, and what I read is a reposted copy on an association's website

Comparing the functions these sources cover with my original twenty-four interview cards, I found three things.

**First, there were many functions I had not practiced at all.** Small talk, why I am leaving, why I want this job, strengths, weaknesses, the Task part of STAR, asking for the question to be repeated, asking about the next step, saying thank you, following up after the interview: I had no card for any of them.

**Second, nobody demonstrates how to talk about failure.** Six sources list a question like "tell me about a time you failed", but none of them shows how to answer it in an interview. I had four cards about failure, all written by me from dictionary patterns. The materials are no help here.

**Third, demonstrations with engineering content come from almost a single place.** The sample answers in the language teaching materials are all set in marketing, sales, or administration. Only the Tech Interview Handbook has engineering content.

The function covered by the most sources is asking the interviewer questions, with eight sources. Next is "why do you want to work for this company", with seven.

## What you can say in this situation

Each sentence is marked as quoted from a source or as a source sentence with my own content swapped in. The content I swapped in uses only facts already written in the two earlier posts.

### 1. Opening and describing my current job

| What I want to say | What works in this situation | Relation to the source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 很高興今天能和您談，謝謝給我這個機會。 | I'm really excited to be speaking with you today. Thank you so much for the opportunity. | Quoted |
| 我負責提升我們 AI agent 產品的可靠性。 | I'm responsible for improving the reliability of our AI agent product. | I filled in the content |
| 我想把自己的能力用在新的挑戰上。 | I'd like to apply my skills to a new challenge. | Quoted |

The opening line comes from the Australian ABC [interview episode](https://www.abc.net.au/education/learn-english/business-english-ep4-interviews/101876886). In the British Council's [You're Hired Episode 5](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/sites/podcasts/files/LearnEnglish-You-re-hired-Episode-05.pdf), there are two interviewers, and the candidate says *Hello, it's very nice to meet you both*.

The BBC episode [Describing your job](https://www.bbc.com/learningenglish/english/features/office-english/260330) separates two versions: to a friend you can say *I mostly work on…*, and in a formal setting you say *I'm responsible for…*. The same episode teaches *I have a background in …, which in practice means …*, for giving the field first and then what you actually do.

I chose *improving* on purpose. My job is to optimize a product that already existed, and saying *building* would inflate my role.

For the reason for changing jobs, the same BBC episode has *I'd like to apply my skills to a new challenge*. The candidate in You're Hired says *I've come as far as I can in my current position… and would love to take on some more responsibility*.

### 2. Three parts of STAR

STAR is Situation, Task, Action, Result. The UK's [National Careers Service](https://nationalcareers.service.gov.uk/careers-advice/interview-advice/the-star-method) has three complete sample answers labelled part by part, the clearest model I read this time.

| Part | What I want to say | What works in this situation | Source wording |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Situation | 在我現在的工作裡，團隊希望 agent 能跨對話記得使用者。 | In my current job, the team wanted the agent to remember users across conversations. | *in my previous digital marketing job, the company wanted to get more people to sign up to a newsletter* |
| Task | 我的任務是找出處理大型附件又不浪費成本的方法。 | My job was to find a way of handling large attachments without wasting money. | *my job was to find a way of getting more people to sign up* |
| Result | 因為這樣，團隊把上限定在 10 MB。 | Because of this, the team set the limit at 10 MB. | *Because of this, the relocation was completed on time and on budget* (British Council) |

The three sources open the Situation sentence in very similar ways: the National Careers Service uses *in my previous … job*, the British Council [interview article](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/level/improve-your-english-level/how-prepare-job-interview-english) uses *In my last role*, and the BBC episode [Selling yourself](https://www.bbc.com/learningenglish/english/features/office-english/240325) uses *when I started in my current role*.

For the Result sentence, the sample answers in the materials all carry a concrete outcome or a number. The BBC version is *through my actions we saw a ten percent increase in productivity*.

I did not make a card for the Action part, because its content differs for every story. The sources demonstrate it with *I organised a meeting with…*, *I sat down with them and we talked about…*, and *I spoke to … to ask him why…*. The MIT STAR page and [Indeed's STAR guide](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/interviewing/how-to-use-the-star-interview-response-technique), which I read earlier, both say to use *I* rather than *we*, so the interviewer can see what you did yourself.

For the project you are proudest of, the line in You're Hired is *the thing I'm most proud of professionally*, and the BBC has *I'm really proud of my record on…*. I made one card from this: *The thing I'm most proud of professionally is the memory work for our agent.*

### 3. When I did not catch the question

| What I want to say | What works in this situation | Relation to the source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 不好意思，可以請您再說一次題目嗎？ | Sorry, can you repeat the question? | Quoted |

This comes from the BBC episode [Interviews part 1](https://www.bbc.com/learningenglish/english/features/job-applications/240923), where a BBC recruiter being interviewed says: *don't be afraid to say 'sorry, can you repeat the question'*. The other sources only give advice, not a sentence, so this card has this one source only.

### 4. Questions for the interviewer

| What I want to say | What works in this situation | Relation to the source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 如果我加入團隊，你們最希望我解決的問題是什麼？ | What would be the most important problem you would want me to solve if I joined your team? | Quoted |
| 團隊目前面臨哪些工程上的挑戰？ | What are the engineering challenges that the team is facing? | The source has company/team; I took team |
| 這個職位平常的一天是什麼樣子？ | What does a typical day look like in this role? | Quoted |
| 這次面試之後，招募流程的下一步是什麼？ | What's the next step in the recruitment process after this interview? | Quoted |

The first three come from the [Tech Interview Handbook](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/final-questions/), and the fourth from the British Council interview article.

This group repeats across sources more than any other:

- Asking about challenges: the Tech Interview Handbook, the British Council (*What do you think the challenges will be for this role?*), and the reposted HBR page (*What are the biggest challenges that I might face in this position?*)
- Asking about a typical day: the Tech Interview Handbook, the National Careers Service (*what does a typical day involve?*), and the reposted HBR page
- Asking about the next step: the British Council and the reposted HBR page (*What are the next steps in the hiring process?*)

The reposted HBR page also has *Can you tell me about the team I'll be working with?* and *What's the most important thing I should accomplish in the first 90 days?*. I already put the first of these among a card's alternative versions in my previous batch, where its source was a different commercial site. It now has one more source.

### 5. Following up after the interview

| What I want to say | What works in this situation | Relation to the source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 想跟進一下，請問面試的結果如何？ | Can I just ask, following up, what the outcome of the interview is? | Quoted |

This comes from the BBC episode [After the interview](https://www.bbc.com/learningenglish/english/features/job-applications/241007). The recruiter being interviewed is describing aloud the gist of an email, not giving an email template, so if you write one you still need to add a greeting and a closing. The same episode also says you can ask for feedback: *please can I have some feedback on the interview*.

## What interviewers ask

I collected fifty-three questions this time and noted the source of each. These are the types that appear in two or more sources:

- Introducing yourself: *Tell me about yourself*
- Motivation: *Why do you want to work for X company?*, *Why do you want to leave your current/last company?*
- Projects: *What is a project that you are most proud of?*, *What is the most challenging aspect of your current project?*
- Failure: *Tell me about a time you failed.*
- Conflict: *Tell me about a time when you had a conflict with a co-worker.*, *Tell me about a time you had a disagreement with your manager.*
- Fit: *Tell me why you will be a good fit for the position.*

The wording above comes from MIT's STAR page and the Tech Interview Handbook's question bank. The full list is in my research notes, and I did not put all of it in this post.

## Can I also say it this way?

| English | Who you say it to and when | Still the same meaning? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I'm really excited to be speaking with you today. | Opening a one-to-one interview | Main version |
| It's very nice to meet you both. | There are two interviewers | Different: a greeting, with no thanks |
| I'm responsible for improving… | Formal setting | Main version |
| I mostly work on… | Informal setting | Yes, more casual (the BBC's distinction) |
| I'd like to apply my skills to a new challenge. | Explaining why you want to change jobs | Main version |
| I would love to take on some more responsibility. | Same situation | Different: the reason is wanting to take on more |
| What's the next step in the recruitment process? | Asking about the process | Main version |
| What are the next steps in the hiring process? | Same situation | Yes; one says recruitment and the other hiring, and the sources do not say it is a regional difference |

## Say it again with different content

Read the Chinese, say it yourself, then check the reference. The content comes from my own work.

1. 在我現在的工作裡，團隊需要可以信任的監控數字。
   - *In my current job, the team needed monitoring numbers it could trust.*
2. 我的任務是找出讓追蹤資料可靠的方法。
   - *My job was to find a way of making the trace data reliable.*
3. 因為這樣，檔案解析改到背景執行。
   - *Because of this, file parsing moved to background workers.*
4. 我最自豪的是我們 agent 的記憶功能。
   - *I'm really proud of the memory work for our agent.*
5. 你覺得這個職位的挑戰會是什麼？
   - *What do you think the challenges will be for this role?*
6. 可以請您給我一些面試的回饋嗎？
   - *Please can I have some feedback on the interview?*

Sentences 5 and 6 are quoted from the sources. The rest are source sentences with the content swapped.

## A short dialogue

This is a practice example I wrote. It is not a real interview, and not a dialogue from the materials.

> A: Thanks for coming in today.
>
> B: I'm really excited to be speaking with you today. Thank you so much for the opportunity.
>
> A: Tell me about a project you're proud of.
>
> B: In my current job, the team wanted the agent to remember users across conversations. My job was to find a way of doing that safely.
>
> A: And what was the result?
>
> B: Because of this, users can now see and delete what the agent remembers.
>
> A: Do you have any questions for us?
>
> B: Yes. What are the engineering challenges that the team is facing?

## Still to verify

- How to talk about failure in an interview: six sources ask the question, and none demonstrates an answer.
- Why I want to work for this company, strengths, weaknesses: the sources have questions and advice but very few sample sentences, so I made no cards for them this time.
- The exact wording of the HBR original: the article is behind a paywall, and I read the reposted page. The reposted page and the video of the same title have the same questions, but individual words differ.
- British and American differences in these phrases: no source addresses them.
- My actual years of experience: one interview card in an earlier post said "five years", which was an example value. I am not sure what number to give, so that card no longer states a number. The other example-value cards now use real content.

## Overall

Eight of the thirteen sentences are quoted from the sources, one of them by picking *team* from *company/team* in the original. The ones worth practicing first are the three STAR openers and the four questions for the interviewer, because every interview uses them and both groups repeat across several sources.

The most useful finding this time is not a sentence but the shape of the gap. The materials teach openings, motivation, and questions for the interviewer thoroughly, yet nobody demonstrates how to talk about failure or about the details of an engineering project. Those two are exactly what my interviews need most, so I still have to write them myself and find someone to look them over. As for the sources, the language teaching materials all come from British and Australian institutions, with no American speaking materials. There was no native-speaker testing and no frequency comparison.
