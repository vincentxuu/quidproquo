The [previous post](/posts/learning/2026-10-12-coding-interview-english-en) practiced what I say on my own initiative in a coding interview. This one deals with a different situation: the interviewer keeps following my answer further down, into places I did not prepare for.

## What I was trying to say

Three things usually happen to me under follow-up questions:

1. I need a few seconds to think but do not know how to ask for them, so I go silent
2. I am asked for a cause that I never verified and can only guess at, and I do not know how to say it without sounding like I am making things up
3. I am asked about a tool I have not used, or a weakness in my design is pointed out, and I do not know how to admit it

One thing to settle first: asking for time is allowed. [Google's interview prep page](https://www.google.com/about/careers/applications/interview-tips/) says so directly:

> Rephrasing questions or asking for clarity is okay, as is telling the interviewer you want a few moments to collect your thoughts

[Amazon's interview page](https://amazon.jobs/content/en/how-we-hire/interview-loop) says you can ask when you lack information:

> If you're asked a question and don't have enough context to give a strong answer, it's totally okay to ask for more information. If none is available, focus on how you'd approach the problem based on what you do know.

Both pages only say that you may do this. Neither gives a sentence to say. I found the sentences elsewhere.

## What you can say in this situation

### 1. Buying thinking time

| What I want to say | What works in this situation | Relation to the source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 讓我想一下。 | Let me think about that for a minute. | Quoted |
| 這個問題很有意思。 | That's an interesting question. | Quoted |
| 這個問題不容易，不過我試著回答看看。 | That's a difficult question, but I'll try and answer it. | Quoted |
| 我沒想過這個問題，不過我會說測試比較重要。 | I have never thought about that, but I'd say testing matters more. | Adapted |
| 我想花一點時間整理一下想法。 | I'd like a few moments to collect my thoughts. | Adapted |

The first four come from [IDP's IELTS speaking advice](https://ielts.com.au/australia/prepare/article-10-tips-ielts-speaking). IDP is one of the organizations that run IELTS, and the fifth tip on that page is called *Pause to think*:

> There is no harm in taking a brief pause to think about what to say. We all do it to process questions.

Its list also has *Let me see* and *That's a good point*. For the fourth sentence it gives only the opening, *I have never thought about that, but...*, and I filled in the second half myself.

**Note that this is advice for a speaking test, not teaching material for job interviews.** Using these sentences in an interview is my own application; my basis is that Google's page says asking for time is fine.

The sixth tip on the same page tells candidates to avoid fillers, and its list includes *Umm*, *Like*, *You know*, and also *Well*. Its reason is that fillers make the examiner think you cannot find the words, so it recommends the complete sentences above instead.

The fifth sentence turns Google's *you want a few moments to collect your thoughts* into the first person.

### 2. When you can only guess

| What I want to say | What works in this situation | Relation to the source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 我的猜測是快取的問題。 | My guess is that it's a caching problem. | Adapted |

[Oxford defines guess](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/us/definition/english/guess_2) as an attempt to give an answer or an opinion when you cannot be certain you are right, with the example *My guess is that we won't hear from him again*. The same entry also has *At a guess, I'd say there's a problem with the fuel pump*, which is another opening you can use.

This sentence is supported only by a dictionary pattern. It has no source from an interview setting.

### 3. Admitting you have not used it, admitting it is not elegant

| What I want to say | What works in this situation | Relation to the source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 我沒用過那個函式庫，不過我會先讀它的文件。 | I haven't worked with that library before, but I'd approach it by reading its docs first. | Adapted |
| 這個做法不太漂亮，但行得通。 | It's not very elegant, but it's workable. | Adapted |

The first half of the first sentence is quoted from [Northeastern University's interview guide](https://careers.northeastern.edu/resources/interview-type-technical/), in the passage headed *Don't bluff*: *Say so honestly: "I haven't worked with that library before, but I'd approach it by…"*. The approach in the second half is mine.

The second sentence comes from [Google's example interview video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XKu_SEDAykw). The interviewer asks what would happen, if the pair of numbers had to be returned, when no pair is found. The candidate describes a way to do it and then judges it unprompted: *I mean it's not very elegant but it's workable*. I dropped the opening *I mean*. The quotation is from the video's automatic captions.

### 4. After an answer, check whether they want more

| What I want to say | What works in this situation | Relation to the source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 需要我再多講一點背景嗎？ | Would you like more context? | Adapted |

Amazon's interview page advises: *A good tip: Pause after your answer and ask if your interviewer would like more context.* It gives no sentence, so I turned the advice directly into a question.

## Can I also say it this way?

| English | Who you say it to and when | Still the same meaning? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Let me think about that for a minute. | You need time to think | Main version |
| Let me see. | Same situation, shorter | Yes (from the same IDP list) |
| I'd like a few moments to collect my thoughts. | The question is open-ended and you need longer | Different: it states that you want a stretch of time, and is more formal |
| Sorry, can you repeat the question? | You did not catch the question | Different: it asks for a repeat, not for time (a BBC sentence, already a card in [an earlier post](/posts/learning/2026-10-08-interview-english-from-teaching-materials-en)) |
| My guess is that… | Offering a guess | Main version |
| At a guess, I'd say… | Same situation | Yes (an example from the same Oxford entry) |

## Say it again with different content

Read the Chinese, say it yourself, and then look at the reference version.

1. 我沒想過這個問題，不過我會說要看團隊。
   - *I have never thought about that, but I'd say it depends on the team.*
2. 我的猜測是少了索引。
   - *My guess is that the index is missing.*
3. 我沒用過那個框架，不過我會先做一個小東西。
   - *I haven't worked with that framework before, but I'd approach it by building something small first.*
4. 這個做法不太有效率，但行得通。
   - *It's not very efficient, but it's workable.*
5. 需要我講更多細節嗎？
   - *Would you like more detail?*

All five are source sentences with the content changed by me; none is quoted from a source.

## A short dialogue

This is a practice example I wrote myself. It is not a real interview and not a dialogue from any teaching material.

> A: Why do you think the page got slower after that release?
>
> B: Let me think about that for a minute.
>
> A: Sure, take your time.
>
> B: My guess is that it's a caching problem.
>
> A: We use our own caching library here. How would you get started with it?
>
> B: I haven't worked with that library before, but I'd approach it by reading its docs first.
>
> A: And the workaround you described earlier, what do you think of it now?
>
> B: It's not very elegant, but it's workable. Would you like more context?

## Still to verify

- **Whether the time-buying sentences work well in a job interview.** The four quoted sentences come from advice for a language test. The job interview sources only say that asking for time is fine and give no example sentence.
- **How to say that I have not verified something.** I wanted a sentence that separates what I have confirmed from what I am guessing. This time I only found the pattern for offering a guess.
- **How to ask when I lack information.** Amazon says you can ask for more information and gives no sentence.
- **The Cambridge exam handbook.** Many articles online quote *That's a good question* and *Well, let me think* and attribute them to a Cambridge exam handbook. I read the full text of the current B2 First handbook for teachers and the passage is not in it, so I did not cite it.
- **British and American differences.** No source explains any.

## Overall

Of the nine sentences, three are quoted from a source and six are adapted. The two to practice first are *Let me think about that for a minute* and *My guess is that…*: one buys time, and the other lets me keep going when I am not sure.

The evidence for this batch is thinner than for the earlier posts. The employers' official pages confirm that you may ask for time, ask questions, and admit what you do not know, but the sentences themselves come from language test advice and a dictionary. The only one actually said in an interview setting is *it's not very elegant but it's workable*, from Google's example interview.
