In 2021 the finance theorist William Bernstein published *The Delusions of Crowds*. In an interview he said that when he first read Charles Mackay's 1841 *Extraordinary Popular Delusions and the Madness of Crowds* in the early 1990s, it seemed irrelevant to "the relatively sedate capital markets of the time" — until the dot-com bubble made the script visible: "I had read the script and I knew how the movie ended."

But the same book ends colder than that. Mackay wrote a similar book 180 years ago and "that didn't seem to help very much"; Bernstein predicts with "near 100% certainty" more financial and religious manias, and the only hope he sees is that the human species evolves its central nervous system wiring. "Short of that, I just don't see how it's going to happen."

That is the question this series is built on: **why does knowing the script not help?** Not a morality tale about stupid people, but an explanation chain from historical cases to brain mechanisms to culture and institutions to platform algorithms — plus what contemporary research has actually corrected in the classics.

## Three honest warnings

**The list is Anglophone and old.** Two of the eleven were originally written in French and German and reach readers through English translations; the author and case circle is overwhelmingly European and American, and six books predate 1965. Contemporary medical literature on mass psychogenic illness is concentrated in South and Southeast Asia and Africa, and this series does not cover it — that is a gap, not a finding.

**The Traditional Chinese editions are a minefield.** *The Crowd* has four zh-TW translations and 30+ simplified ones, with a public edition-by-edition comparison; *Thinking, Fast and Slow*'s Hung Lan translation drew a 2013 return controversy; *Crowds and Power* is out of print in zh-TW; *The Paranoid Style* has no zh-TW edition; *The Constitution of Knowledge* has no Chinese edition at all. Each post states the edition situation and links the original where it matters.

**Layer 5's effect sizes are disputed.** On 5 June 2024 *Nature* published both sides on the same day: Budak et al. argue average exposure is low and concentrated in a fringe; Ecker et al. call that "selective reading of the available evidence" and say corrections are only partially effective. The series puts both sides side by side, with their evidence grades marked (a formal Perspective versus a comment with declared competing interests).

## How each post is built

One book per post, four fixed segments: **the original sentence** (English plus translation; books without a zh-TW edition are marked), **where it sits in the chain**, **what later research corrected** (criticisms included, no side picked), and **one criterion you keep**. Every post ends by asking the same question — "what does this mechanism look like in 2026 Taiwan?" — answered with editions, the fact-checking ecosystem, and trust data, not slogans.

## Three ways to read it

- **Full path**: order 0 through 11, all five layers.
- **Financial bubbles**: order 1 (Mackay) → 2 (Bernstein) → 6 (Kahneman).
- **Anti-intellectualism and post-truth**: order 7–8 (both Hofstadter books) → 9–11 (McIntyre, Rauch, Nichols).

This is a thematic reading map, not a strict course; one path alone is coherent.
