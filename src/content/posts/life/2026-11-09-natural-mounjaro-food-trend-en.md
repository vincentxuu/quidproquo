"Natural Mounjaro" (天然猛健樂) is a nickname that went viral on Threads in Taiwan in October 2026. It refers to a low-calorie meal of canned tuna in water, boiled egg, cucumber and plain yogurt, mixed together and wrapped in nori. People link it to the prescription weight-loss drug Mounjaro (active ingredient tirzepatide) because it keeps them full and off snacks. If a friend sent you the recipe and you are wondering whether to try it, here is the short version: the meal itself is fine, but the "Mounjaro" part has no pharmacological basis. It is a catchy name.

## What it is: a low-calorie nori wrap

In late October, [ELLE Taiwan](https://www.elle.com/tw/beauty/health/a74173750/threads-mounjaro-diet/) and [Women's Health Taiwan](https://www.womenshealthmag.com/tw/food-nutrition/recipes/a74212631/threads-popular-weight-loss-meal/) both ran pieces on it. The latter describes the method: dice or julienne the cucumber, drain the tuna, mix with the other ingredients, and wrap in a sheet of nori like a Japanese hand roll. Common add-ins are boiled egg, corn, plain (Greek) yogurt, or a few drops of olive oil. People also call it "natural weight-loss jab" or "poor man's Mounjaro."

A one-serving reference from dietitian Tsai Yi-Fang at [IQ Health](https://www.iqhealth.com.tw/natural-glp1-mounjaro-diet-seaweed-tuna-recipe/) (in Chinese):

- 2 to 5 small sheets of plain, unseasoned nori
- 100 to 150 g cucumber
- 80 to 100 g canned tuna in water, drained
- 1 boiled egg
- 50 to 80 g corn kernels
- 1 tablespoon plain Greek yogurt, or 1 teaspoon extra-virgin olive oil

She estimates the whole serving at roughly 225 to 300 kcal, depending on portions and seasoning.

The meal has a Korean precursor. In late April 2026, [Health Chosun](https://health.chosun.com/site/data/html_dir/2026/04/30/2026043003369.html) (in Korean) reported endocrinologist Woo Chang-yoon saying in an interview with a health outlet: "Eggs with olive oil, that is Wegovy." Korean social media then dubbed soft-boiled egg drizzled with olive oil, served with Greek yogurt and black pepper, "천연 위고비" ("natural Wegovy"), and [Channel A](https://v.daum.net/v/20260525200200039) (in Korean) ran a news segment on it in late May. According to IQ Health, Threads users in Taiwan turned that Korean version into the tuna-and-nori wrap about half a year later.

## Why it gets compared to Mounjaro

Tirzepatide acts on both GIP and GLP-1 receptors, suppressing appetite, increasing fullness and slowing gastric emptying. [UDN Health](https://health.udn.com/health/story/6032/9826435) (in Chinese) quotes Taiwan's FDA reminding the public that it is a prescription drug that requires a doctor's evaluation.

GLP-1 is a hormone your body already makes. According to a registered dietitian quoted by [WeightWatchers](https://www.weightwatchers.com/us/blog/natural-ozempic), the gut releases it after you eat; it tells the pancreas to release insulin, slows stomach emptying, and tells the brain you are full. Protein and fat are potent triggers, and fiber that gut bacteria ferment into short-chain fatty acids can also stimulate its release.

That is where the comparison comes from: a high-protein, high-fiber meal makes the gut release a bit more GLP-1, so it "feels like" Mounjaro. Dietitian Chiu Shih-Hsin told [TVBS Health](https://health.tvbs.com.tw/regimen/366981) (in Chinese) that the logic holds up, since tuna, eggs and yogurt are all quality protein that can "wake up the fullness mechanism with food."

## How food differs from the drug

| | "Natural Mounjaro" (food) | Mounjaro (drug) |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanism | Protein and fiber modestly raise the GLP-1 your gut releases | Mimics incretin hormones, acting on both GIP and GLP-1 receptors |
| Duration | Short-lived | One injection per week |
| Where weight loss comes from | Mostly the lower calories in one meal | Pharmacological appetite suppression and slower gastric emptying |
| Clinical evidence | Media cite small protein studies and personal anecdotes | Large randomized trial (SURMOUNT-1) |
| Access and risks | Supermarket ingredients; watch sodium, methylmercury and iodine | Prescription only; common GI side effects; contraindicated with a family history of medullary thyroid cancer |

The scale of the gap is clearest in trial results. Chiu cites SURMOUNT-1: after 72 weeks, the highest-dose group lost about 20.9% of body weight on average versus about 3.1% on placebo.

The high-protein research is much smaller. The study he cites had only 19 participants: doubling the share of calories from protein, with no calorie limit, made them eat less on their own. His reading is that high protein "makes it easier for you to do it," not "lets you lose weight without effort."

A rise in the hormone does not guarantee you eat less, either. Chiu relays another experiment in which participants drank a large dose of whey protein and their blood GLP-1 and PYY rose clearly, yet their free-choice food intake a few hours later did not drop. WeightWatchers says the same thing: protein and fiber can raise your own GLP-1, but the amount and duration are far below the drug, which acts for about a week per injection.

## Why people really did lose weight

Dietitian Yang Hsi-En told Women's Health it plainly: "This combo really does help you lose weight! But it isn't magic fat-burning. It's a cheap, filling tool that brutally creates a calorie deficit."

- **Calories cut by more than half.** A pork-chop bento or takeout spicy hot pot easily runs 800 kcal or more; this nori wrap is about 250 kcal, saving roughly 600 kcal a day.
- **Chewing and volume.** Cucumber is nearly all water and takes a lot of chewing; the tuna's protein digests slowly.
- **Early losses are mostly water.** The potassium in cucumber helps the body flush extra sodium and water, so the scale drops fast at first.

So "lost 2 kg in a week" reports are not evidence of fat loss.

A nutrition academic writing in [The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/what-is-oatzempic-does-it-actually-work-for-weight-loss-269603) adds that no scientific study has tested the oatzempic drink. [Virta Health](https://www.virtahealth.com/blog/natural-mounjaro-recipes-whats-behind-the-viral-hype) and WeightWatchers also mention apple cider vinegar drinks and berberine supplements being sold under similar names.

I did not find a recipe craze in mainland China. What circulates there is a different form: e-commerce "food-grade GLP-1" and "oral natural GLP-1" slimming products, mostly pressed candies or solid drinks. [Beijing Daily](https://xinwen.bjd.com.cn/content/s67ec8140e4b08edd28f70704.html) (in Chinese) quotes Yang Li, deputy director of pharmacy at Beijing Tiantan Hospital: the ingredients of such products are often unclear, some may contain illegal additives such as sibutramine, and the side effects resemble the discomforts common in dieting, so they are hard to notice.

The pattern is the same everywhere: take a famous drug name, add "natural" or "poor man's," and attach a cheap recipe anyone can make. Argentina's "Mounjaro de los pobres" is nearly the same name as Taiwan's 窮人版猛健樂, "poor man's Mounjaro." Professionals respond the same way too: put the food or herb next to the drug and the scale is different. India's [Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/lifestyle/health/is-there-such-a-thing-as-natural-ozempic-doctor-explains-why-no-food-can-mimic-what-a-weight-loss-drug-does-101739767347187.html) relays the bluntest version from UK NHS surgeon Karan Rajan: "Ozempic is a drug. Broccoli is broccoli."

Next time you see a "natural + drug name" recipe, check three things:

1. **Drop the name and look only at ingredients and portions, then estimate the calories of one meal.** If it is very low, you already know where most of the effect comes from.
2. **Look at what it uses as proof.** Personal anecdotes have no control group and do not rule out water changes.
3. **Look at how long and how much it asks you to eat.** If it asks you to live on one thing all day, worry about calories and nutrients.

## How to eat it sensibly

- **Replace one meal a day.** Yang suggests swapping out a greasy takeout meal first, dinner in particular. Eating this for two or more meals can push your daily total under 800 kcal, which risks muscle loss and fast regain once you return to normal eating.
- **Add some fat and starch.** A few drops of extra-virgin olive oil or a quarter avocado, plus 2 spoonfuls of corn or diced sweet potato, keeps you full longer and cuts the urge for fried chicken or bubble tea at bedtime.
- **Pick tuna in water and plain nori.** Oil-packed cans are higher in calories, and Korean sesame-oil nori is high in sodium. Chiu also warns that cans, nori and seasonings stack up sodium fast, so people with high blood pressure or kidney disease should be careful.
- **Rotate the tuna.** Tuna is a large fish and eating it daily builds up methylmercury; Yang suggests swapping in shrimp, shredded chicken breast, edamame or tofu on 2 to 3 days a week. Pregnant, trying-to-conceive and breastfeeding people should be more conservative.
- **If you have hyperthyroidism, limit the nori.** Nori is relatively high in iodine; adjust the amount as your doctor or dietitian advises.

## When to see a doctor

If you have obesity or a medical need to lose weight, a doctor can evaluate which approach suits you. UDN Health quotes a pharmacist: Mounjaro commonly causes nausea, diarrhea, vomiting, constipation and abdominal pain, and the dose is raised gradually from a low start; it is contraindicated for people with a family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma or multiple endocrine neoplasia type 2; and weight tends to rebound after stopping if good eating habits were not built. This meal replaces neither the evaluation nor the drug.

## Bottom line

"Natural Mounjaro" is a low-calorie, adequately protein-rich, chewy meal that works well in place of a greasy takeout lunch or dinner. Treat it as a tool for cutting the calories of one meal and it does its job. Chiu's conclusion works as the instructions: "Eat it wisely and it's a decent fat-loss meal; expect it to work like a drug and you'll be disappointed."
