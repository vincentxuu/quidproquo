[The previous piece](/posts/policy/2026-08-06-taiwan-drone-regulation-guide-en) covered who needs a licence. This one covers **how you get one**: how many tiers, how long, how much.

Start with the most commonly misunderstood point: **most people don't need one at all.** Individuals flying under 2kg recreationally are exempt ([though 250g and above still requires registration](/posts/policy/2026-08-06-taiwan-drone-regulation-guide-en)). The people who genuinely need one are individuals flying 2kg or heavier, and **anyone flying under a company's name** — a drone owned by a legal entity requires a professional licence regardless of weight.

## Three licence classes

| Class | Age | How to get it | What it permits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Student | 14 | **No test**, issued on application | Learning to operate under 25kg with a general/professional holder supervising |
| General | 18 | **Written test** | Individually owned, 2kg to under 15kg with navigation equipment |
| Professional | 18 | Experience + **medical exam** + **written and practical** | Drones owned by legal entities / agencies / schools, and individually owned 15kg and above |

The student licence gets overlooked but is the best value on the list: **available at 14, no test, NT$250**, and it makes supervised practice legal. Anyone planning to take the practical test should get this first.

## Professional tiers have two dimensions

Most people only see one of them.

**Dimension one: weight bracket (basic tiers)**

| Tier | Maximum takeoff weight |
|---|---|
| I (may be annotated "under 2kg") | Under 25kg |
| II | 25kg to under 150kg |
| III | 150kg and above |

**Dimension two: restriction waivers (advanced tiers, three groups)**

Advanced tiers exist to **waive operating restrictions**, grouped per Article 99-14 of the Civil Aviation Act:

| Group | Waives |
|---|---|
| **G1** | Night flight, beyond-visual-line-of-sight operation, flight above 400 feet |
| **G2** | Dropping or spraying objects (agricultural spraying, delivery) |
| **G3** | Flight over crowds or outdoor assemblies |

**Two things that must be stated plainly:**

1. **You test for as many groups as you need.** Night flying plus dropping means G1 and G2 — two practical tests, two fees.
2. **An individual holding an advanced licence still cannot waive anything.** Under the regulations, only government agencies, schools, and legal entities may apply for waiver permits, and the application must be filed **15 days before the activity** with an activity plan; the permit is valid **3 months** (6 months for agriculture-registered entities, 1 year for agencies performing official duties). For an individual, an advanced licence means "qualified to perform such missions when employed by a legal entity," not "allowed to shoot at night."

## Sequential qualification: no skipping tiers

Professional licences must be climbed in order. Annex 9 of the regulations sets the experience thresholds:

- For **advanced Ia** (no weight annotation) or **basic II**: hold basic I (no weight annotation) for **1 month or more**
- For **advanced Ib**: hold advanced Ia (no weight annotation) for **1 month or more**
- For **advanced IIc**: hold both basic II and advanced Ib for **3 months or more each**
- For **basic III**: hold advanced IIc for **3 months or more**
- For **advanced IIId**: hold basic III for **1 month or more**

Stack those requirements and **reaching the top bracket realistically takes 18 months to two years**. That's by design, not administrative slowness — so if your career plan needs an advanced licence, start accumulating the prerequisite months early.

## The written test

- **Format**: online question bank, computer-administered, score shown immediately
- **Pass mark**: **80 out of 100**
- **Four subjects** (per Annex 9): the Civil Aviation Act and related regulations, basic flight principles, meteorology, emergency handling and flight decision-making
- **Validity**: for a professional licence, the practical test must be completed **within one year** of passing written, or the written result lapses

The CAA publishes [the test specification and question bank](https://www.caa.gov.tw/Article.aspx?a=3821&lang=1). Read the specification before drilling questions — regulations carry the heaviest weighting and get amended on a rolling basis, so memorizing an old bank will bite you. (in Chinese)

## The practical test

**You supply your own aircraft.** Per Article 21, candidates must bring a drone meeting Annex 12 specifications. Basic-tier reference airframe specs (per CAA guidance):

| Configuration | Reference airframe |
|---|---|
| Fixed-wing | Rectangular wing, span ≥180cm, chord ≥28cm, takeoff weight ≥6.5kg or meeting Ib spec |
| Helicopter | Rotor radius 70–90cm, takeoff weight ≥4.5kg or meeting Ib spec |
| Multi-rotor | Diagonal motor axis distance >90cm, takeoff weight ≥4kg or meeting Ib spec |

**Content** covers pre- and post-flight checks, mission setup, mission execution, mission conclusion, and emergency procedures.

The two things candidates most commonly fail on are **attitude mode** (manual flight without GPS assistance) and **route orientation**. Neither is a written trap — they're capability gates. When GPS drops, the aircraft switches to attitude mode automatically, so unfamiliarity means encountering it for the first time under test conditions.

**Retests**: failed sections may be retested **30 days after** receiving results.

## Medical examination

Professional licences only. Per CAA guidance:

- Standard cases use the **passenger-car driving licence registration form** under the small passenger vehicle medical criteria — **the motorcycle licence form is not accepted**
- Must be performed at a **medical institution designated by the highway monitoring authority** or a teaching hospital (or via civil aviation personnel medical certification)
- Covers left, right, and binocular vision, night vision, and visual field
- Only operators of drones **150kg and above** need the Class B aviation personnel standard
- Bring the **original certificate, valid within one year**, on practical test day (not required for endorsements or retests)

## Fees: here are the statutory numbers

Circulating fee figures contradict each other — I've seen "NT$500 practical test," "NT$500 certificate," and "NT$250 certificate" in different guides. The authority is **Annex 17, Fee Schedule** of the Remotely Piloted Drone Management Regulations:

| Item | Fee |
|---|---|
| Drone registration | NT$50 |
| Student licence | NT$250 |
| General licence | NT$250 |
| Professional licence | NT$500 |
| Written test | NT$200 |
| Basic-tier practical test | NT$1,200 |
| Advanced practical test | **NT$1,200 per group** |
| Inspection certificates | NT$500 |

Totaled by common path:

```
General licence      written 200 + certificate 250              = NT$450
Professional basic   written 200 + practical 1,200 + cert 500    = NT$1,900
Each advanced group  practical 1,200                             = NT$1,200/group
```

**These are statutory fees only** — they exclude the medical exam, training courses, and the cost of supplying your own test aircraft. The last two are where the real money goes. A machine meeting the basic-tier reference spec (multi-rotor with >90cm diagonal, 4kg+ takeoff weight) is not cheap on its own.

## Full timeline

```
Register the drone (250g and above)
      ↓
[optional] Student licence (age 14, NT$250, no test) → supervised practice
      ↓
Written test (NT$200, 80 to pass)
      ↓
   ┌──────────────┴──────────────┐
   │                             │
General licence            Medical exam (car-licence standard)
apply within 30 days              ↓
pay NT$250                 Practical test (NT$1,200)
                           ※ within 1 year of passing written
                                  ↓
                           Apply within 30 days, pay NT$500
                           (one 30-day extension available)
                                  ↓
                           Accumulate months → climb tiers / add G1–G3
```

Licences are valid **3 years**, renewed **within 3 months before expiry**. General licence renewal **requires no retest**; professional renewal requires a fresh medical exam and a renewal test (written under 25kg, written plus practical at 25kg and above).

## Where the test centres are

Per CAA guidance, approved practical test sites are in **Taipei, New Taipei, Taoyuan, Miaoli, Taichung, Yunlin, Tainan, Yilan, Hualien, and Pingtung**, with more under evaluation. Written tests are available in the north, center, and south.

All registration, changes, results, and issuance happen online through the [Drone Management Information System](https://drone.caa.gov.tw/). Withdrawals must be filed **4 days before** the test date; miss that and you re-register and re-pay after the test date passes. (in Chinese)

## Three practical judgments

1. **Confirm you actually need one.** Individuals under 2kg flying recreationally are exempt. Taking the general test to "feel more professional" buys privileges you won't use unless you fly 2kg or heavier.
2. **If you're going professional, start accumulating months early.** The sequential system makes time an incompressible cost. NT$1,900 is not the barrier; 18 months of prerequisites is.
3. **Fees are cheap; the test aircraft and practice are not.** NT$450 or NT$1,900 makes it easy to underestimate the commitment — a spec-compliant airframe plus enough practice hours is where the spending actually happens.
